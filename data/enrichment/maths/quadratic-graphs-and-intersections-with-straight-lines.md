# Enrichment dossier — quadratic-graphs-and-intersections-with-straight-lines

**CCEA** GCSE Mathematics (2210), strand NA · statement **M7-NA-10** · introduced **M7**, examined **M7 and M8** · tier **H** · calculator: either · difficulty 4
**CCEA statement:** generate points and plot graphs of simple quadratic functions and use these to find approximate solutions for points of intersection with lines of the form y = mx + c
**CCEA Teacher Guidance example:** solve y = 5x − 6 and y = x² by drawing the graph of each function
**Prerequisites in our taxonomy:** `plotting-quadratic-graphs` (M6-NA-09), `simultaneous-equations-graphically` (M6-NA-08)
**Sibling topic:** `graphical-solution-of-quadratic-equations` (M8-NA-09) — the harder "which line should I draw?" version
**Compiled** 19 September 2026 · 45 minutes
**Headline** This content is **Foundation tier on AQA and Edexcel** and **Higher tier on CCEA**. That is unusually good news: the other boards' Foundation material is written for learners meeting the idea for the first time, which is exactly the register our note needs, and there is a lot of it. The best single borrowing is the **subtraction layout** for "which line do I draw" — set the target equation under the given curve and subtract like column arithmetic — because our own examiner says only the strongest candidates could do that step, and it is the one part of this topic with a reliable procedure.

## 2. Teaching angles worth recreating (in our own words)

1. **The subtraction layout for "which line do I draw".** *Third Space Learning's GCSE guide "Solving quadratic equations graphically" states the principle — manipulate the equation so one side matches the function already graphed — and it is the same move Corbettmaths makes in video 267d.* What is worth recreating is the **layout**, not the sentence: write the given curve on one line, the target equation (with 0 on the right) directly beneath it, rule a line, and subtract, exactly like a column subtraction. What is left is the line to draw. CCEA's own M8 Teacher Guidance gives the worked case — given the graph of y = 6x − x², solve 2 + 5x − x² = 0 — and the subtraction produces y = x − 2. Our examiner says only the strongest candidates reached this step; a layout they can copy is worth more than an explanation they cannot.

2. **The solutions are the x-coordinates, and there are usually two of them.** *A discipline Third Space Learning, Maths Genie and Corbettmaths all impose, and which our own examiner evidence demands: "intersections rarely given; roots not both given".* The crossing is a point; the answer is a number. Draw the dashed line **down from each crossing to the x-axis** every single time, in every figure, and make the answer line ask for two values. This one habit is worth two marks on a three-mark part.

3. **Graphical answers are estimates, and that is allowed.** *Third Space Learning names this as the method's defining limitation.* CCEA's own statement says "approximate solutions". Say plainly that she is reading off the grid, that the expected accuracy is about half a small square, and that a question which wants exact roots will say "solve algebraically". This removes the anxiety that makes candidates abandon a correct reading.

4. **Two curves, or one curve and one line — it is the same question.** *CCEA's Teacher Guidance for M7-NA-10 is "solve y = 5x − 6 and y = x² by drawing the graph of each function", which is the simultaneous-equations framing; AQA and Edexcel file the same content under A19, simultaneous equations.* Making that link explicit — "where two graphs cross, both equations are true at once, which is what 'simultaneous' means" — joins this topic to `simultaneous-equations-graphically` (already a prerequisite in our taxonomy) and to `simultaneous-equations-linear-and-non-linear` in M8. One idea, three topics.

5. **Predict the number of crossings before drawing anything.** *A move Dr Frost's decomposition style encourages: separate "how many solutions?" from "what are they?".* Slide a line up and down a fixed parabola and there are only three outcomes: two crossings, one (the line just touches), or none. Asking "how many solutions will this have?" before any reading turns a mechanical exercise into a thinking one, and it is how a question about "no real solutions" becomes answerable.

6. **The plotting mistakes are arithmetic mistakes.** *Third Space Learning's quadratic-graphs page lists four, and two of them are number errors, not graph errors: squaring a negative wrongly — writing (−3)² as −9 — and ignoring the sign of the x² coefficient, which decides whether the curve opens up or down.* Its fix for the first is to use brackets on the calculator. The third is drawing a pointed vertex instead of a smooth turn. All three belong in our diagnostics as named distractor routes, because each produces a *specific* wrong table and therefore a specific wrong curve.

## 3. The representation to rebuild as our own SVG

**Hero figure: the curve, the line, the two crossings, the two readings.** Build it as a generator; it serves this topic, its two prerequisites and its M8 sibling.

`viewBox` about `0 0 760 620`.

- **Axes** with arrowheads, x from −2 to 7, y from −4 to 12, labelled `x` and `y` at the ends. **Gridlines every 1 unit with five small squares per labelled step** — the grid must match the plotting granularity because the reading tolerance is half a small square.
- **The parabola** y = ax² + bx + c drawn as a smooth path from computed sample points — never hand-authored control points, and never with a pointed vertex.
- **The straight line** y = mx + c across the full width.
- **The two intersection points** ringed with small open circles, with their coordinates computed from the quadratic formula (in the generator only).
- **From each crossing, a dashed vertical down to the x-axis**, ending in a short solid tick on the axis, with the x-value printed below the axis in a boxed label.
- **A caption strip** at the foot: *the solutions are the x-values where the graphs cross:* `x = …` *and* `x = …`.
- **Generator parameters:** `quadratic: {a, b, c}`, `line: {m, c}`, `showLine: boolean` (off, for "draw the line yourself"), `showDrops: boolean`, `showReadings: boolean`, `xRange`, `yRange`, and `gridStep`. Assert that both intersections lie inside the drawn window, and assert no emitted coordinate is `NaN` when the discriminant is negative.
- Accessibility: the line and the curve distinguished by **dash pattern plus an end-of-line label** (`y = x²`, `y = 5x − 6`), never by colour; `<title>` naming both graphs and saying the crossings are ringed.

**Second figure: the subtraction layout.** `viewBox` about `0 0 680 260`. Three lines of mathematics, right-aligned on the equals signs, laid out as a column subtraction:

```
        the curve you are given      y = 6x − x²
  the equation you must solve      0 = 2 + 5x − x²      (subtract)
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────
        the line to draw             y = x − 2
```

with a ruled line before the answer and a bracket to the right labelled *so draw y = x − 2 on the same axes and read the x-values where it cuts the curve*. Rendered as SVG text with a drawn rule, not as a table. **Generator parameters:** `curve` and `target` as coefficient triples, with the line computed by subtraction, so the same generator makes every worked example, every twin and every faded version, and no coefficient is ever typed twice.

**Third figure: three crossings, one crossing, none.** Three small panels side by side, the *same* parabola in each, with the line at three heights. Captions: *two solutions · one solution (the line just touches) · no real solutions*. Twenty lines of SVG, and it is the whole answer to "explain why this equation has no solutions".

## 4. Practical variants — not applicable; the equivalent is calculator and grid discipline

There is no practical here, but there is a procedural equivalent worth recording, because CCEA examines this topic on **both** papers of the completion units:

- **M7/M8 Paper 1 is non-calculator**, so the table of values must be computed by hand — which is precisely where the (−3)² = −9 error lives. On **Paper 2** the fix is bracket discipline on the calculator, exactly as Third Space Learning recommends.
- **Reading tolerance:** half a small square of the printed grid, never a whole one. Our own FM2 review set this rule; apply it to every `points-line` and `curve` answer spec in this topic.
- **The line must actually be drawn.** Our examiner's first complaint is that the line was not drawn at all. If our item is a `graph` part, make drawing the line its own scored element rather than folding it into the reading.

## 5. Question types the other boards use that CCEA also rewards

1. **"On the grid, draw the graph of y = … and use it to solve …" [3].** The standard shape on every board. The mark-scheme habit: **one mark for the correct line drawn, one for identifying both intersections, one for both x-values within tolerance.** Our own Summer 2024 M7 Paper 1 Q10 evidence says all three were lost separately — the line was not drawn, the intersections were rarely given, and when roots were given only one appeared. So author it as three scored elements with three distinct feedback messages, not one all-or-nothing numeric.
2. **"By drawing a suitable straight line, solve …" [3–4].** The Higher/M8 version: the line is not given. Marks for the correct line, then the readings. This is where the subtraction layout earns its place. AQA and Edexcel both flag it as Higher-only ("including those that require rearrangement"); CCEA sets it at M8.
3. **"Use the graph to estimate the solutions of … Give your answers to 1 decimal place." [2].** Two marks, one per root. The accuracy instruction in the stem is what lets the engine enforce the written form, so use those words when the accuracy is the point.
4. **"Explain why the equation … has no solutions" [1–2].** Read off the three-panel figure: the line does not cross the curve. The phrase the schemes want is **no real solutions**, and "there are no solutions" is the near-miss worth an explicit reject.
5. **"Write down the coordinates of the turning point" [1].** A11 at Foundation on the other boards, and CCEA reaches it through symmetry (its M7-NA-09 guidance says "using symmetry, the turning point"), **not** through completing the square. Keep the symmetry route and mark the completing-the-square route `notonspec`.

**Encoding note for the author, from the G7 rules.** A two-root answer is never a numeric spec holding the first root: encode as kind `algebraic`, latex `x=2, x=3`, equivalence `equivalent`, variables `["x"]`. A coordinate pair of intersections is encoded as `algebraic` with latex written as tuples, `(2, 4), (3, 9)`, variables `["x", "y"]`. Both engine behaviours exist precisely for this topic.

## 6. Misconception framings worth borrowing

| Misconception | The framing that fixes it | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Giving the coordinates of the crossing instead of the x-values | The crossing is a **place**; the solution is a **number**. Drop a dashed line to the x-axis every time and read from there | our Summer 2024 M7 finding ("roots not both given"); universal in the guides |
| Giving only one root | A line usually cuts a parabola **twice**. Before answering, count the crossings | our Summer 2024 M7 finding |
| Not drawing the line at all | The line is a scored element. If the question names a line, it is asking you to draw it | our Summer 2024 M7 Paper 1 Q10 finding |
| Not knowing which line to draw | The subtraction layout: curve, minus target, rule, line | our Summer 2024 M8 Paper 1 Q4 finding ("only the strongest") |
| (−3)² read as −9 | Brackets on the calculator; on Paper 1, say "negative times negative is positive" out loud while filling the table | Third Space Learning names this explicitly |
| A pointed vertex | A parabola turns smoothly. If your curve has a corner, one of your table values is wrong | Third Space Learning |
| Forgetting the sign of the x² coefficient | Positive opens upwards, negative opens downwards. Decide the shape **before** plotting, then use it to check the finished curve | Third Space Learning |
| "There are no solutions" for a negative discriminant | The phrase is **no real solutions**, and graphically it means the line misses the curve | Third Space Learning notes the distinction matters |
| Treating a graphical answer as exact | The statement says *approximate*. Read to half a small square and say so | CCEA's own wording of M7-NA-10 |

Our taxonomy records two examiner entries on this topic (Summer 2024 M7 and Summer 2023 M8). The Summer 2023 one is positive — graphical simultaneous equations were **over 60% correct** on M8 Paper 1 Q2 — and the honest "In the exam" panel should say so: the reading is not the hard part; the drawing and the completeness are.

## 8. Scope note

**Beyond CCEA — `notonspec` if it appears:**
- **completing the square**, including to find the turning point (AQA A11 Higher, A18 Higher). CCEA reaches the turning point by **symmetry**
- **iteration** to find approximate solutions (A20, Higher on both boards) — this sits beside our content in every AQA/Edexcel Higher "solving equations" page
- the **discriminant** b² − 4ac as a named test for the number of roots. Use the count of crossings instead; the algebraic test belongs to `quadratic-formula-and-harder-quadratic-equations` only as far as CCEA takes it
- **transformations of quadratic graphs** (A13 Higher: sketch translations and reflections of a given function)
- sketching **cubic, reciprocal, exponential and trigonometric** graphs (A12 additional/Higher) — CCEA covers cubic and reciprocal in `recognising-and-sketching-linear-quadratic-cubic-and-reciprocal-graphs`, but not in this topic
- **solving a linear/quadratic pair algebraically** — that is M8-NA-07, a different CCEA topic

**CCEA-specific:**
- the tier placement: **Higher (M7, grades B–E; M8, grades A\*–C)** where AQA and Edexcel set the same codes at Foundation
- the phrase **"approximate solutions"** in the statement itself, which licenses grid-accuracy answers
- the split between M7-NA-10 (the line is given) and M8-NA-09 (work out which line to draw) as **two separate topics**, which no other board separates
