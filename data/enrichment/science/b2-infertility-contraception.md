# Enrichment dossier — b2-infertility-contraception

**CCEA** Double Award Science, B2 §2.3 Reproduction, Fertility and Contraception · outcomes 2.3.6 (**Higher only**), 2.3.7 (Foundation) · tier mixed · difficulty 2 · no prescribed practical
**Prerequisite in our taxonomy:** `b2-sex-hormones-menstrual-cycle`
**Compiled** 19 September 2026 · 45 minutes
**Headline** CCEA classifies contraception as **mechanical / chemical / surgical**. AQA and Edexcel classify it as **hormonal / non-hormonal** or **hormonal / barrier**. Every borrowed resource will therefore sort the same methods into the wrong boxes for her mark scheme. The fix is a representation that does not sort at all: draw the **chain of events pregnancy needs**, and show where each method cuts it. The chain explains the mechanism (which is where the marks are), and the CCEA category can then be attached as a label.

## 2. Teaching angles worth recreating (in our own words)

1. **The chain, and where each method cuts it.** *Synthesised from AQA §4.5.3.4, which describes every method by its mechanism rather than its category, and from Oak National Academy's KS4 contraception lesson, whose first learning point is "hormones disrupt the menstrual cycle to prevent pregnancy".* Pregnancy needs a complete chain: an ovum matures → it is released → sperm are deposited → sperm reach the ovum in the oviduct → fertilisation → implantation. Break any link and there is no pregnancy. The pill and the implant break **link 1/2**; the condom breaks **link 3/4**; female sterilisation breaks **link 4** by cutting the oviduct; male sterilisation breaks **link 3** by cutting the sperm tube. This is one picture that answers every "explain how X works" part on the paper.

2. **Four columns, always the same four.** *Oak's contraception lesson compares methods across mechanism, effectiveness, risks and — the column everyone forgets — STI protection.* CCEA's outcome is the only one of the three boards that puts STI protection inside the contraception statement, so that column is not optional for us. Build the comparison table with exactly those four columns and never vary them, so a new method slots in without new learning.

3. **"Effective" is a claim about people, not about chemistry.** *Oak names three misconceptions at once: that the pill protects against STIs, that all contraception is 100% effective, and that hormonal methods have no side effects.* The second is the one with teaching value: the pill's failure rate is dominated by missed doses, not by the drug failing. That single fact makes the evaluate part answerable, and it is honest.

4. **IVF as six ordered steps, with the hormones only at step 1.** *Oak's "Using hormones to treat infertility" sequences IVF as: give hormones to mature several eggs → trigger release → collect the eggs → mix with sperm in the laboratory → let the fertilised eggs divide into balls of cells → transfer one or two into the uterus.* AQA §4.5.3.5 gives the same order. CCEA's 2.3.6 is the same three-clause statement compressed. Teach the six steps; the compression is the revision card, not the lesson.

5. **Evaluate means both sides plus a decision.** *AQA flags §4.5.3.4 and §4.5.3.5 against WS 1.3 and WS 1.4 — "make decisions based on the evaluation of evidence and arguments" — and its own drawback list for IVF is deliberately three items long.* CCEA's QWC and "discuss" parts reward the same structure. Give her a three-part skeleton in the "In the exam" panel: one benefit with a reason, one drawback with a reason, one sentence that actually decides, referring to the person in the question. That skeleton is worth more than any content here.

6. **Name the ethical frame without taking a side.** *AQA's phrasing — issues that "cannot be answered by science alone" — is the right teaching sentence for a 16-year-old in Northern Ireland,* where some of this is locally contested. Present the positions as positions held by people, give the biology accurately, and let the answer be hers. CCEA's own wording, "can raise ethical issues for some people", is deliberately neutral; match it.

## 3. The representation to rebuild as our own SVG

**Hero figure: "One chain, four cuts."** `viewBox` about `0 0 1000 430`.

- **A single left-to-right chain of six linked boxes**, each numbered, drawn as rounded rectangles joined by short connectors:
  1. an ovum matures in the ovary
  2. the ovum is released (ovulation)
  3. sperm are deposited in the vagina
  4. sperm travel to the ovum in the oviduct
  5. fertilisation — one zygote
  6. implantation in the uterus lining
- **Below the chain, four intervention markers**, each drawn as a short vertical stem rising to the connector it cuts, with a small break symbol on the connector itself (two short parallel strokes across it — not a colour, not an emoji):
  - **the pill / the implant** → cuts between 1 and 2, label "changes hormone levels, so no ovum develops or is released" and the CCEA tag **chemical**
  - **the condom** → cuts between 3 and 4, label "a barrier: sperm never reach the ovum" and the tag **mechanical**, with a second line "also stops the pathogens that cause HIV and chlamydia passing between partners"
  - **male sterilisation** → cuts between 2 and 3 on the sperm side, label "the sperm tube is cut, so sperm cannot leave" and the tag **surgical**
  - **female sterilisation** → cuts between 3 and 4 on the ovum side, label "the oviducts are cut, so ovum and sperm cannot meet" and the tag **surgical**
- **Generator parameters:** `methods: string[]` so a gate can show the chain with one cut hidden and ask which method belongs there; `showTags: boolean` to hide the mechanical/chemical/surgical labels for a retrieval version; `extras: boolean` to add the greyed IUD cut between 5 and 6 with a `notonspec` caption, for a learner who has read an AQA page.
- The chain's boxes 1–2 should be visually aligned with the day axis of the cycle figure in `b2-sex-hormones-menstrual-cycle` if both appear in one note, so the two diagrams read as one story.

**Second figure: the IVF flow.** `viewBox` about `0 0 960 260`. Six numbered panels in a row, each a small line drawing with a one-clause caption: ① hormones given, several follicles in one ovary; ② ova collected with a fine needle; ③ ova and sperm together in a dish; ④ a fertilised ovum; ⑤ a ball of cells after two or three days; ⑥ one or two balls of cells placed into the uterus. A bracket under ① and ② labelled "this is the only part hormones do"; a bracket under ③–⑤ labelled "*in vitro* — in glass, outside the body", which is the etymology and the whole name. Add a small side box with the three honest drawbacks (stressful, success rates are not high, multiple births carry risk), because those three are marks on both CCEA and AQA.

## 4. Practicals CCEA also examines

None prescribed, and there is no meaningful laboratory activity here on any board. The real transferable skill CCEA does examine in this area is **reading a success-rate table or bar chart** — IVF live-birth rate against the mother's age is the standard data set, and a "describe the trend, then suggest why" pair is a legitimate Unit 7 Booklet B shape. Build one such item from figures we can cite, or from clearly-labelled invented data; do not reproduce a copyrighted chart.

## 5. Question types the other boards use that CCEA also rewards

1. **"Explain how the contraceptive pill prevents pregnancy" [2].** AQA's default. The mark-scheme habit: **one mark for the hormonal change, one for the consequence** ("changes hormone levels" + "so no ovum matures or is released"). A candidate who writes only "it stops you getting pregnant" scores zero, and the feedback should name the missing half.
2. **"Evaluate the use of IVF" [4–6, or QWC].** AQA and Edexcel both use "evaluate"; CCEA rewards the same structure and bands it. Author it as a `text-long` QWC part with indicative points split into benefits, drawbacks and a decision, and make the band descriptors require the decision sentence for the top band.
3. **"Give one advantage of using a condom rather than the pill" [1].** The answer CCEA wants is the STI one. Low tariff, high discrimination, and it is the item most likely to be missed by a learner who revised from an AQA page.
4. **Classification item.** "Which of these is a chemical method of contraception?" as an `mcq`, with distractors drawn from the other two CCEA categories. This exists precisely because the borrowed resources use different categories, and it is our job to inoculate her.
5. **Ordered list.** "Put the stages of IVF in order." `order` answer kind; six items; a direct use of the second figure.

## 6. Misconception framings worth borrowing

| Misconception | Framing | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The pill protects against STIs | Only a barrier stops something *passing between* people. The pill changes what happens inside one body | Oak lists this first among its contraception misconceptions |
| All contraception is 100% effective | Most failures are people, not chemistry — a missed pill, a late one, an illness. Say it once and it makes the evaluate part answerable | Oak |
| Hormonal methods have no side effects | Name two plainly and move on; over-claiming either way costs trust | Oak |
| IVF usually works | Success rates are well under half per cycle and fall with the mother's age. This is also the data item in §4 | Oak; AQA's own drawback list |
| IVF lets parents choose a baby's traits | Embryo testing looks for disease, not traits. One sentence, and it is `notonspec` for CCEA but worth saying because she will have heard the claim | Oak |
| "Sterilisation" is one operation | Two different operations cutting two different tubes; the chain figure shows them at different links | follows from the representation |
| Sorting methods into hormonal/non-hormonal | CCEA marks mechanical, chemical and surgical. Give the CCEA tag on every method, every time | verified against CCEA 2.3.7 and AQA §4.5.3.4 |

No CCEA examiner finding is recorded against this topic in our taxonomy, so the "In the exam" panel must not invent one; the honest content there is the tariff pattern, the command words and the classification trap.

## 8. Scope note

**Beyond CCEA — `notonspec` if used:**
- **FSH and LH by name**, in contraception or in fertility treatment (AQA names both; CCEA says "hormones")
- **clomifene** (Edexcel CB7.8 only)
- diaphragms, intrauterine devices, skin patches, injections, spermicides, abstinence as a named method (all AQA §4.5.3.4; CCEA names only condoms, the pill, implants and sterilisation)
- the mechanism "inhibits FSH production" for the pill — CCEA's wording is "change hormone levels and stop ovum development"
- embryo screening and trait selection; egg freezing; surrogacy
- named STIs beyond HIV and chlamydia

**CCEA-only or CCEA-specific:**
- the **mechanical / chemical / surgical** classification
- the **STI link inside the contraception outcome** (HIV and chlamydia named)
- "transfer of several embryos into the uterus" as the CCEA phrasing of the last IVF step
- 2.3.6 being **Higher only**: a Foundation learner needs 2.3.7 fluently and can leave IVF to a `mustknow` aside
