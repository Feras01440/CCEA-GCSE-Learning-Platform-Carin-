# Enrichment dossier — c2-alcohols-fermentation

**CCEA** Double Award Science, **C2** §2.5 Organic Chemistry · outcomes **2.5.20, 2.5.21 (H)**, 2.5.22 (F) · tier mixed · difficulty 4 · no prescribed practical
**CCEA must-recall:** **Higher** — alcohols `CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH` with the **–OH** functional group: methanol CH₃OH, ethanol C₂H₅OH, propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol (liquids). **Higher** — complete combustion of an alcohol gives CO₂ and water; incomplete gives CO (and C) and water. **Fermentation** — sugar solution + **yeast**, **25–37 °C**, **anaerobic** (air excluded); yeast enzymes convert sugar to **ethanol and carbon dioxide**.
**Prerequisite in our taxonomy:** `c2-homologous-series-alkanes`
**Compiled** 20 September 2026 · 30 minutes
**Headline** The alcohols themselves are in **AQA Chemistry 8462 §4.7.2.3 only**, and Edexcel 1SC0 touches fermentation in a single clause inside **CC7.8** ("enzymes are used in the production of alcoholic drinks"). So the organic half is separate-science material. The important scope note is a tier one: **CCEA puts the alcohols at Higher and fermentation at Foundation**, which is unusual and means a Foundation learner needs the yeast process fluently and the homologous series not at all. Our Summer 2024 C2 Higher evidence names the real difficulty plainly: **organic chemistry — general formulae, naming, drawing full structural formulae — is the topic candidates struggle with most**.

## 2. Teaching angles worth recreating (in our own words)

1. **One functional group, one family, one general formula.** *AQA 8462 §4.7.2.3's framing, and the reason the homologous-series idea is worth reusing.* Alcohols are alkanes with an H swapped for an **–OH**. That single substitution gives `CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH`, explains why they are liquids where the small alkanes are gases, and lets her generate any member from its carbon count. Since `c2-homologous-series-alkanes` has already established the pattern idea, this is a second application of a model she owns rather than a new one.

2. **Propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol: the number says where the OH is.** *CCEA names both isomers, which is more specific than AQA's list and is a Higher discriminator.* Three carbons in a row; the –OH on the end carbon is propan-1-ol, on the middle carbon propan-2-ol. Drawing both side by side with the position numbered makes the naming convention visible, and it is the only place in CCEA chemistry where positional isomerism appears.

3. **Combustion is the alkane rule with the same products.** *AQA's general combustion treatment (§5.9.3.1 for hydrocarbons) transfers directly.* Complete: carbon dioxide and water. Incomplete: carbon monoxide and/or carbon (soot) and water. The alcohol's own oxygen atom means slightly less oxygen is needed from the air, but the products are the familiar ones — so this half is a transfer, not new learning, and should be taught as such.

4. **Fermentation is four conditions, and each one has a reason.** *The Foundation half, and the one CCEA examines in detail where the other boards do not.* **Sugar** (the raw material), **yeast** (supplies the enzymes), **25–37 °C** (warm enough for the enzymes to work fast, cool enough not to denature them), and **anaerobic — air excluded** (oxygen would let the yeast respire aerobically to CO₂ and water instead of making ethanol). Pairing each condition with its reason turns a list of four into an argument, and the temperature reason reuses `b1-enzyme-factors` directly.

5. **The airlock is the anaerobic condition made visible.** *A standard demonstration in both chemistry and biology teaching.* A bubbler lets carbon dioxide out and keeps air from getting in. Naming what the apparatus is *for* explains both the "anaerobic" requirement and the CO₂ product in one object, and it is a diagram CCEA can ask her to label.

6. **Say it is the same reaction the biology lesson calls anaerobic respiration.** *Supported by AQA 8464 §4.4.2.1 and by CCEA's own `b1-anaerobic-respiration`.* Yeast respiring without oxygen produces ethanol and carbon dioxide; the chemist calls it fermentation and the biologist calls it anaerobic respiration. Making that explicit is cheap and it converts two half-remembered topics into one.

## 4. Practical variants CCEA also examines

No prescribed practical on this outcome, but fermentation is one of the cheapest real experiments in C2 and CCEA examines its method points:

| Variant | Source | What is measured | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fermentation with an airlock**, counting bubbles per minute | standard in both chemistry and biology schemes | rate of CO₂ production | Gives a rate against temperature, which reuses `c2-measuring-rates` and `b1-enzyme-factors` in one activity |
| **Limewater test on the gas** | CCEA `c2-carbon-dioxide-preparation` | qualitative | Confirms the CO₂ product and links two C2 topics |
| **Mass loss on a balance** | AQA/Edexcel rate practicals | mass of CO₂ escaping | An alternative measure, and a good "suggest another method" part |
| **Distilling the product** | AQA 8462's alcohol-as-fuel material | — | **Beyond CCEA**; name it only as what happens next industrially |

The transferable Unit 7 demands: **controlling temperature**, **excluding air and saying why**, and **explaining a trend in a rate against temperature graph** — including why the rate falls again above about 40 °C, which is the enzyme link.

## 5. Question types the other boards use that CCEA also rewards

1. **"Name this alcohol / draw the structure of propan-2-ol" [1–2].** Higher. CCEA's Summer 2024 evidence says naming and drawing full structural formulae is the biggest difficulty in organic chemistry, so set both directions.
2. **"Write the molecular formula of the alcohol with four carbon atoms" [1].** Uses the general formula rather than recall.
3. **"Write an equation for the complete combustion of ethanol" [2].** Balancing with the alcohol's own oxygen is the trap; our `equation` answer spec is case-sensitive, as chemistry is.
4. **"State the conditions needed for fermentation" [3].** Yeast, warm temperature with a stated range, absence of air. One mark each.
5. **"Explain why the mixture must be kept away from air" [2].** The anaerobic reason — with oxygen the yeast respires aerobically and no ethanol is made.
6. **"Explain why the rate of fermentation falls above 40 °C" [2].** The enzyme link, and a genuine synoptic item.

## 6. Misconception framings worth borrowing

| Misconception | The framing that fixes it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Structural formulae drawn with wrong bonding | Every carbon needs four bonds. Count them before moving on | **CCEA S2024 C2 Higher** — naming and drawing named as the hardest thing in organic chemistry |
| propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol treated as the same | The number gives the carbon the –OH is attached to. Draw both together and number the chain | CCEA names both; AQA does not |
| "Alcohol" means ethanol only | Ethanol is one member of a family; methanol is toxic, and both are alcohols | AQA 8462 §4.7.2.3's homologous-series framing |
| Fermentation needs oxygen | It is **anaerobic**. Oxygen would give aerobic respiration and no ethanol | CCEA `mustRecall`; the airlock figure |
| Any warm temperature will do | 25–37 °C: warm enough for the enzymes, not hot enough to denature them | CCEA's stated range; links to `b1-enzyme-factors` |
| Yeast is a chemical | It is a living organism supplying enzymes. That is why temperature and pH matter | the biology link |
| Combustion products differ for alcohols | Same products as hydrocarbons: CO₂ and water complete, CO and carbon incomplete | AQA's general combustion treatment transfers |
