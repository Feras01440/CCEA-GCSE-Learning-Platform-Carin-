# Enrichment dossier — c2-aluminium-extraction

**CCEA** Double Award Science, **C2** §2.7 Electrochemistry · outcomes 2.7.4, 2.7.5 · tier **F** · difficulty 4 · no prescribed practical
**CCEA must-recall:** **alumina (Al₂O₃)** is purified from the ore **bauxite** and dissolved in molten **cryolite** to **lower the melting point**; electrolysed with **graphite** electrodes. **Aluminium forms at the cathode**; **oxygen at the anode reacts with the graphite** (`C + O₂ → CO₂`) so the anodes **burn away and must be replaced** periodically. **Recycling** aluminium uses only a fraction of the energy of extraction and saves waste.
**Prerequisite in our taxonomy:** `c2-electrolysis-molten-salts`
**Compiled** 20 September 2026 · 30 minutes
**Headline** AQA §5.4.3.3 is a good match and supplies the two explanation prompts CCEA marks — *"explain why a mixture is used as the electrolyte"* and *"explain why the positive electrode must be continually replaced"* — which are exactly the two hardest marks in CCEA's QWC question. But the vocabulary diverges: **"bauxite" returns zero hits in AQA 8464, AQA 8462 and Edexcel 1SC0**, and CCEA's Summer 2025 evidence records **bauxite confused with haematite**. The other recorded errors are equally precise and equally fixable: **cryolite lowers the melting point of the alumina, not of the aluminium**, and the **anode reaction `C + O₂ → CO₂` was omitted**.

## 2. Teaching angles worth recreating (in our own words)

1. **Three names, three different things — sort them before anything else.** *CCEA Summer 2025 C2 Foundation records **bauxite confused with haematite**, and "bauxite" appears in no other board's specification, so this must be taught from CCEA alone.* **Bauxite** is the *ore* dug out of the ground; **alumina (Al₂O₃)** is the purified aluminium oxide got from it; **cryolite** is a *different compound* added to the melt. Haematite is the iron ore and belongs to `c2-rusting-and-iron`. Put all four in a sorting gate at the start; the confusion is a naming problem, not a chemistry one.

2. **Cryolite lowers the melting point of the alumina — say whose melting point.** *CCEA Summer 2025 records exactly this error, plus "boiling versus melting point".* Aluminium oxide melts at over 2000 °C, which would be ruinously expensive to maintain. Dissolving it in molten cryolite gives a melt at a much lower temperature, so **less energy is needed**. The sentence must name the substance: *cryolite lowers the melting point of the aluminium oxide*. AQA's own prompt — "explain why a mixture is used as the electrolyte" — is the same mark, and its wording is a good model.

3. **The anode burns away, and the equation is the reason.** *CCEA records the anode reaction being omitted; AQA asks candidates to "explain why the positive electrode must be continually replaced".* Oxygen is produced at the anode. The anode is made of carbon. At that temperature the two react: `C + O₂ → CO₂`. So the anodes are consumed and have to be replaced, which is part of the cost. The chain — oxygen forms → the anode is carbon → they react → the anode wears away — is four links and the equation is the middle of it.

4. **Which electrode gets which, from the previous topic's one sentence.** *Reuses `c2-electrolysis-molten-salts` angle 3 exactly.* Al³⁺ is positive, so it goes to the **negative cathode**; O²⁻ is negative, so it goes to the **positive anode**. Nothing new is needed, and saying so out loud is the point — this topic is the previous one applied to an industrial case.

5. **Recycling wins on energy, and the number is worth having.** *CCEA's own wording — "only a fraction of the energy" — and Edexcel CC4.10 asks for an evaluation.* Melting scrap aluminium takes a small fraction of the energy that electrolysing alumina does, because the hard part — tearing aluminium away from oxygen — has already been done. Framing recycling as *skipping the expensive step* explains the saving instead of asserting it.

6. **Why electrolysis at all — one sentence borrowed from AQA.** *AQA §5.4.3.3 states it plainly and CCEA has the same reasoning in `c2-displacement-and-extraction`.* Aluminium is **too reactive** to be extracted by heating its oxide with carbon, so electricity has to do the work instead. That single sentence links this topic to the reactivity series and answers "why is aluminium expensive?" in the same breath.

## 4. Practical variants CCEA also examines

No prescribed practical; industrial electrolysis cannot be done at school. What is examinable:

- **The molten lead bromide demonstration** from `c2-electrolysis-molten-salts` is the laboratory model — same principle, visible products. A question can ask her to relate the two.
- **Melting and recasting scrap aluminium** (foil, drink cans) is a genuinely doable demonstration of angle 5, and produces the contrast the energy bar shows.
- CCEA sets this as a **QWC extended answer** (Summer 2025), so the transferable **Unit 7** demand is **describing an industrial process in a logical sequence with reasons**, not apparatus handling.

## 5. Question types the other boards use that CCEA also rewards

CCEA's Summer 2025 evidence shows this set as a **QWC / extended-response** item, which is the shape to author.

1. **"Describe how aluminium is extracted from its ore" [6, QWC].** Indicative points: bauxite purified to alumina; dissolved in molten cryolite to lower the melting point; electrolysed with graphite electrodes; aluminium at the cathode; oxygen at the anode; the anodes react with oxygen and are replaced. Band descriptors should reward the **sequence and the reasons**, not the vocabulary alone.
2. **"Explain why cryolite is added" [2].** Lowers the melting point **of the aluminium oxide**, so less energy is needed. Author "lowers the melting point of aluminium" as a named `commonError`.
3. **"Explain why the anodes have to be replaced regularly" [3].** AQA's own prompt. Marks for oxygen forming, for the carbon reacting with it, and for the equation.
4. **"Give two advantages of recycling aluminium" [2].** Energy and waste; Edexcel CC4.10 adds the economic framing.
5. **"Explain why aluminium is not extracted by heating its oxide with carbon" [2].** AQA §5.4.3.3's reactivity reasoning; links to `c2-displacement-and-extraction`.
6. **"Write the half equation for the reaction at the cathode" [2].** Higher-style, reusing the previous topic's method.
