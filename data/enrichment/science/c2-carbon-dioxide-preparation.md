# Enrichment dossier — c2-carbon-dioxide-preparation

**CCEA** Double Award Science, **C2** §2.9 Gas Chemistry · outcomes 2.9.8, 2.9.9 · tier **F** · difficulty 3 · **Prescribed Practical C6**
**CCEA practical C6:** investigate the preparation, properties, tests and reactions of the gases **hydrogen, oxygen and carbon dioxide**
**CCEA must-recall:** CO₂ — **calcium carbonate + hydrochloric acid**, collected by **downward delivery** (denser than air) or over water; uses: fizzy drinks, fire extinguishers. **CO₂ + water → carbonic acid** (weakly acidic, pH about 5–6). **CO₂ + limewater:** `Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O` (**milky**); with **excess CO₂ the precipitate redissolves**: `CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ → Ca(HCO₃)₂` (colourless).
**Prerequisite in our taxonomy:** `c2-hydrogen-oxygen-preparation`
**Compiled** 20 September 2026 · 32 minutes
**Headline** This topic carries **Prescribed Practical C6**, and the practical has **no equivalent on either board** — AQA §5.8.2.3 and Edexcel CC3.12b give the limewater *test* and nothing else. Our Summer 2024 C2 Foundation evidence lands squarely in the gap: **the thistle funnel, delivery tube and gas jar were not named or drawn correctly**, and "thistle funnel" and "downward delivery" both return **zero hits** in all three specifications. The second half is better still as a teaching opportunity: the **redissolving of the precipitate in excess CO₂** is examinable on CCEA and is taught by neither board — and it is the part that makes limewater more than a colour change.

## 2. Teaching angles worth recreating (in our own words)

1. **One apparatus generator, three gases — and the collection method is the variable.** *Reuses the bench figure from `c2-hydrogen-oxygen-preparation` and is the reason to build it parameterised.* The reaction vessel and delivery tube are the same; what changes is where the gas goes. Hydrogen and oxygen are insoluble, so **over water**. Carbon dioxide is **denser than air**, so it can be collected by **downward delivery** into an upright gas jar — the gas sinks and pushes the air out of the top. Choosing the method *from a property* is the transferable skill, and it is what Prescribed Practical C6 examines across all three gases.

2. **The thistle funnel has a job, and its tip must be under the liquid.** *CCEA Summer 2024 records it not being named or drawn correctly, and no borrowed resource contains the word at all.* It lets acid be added without opening the flask and letting the gas escape. Its **tip must dip below the acid surface**, otherwise the gas simply comes back up the funnel instead of going down the delivery tube. That "why" is what makes the drawing detail memorable rather than arbitrary, and it is a natural find-the-mistake item.

3. **Limewater going milky has a substance behind it.** *Both boards stop at "turns milky"; CCEA gives the equation.* The milkiness is solid **calcium carbonate** forming — `Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O`. Naming the white solid converts an observation into chemistry and sets up the next angle, which is the part that separates candidates.

4. **Keep bubbling and it goes clear again — and that is examinable.** *CCEA-only: `CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ → Ca(HCO₃)₂`.* With **excess** carbon dioxide the calcium carbonate reacts further to form **calcium hydrogencarbonate**, which is **soluble**, so the cloudiness disappears. Presenting the pair as a two-stage story — *milky, then clear again* — is far more memorable than either equation alone, and it explains why a test must not be over-run.

5. **CO₂ dissolves to make an acid, and that is why fizzy drinks are sour.** *CCEA's 2.9.9 and its pH 5–6 figure.* Carbon dioxide + water → **carbonic acid**, a **weak** acid — which links directly to `c2-carboxylic-acids` and to AQA §5.4.2.5, where **carbonic acid is named as an example of a weak acid**. That cross-reference is worth making: the weak-acid idea she learnt there is the same idea here.

6. **Both uses follow from a property.** *CCEA names two.* **Fizzy drinks**: CO₂ is soluble under pressure and gives the sour tang of carbonic acid. **Fire extinguishers**: it is denser than air, so it blankets the fire and excludes oxygen, and it does not burn. Same one-property-one-use discipline as the previous topic.

7. **Limewater is also the carbonate test — say so once.** *AQA Chemistry 8462 §4.8.3.3 uses it that way, and CCEA meets carbonates in `c1-reactions-of-acids` and `c2-carboxylic-acids`.* Any carbonate plus an acid gives CO₂, which turns limewater milky, which is why the same test appears in three different topics. Naming the connection saves teaching it three times.

## 4. Practical variants CCEA also examines

**Prescribed Practical C6** covers all three gases — preparation, properties, tests and reactions — so this topic is where the practical is tagged and where its write-up belongs.

| Variant | Source | What changes | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Marble chips + dilute HCl, downward delivery** | CCEA's own | the named method | The apparatus is the marked object, and the thistle funnel is the recorded failure |
| **Collecting over water instead** | CCEA's own alternative | CO₂ is somewhat soluble, so a little is lost | A genuine "suggest why over-water collection is less good for this gas" item |
| **Marble chips + acid for a rate experiment** | AQA required practical 11; Edexcel CC7.1a | measures gas volume against time | Same reaction, different purpose — it is `c2-measuring-rates` and Prescribed Practical C4. Worth naming so the two are not confused |
| **Limewater over-bubbled deliberately** | CCEA 2.9.9 | milky then clear | The distinctive observation; do it on purpose rather than as an accident |
| **CO₂ poured onto a candle** | classic demonstration | the flame goes out | Shows "denser than air" and "does not support combustion" in one, and explains the extinguisher use |

**The Unit 7 demands CCEA marks here:** naming and drawing the apparatus in 2-D, having the **thistle funnel tip below the liquid**, choosing a collection method **and justifying it from a property**, and describing the limewater observation precisely.

## 5. Question types the other boards use that CCEA also rewards

1. **"Name the apparatus labelled A, B and C" [3].** The recorded failure; a `label` item on our own figure, with the thistle funnel among them.
2. **"Draw a labelled diagram to show how carbon dioxide can be prepared and collected" [4].** Marks for the reactants, a sealed flask with the funnel tip under the acid, the delivery tube, and an upright gas jar.
3. **"Explain why carbon dioxide can be collected by downward delivery" [1].** It is denser than air.
4. **"Describe the test for carbon dioxide and the result" [2].** Limewater turns milky. Both boards set this, so borrowed material is accurate here.
5. **"The gas was bubbled through limewater for several minutes. Describe and explain what was seen" [3].** CCEA-only. Milky first, then clear again as the precipitate redissolves in excess CO₂.
6. **"Write the equation for the reaction of carbon dioxide with limewater" [2].** Our `equation` answer spec is case-sensitive.
7. **"Explain why carbon dioxide is used in fire extinguishers" [2].** Denser than air and does not support combustion.

## 6. Misconception framings worth borrowing

| Misconception | The framing that fixes it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Thistle funnel not named or drawn | It has a job — adding acid without letting gas out — and its **tip must be under the liquid** | **CCEA S2024 C2 Foundation** |
| Gas jar drawn inverted for downward delivery | Upright, with the tube to the bottom, because the gas is denser than air and fills from below | the `wrongVersion` figure |
| Limewater "turns white" or "a precipitate forms" without naming it | It turns **milky**, and the solid is **calcium carbonate** | AQA §5.8.2.3's wording plus CCEA's equation |
| The clear result read as a negative test | Going clear again means **too much** CO₂, not none. Two stages, one story | **CCEA-only content** — neither board teaches it |
| Carbonic acid called strong | It is a **weak** acid, pH about 5–6 — the same idea as in `c2-carboxylic-acids`, and AQA §5.4.2.5 names carbonic acid as a weak acid | the cross-reference |
| Collection method treated as arbitrary | Choose it from a property: insoluble → over water; denser than air → downward delivery | the sorting card |
| This preparation confused with the rates practical | Same reaction, different purpose. C6 prepares the gas; C4 measures the rate | the practical table |
