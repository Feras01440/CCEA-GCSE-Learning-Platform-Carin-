## 2. The book's methods, in order (our words)

**B6a, washing line (the factor: leaf area).**
1. Water in leaves gives support and feeds photosynthesis in the palisade and spongy layers; it evaporates from the mesophyll into the air spaces and diffuses out through the stomata, which is transpiration. Expected: the smallest leaf loses least (p. 26).
2. Tie string between two stands. Take a small, a medium and a large leaf of **one species**. Weigh each to one decimal place and clip it to the line (p. 26).
3. After 24 hours, reweigh each and subtract: loss in mass (p. 26).

**B6b, bubble potometer (the factor: wind).**
1. Put the potometer under water in a sink so that no air is trapped, push the cut shoot in **under the water** so it fits tightly, then lift it out and clamp it (p. 29).
2. Open the tap and draw a little water into the syringe so an air bubble enters the end of the capillary at the start of the scale; close the tap; mark where the bubble is (p. 29).
3. Start the timer; after ten minutes read the bubble's new position with a ruler (p. 29).
4. Open the tap and push water in from the syringe to send the bubble back to the mark; close the tap; switch on a fan half a metre from the shoot; time ten minutes again (p. 29).
5. Rate per hour = distance in ten minutes × 6 (p. 30).

**B6c, weight potometer (the factor: temperature).**
1. Put 250 cm³ of water in a conical flask; push the base of a shoot through a one-hole bung into the water; seal round the bung with petroleum jelly (Vaseline); stand the flask on a balance and record the mass of flask and contents (p. 31).
2. Set up an identical second shoot. One flask goes to a warm room, the other to a cool room, each on its own balance, for 24 hours (p. 31).
3. Rate of water loss in g per hour = change in mass ÷ 24 (p. 32).

## 3. Where the book is better than our note

- **The washing line as a real experiment.** The book gives the set-up, the single species, one decimal place and the subtraction, and asks for one controlled variable, the independent and dependent variables, the trend and an explanation (p. 27). CCEA 2022 Higher Booklet A Q1(e)–(f): independent, surface area (size) of leaf; dependent, loss in mass (2 marks); trend, a bigger loss from a bigger leaf (1 mark). Our note has one sentence on the method; bundle item 0009 works a percentage loss for four leaves but asks no variable.
- **Leaf area as the factor**, which is the eGuide dossier's top gap for our note: here it has a whole practical.
- **Setting up the bubble potometer** (p. 29): assemble and insert the shoot under water so no air enters; a tight fit; mark the bubble's start; reset to the mark with tap and syringe. CCEA's 2024 report found the reset among the less well known parts; the 2024 scheme's 1 mark goes to using the syringe to drive the bubble back to the start. Our note says "open the tap on the reservoir" and mentions the syringe as an alternative; the engine accepts both (probe, 7 Oct). The underwater assembly and the tight fit are missing from our note.
- **The fan at a fixed distance** (0.5 m, p. 29): a controlled variable in the wind version, and a validity point.
- **Rates scaled to an hour** (pp. 30, 32): ×6 from a ten-minute reading, ÷24 from a day. Our note works its bubble rates per minute and never scales a reading to an hour. On our own figures: a bubble that moves 2.4 cm in ten minutes is moving at 14.4 cm per hour; a flask that loses 9.6 g in 24 hours loses 0.4 g per hour.
- **The weight potometer seals the flask round the bung with petroleum jelly** and weighs the flask, water and shoot together (pp. 31–32). Our note (and the 2021 CCEA paper) floats a layer of oil instead. Both stop water leaving except through the shoot; the book's Question 2 asks what the jelly is for.

## 4. Where the book is worse, and what it leaves out

- **No explanation of why the bubble moves**, and no direction (towards the shoot). Our note's airtight-column explanation is better.
- **Each condition run once.** No repeats, no mean, in any of the three methods.
- **Area is not the only difference between the leaves.** Leaves of different sizes can also differ in age and thickness, so three single leaves are not a clean test of area alone; the book never raises it. CCEA's 2022 question asks only for the trend, so this is an evaluation point for strong answers, not a mark.
- **Units on rates are incomplete**: the bubble table heads its rate column "per hour" with no length unit (p. 30); the weight table writes "g /hour" (p. 32). Our note's habit (a unit that falls out of the division, such as mm per minute) is the one to keep.
- **Only three factors** (area, wind, temperature) are run; humidity and light appear only in a list (p. 28).
- **No graph** of any kind; our note has four sketch graphs and a day-cycle graph.

## 6. Calculations and data tasks

- **Loss in mass** (B6a): starting minus final mass (p. 26).
- **Distance moved** (B6b): final minus starting position, in cm, in ten minutes; ×6 for per hour (p. 30).
- **Change in mass** (B6c): starting minus final mass of flask and contents; ÷24 for per hour (p. 32).
- CCEA's own extensions, which our bundle already has some of: which change had the greater effect, with data (2024 Higher Booklet B Q4(d); our items 0010, 0014); percentage increase (our items 0005, 0014).

## 8. Definitions and wording

- **Transpiration** (pp. 26, 28): evaporation from mesophyll cells, then diffusion through the air spaces and out at the stomata. Same as 2.1.4 and ours.
- **Uptake against loss.** The specification titles the weight potometer as measuring **uptake**; the book's B6c, correctly, calls the mass change the rate of water **loss**, because the shoot is weighed with the flask, so the balance registers only what leaves the whole system through the leaves. Uptake and loss are close but not equal (some water stays in the shoot). CCEA's 2021 scheme speaks of water lost from the leaves.
- **Book slips, not to copy:** "Repeat step 10" written at step 10 (it means step 9, p. 26); "Dedependent" and "surface are" (p. 27); "glasswear" (p. 28); "each beaker" for each flask in the rate rule (p. 32); the rate headings' missing units (section 4).

## 10. Pitfalls and precautions the book builds in

- One **species** for every leaf (p. 26); **identical** shoots for the two rooms (p. 31).
- Shoot inserted **under water**, a **tight** fit, no air in the apparatus (p. 29).
- **Mark** the bubble's start and return it to the same mark (p. 29).
- Seal round the bung so water leaves **only** through the shoot (p. 31).

## 12. Anything in our note the book suggests is wrong or misleading

**One conflict.** Our note (block [29]) says of the weight potometer that "the mass lost is the water taken up", and bundle item `q.science.b2.b2-transpiration-potometer.0008(b)` answers "so the only mass lost is the water the shoot took up". The book calls the measured change the rate of water **loss** by transpiration (p. 32), and CCEA's 2021 Higher scheme (Q3(a)(ii), 1 mark) accepts, beside stopping evaporation from the flask, the point that the leaves become the only route by which water leaves. The scheme and the physics side with the book: the balance weighs the shoot too, so it measures water transpired, which is close to, but not the same as, water taken up. Engine probe on 7 Oct (working tree): an answer making that point in the scheme's own words scores **0 of 1** on item 0008(b) (exact input in the git-ignored `scratchpad/probes/dossier-biology-practical/probe-scheme-phrasings.mts`), whose keyWords hold only forms of "evaporate". Teach: "the mass lost is the water lost through the leaves, which is about the same as the water the shoot takes up"; widen the item's acceptance to the scheme's alternative (credit-only).
