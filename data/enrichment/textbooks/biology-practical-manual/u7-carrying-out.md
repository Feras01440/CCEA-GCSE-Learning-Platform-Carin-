# u7-carrying-out: Practical Manual dossier (using apparatus, across the biology practicals)

**Book.** CCEA's GCSE Double Award Science Biology Practical Manual (an eGuide), © CCEA 2024, 34 printed pages (git-ignored under `docs/sources/textbooks/`). Page numbers are the book's printed numbers; the PDF page is the printed number plus 2.
**Ours.** `packs/science/content/u7/u7-carrying-out/note.blocks.json` and `bundle.json`, read in full on 7 Oct 2026.
**Spec.** Unit 7, Carrying out an experiment (our skill id U7.2.1): use apparatus correctly, skilfully and safely. Of the specification's apparatus list, the book handles the Bunsen burner with heatproof mat, tripod and gauze; beakers, conical flasks, boiling tubes and glass rod; measuring cylinders; quadrat, measuring tape and line transect; potometer; Visking tubing; electronic balance; ruler; stopclock; thermometer; and, under "any other", syringes, a cork borer, forceps, water baths, a light meter and pH and soil-moisture probes.
**Scheme evidence.** Booklet A tasks the book reproduces: 2021 (catalase), 2022 Higher (washing line), 2023 (food), 2025 (potato osmosis), all both tiers where held (`docs/sources/papers/science/<series>/U7-*-Biology-BkA-*`); 2026 Booklet A (paper only). Chief Examiner's reports 2023–2025, Unit 7 Booklet A.
**Verdict.** The book is **richer** than our note for biology apparatus: our note handles the food burn and the starch test well (its method matches the book's almost step for step) but has nothing on Visking tubing, the potometer, the quadrat and tape, the cork borer, the water bath or measuring froth with a ruler, all of which the book walks through. Our note is richer on reading instruments (the meniscus, smallest steps), on writing observations and on chemistry apparatus, which the biology book does not touch.

## 2. What the book teaches about each piece of apparatus (our words)

| Apparatus | Book's technique (page) |
|---|---|
| Forceps, hot water, ethanol | Every transfer of the leaf by forceps; ethanol tube stood in hot water at once; no flame (p. 4). |
| Clamp, stand, boiling tube, Bunsen, mounted needle | Tube clamped off-vertical; food lit in the flame and held under the tube until burnt out; relit if it goes out; wire-mesh dish with tongs as an alternative (p. 9). |
| Thermometer | Read before burning; stir, then read again at once (p. 9). |
| Syringes | Sized to the volume: 5 cm³ for 5 cm³ of enzyme, 10 cm³ for 10 cm³ of peroxide (p. 13); a 10 cm³ syringe on the potometer's side arm to set and reset the bubble (p. 29). |
| Water baths | Each with its own thermometer; tubes stand at least five minutes before mixing; the mixed tube goes back into the bath (p. 13). |
| Ruler | Held against the outside of the tube, zero at the liquid's surface, froth read in mm (p. 13); cylinders cut to 40 mm (p. 19); bubble position read in cm (p. 29). |
| Quadrat, tape, probes | Tape from the wood's edge outwards; quadrat at fixed distances; light, soil moisture and pH taken in the same quadrat (p. 16). |
| Cork borer and knife | On a tile; cylinders bored from one potato; skin trimmed from the ends (p. 19). |
| Electronic balance | One decimal place; blot first; weigh flask and contents together for the weight potometer (pp. 19, 31). |
| Visking tubing | Soaked; knotted at one end with a short tail; rubbed open; filled by syringe; knotted; dried outside; weighed (pp. 22–23). |
| Bubble potometer | Assembled under water; shoot pushed in under water for a tight fit; clamped; bubble let in at the start of the scale through the tap; start marked; reset to the mark (p. 29). |
| Conical flask, bung, petroleum jelly | Shoot through a one-hole bung, jelly sealed round it (p. 31). |

## 3. Where the book is better than our note

- **Five pieces of apparatus our note never handles**: Visking tubing, the potometer, the quadrat and tape, the cork borer, the water bath. All are on the specification's list (the first three by name, the others as general apparatus). CCEA's Booklet A tasks used water baths (catalase, 2021) and the cork borer (potato, 2025), and in 2026 a weighed tube of leaves under oil, a weight-potometer design; its Booklet B papers set the bubble potometer (2024 Higher), the quadrat (2019, 2021–2025) and weighed Visking bags (2026 Higher).
- **A ruler used on something that is not a length of solid**: the froth (p. 13). The reading has a stated zero (the liquid's surface), a direction and a unit. Our note's "read every instrument to its smallest step" has no example of where to put the zero.
- **The balance used as Booklet A uses it in biology**: to one decimal place, every time (pp. 9, 19, 26). The 2019, 2022, 2023 and 2025 Booklet A tasks all ask for masses, changes or averages to one decimal place and mark it. Our note's example balance reads to 0.01 g and its twist paragraph models two places; both are right for chemistry, but a biology example at one place would match the tasks she will sit.
- **Equilibrating in a water bath** before mixing (p. 13): technique and reason together (CCEA 2021 Foundation Booklet A Q1(g)).

## 4. Where the book is worse, and what it leaves out

- **No reading technique.** Nothing on the meniscus, eye level, or the smallest step; our note's measuring-cylinder See it is the better teaching, and CCEA's 2023 Foundation Booklet A Q1(f)(i) tested it (16 cm³, read at the bottom of the meniscus).
- **No observations in words.** The book asks for one observation (a Visking bag, p. 24) and two colours (p. 5) but never says how to write an observation. Our note's observation-against-deduction section is better, though all its examples are chemistry.
- **No taring.** CCEA's 2026 Booklet A tells candidates to zero the balance with an empty beaker on it before weighing a tube; the book never mentions it.
- **Safety stays generic** (see `u7-planning.md`, section 4).

## 10. Pitfalls and precautions the book builds in

- Forceps, not fingers, for a hot or ethanol-soaked leaf (p. 4).
- No flame near ethanol (p. 4).
- Five minutes in the bath; the mixed tube back in the bath (p. 13).
- Shoot inserted under water; no air bubbles in the potometer except the one being timed (p. 29).
- Dry the outside before every weighing (pp. 19, 22–23).

## 11. GAPS in our note, ranked

1. **Three biology apparatus sections are missing.** Add short `variant` sections, each explain → show → check: Visking tubing (fill, knot, dry, weigh; Your turn on why the outside is dried); the bubble potometer (assemble under water, tight fit, bubble in at the start, mark, reset; Your turn on how to reset, the 2024 report's weak point); the quadrat and tape (transect along a tape; random coordinates from two tapes at right angles; abiotic readings in the same quadrat).
2. **A ruler on froth.** One See it: where the zero goes, which way to read, the unit (mm), after a fixed time.
3. **A biology balance example at one decimal place** beside the chemistry two-place one, with the trailing zero (the 2025 report: a percentage written 13 where 13.0 was needed; the same rule holds for a mass).
4. **The water bath.** One sentence and a gate in the enzyme context: both solutions stand in the bath before mixing, and the reason.
5. **A biology observation.** Add one biology case to the observation section (a Visking bag after a day; a leaf after iodine), so observation-against-deduction is not chemistry-only.

## 12. Anything in our note the book suggests is wrong or misleading

Nothing. Our starch-test and food-burning sections already follow the book's method closely; the one difference is the light time before the starch test (our note says a day, as the book does; our b1 note says 48 hours; see `pp-b1`).
