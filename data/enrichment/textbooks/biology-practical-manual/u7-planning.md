# u7-planning: Practical Manual dossier (planning, across the biology practicals)

**Book.** CCEA's GCSE Double Award Science Biology Practical Manual (an eGuide), © CCEA 2024, 34 printed pages (git-ignored under `docs/sources/textbooks/`). Page numbers are the book's printed numbers; the PDF page is the printed number plus 2.
**Ours.** `packs/science/content/u7/u7-planning/note.blocks.json` and `bundle.json`, read in full on 7 Oct 2026.
**Spec.** Unit 7, Planning an investigation (our skill ids U7.1.1–U7.1.8): variables; a hypothesis; a method; a risk assessment; apparatus suited to accurate results; a results table with headings and units; a diagram of the apparatus; steps for validity and reliability.
**Scheme evidence.** CCEA U7 Biology papers, both booklets, 2019 and 2021–2025 (`docs/sources/papers/science/<series>/U7-*-Biology-*`), the 2026 papers (no schemes yet), and the Unit 7 sections of the Chief Examiner's reports 2023–2025 (`docs/sources/science/examiner-reports/`). The book has no planning chapter: everything below is gathered from its ten investigations.
**Verdict.** The book is **richer** than our note **for biology**: our planning note teaches every skill through physics and chemistry examples (personal power, moments, density, a polystyrene cup, a gas syringe) and only three biology contexts (a catalase hypothesis, protease validity, a quadrat estimate). The book supplies a biology example for nearly every planning skill, and two ideas our note lacks: the control tube, and the precautions that make a biology measurement valid. It is thinner on hypotheses, risk and diagrams, where it models poor habits.

## 3. Where the book is better than our note

- **Biology examples for each planning skill.** Our note's variables See it is a metre rule; its method See it is a chemistry cup; its table See it is moments; its apparatus diagram is a gas syringe. A Booklet B biology plan (the 2021 crisp six-marker; the 2019 and 2025 fieldwork six-markers; the 2026 crisp six-marker) needs biology habits: the volume of water and the distance of the food as controls, the key in fieldwork, the equilibrated bath.
- **The control tube.** Our note teaches controlled variables only. The book's catalase Question 2 is the other meaning: a whole extra tube with the enzyme removed or killed, the volume matched. CCEA pays a mark for the matched volume (2021). Our bundle item 0014(b) asks this once, in an amylase context, for one mark.
- **Precautions named for what they protect.** The book's methods carry the validity precautions CCEA rewards: equilibrate (2021 Foundation Booklet A Q1(g)); blot dry (our osmosis bundle item 0009(a)); seal so water leaves only through the shoot (2021 Higher Booklet B Q3(a)(ii)). Our note's validity See it has one (the water bath), in a protease context.
- **Instrument sized to the amount** (pp. 8, 12, 31). Our note has "choose one whose smallest step suits the size of the reading" without a biology example.

## 4. Where the book is worse, and what it leaves out

- **Safety is generic.** Almost every safety line is "take care with ...". CCEA's 2023 and 2024 reports single out general answers (tie your hair back; tell someone; keep away) as what weaker candidates wrote where a specific action was credited, and our note's hazard, risk and control pattern is exactly right. The book's food-burning table (p. 10) is the one good model: a specific hazard, what could happen, a specific action.
- **No hypotheses**, only expected results without reasons. Our note's testable-hypothesis section (direction plus scientific reason) is the teaching; the book's expected results are good material for a Your turn ("add the reason").
- **Drawings that would not earn a diagram mark.** The food set-up (p. 9) has no labels; the potometer (p. 29) labels only the syringe. Our note's four-rule diagram section is better; do not use the book's drawings as models.
- **No repeats planned**, except the food investigation's question on why repeat (p. 10). CCEA's Booklet A tasks often add another group's results and an average (2021, 2024, 2025).
- **A few table headings break the convention**: a percentage column with a unit of grams (p. 20), a rate column "per hour" with no length unit (p. 30), light in arbitrary units while the method asks for low, medium, high (p. 16).

## 8. Definitions and wording

- **Controlled variable** (kept the same) and **control** (an extra set-up that shows the result needs the factor being tested) must be taught as different words. The book uses both senses without defining either.
- **Independent / dependent**: the book's own pair (p. 27). Same as ours.
- **Book slip:** "Dedependent" in the washing-line answer lines (p. 27).

## 11. GAPS in our note, ranked

1. **Biology in the variables section.** Add a Your turn (or a twin) on the washing line: identify the independent, dependent and one controlled variable for three leaves of one species. It is CCEA's 2022 Booklet A shape and the book's p. 27.
2. **Control tube against controlled variable.** A `why` callout and a gate: the catalase control holds 10 cm³ of peroxide and 5 cm³ of water or boiled catalase; option notes for "no enzyme and nothing in its place" (the volume changes) and "a different temperature" (that is the independent variable).
3. **From expected result to hypothesis.** Take one of the book's expected results (the larger leaf loses more water) and add the reason (more area, more stomata, more routes for vapour); this is our hypothesis rule on a biology case.
4. **Validity precautions by what they protect.** A short list or match: equilibrate (the reaction runs at the bath's temperature), blot (surface liquid would add mass), seal (water leaves only through the shoot), one length (equal surface for water to cross), shoot under water (no air to break the water column).
5. **Instrument sized to the amount.** A gate on a biology case: 20 cm³ of water, which measuring cylinder (25 cm³, not 100 cm³).

## 12. Anything in our note the book suggests is wrong or misleading
