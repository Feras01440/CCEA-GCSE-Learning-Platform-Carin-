## 2. The book's method, in order (our words)

1. Context (p. 12): enzymes are biological catalysts; catalase, found in cells, speeds the breakdown of hydrogen peroxide, a harmful product of the cell's own chemistry, into oxygen and water.
2. Label four boiling tubes. Syringe 5 cm³ of catalase solution into tubes 1 and 3 (5 cm³ syringe) and 10 cm³ of the peroxide into tubes 2 and 4 (10 cm³ syringe) (p. 13).
3. Stand tubes 1 and 2 in a 20 °C water bath and tubes 3 and 4 in a 30 °C bath, each with its own thermometer, for at least five minutes (p. 13).
4. Take out tubes 1 and 2, tip the peroxide into the enzyme, put the mixture back in its bath and start the timer at once (p. 13).
5. After three minutes, hold a ruler against the outside of the tube and measure the froth, in mm, **from the surface of the liquid upwards** (p. 13).
6. Repeat steps 4–5 with tubes 3 and 4 at 30 °C, and record both heights in a table headed temperature and height of froth with their units (p. 13).

## 3. Where the book is better than our note

- **It is CCEA's method.** The froth version was the 2021 Booklet A (at 20 °C and 50 °C); the protease-milk version was the 2024 Booklet A and the 2026 Higher Booklet B Q3; a potato-catalase froth set-up was November 2022 B1. The amylase version has appeared once, as a B1 data question (March 2019, both tiers: samples tested with iodine every five minutes), never as a Unit 7 task. Our b1 note's whole practical section, its figure and bundle items 0007, 0008 and 0010 use amylase only.
- **Equilibrating both reactants** (p. 13): five minutes in the bath, each tube separately, before mixing. CCEA 2021 Foundation Q1(g) gives 1 mark for the point that both solutions start the reaction already at the set temperature; the 2024 report notes that most candidates saw the wait lets enzyme and substrate warm to the temperature being tested. Our b1 note has the idea; see gap 5 for how its item marks it.
- **A dependent variable she can measure with a ruler**, with the measuring rule stated: above the liquid, against the outside of the tube, in mm, after a fixed time (p. 13). CCEA 2021 Higher Q1(f): the dependent variable is the height of the froth.
- **The control tube** (p. 14, Question 2): the book says a control would also hold 10 cm³ of peroxide and asks what else. CCEA 2021 Higher Q1(g), two marks: **5 cm³**; of **water, boiled enzyme, or another named enzyme**. One mark is for matching the volume. Our b1 note never mentions a control; u7-planning item 0014(b) asks it in an amylase context for one mark and accepts water or boiled enzyme.
- **Two controlled variables fall straight out of the method**: the three minutes, and the volumes (or concentrations) of peroxide and of enzyme (CCEA 2021 Foundation Q1(h)).

## 4. Where the book is worse, and what it leaves out

- **Two temperatures only.** No optimum can be found and no graph drawn; our b1 note's six-temperature time graph, read the right way up, is what CCEA asks (2024 Foundation Booklet A Q1(c)–(d); 2026 Higher Booklet B Q3).
- **No repeats in the book's version.** The 2021 paper added a second group's results and an average; the book drops them, so reliability is never touched.
- **Froth is a rough measure.** CCEA's own 2021 Higher scheme lists its sources of error: froth height hard to read, stirring not the same, the bath temperature dropping, timing, tubes of different sizes. The book does not discuss any.
- **The enzyme definition is short of the specification's**: it omits that enzymes are proteins and are not used up (1.4.1). Our note has both.
- **No answers,** and its Question 3 has changed meaning (see section 7).

## 5. Diagrams and photographs

None in the book for B3. The only visual is the results table: temperature in °C, height of froth in mm, rows for 20 and 30. Our b1 note has a water-bath and spotting-tile figure (amylase) and u7-planning a water-bath figure (protease and milk). A catalase figure would need: two water baths with thermometers, four labelled boiling tubes, two syringes of different sizes, and a mixed tube with a ruler held against it, the zero of the ruler at the liquid's surface.

None in the book. CCEA's own versions add: an average of two groups to the nearest mm (2021), a time in minutes and seconds converted to seconds and averaged, with the A + B ÷ 2 calculator slip reported in 2024, and a point-to-point line graph (2024 Foundation Booklet A; the report: straight lines between the points, not a best-fit line). Our b1 note already covers the mean and the slip.

## 7. The book's questions, and what CCEA's schemes credit

| Book question (page) | Scheme answer and source |
|---|---|
| 1: the dependent variable (p. 14) | The height of the froth (2021 H BkA Q1(f)). |
| 2: what else is in the control tube (p. 14) | 5 cm³; of water / boiled enzyme / another named enzyme, two marks (2021 H BkA Q1(g)). |
| 3: explain the froth at 30 °C (p. 14) | **Not the 2021 answer.** At 50 °C (2021) the scheme credits denaturation and a changed active site. At 30 °C against 20 °C, both temperatures sit below the optimum of the catalase sources schools use, so the expected result is **more** froth: the molecules have more kinetic energy, so successful collisions and enzyme-substrate complexes become more frequent and more oxygen is released (the chain CCEA credits, 3 marks, for the faster 40 °C reaction in 2024 F BkA Q1(b)). |

An author writing a model answer for the book's Question 3 must not copy the 2021 scheme: the book's change of temperature reverses the expected result.

## 8. Definitions and wording

- **Catalase** speeds the breakdown of hydrogen peroxide to oxygen and water (p. 12). The froth is oxygen trapped in the liquid.
- **Control** here means a **control tube**, a whole extra experiment with the enzyme removed or killed. It is not a controlled variable. Our notes' text uses only the second sense (one u7-planning bundle item, 0014(b), asks the first); the book uses the first. Both words must be taught, side by side.
- **Book slip:** the text layer of the results table loses the degree sign ("Temperature /C"); the page itself shows it.

## 9. Higher-tier-only content

None in the book. (Inhibitors, the Higher bullet of 1.4.2, are in our note only.)

## 10. Pitfalls and precautions the book builds in

- Eye protection; care with hydrogen peroxide (p. 12).
- **At least five minutes** in the bath before mixing (p. 13).
- Start the timer **as soon as** the liquids are mixed, and put the tube **back in the bath** for the three minutes (p. 13).
- Measure the froth **above the liquid**, not from the bottom of the tube (p. 13).

## 11. GAPS in our note, ranked

1. **CCEA's methods are missing.** Rebuild the B3 section around the catalase froth method as the main See it (equilibrate, mix, three minutes, froth above the liquid in mm), then a short variant on protease and milk (time for a cross to show through). Keep amylase as a third variant (CCEA's March 2019 B1 data question used it). Recreate the figure described in section 5.
2. **No control tube.** Teach a control tube against a controlled variable, then a Your turn: the control holds 10 cm³ of peroxide and what else? Two marks, volume and substance (5 cm³ of water or boiled catalase). Option notes for "nothing else" (no volume matched) and "more peroxide" (not a control).
3. **Variables named for the froth version.** Independent: temperature; dependent: height of froth after three minutes; controlled: time, volumes and concentrations of both solutions (2021 F BkA Q1(h)).
4. **Explanations keyed to the temperatures given.** A two-row choice: 20 → 30 °C (collisions, complexes, more froth) against 20 → 50 °C (denatured, active site changed, less froth). This is the trap in the book's Question 3.
5. **Item `q.science.b1.b1-enzyme-factors.0007(c)` refuses scheme wording.** Engine probe on 7 Oct (working tree): an answer in the 2021 scheme's own terms (both solutions at the set temperature before the start; CCEA 2021 F BkA Q1(g), 1 mark) scores 0 of 1, and one in the 2024 report's terms (enzyme and substrate warmed to the reaction temperature) scores 0 of 1. The exact inputs are kept in the git-ignored `scratchpad/probes/dossier-biology-practical/probe-scheme-phrasings.mts`. Its keyWords need "correct temperature", "reaction temperature" and "temperature of the water bath". Credit-only widening in place.
6. **Sources of error in froth.** One line listing two (froth hard to read against a ruler; bath temperature falling while tubes are out), from the 2021 Higher scheme.

## 12. Anything in our note the book suggests is wrong or misleading

Nothing in the theory. The risk is emphasis: a student who learns only the amylase spotting-tile method meets a froth or a milk tube in Booklet A with no rehearsal of its variables, its control or its measuring rule.
