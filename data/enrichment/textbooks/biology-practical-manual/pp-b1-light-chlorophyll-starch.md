## 2. The book's method, in order (our words)

1. The idea first (p. 2): chloroplasts hold chlorophyll, which traps light; carbon dioxide and water become glucose and oxygen; the process takes in energy, so it is endothermic; glucose is stored as starch; iodine solution goes from yellow-brown to blue-black where starch is.
2. Teacher's preparation (p. 2): two days in a dark cupboard to destarch, then a strip of dark card clipped over part of one leaf, then a day in bright light before the lesson. For B1b (p. 6) the same, without the card.
3. Detach the covered leaf and, **before the card comes off**, lay the leaf in a box on the worksheet, trace round it and draw and label where the card is (p. 4, Question 1(a)).
4. Half fill a 250 cm³ beaker with hot water on a heatproof mat. Remove the card. With forceps, put the leaf in the hot water for one minute (p. 4).
5. With forceps, move the leaf into 15 cm³ of ethanol in a boiling tube, gently so it does not tear, and stand the tube in the hot water at once until the leaf has lost its green colour (p. 4).
6. Lift the leaf out and dip it in the hot water for about ten seconds (p. 4).
7. Spread it flat on a white tile or in a Petri dish and cover it with iodine solution (p. 4).
8. Read the two regions; say why each of three steps was done; name the factor; explain the covered region (p. 5). B1b repeats steps 4–7 on a variegated leaf and asks for the white region's colour and the reason (p. 7).

There is no Bunsen burner in either apparatus list: the hot water is supplied, so no flame is ever near the ethanol.

## 3. Where the book is better than our note

- **The covered region is recorded before the card is removed** (p. 4). Once the leaf has been through ethanol it is pale all over and the strip's position is lost; tracing it first is what makes the iodine result readable. CCEA examines exactly this: 2021 Foundation Booklet B Q1(c)(iii) gives the card's outline as a dotted line and asks her to shade where starch would be (the mark is for shading above and below the box), and 2024 Foundation Q2(d)(i) repeats it with the card across the leaf's centre, asking why that region tested negative. Neither of our notes asks her to locate the covered region on a drawing.
- **No flame at all.** The book removes the hazard instead of controlling it: hot water from a supply, ethanol warmed in that water. Our b1 note draws a Bunsen under the boiling water and teaches "turn it off first", which is the answer CCEA credits when a Bunsen is in the set-up (2024 Foundation Q2(b)(ii); 2021 Foundation Q1(b)(ii) credits "ethanol is flammable"). Both are right; the book's version is the safer habit and worth one sentence beside ours.
- **The ethanol stage ends on what she sees** (the leaf is no longer green), not on a clock. Our notes say "until it turns pale" in u7-carrying-out but give no end point in the b1 note.
- **Handling.** Forceps for every transfer; the leaf is fragile after ethanol; it must be spread fully flat so no fold hides a region (p. 4). Our notes mention brittleness and the tile but not the forceps.
- **One variable per leaf, stated as the factor investigated** (p. 5, Question 1(d)): the answer is a single word, light (CCEA 2021 Foundation Q1(c)(iv) credits light or sunlight).

## 4. Where the book is worse, and what it leaves out

- **No reasons.** The three "explain why" questions on p. 5 are the whole teaching of the reasons, and the book prints no answers. Our note's four reasons, with the examiner evidence on the flame, are far stronger.
- **Only light and chlorophyll.** Sodium hydroxide to remove carbon dioxide and the oxygen from pondweed (both in 1.2.3) are absent; our note has both, with the glowing-splint test.
- **Safety is "take care with ethanol"** (pp. 3, 7). On this very step in 2024 the scheme credited only turning off the Bunsen, and the report noted candidates who gave general instructions such as tying hair back instead; the book's lines are not a model answer.
- **No comparison wording for the conclusion.** Our note's rule (name both regions of the same leaf) is what turns a result into evidence; the book does not teach it.
- **Timings differ from CCEA's own papers.** The book gives a day of light and a minute in hot water; CCEA's papers use 48 hours of light (2021 and 2024 Foundation) or two days (2026 Higher Q1), and 30 seconds (2021) or five minutes (2026) in boiling water. None of these is wrong; the timing is not what the marks are for.

## 7. The book's questions, and what CCEA's schemes credit

The book prints no answers. These are the matching scheme lines, by file, so an author can write model answers that score.

| Book question (page) | Scheme answer and source |
|---|---|
| 1(b): colour of the covered and uncovered regions (p. 5) | Covered stays yellow-brown; uncovered turns blue-black (the colours of 1.3.1). |
| 1(c): why hot water at step 5 (p. 5) | Killing the leaf, which halts its reactions: 1 mark (2021 F BkB Q1(b)(i), step 1). |
| 1(c): why the second dip at step 9 (p. 5) | To soften the leaf (2024 F BkB Q2(b)(i), stage 3; 2021 F BkB Q1(b)(i) gives the dip itself as the step). |
| 1(c): why iodine at step 11 (p. 5) | To test for starch. |
| 1(d): the factor investigated (p. 5) | Light / sunlight (2021 F BkB Q1(c)(iv)). |
| 1(e): explain the covered region (p. 5) | No light reached it, so no photosynthesis, so no starch, so the iodine stays yellow-brown (the chain CCEA credits for the sodium hydroxide leaf in 2024 F BkB Q2(d)(ii): yellow-brown; no photosynthesis; no starch). |
| B1b Q1–2: the white region and why (p. 7) | Yellow-brown; no chlorophyll or chloroplasts, so no photosynthesis, so no starch (2022 BkB six-marker, indicative points 4–6, both tiers). |

One scheme fact the book does not state: destarching is credited as "in the dark" plus a time of **24 to 48 hours, one day or two days** (2021 F BkB Q1(a)(i)), where the book gives only 48 hours. The reason for destarching, which the book does give (p. 2), is credited as showing that any starch found was made during the experiment (2024 F BkB Q2(a)(i)).

## 8. Definitions and wording

- **Iodine solution**: yellow-brown, turning blue-black with starch (p. 2). Same as ours and 1.3.1.
- **Variegated**: a leaf patterned green and white, whose pale areas lack chloroplasts (p. 6). Ours says no chlorophyll; schemes accept chlorophyll, chloroplasts or "no photosynthesis".
- **Endothermic** is named (p. 2), as 1.2.1 requires; our practical note does not need it.
- **Book slips, not to copy:** the method removes the leaf from the plant at step 1 and then removes the card "from the plant" at step 4 (it is on the detached leaf by then); the text layer of p. 2 loses the subscripts of the balanced equation (the page itself is correct).

## 9. Higher-tier-only content

Only the balanced equation on p. 2, printed in bold (the bold part of 1.2.2). It belongs to `b1-photosynthesis-equation-limiting-factors`, not to this practical.

## 10. Pitfalls and precautions the book builds in

- Trace the leaf and mark the card **before** removing it (p. 4).
- Forceps for every transfer; do not tear the leaf in the ethanol (p. 4).
- Ethanol is warmed only in hot water; the tube goes in straight away (p. 4).
- Spread the leaf completely flat before adding iodine (p. 4).

## 11. GAPS in our note, ranked

1. **Locating the covered region.** Add a Your turn after the light set-up: a leaf outline with the card's position dashed (our own shape, say a band across the middle third); she taps or shades the regions that turn blue-black. Option notes for "the covered band" (reversed) and "the whole leaf" (destarching forgotten). This is the 2021 F Q1(c)(iii) and 2024 F Q2(d) shape.
2. **Destarching time is marked too narrowly.** CCEA credits 24 to 48 hours (2021 F BkB Q1(a)(i)). Engine probe on 7 Oct (working tree): gate `g3` refuses "24" and "1 day"; bundle `q.science.b1.b1-photosynthesis-investigations.0002(a)` gives "put it in a dark cupboard for 24 hours" 1 of 2 although its own scheme line accepts 24–48 hours (its keyWords hold only 48, two days, 2 days). Credit-only widening in place.
3. **A flame-free set-up beside the Bunsen one.** One sentence and a figure variant: hot water from a kettle, no burner; keep "turn the Bunsen off" as the answer whenever a Bunsen is drawn.
4. **Harmonise the light time across our notes.** The b1 note says 48 hours in the light; u7-carrying-out says a day; the book says a day; CCEA's papers say 48 hours (2021, 2024) or two days (2026). Write "a day or two in bright light" in both notes.
5. **The ethanol end point and the forceps.** "Until the leaf is no longer green", and "move it with forceps" in the method paragraph of the b1 note.
