## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. Health as freedom from both kinds of disease; what illness costs the NHS (buildings, treatment, staff) and the working days lost (p. 77).
2. Communicable diseases are caused by microorganisms: bacteria, viruses, fungi (p. 77).
3. A three-part table, one part per group, giving each disease's spread, prevention and treatment (p. 78).
4. A matching task (disease to microorganism type) and a table to complete (prevention and treatment) (pp. 79–80).
5. Aseptic technique defined: growing one kind of microorganism, usually bacteria, without contamination (p. 80).
6. The principles: sterilise media and apparatus in an autoclave beforehand; work beside a flame; wipe benches; wash hands; keep lids on and lift them only slightly and briefly; never lay a lid on the bench (p. 80).
7. Tape the dish partly shut, incubate below 25 °C to hold back pathogens, autoclave everything afterwards (p. 81).
8. Twelve numbered steps across two photographs, from flaming the loop to examining the plate after 48 hours (pp. 81–82).

## 3. Explanations and analogies the book uses that our note lacks

- **Tuberculosis is prevented by more than a vaccine** (p. 78). The book names the BCG vaccine and adds good housing (less crowding) and prompt treatment of people with the disease, which cuts spread. Our note gives vaccination only.
- **Potato blight is prevented in the field** (p. 78): plant disease-free seed potatoes in clean soil, and do not replant where infected plants grew. Our note gives fungicide only (the book lists fungicide spraying too).
- **HIV also passes from mother to baby** (p. 78): across the placenta or in breast milk, besides sex and shared needles. Our note gives sex and blood only.
- **Rest and fluids** (p. 78). Salmonella food poisoning is usually treated with rest and plenty of fluids, antibiotics being added in some cases; colds and flu are treated with rest and fluids. Our note treats salmonella with antibiotics alone.
- **Salmonella prevention across the kitchen** (p. 78): hygiene when preparing, thawing, cooking and storing food. Our note says thorough cooking and hand washing.
- **Never put a lid on the bench** (p. 80) and **wash hands before and after** (p. 80). Our note keeps the culture bottle's lid in her hand (good) and mentions hand washing at the end; it does not state the rule for the Petri dish lid.

## 5. Worked examples, calculations and data tasks

- **Matching** (p. 79): five diseases to three pathogen types.
- **Table to complete** (p. 80, Test Yourself 2.6.1): prevention and treatment for flu, HIV, tuberculosis and chlamydia from a list (condom, antibiotics, vaccination), each word usable more than once.
- **Three aseptic recalls** (p. 82): how apparatus is sterilised beforehand (autoclave); why the bottle neck is flamed (to kill bacteria on it); why the dish lid is only partly lifted (to keep airborne microorganisms out). CCEA 2023 Unit 7 Higher Booklet B Q3 asked the same three kinds of thing (items sterilised by autoclave; the reason for the part-open lid; the reason for 25 °C: `docs/sources/papers/science/2023-Summer/U7-H-Biology-BkB-MS-29145.txt`).
- The twelve steps (pp. 81–82) line up almost exactly with the nine-point indicative content of CCEA's 2024 Unit 7 Booklet B six-mark aseptic question (`docs/sources/papers/science/2024-Summer/U7-H-Biology-BkB-MS-47731.txt`), which also requires at least one point about the dish (lid at an angle or not fully open; bacteria onto the agar; lid back on, label or tape) for the top band.
- No calculations. Our outbreak-percentage work is an addition, well aimed at the "Mathematical content" box (tables of numbers of people with diseases).

## 7. Definitions and wording, and slips in the book

- **Health** (p. 77): being free of communicable and non-communicable disease. Same as ours.
- **Aseptic technique** (p. 80): procedures that let one type of microorganism be grown safely without contamination by others. Same idea as ours.
- **"Partly sealed".** The book tapes the dish only partly and incubates below 25 °C; the specification says "sealed" and a maximum of 25 °C; the 2024 Unit 7 scheme credits "tape the Petri dish". Our note says the lid is taped on and "sealed". Recommendation: write "taped shut with a few strips (not sealed all the way round)", which satisfies all three sources.
- **Why work beside the flame.** The book says working near the flame leaves the air round the plate free of bacteria; that overstates it. Our note's reason, rising hot air carrying microorganisms away and so reducing the risk, is the accurate one (and matches the specification's "reduce the risk").
- **Slips in the book's table (p. 78), not to copy:** colds and flu are said to spread from someone infected "with the bacteria" (they are viruses); the potato-blight "treatment" cell holds a prevention measure; HPV is missing from the table.

None (no bold text on pp. 75–82).

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Keep lids on and lift them **slightly** and **briefly** (p. 80).
- Never set a lid **down on the bench** (p. 80).
- Incubate **below 25 °C** to limit pathogens (pp. 81–82).
- Autoclave cultures **before** disposal (p. 81).

## 10. GAPS in our note, ranked

1. **Prevention is thinner than it could be for tuberculosis and potato blight.** Add BCG by name, better housing and prompt treatment for tuberculosis; disease-free seed potatoes, clean soil and no replanting where blight was for potato blight; a Your turn "give one way, other than vaccination, to reduce the spread of tuberculosis".
2. **HIV's third route is missing.** Add mother to baby (placenta, breast milk) to the routes figure and text.
3. **Treatment is drug-only.** Add rest and fluids as the usual treatment for salmonella, colds and flu, antibiotics being for bacterial cases that need them.
4. **"Sealed" wording.** Change to "taped with a few strips, not sealed all the way round" (section 7).
5. **The dish-lid rule.** Add "the Petri dish lid is never put down on the bench" beside the bottle-lid rule, and "wash hands before and after" at the start of the procedure.

## 11. Anything in our note the book suggests is wrong or misleading

Nothing is wrong. Treating salmonella with antibiotics alone (section 5 of our note) is incomplete rather than wrong; see gap 3.
