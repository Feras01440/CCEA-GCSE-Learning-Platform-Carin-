## 1. Book chapter and pages

Section 2.3: "Sex hormones" p. 26 (with a two-column table of changes at puberty); "Menstrual cycle" pp. 27–28 (narrative and exam tip p. 27; Test Yourself 2.3.2 p. 28). Answers p. 100.

## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. Before gametes are made, puberty brings physical and emotional changes, the secondary sexual characteristics; testosterone from the testes, oestrogen from the ovaries (p. 26).
2. A two-column table of those changes for boys and for girls (p. 26).
3. From puberty to the menopause, one mature ovum a month; each month the uterus builds a thick lining ready for implantation; if there is no fertilisation, the ovum and the lining are lost in a period (p. 27).
4. The 28-day calendar: bleeding on days 1–5; a new lining built under oestrogen; the ovum released on day 14 (ovulation); oestrogen peaks just before that and then drops (p. 27).
5. Progesterone rises just after ovulation, stays level, and keeps the lining thick enough for implantation; when it falls, the lining is shed (p. 27).
6. If fertilisation happens, progesterone is made throughout pregnancy, keeping the lining and supporting the developing amnion, fluid and foetus (p. 27).
7. Exam tip: the shapes of the three curves (lining, oestrogen, progesterone) against the day axis (p. 27).
8. Self-test on one hormone's curve with three marked points (p. 28).

## 3. Explanations and analogies the book uses that our note lacks

- **The pregnant branch** (p. 27). If an embryo implants, progesterone keeps being made and the lining is kept, so no period follows. Our note only follows the non-pregnant cycle ("when progesterone falls, the lining sheds"). CCEA 2022 Foundation Q4(c)(ii) asked her to continue the lining graph beyond day 28 for a pregnancy; the scheme's answer is a line that stays at the same level (`docs/sources/papers/science/2022-Summer/B2-F-Biology-MS-21578.txt`).
- **The ovum goes too** (p. 27). An unfertilised ovum leaves with the lining. Our note does not say what becomes of the ovum.
- **Events read from a hormone curve** (p. 28). Given only an oestrogen-shaped curve with a flat low start, a peak and a fall, she must name the process in the flat stretch (menstruation) and at a point just past the peak (ovulation). Our graph checks read the lining's thickness, not events from a hormone curve.
- **Puberty in full** (p. 26). The book's list includes growth in height, hair in the armpits and pubic area in both sexes, chest hair, a deeper voice and genital growth in boys, breast development and the start of periods in girls, and greater sexual awareness in both. Our figure gives three changes per hormone, including wider shoulders and hips, which the book does not list (CCEA 2025 Foundation's tick table does include hips widening, so ours is safe).

## 5. Worked examples, calculations and data tasks

- **Test Yourself 2.3.2** (p. 28): name the process between A and B, describe it (the lining and the unfertilised ovum leave through the vagina with some blood), name the process at C, describe it (the ovary releases an ovum, which begins its journey along the oviduct).
- **Shapes to know** (p. 27, exam tip): lining rises between about days 5 and 14, holds steady to day 28, then falls unless there is a pregnancy; oestrogen rises after day 5, peaks at day 14 and falls back; progesterone rises from about day 14, holds for several days and falls near day 28 unless there is a pregnancy.
- No calculations. Ours adds lining-thickness readings and differences, ovulation day for non-28-day cycles and the fertile window as a percentage; all sound.

## 6. Practice question types

- **Name** the process shown between two points, or at a point (1 each).
- **Describe** what happens during it (1–2).
- **Complete** a lining graph for part of the cycle, and **continue** it for a pregnancy (CCEA 2022 Foundation Q4: 2 + 1).
- **Name** the hormone that builds up and keeps the lining after ovulation (circle from a list: CCEA 2022).

## 7. Definitions and wording

- **Ovulation** (p. 27): release of the ovum from the ovary on about day 14. Same as ours.
- **Menstruation** (p. 27): the lining and the unfertilised ovum lost through the vagina, days 1–5. Same as ours, plus the ovum.
- **Progesterone's job** (p. 27): keeps the lining thick enough for implantation. Same as ours ("maintains").
- **A modelling difference: the lining after day 14.** The book (p. 27) has the lining thickening between days 5 and 14, then **holding steady** to day 28. Our lower graph keeps rising after ovulation (7.0 mm on day 14, 10.0 mm on day 20, 12.0 mm on day 24), and our worked example reads 11.0 mm of growth from day 5 to day 24. CCEA's own 2022 wording ("building up and maintaining" between day 14 and day 28) allows some further thickening, so our figure is not wrong, but it disagrees with the book's picture. Safest: let the lining rise gently after day 14 and level off well before day 28, so either reading is compatible; keep all our numeric answers consistent with whatever curve is drawn.

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Learn the key days: **1–5** and **14** (p. 27).
- Each curve has a shape tied to those days; learn the shape, not just the peak (p. 27).
- If a pregnancy starts, the curves do **not** fall at day 28 (p. 27).

## 10. GAPS in our note, ranked

1. **No pregnant branch.** Add a `variant` section: explain that implantation keeps progesterone high, so the lining is kept and no period follows; See it continuing the lining graph past day 28 for a pregnancy (level line) and for no pregnancy (a fall); Your turn on the pregnancy case (CCEA 2022 shape).
2. **No event-naming from a hormone curve.** Add a Your turn on a single unlabelled hormone curve: name the event in the early flat stretch and at the point just after the peak.
3. **The lining curve's shape after day 14.** Redraw so it levels off before day 28 (see section 7) and re-check every reading in the bundle against the new curve.
4. **What happens to the ovum.** One line: an unfertilised ovum leaves with the lining.
5. **Puberty list.** Add height and pubic and armpit hair for both sexes to the figure.

## 11. Anything in our note the book suggests is wrong or misleading

Nothing is wrong. The lining curve's continued rise after ovulation differs from the book's steady plateau (section 7); both are defensible, but the figure should not force a reading that contradicts CCEA's "stays at the same level" answer for a pregnancy.
