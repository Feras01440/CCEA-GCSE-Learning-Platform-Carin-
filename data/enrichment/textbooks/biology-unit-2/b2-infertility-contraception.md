Section 2.3: "Infertility" pp. 28–30 (causes and IVF p. 28, exam tips and worked example p. 29, Test Yourself 2.3.3 p. 30); "Contraception" pp. 31–33 (table p. 31, exam tip and worked example p. 32, Test Yourself 2.3.4 p. 33). Answers p. 101.

## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. (Higher) Causes of infertility in women and in men, listed (p. 28).
2. (Higher) IVF as a sequence: hormones so that several ova mature; ova collected; sperm added in a dish of nutrient fluid; any embryo left a few days to divide into a ball of cells; checked for normal development; placed in the uterus to implant (pp. 28–29).
3. Exam tips: fertilisation in IVF happens in a dish, not the oviduct; gametes by meiosis, embryo cells by mitosis (p. 29).
4. Contraception introduced with the ethical point first: some people will not use it for religious or moral reasons (p. 31).
5. A table: each method's group (mechanical, chemical, surgical), how it works, and its advantages and disadvantages (p. 31).
6. Data interpretation: how reliable methods are (p. 32 tip; p. 33 table).

## 3. Explanations and analogies the book uses that our note lacks

- **More causes, and the ones CCEA credits** (p. 28). The book adds a hostile vagina (for women) and erectile dysfunction (for men) to the causes we list. CCEA's schemes credit both: 2024 Higher Q6(f) lists lack of sperm, sperm without flagella, erectile dysfunction or impotence, blocked oviducts, lack of ova, a hostile vagina or uterus, lack of hormones (`docs/sources/papers/science/2024-Summer/B2-H-Biology-MS-47961.txt`); 2025 Higher Q6(a)(ii) credits a hostile vagina. Our note gives five causes and misses both.
- **The embryo is checked before transfer** (p. 28). After a few days of division the embryo is watched to see that it is developing normally. CCEA 2021 Higher Q7(b) gave four marks for the IVF sequence including *left for several days (up to five)* and *checked for normal development* (`docs/sources/papers/science/2021-Summer/B2-H-Biology-MS-18444.txt`). Our IVF figure goes straight from "growth into balls of cells" to transfer.
- **Perfect use against real use** (p. 33). A method's expected failure rate and its failure rate in real life can differ; the gap is people (a missed pill, a condom put on wrongly). An implant or a sterilisation leaves nothing to forget, so its two rates are close. Our note has one survey chart and never separates the two ideas.
- **Each method's practical trade-offs** (p. 31). Condoms (male and female) are cheap, easy and protect against infections but fail if misused; the pill needs a prescription, does not suit everyone and can be forgotten; an implant lasts a long time but must be fitted by a professional; sterilisation is permanent and done by a doctor, under local anaesthetic for a man and general for a woman. Our note gives the condom-versus-pill contrast and sterilisation's permanence, but no grid.

## 5. Worked examples, calculations and data tasks

- **IVF recall chain** (p. 29): where ova are made; where fertilisation happens in IVF; where the embryo goes afterwards; why hormones are given first. One mark each.
- **Vasectomy diagram** (p. 32): mark the cut; explain how a male condom prevents pregnancy (it holds the sperm so they cannot reach the ovum); one other benefit (fewer infections passed on during sex).
- **Reliability table** (p. 33): pick the most reliable method; suggest why the pill's real-life rate exceeds its expected rate; explain how an implant works.
- No arithmetic. Ours adds percentage calculations from counts and clinic data; both sound.

## 6. Practice question types

- **Give** causes of infertility (1–2; CCEA wants one male and one female).
- **Describe** the IVF sequence in order (4, CCEA 2021 Higher).
- **Explain** how a named method works (2: cause and consequence, as our note already insists).
- **Mark** the site of a vasectomy on a diagram (1).
- **Use the table** to choose the most reliable method; **suggest** a reason for a difference between two rates (1 each).

## 7. Definitions and wording

- **How the pill works** (p. 31): female hormones that stop ova being produced. CCEA 2023 Higher accepts *stops egg or ovum production*, *prevents ovulation* or *stops the egg maturing*; our note's "changes hormone levels, so no ovum develops or is released" matches.
- **Groups.** The book's table uses exactly CCEA's three words, mechanical, chemical and surgical, confirming our note's emphasis.
- **Male sterilisation** (p. 31): the sperm tubes are cut and tied near where they begin, so the semen carries no sperm. Same as ours.

## 8. Higher-tier-only content in the book

Printed in bold, pp. 28–30: the causes of infertility, the whole IVF account, the IVF exam tip and worked example, and Test Yourself 2.3.3. Our note marks the infertility section Higher.

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- In IVF, fertilisation happens in a **dish**, not in the oviduct (p. 29).
- Hormones at the start of IVF are for **several ova** (p. 29).
- Expect questions that interpret **effectiveness data** (p. 32).
