## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. Antibiotics are chemicals from fungi that kill bacteria or slow their growth; penicillin was the first (p. 86).
2. The disc test: discs soaked in antibiotics on a bacterial lawn; clear areas where bacteria died; the wider the area, the more effective (p. 86).
3. (Bold) Overuse and mutation have produced resistant bacteria such as MRSA (p. 87).
4. (Bold) Hospital patients are often weaker, so spread matters more; four measures, each with its reason (p. 87).
5. (Bold) Incidence data: a bar chart of cases by health trust and year, a trend table, a percentage decrease (pp. 87–88).
6. The second infection: memory lymphocytes make the specific antibody quickly and in large numbers, and it lasts; a graph of the two responses (pp. 88–89).
7. Vaccines contain a modified pathogen carrying the same antigens but causing no disease; enough antibody means immunity; memory remains as levels fall (p. 89).
8. Why vaccines matter most for viral diseases (they are hard to treat), with tetanus as a bacterial example; vaccine against antibody injection for tetanus (pp. 89–90).
9. (Bold) Boosters: faster, higher, longer, because of memory lymphocytes; a first-dose and booster graph with three differences to give (pp. 90–91).

## 3. Explanations and analogies the book uses that our note lacks

- **No clear zone has two possible meanings** (p. 88 tip). Either the bacteria are resistant to that antibiotic, or the disc's antibiotic was too weak to kill them. Our note says flatly that no zone "means the bacteria are resistant". CCEA 2023 Unit 7 Higher Booklet B Q3(c)(i) describes the result without the word resistant (no clear zone, kills no bacteria, does not prevent growth: `docs/sources/papers/science/2023-Summer/U7-H-Biology-BkB-MS-29145.txt`). Safer wording: "not effective against these bacteria at this concentration; they may be resistant".
- **Each hospital measure with its reason** (p. 87). Hand washing physically removes bacteria; sanitiser chemicals kill them; isolation cuts the number of people a patient meets; gloves and protective clothing stop staff carrying bacteria from one patient to the next. Our note lists the measures (and alcohol gel, disinfecting wards, fewer prescriptions) but not a reason for each; questions asking how staff might have cut the number of cases reward the reason.
- **Antibody injection against vaccine, one disease** (p. 90). For tetanus, an injection of ready-made antibodies protects at once but briefly (passive); vaccination protects slowly but for years (active). It is given after a cut when a person cannot remember being vaccinated. CCEA 2021 Higher Q8 built a whole question on this contrast (`docs/sources/papers/science/2021-Summer/B2-H-Biology-MS-18444.txt`). Our note teaches vaccines and boosters but has no side-by-side of the two kinds of injection.
- **Why vaccines matter most for viruses** (p. 89). Antibiotics cannot treat viral infections, so prevention by vaccination carries the load. Our note says antibiotics do nothing against viruses and, separately, that vaccines work; it never joins the two.

## 5. Worked examples, calculations and data tasks

- **Two discs** (p. 86): describe and explain the result (one antibiotic killed many bacteria X, shown by its wide clear area; the other killed none).
- **Test Yourself 2.6.2** (pp. 87–88, Higher): complete a trend table for one trust from the chart (decreased, increased, decreased); name the trust with the same trend; calculate the percentage decrease for another trust between two years (the answer on p. 106 is a fall from 20 to 4, 80 %); suggest two ways staff could have brought the cases down.
- **Three differences** (p. 91, Higher): after the booster the antibodies come faster, in greater numbers, and last longer.

- **Use the graph** to complete a table; **name** the matching trend (1 each).
- **Calculate** a percentage decrease, **showing working** (2).
- **Suggest** two measures to reduce MRSA (2; each with its reason is safer).
- **Use the graph**: three ways the second response differs from the first (3).

## 7. Definitions and wording, and one book error

- **Antibiotic** (p. 86): a chemical made by fungi that kills bacteria or reduces their growth. Same as ours.
- **Vaccine** (p. 89): a modified form of the pathogen with the same antigens, which does not cause the disease. Same as ours.
- **Booster** (p. 90): a later dose of the same vaccine that raises antibody levels faster, higher and for longer. Same as ours.
- **Book error: what the tetanus vaccine contains.** The book says it holds a modified form of the tetanus bacteria. The vaccine is in fact made from the bacterium's inactivated toxin, not the bacterium. CCEA's 2021 paper avoided saying what the vaccine contains. If we add tetanus, say only that the vaccination gives active immunity; do not state its contents.

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Larger clear area, more effective; **no** clear area, too weak **or** resistant (p. 88).
- MRSA questions come as incidence data or disc experiments (p. 88).
- First dose against booster: **faster, higher, longer** (p. 90).

## 10. GAPS in our note, ranked

1. **"No zone means resistant" is too strong.** Rewrite the clear-zone section: no zone means the antibiotic did not kill these bacteria at that strength; they may be resistant, or the dose too weak; add a Your turn asking for the two possible reasons.
2. **No tetanus contrast of injection against vaccine.** Add a `variant` section: See it on a person who cuts herself and cannot remember her tetanus jabs (antibody injection: immediate, short-lived, passive; vaccination: slower, long-lasting, active, with memory); Your turn on which to give and why (CCEA 2021 Higher Q8).
3. **Hospital measures lack their reasons.** Pair each measure with one reason (removes, kills, fewer contacts, no carrying between patients); Your turn "suggest two ways staff reduced MRSA cases, with a reason for each".
4. **No percentage decrease from incidence data.** Add a See it on our own grouped bar chart: read two bars, subtract, divide by the starting value, times 100.
5. **Why vaccination matters most for viruses.** One sentence joining our two existing facts.

## 11. Anything in our note the book suggests is wrong or misleading

- Section 2 of our note says that no clear zone "means the bacteria are resistant". The book (p. 88) gives a second possible reason, an antibiotic too dilute to kill them, and CCEA's 2023 practical scheme describes the result without claiming resistance. The claim is misleading as it stands (gap 1).
