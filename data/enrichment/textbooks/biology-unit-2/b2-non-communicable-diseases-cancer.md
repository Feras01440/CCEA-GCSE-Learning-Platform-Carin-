## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. Non-communicable diseases are not caused by microorganisms (p. 91).
2. Two kinds of cause: inherited (a gene that makes some people more likely to get certain diseases, such as some cancers) and lifestyle (p. 91).
3. Poor diet and too little exercise lead to obesity and to cholesterol building up in arteries, and so to heart attacks and strokes (p. 91, pointing forward to pp. 94–97).
4. Too much sugar in the diet is linked to diabetes, a condition where too little insulin is made to control blood glucose; treatment by diet and exercise or by insulin; long-term harm to eyes, kidneys, heart and brain (p. 91).
5. Alcohol: binge drinking (a lot in a short time) harms the liver, judgement and behaviour; drinking in pregnancy can cause foetal alcohol syndrome; weekly limits (p. 92).
6. A blood-alcohol graph to interpret (p. 92).
7. Tobacco smoke's three harmful parts in a table: tar (bronchitis, emphysema, lung cancer), nicotine (heart rate, addiction), carbon monoxide (takes the place of oxygen on haemoglobin) (p. 93).
8. Cancer: mitosis out of control makes tumours; benign (in a capsule, not spreading, often removable) against malignant (not in a capsule, spreading, often through the blood, harder to treat); how to read a diagram of cancer cells (p. 97).
9. Cancers linked to lifestyle: cervical (HPV, spread in sex; condoms and the HPV vaccine), lung (tar), skin (ultraviolet from the sun or sunbeds; shade, sunscreen, covering up, a hat) (p. 98).

## 3. Explanations and analogies worth recreating (our own words)

- **Two columns of cause** (p. 91): what you inherit and how you live; most non-communicable diseases involve both. A two-column figure makes the specification's "interaction" concrete.
- **Energy in against energy used** (specification 2.6.9; the book implies it): obesity follows when food energy exceeds the energy used in exercise. A balance picture would carry it.
- **Each chemical, one set of effects** (p. 93). The tobacco table's strength is its pairing: tar with the lungs' structure (airways narrowed, alveoli damaged, abnormal division), nicotine with the heart and addiction, carbon monoxide with the blood's oxygen. CCEA's 2025 six-mark question gave a mark only for an effect **linked to the right substance** (`docs/sources/papers/science/2025-Summer/B2-H-Biology-MS-67231.txt`, Q4), so the pairing is the teaching point.
- **Reading a cancer diagram** (p. 97 tip): describe only what the drawing shows. Cancer cells are usually drawn larger, with larger nuclei and thicker membranes, and irregularly arranged; a capsule means benign; cells breaking away mean malignant.

## 5. Worked examples, calculations and data tasks

- **Smoking** (p. 93): name the chemical behind emphysema (tar); explain emphysema (alveoli damaged, less surface for gas exchange, less oxygen into the blood); describe carbon monoxide's effect (the blood carries less oxygen).
- **Cancer cells** (p. 98): three differences from the diagram; name the kind of tumour held in a capsule that stays where it is (benign).
- **Blood alcohol** (p. 92, Test Yourself 2.6.3): how the graph shows binge drinking, with data; the time spent over the driving limit, with working; the organ most harmed by long-term drinking (liver); the condition a baby may be born with (foetal alcohol syndrome). CCEA 2021 Higher Q2 asked for the liver and foetal alcohol syndrome in just this way (`docs/sources/papers/science/2021-Summer/B2-H-Biology-MS-18444.txt`).

## 6. Practice question types, with CCEA evidence

- **Six-mark extended writing on smoking**, one mark per effect linked to its chemical (2025 both tiers).
- **Explain** a passive-smoking data table (2023 Foundation: nicotine is addictive; exposure fell after a ban: `docs/sources/papers/science/2023-Summer/B2-F-Biology-MS-29142.txt`).
- **Give three differences** between cancer and normal cells from a diagram; **name** the tumour type (2021 Higher Q3; 2024 Higher Q4 indicative content: larger nucleus, thicker membrane, larger cells, irregular arrangement, malignant, not encapsulated, spreading).
- **Name** the cause of cancer cells (uncontrolled cell division) and a cause of skin cancer (UV) (2024 Higher Q4(a)).
- **Suggest** how to reduce cervical cancer risk (HPV vaccine, fewer partners, condom: 2021 Higher Q3(c)).

## 7. Definitions and wording, and statements not to copy

- **Non-communicable disease** (p. 91): one not caused by a microorganism, so it cannot be caught.
- **Benign / malignant** (p. 97): encapsulated and not spreading / not encapsulated and able to spread. Same as the specification.
- **Emphysema** (p. 93): damaged alveoli, so less surface area for gas exchange. **Bronchitis**: narrowed bronchi and bronchioles.
- **Diabetes wording.** The book's single sentence (too little insulin) matches CCEA's own B1 description of Type 2 (1.6.7: a progressive condition linked to lifestyle and obesity, in which the pancreas gradually makes less insulin, controlled first by diet and later perhaps by insulin). Use the B1 wording, and tie it to obesity as 2.6.10 requires.
- **Not to copy: the suggestion that light drinking may do good** (p. 92). That claim is contested and is not in the specification; leave it out.
- **Not to copy: two slips in the p. 106 answers.** The binge-drinking answer quotes the peak in "units" (the axis is mg per 100 cm³), and the time-over-the-limit answer takes the crossings as half an hour and 10 hours, whereas the book's own curve crosses the limit at about 0.2 h on the way up and a little after 10 h on the way down (checked on the rendered page). Build our own graph with crossings on grid lines, and read both from it.

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Learn **which chemical** in smoke does **which** harm (p. 93).
- On a cancer diagram, describe **what is drawn**; capsule means benign, spreading means malignant (p. 97).
