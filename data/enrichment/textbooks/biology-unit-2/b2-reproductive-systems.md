## 1. Book chapter and pages

Section 2.3 (human reproduction, including fertility treatment and birth control): learning outcomes and "Mathematical content" pp. 21–22 (the maths is menstrual-cycle graphs); "Male reproductive system" pp. 22–23 (diagram p. 22, function table p. 23); "The female reproductive system" pp. 23–24 (diagram p. 23, function table and exam tip p. 24).

## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. Purpose first: the male system makes sperm, which fertilise an ovum to give a zygote, then an embryo, a foetus and a baby (p. 22).
2. A side-view diagram of the male system with seven labels, then a table of what each part does (pp. 22–23).
3. The female system's purpose: ova, one a month after puberty; once fertilised, the ovum moves on to the uterus and grows into a baby over nine months (p. 23).
4. A front-view diagram of the female system with four labels, then a table of what each part does, listing the uterus lining separately from the uterus (pp. 23–24).
5. A study tip: hide the labels and the table's right-hand column and test yourself (p. 24).

## 3. Explanations and analogies the book uses that our note lacks

- **The whole life-cycle chain in one line** (p. 22): gamete, zygote, embryo, foetus, baby. Our note stops at where fertilisation and implantation happen; the chain is a useful forward link to `b2-fertilisation-pregnancy`.
- **Uterus and uterus lining are two answers** (p. 24). The lining is where the fertilised ovum attaches and implants; the uterus is the organ where the baby develops. Our female figure labels the uterus but not its lining; the note mentions the "thick lining" only in passing. CCEA 2024 B2F asked her to circle the uterus lining (`docs/sources/papers/science/2024-Summer/B2-F-Biology-MS-47725.txt`, Q3(b)(i)).
- **Function words** (p. 23 table): the prostate's fluid both *carries and nourishes* the sperm; the scrotum *keeps the testes outside the body cavity and shields them*; the penis *transfers sperm into the vagina*; the vagina is *where sperm are deposited*; the cervix is the *neck or opening* of the uterus. Our note has "adds fluid" for the prostate (the bundle accepts "nourishes"), "ring of muscle" for the cervix, and no function for the penis or vagina in the note itself.

## 6. Practice question types

The exam tip (p. 24) names the two CCEA shapes: **name** the parts on a diagram, and **give the function** of a named part. Our note's worked example and bundle already practise both, with the scheme's verbs.

## 7. Definitions and wording

- **Testes** (p. 23): make sperm and testosterone. **Ovaries** (p. 24): release one ovum a month and make oestrogen. Same as ours.
- **Oviducts** (p. 24): carry ova towards the uterus and are where fertilisation happens. Same as ours.
- **Scrotum.** The book gives a protective job (it keeps the testes outside and guards them); our note gives the temperature reason (sperm are made best a little below body temperature). Both are sound; neither is in the specification's wording. Keep ours and add "protects".
- **Cervix.** Book: the neck or opening of the uterus. Ours: a ring of muscle at the base of the uterus. Compatible; accept either in answers.

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Practise by **covering the labels** and the function column and recalling them (p. 24).
- The urethra carries sperm and, **at other times**, urine (p. 23).

## 10. GAPS in our note, ranked

1. **No female function grid, and the uterus lining is not its own answer.** Add a six-row female grid figure and a Your turn asking where a fertilised ovum implants (answer: the uterus lining, not "the uterus").
2. **Missing functions for the penis and the vagina, and "nourishes" for the prostate.** Add one line each in the function sections; the prostate's fluid carries and nourishes the sperm.
3. **No hide-the-labels self-test.** Add a label-the-diagram Your turn with all labels hidden (both systems), matching the book's revision tip.
4. **No life-cycle chain.** One line: gamete, zygote, embryo, foetus, baby, as the bridge to the next topic.
5. **Scrotum: add "protects"** beside our temperature reason.
