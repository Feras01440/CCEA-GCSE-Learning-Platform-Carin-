## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. Sperm are made in the testes by meiosis, so each carries 23 chromosomes, half the usual amount, in its nucleus (p. 24).
2. The flagellum lets it swim to the oviduct; (Higher) mitochondria supply the energy for that movement by respiration (p. 24).
3. Fertilisation in the oviduct when an ovum is present: the two nuclei fuse, giving a diploid cell of 46 chromosomes, the zygote (p. 25).
4. The zygote divides repeatedly by mitosis on its way to the uterus, giving a ball of identical cells, the embryo (p. 25).
5. Implantation: the ball attaches and sinks into the uterus lining; its cells then differentiate into tissues and organs over nine months (p. 25).
6. The placenta forms from the part of the lining where the embryo implanted; it lets substances diffuse between the two blood supplies, which never mix; (Higher) its villi enlarge the surface (p. 25).
7. The umbilical cord, with an artery and a vein, carries the exchanged substances to and from the foetus; named substances each way (p. 25).
8. The amnion forms round the foetus and amniotic fluid between them cushions it (p. 25).

## 3. Explanations and analogies the book uses that our note lacks

- **Where the placenta comes from** (p. 25). It grows from the patch of uterus lining where the embryo implanted. Our note introduces the placenta as a finished structure.
- **What the cord contains** (p. 25): one artery and one vein. Our note says the cord carries blood, not how.
- **Named nutrients** (p. 25): minerals, vitamins, glucose, amino acids and oxygen cross to the foetus; carbon dioxide and urea cross back. Our note says "dissolved nutrients" throughout; an example or two gives her words to write when a question says "name one substance".
- **Two divisions, two places** (p. 29 tip): gametes are made by meiosis in the ovaries and testes; the cluster of identical cells that develops into the embryo is made by mitosis. Our note says both, but not side by side as a one-line rule.

## 5. Worked examples, calculations and data tasks

- **Three one-mark recalls** (p. 26): the exchange organ (placenta); one substance going from foetus to mother (carbon dioxide or urea); the process of exchange, which is diffusion.
- **The self-test on p. 26 (2.3.1)**: explain the placenta's adaptation (answer on p. 100: villi give a large surface area); identify the lettered structure (amniotic fluid) and give its job (cushions and protects).
- **Test Yourself 2.3.3** (p. 30): the staged diagram; name the division making gametes, the process joining them and the cell made, where the next stage happens in IVF, and the division after that.

Our note's doubling count, glucose percentage drop, surface-area ratio and sperm speed are all additions of ours; CCEA's 2024 Higher paper asked for a cell count after divisions (Q6(c)(i), answer 16: `docs/sources/papers/science/2024-Summer/B2-H-Biology-MS-47961.txt`), so the doubling work is well aimed.

## 7. Definitions and wording

- **Zygote** (p. 25): the diploid cell made when the sperm and ovum nuclei fuse. **Embryo** (p. 25): the ball of identical cells made from it by mitosis. Same as ours.
- **Implantation** (p. 25): the ball of cells attaching to and embedding in the uterus lining. Same as ours ("lining" is the scheme's word; 2024 Higher Q6(d) credits *lining of uterus*).
- **Where the mitochondria are.** The book places them *in the flagellum*; its own drawing puts them in a short band behind the head. Our note says the *middle piece*, which is the accurate term; the specification says only "mitochondria for energy production". CCEA's 2021 Foundation scheme allows a reference to mitochondria as a sperm adaptation. Keep "middle piece"; accept "near the base of the tail".
- **Placenta adaptations.** The specification names only the large surface area and the villi. Our note adds a thin barrier and a good blood supply; CCEA's 2024 Foundation scheme (Q3(b)(ii)) credits *large surface area / thin / permeable*, so the extra features are safe.

## 8. Higher-tier-only content in the book

Printed in bold: the mitochondria in the sperm supplying energy for swimming (p. 24), and the villi giving the placenta a large surface area (p. 25). Our note flags both as Higher.

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Gametes come from **meiosis**; the ball of cells comes from **mitosis** (p. 29).
- The two circulations, hers and the foetus's, stay **separate**; substances cross by diffusion (p. 25).

## 10. GAPS in our note, ranked

1. **"Dissolved nutrients" is never unpacked.** Add named examples (glucose, amino acids, vitamins, minerals) to the placenta section and a Your turn "name one substance that crosses to the foetus other than oxygen".
2. **The cord's contents and the placenta's origin are missing.** Label an artery and a vein in the cord; one sentence that the placenta grows where the embryo implanted.
3. **No staged diagram asking for the division at each step.** Add a Your turn on a four-stage diagram (gamete formation, fertilisation, zygote, divisions), meiosis first and mitosis after.
4. **The meiosis/mitosis one-line rule.** Add it as a `mustknow` callout at the mitosis section.

## 11. Anything in our note the book suggests is wrong or misleading

Nothing. (The book's "mitochondria in the flagellum" is the looser statement; ours is the more precise.)
