## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. A stained smear photograph with the four parts pointed out (p. 11).
2. A table giving each part a description, a job and its adaptations; the white cells split into two kinds with different nuclei and different jobs (p. 11).
3. Haemoglobin with iron loads oxygen in the lungs and unloads it in the tissues, where it is used in respiration; plasma delivers the materials cells use for respiration, growth and building molecules (p. 12).
4. Higher: animal cells have no wall, so red cells in water swell and burst; in the body this does not happen because plasma matches the cells' contents, held so by the kidneys; stored blood is kept in a matching solution (p. 12).
5. The three vessels by pressure, direction and usual oxygen state; capillaries as the exchange vessels (p. 12).
6. Cross-sections of the three vessels with the wall layers named, then a tick table of five features (p. 13).
7. One paragraph each: the artery's three-layer wall with a thick muscle and elastic middle; the vein's wider lumen, thinner middle layer and valves; the capillary's one-cell wall (pp. 13–14).
8. How to answer a comparison: list each vessel's points separately first, then compare (p. 14).

## 3. Explanations and analogies the book uses that our note lacks

- **Two white cells, told apart by the nucleus** (p. 11). Lymphocytes have one large round nucleus and make antibodies; phagocytes have a lobed nucleus and engulf microorganisms. A photograph may show only one kind. Our figure draws one white cell with a lobed nucleus and the text never names the two kinds (they appear only in the defence note).
- **Why red cells do not burst in you** (p. 12, Higher). The plasma is kept at the same solute concentration as the inside of the red cells, by the kidneys, so there is no overall movement of water; blood stored for transfusion is kept in a solution of matching concentration. Our note has the drip example ("never pure water") but not the matching-concentration reason or the kidney link.
- **What the tissues do with the cargo** (p. 12). Oxygen for respiration; food molecules for respiration, growth and making carbohydrates, fats and proteins. Our note lists the cargo but not its use.
- **Similarities, not only differences** (p. 14, Test Yourself and answer p. 99). Arteries and veins both have an outer layer, a muscle and elastic layer and a thin inner lining. Our tick-table twist gets the shared muscle-and-elastic row right, but never asks for similarities in words.

- **Give** what two vessels share and how they differ (2 + 2).
- **Give** one difference in function (1).
- The compare tip (p. 14) implies the six-mark extended comparison CCEA set in 2019 (both tiers, Q11/Q4: arteries against veins; indicative content in `docs/sources/papers/science/2019-Summer/B2-F-Biology-MS-9964.txt`).

## 7. Definitions and wording

- **Red cell adaptations** (p. 11): biconcave for a large surface area; no nucleus, leaving more room for haemoglobin; haemoglobin contains iron. Same as ours.
- **Platelets** (p. 11): the book says they release substances that bring about the change of fibrinogen to fibrin, which is more accurate than "platelets convert fibrinogen to fibrin". Our note uses the specification's shorter wording; both are creditworthy (2025 B2F Q1 matching task credits the fibrinogen link). No change needed, but the book's wording is the better explanation if a sentence of mechanism is added.
- **Lysis** (p. 12, Higher): bursting of a red cell that has taken in too much water.
- **Book statement not to copy:** p. 13 calls arteries the *largest* vessels in the body. That is not a safe claim at GCSE (the vein has the wider lumen, which the book itself says a few lines later). Our note makes no such claim; keep it that way.

## 8. Higher-tier-only content in the book

Cell lysis (p. 12, printed in bold): no wall, water enters, the cell bursts; plasma matched to cell contents; kidneys and homeostasis; transfusion blood stored in a matching solution. Our note has the first half as a Higher section.

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Red cells are **smaller** than white cells (p. 12).
- A photograph may show only **one** of the two white cell types, and may or may not show platelets (p. 12).
- In a comparison, write the points for **each** vessel separately before comparing (p. 14).
- Arteries **and** veins both have muscle and elastic tissue; only the amount differs (p. 13 table).

## 10. GAPS in our note, ranked

1. **The two white cells are not taught as things to recognise.** Add an example/non-example pair to the smear section: lymphocyte (one round nucleus, makes antibodies) beside phagocyte (lobed nucleus, engulfs), with a Your turn "which cell makes antibodies?" on a new drawing.
2. **Why lysis does not happen in the body (Higher).** Extend the Higher section with a See it: pure water (cells burst), a solution matching the cell contents (no overall movement, cells unchanged), a stronger solution (cells shrink); then the reason transfusion blood is kept in a matching solution and the kidneys' part.
3. **Similarities in words.** Add a See it that answers a four-mark "what do an artery and a vein share, and how do they differ" item, one mark per line.
4. **Unlabelled wall layers.** Label outer layer, muscle and elastic layer, and inner lining on our vessel figure.
5. **Cargo without purpose.** One sentence: the tissues use the oxygen and glucose for respiration, and amino acids for growth.

## 11. Anything in our note the book suggests is wrong or misleading

Nothing. (The book's own p. 13 "largest vessels" line is the doubtful statement; see section 7.)
