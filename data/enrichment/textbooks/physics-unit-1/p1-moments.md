# p1-moments: textbook dossier

**Book.** CCEA's Higher Tier eGuide to Physics Unit 1 of Double Award Science, © CCEA 2023 (git-ignored under `docs/sources/textbooks/`). Page numbers are the book's printed numbers; the PDF page is the printed number plus 4.
**Ours.** **No note and no bundle yet.** The slug exists in our taxonomy (difficulty 5, among the worst-answered areas) and the P1 deck (`data/decks/science/P1.json`) has 14 flashcards for it; `packs/science/content/p1/` has no folder. This dossier is a briefing for the first author.
**Spec.** P1 1.2.17, 1.2.18, 1.2.19 and Prescribed Practical P3 (all Foundation).
**Verdict.** **Thinner** (nothing authored). This is the book's strongest force topic: a correct definition with direction and unit, the Principle of Moments with what equilibrium means, the support force as the sum of the downward forces, and six exercises that hit three of CCEA's reported traps (perpendicular distance, distances from the pivot, adding rather than subtracting at the support). It defers the practical (P3) to another guide and never says why the perpendicular distance is the one that counts.

Answers pp. 71–72.

## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. Forces can turn things as well as move and stretch them; the turning effect is a moment (p. 25).
2. Moment about a point: force multiplied by the perpendicular distance from that point to the force; unit N m (or N cm, N mm); every moment is clockwise or anticlockwise (p. 25).
3. A lever with a pivot and one downward force: moment = F × d, clockwise here (p. 25).
4. The Principle of Moments: in equilibrium, total clockwise moment about a point equals total anticlockwise moment about the same point (p. 25).
5. Equilibrium means no resultant force and no resultant moment; the balance written as F1 × d1 = F2 × d2 (p. 25).
6. A beam on a central pivot with a load each side: the pivot pushes up with the sum of the two loads (p. 26).
7. A worked example with all four steps (one moment, the unknown force, the pivot force, the assumption), then exercises (pp. 26–27, 30).

## 3. Explanations and devices worth recreating (our own words)

- **The support carries the sum** (p. 26). Upward forces must equal downward forces, so a pivot under two loads pushes up with both added. The Summer 2025 Unit 7 report says candidates subtracted instead; the 2022 Summer Unit 7 Higher Booklet B scheme (Q4(c)) wants the sum and the direction "upwards".
- **The perpendicular distance, not the length of the arm** (p. 30, guillotine; answer p. 71). A handle on a slanted arm: the moment uses the horizontal gap between the pivot and the downward force's line of action, not the length along the arm. The answer says this is the point of the question. The Summer 2023 report (Higher Q8(b)) says candidates used the distances printed on the diagram rather than the distances from the pivot.
- **Find the centre of gravity, then use it** (p. 30, non-uniform plank). Balance an uneven plank once to locate its centre of gravity; balance it again at its middle with a known small weight; take moments about the middle to find the plank's weight. This is 1.2.18 (a practical task to find the weight of an object) in its classic form, and the book is the only place in our sources that works it.
- **Weight acts at the centre of gravity** (p. 27, snooker cue). An object's own weight is one of the two forces, placed at its centre of gravity: 1.2.19's "one of which could be the object's weight".
- **State the assumption** (p. 26). The bar's own weight was ignored; saying so is a mark-worthy habit.

## 5. Worked examples, calculations and data tasks

- **Training bar** (p. 26): the load's moment about the pivot with its direction; the lifting force from the Principle of Moments; the pivot's force from up equals down; the assumption.
- **Car-park barrier** (p. 27): the clockwise moment of the barrier's weight; the force on the short side that just lifts it.
- **Paving slab** (p. 27): the smallest force to lift one edge, pivoting about the other (the weight acts half-way).
- **Snooker cue** (p. 27): the moment of a small force at the tip, with direction; then the cue's weight acting at its centre of gravity.
- **Guillotine** (p. 30): moment with the perpendicular distance and direction, in N cm.
- **Non-uniform plank** (p. 30): its weight, then the support force (the sum of the two downward forces).

All answers are on pp. 71–72 and check out.

## 6. Practice question types, with CCEA evidence

- **State** the Principle of Moments (2–3; 2018 November Higher Q9(a), scheme: in equilibrium or balanced, about any point or the pivot, plus a unit; 2019 November and 2024 November Higher similar). The Summer 2023 report says many left out "about the pivot".
- **Give** the unit of a moment (1; March 2026 Higher Q4(a): N/m and N/cm were the commonest wrong units).
- **Give** the direction of a moment (1; 2022 November Higher Q8, the weight of a rule, after "define the moment of a force in words"; 2022 Summer Unit 7 Higher Booklet B Q4(a): anticlockwise).
- **Calculate** an unknown force or distance by the Principle of Moments (3–5; a moments item is in 12 of the 24 Higher P1 papers we hold, for example 2018 November, 2019 November, 2024 November, March 2026 Higher Q4(b), Summer 2026 Higher Q8).
- **Calculate** the support force (1–2; 2022 Summer Unit 7 Higher Booklet B Q4(c): the sum, upwards).
- **Unit 7, Practical P3**: verifying the principle with a metre rule and weights, or finding an unknown weight.

## 7. Definitions and wording

- **Moment** (p. 25): the book's definition matches the specification (1.2.17) and the glossary.
- **Principle of Moments** (p. 25): the book says "about any point … about the same point"; the glossary says "about the pivot"; the 2018 November scheme accepts "about any point/pivot". All three earn the mark; teach the glossary's version with "in equilibrium".
- **Unit**: N m, N cm or N mm (p. 25); never N/m (March 2026 report).
- **Equilibrium** (p. 25): no resultant force and no resultant moment. Not in the specification's wording, but it explains the principle and the support force together.
- **Centre of mass**: the exercises on p. 27 say "centre of mass" after the book's own tip (p. 28) insists on "centre of gravity"; see `p1-centre-of-gravity-stability`.

## 8. Higher-tier-only content in the book

None (all Foundation), but CCEA sets the harder multi-step moments items on the Higher paper.

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Forces have directions such as up and left; moments are only ever clockwise or anticlockwise (p. 27).
- Measure every distance from the pivot (pp. 27, 30).
- An object's own weight is drawn from its centre of gravity (p. 27).
- Up equals down: the support carries the total load (p. 26).

## 10. GAPS: what our first note must teach, ranked by exam weight

1. **Moment = force × perpendicular distance, with unit and direction.** Explain why the perpendicular distance (a push along the arm turns nothing); See it on a spanner; Your turn on a slanted arm with both lengths marked; a gate on "N m, not N/m".
2. **The Principle of Moments in CCEA's words**, then a balance calculation where the distances must first be worked out from a metre-rule scale (cite the 2023 report).
3. **The support force** as the sum of the downward forces, with direction (cite the 2025 Unit 7 report and the 2022 Unit 7 scheme).
4. **An object's own weight as one of the forces**, acting at its centre of gravity (uniform beam at its middle).
5. **Finding a weight by moments** (1.2.18 and P3): the two-step uneven-plank method on our own numbers, then the P3 method (pivot at the centre of gravity, known weight one side, slide the other until balanced, record both distances, compare).
