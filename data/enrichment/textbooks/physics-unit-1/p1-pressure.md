**Ours.** **No note and no bundle yet.** The slug exists in our taxonomy and the P1 deck (`data/decks/science/P1.json`) has 7 flashcards for it; `packs/science/content/p1/` has no folder. This dossier is a briefing for the first author.

**Verdict.** **Thinner** (nothing authored). The book is adequate: P = F/A with the units following the area, a table of five everyday situations and four calculations. ## 1. Book chapter and pages

Section 1.2: learning outcomes pp. 14–15; "Pressure" pp. 23–24 (the equation and units, the pascal, a table of everyday situations, one worked example, Test Yourself 1.2.2). Answers p. 70.

## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. Pressure defined by its equation, P = F/A, force in newtons, area in mm², cm² or m², pressure in the matching N/mm², N/cm² or N/m² (p. 23).
2. N/m² renamed the pascal, with a short biography of Pascal (p. 23).
3. Five everyday situations, each with its explanation: a sharpened knife, an ice-skate blade, a stiletto heel on a wooden floor, a house on a concrete raft, caterpillar tracks (p. 24).
4. One worked example (force of the wind on the side of a lorry) and three self-test items (p. 24).

## 3. Explanations and devices worth recreating (our own words)

- **The unit follows the area** (p. 23). Area in cm² gives N/cm², in mm² gives N/mm², in m² gives N/m² (the pascal). This is the specification's own rule (1.2.15: questions may use cm² or mm², and no conversion to m² is expected). CCEA's Summer 2024 Higher Q4(iii) (`docs/sources/papers/science/2024-Summer/P1-H-Physics-MS-47741.txt`) had an area in cm² and the answer 200 N/cm²; the report says many wrote Pa and lost the unit mark. Our note should make this its central rule, not a footnote.
- **Same force, different area, paired** (p. 24). The knife and the heel (small area, large pressure) against the raft and the tracks (large area, small pressure). Teaching them as pairs makes the inverse relationship visible.
- **Rearranging both ways** (p. 24). The self-test asks for a pressure, then an area, then a weight, so all three arrangements of the equation are practised.

## 5. Worked examples, calculations and data tasks

- **Wind on a lorry** (p. 24): force from pressure × area, in pascals and square metres.
- **Drawing-pin tip** (p. 24): a tiny area in standard form, giving a pressure of order 10⁹ Pa.
- **Car tyres** (p. 24): the total contact area from the weight and the pressure in N/cm², then shared among four tyres.
- **Concrete cube** (p. 24): the area of one face from its side, then the weight from pressure × area.

## 6. Practice question types, with CCEA evidence

- **Calculate** a pressure, keeping the given units and giving the matching unit (4; Summer 2024 Higher Q4(iii), area 0.01 cm², "do not change any units"; 2020 March Higher Q4, a drawing pin).
- **Calculate** an area or a force from a pressure (4; 2024 March Higher Q7(b)(i), a car's tyres from its weight and pressure, area in m²).
- **Compare** pressures where the force is the same and the areas differ, and explain (2; Summer 2024 Higher Q4(ii): greater on the finger, smaller area; the report says "surface area" was the commonest wrong wording, and the scheme wants area or contact area). The same item's part (i) is the force-pair question in `p1-forces-and-resultant`.
- **Predict** the effect of a change in area (1; 2024 March Higher Q7(b)(ii): air let out of the tyres, larger area, pressure decreases).
- **Tick** the design features that stop a vehicle sinking into soft ground (2; 2019 November Higher Q1(b): small weight and large tyre area; the two centre-of-gravity options are distractors).
- **Choose** which object exerts least pressure and say why (3; 2025 November Higher Q5(a): largest area, same weight), then a calculation in N/m² and the same value in Pa (Q5(b)).

## 7. Definitions and wording, and a doubtful statement

- **Pressure** (p. 23): defined only through the equation. The specification's 1.2.14 words it as the force exerted per m², with 1 Pa = 1 N/m². Teach the specification's sentence.
- **Doubtful: the ice skate** (p. 24). The book says the blade's high pressure produces a film of water that makes skating easy. The pressure-melting explanation is disputed by physicists (the pressure under a blade lowers the melting point by only a little); it is not on the specification. Do not reuse it.
- **Wording**: "area" or "contact area", never "surface area", for the area in P = F/A (Summer 2024 report).

## 8. Higher-tier-only content in the book

None (all Foundation).

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Keep the area in the unit given and name the pressure unit to match (p. 23).
- The tyre item shares the total area among the tyres at the end, not the beginning (p. 70).

## 10. GAPS: what our first note must teach, ranked by exam weight

1. **P = F/A with the unit matched to the area.** Explain; See it in cm² (answer in N/cm²); Your turn in mm²; a non-example that writes Pa for an area in cm² (cite the 2024 report).
2. **Same force, two areas.** A paired figure and a two-mark explain ("greater pressure because the area is smaller"), with "surface area" shown as the phrase to avoid.
3. **Rearranging for area and force**, each with a twin.
4. **Everyday situations** (1.2.16): knife and caterpillar tracks first, as the specification names them, then two of our own.
5. **Effect of a change** in area or force (one-mark predictions).
