# p1-energy-forms-conservation-efficiency: textbook dossier

**Book.** CCEA's Higher Tier eGuide to Physics Unit 1 of Double Award Science, © CCEA 2023 (git-ignored under `docs/sources/textbooks/`). Page numbers are the book's printed numbers; the PDF page is the printed number plus 4.
**Ours.** **No note and no bundle yet.** The slug exists in our taxonomy and the P1 deck (`data/decks/science/P1.json`) has 10 flashcards for it; `packs/science/content/p1/` has no folder. This dossier is a briefing for the first author.
**Spec.** P1 1.4.1, 1.4.2, 1.4.3, 1.4.4, 1.4.11, 1.4.12 (all Foundation).
**Verdict.** **Thinner** (nothing authored). The book is good here: it separates energy forms from energy resources, insists on the specification's wording of the conservation principle, draws four energy transfer diagrams (three as flow boxes, one with joules) and works three efficiency problems, the hardest of which needs conservation of energy first. Its tip that examiners accept efficiency as a decimal is backed by the schemes. It gives no "is this a form of energy?" practice, though CCEA's reports show that is where Foundation marks are lost.

## 1. Book chapter and pages

Section 1.4 (Energy): learning outcomes pp. 38–39; "Energy Forms and Resources" pp. 39–40 (a table of four forms, then the others); the Principle of Conservation of Energy p. 40; energy transfer diagrams and the joule p. 41; an energy diagram with numbers p. 41; "Efficiency" pp. 42–43 (definition, a light-bulb illustration, the equation, three worked examples, an exam tip).

## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. Forms (the ways energy appears) against resources (where we get it) (p. 39).
2. A table of four forms with a definition and example resources: chemical, gravitational potential, kinetic, nuclear (p. 39).
3. The remaining forms in a sentence, with strain energy explained by a wind-up toy (p. 40).
4. The conservation principle: other books phrase it as energy never being made or lost, but the book recommends the specification's wording (p. 40).
5. Energy transfer diagrams for a struck match, a catapult and an electric bell; the joule pictured as lifting a 100 g apple through 1 m (p. 41).
6. A food mixer's diagram with numbers: find the missing output by subtraction (p. 41).
7. Efficiency: a bulb that takes in 100 J each second and gives out 5 J of light; efficiency = useful output ÷ total input; no units; always less than 1 (p. 42).
8. Three worked examples, the last needing conservation first (pp. 42–43).

## 3. Explanations and devices worth recreating (our own words)

- **Forms against resources** (p. 39). A two-column distinction stops "coal" or "wind" being given as a form of energy. CCEA's reports list the opposite error too: power, weight, pressure and friction offered as forms (Summer 2023, 2024 and 2025 Foundation; March 2026 Foundation).
- **Use the specification's sentence** (p. 40). The book explicitly ranks the specification's wording of the conservation principle above the common "never made or lost" version. CCEA's glossary gives the specification's sentence, but the 2024 March Higher scheme (Q6(a)) credits either version for its first mark and "can be changed from one form to another" for the second, so the book's preference is advice, not a marking rule.
- **Find the missing output first** (pp. 41, 43). In the mixer and the car engine, one output is unknown; conservation gives it by subtraction, and only then can the efficiency be found. The book calls this the hardest kind of efficiency question.
- **A bulb as a per-second picture** (p. 42). 100 J in, 5 J of light out, 95 J of heat: efficiency 0.05 or 5 %. It makes "efficiency" concrete before the equation appears.
- **Decimal is enough** (p. 43, exam tip). The specification says decimal or percentage; the book adds that a decimal avoids a step. CCEA's schemes accept either (March 2026 Higher Q6(a) gave 45 %; Summer 2024 Higher Q8(b)(i) worked with 0.8).

## 5. Worked examples, calculations and data tasks

- **Mixer** (p. 41): missing sound energy by subtraction.
- **Kettle** (p. 42): input from power × time, useful output given; efficiency as a decimal; where the wasted energy went.
- **Motor lifting a load** (pp. 42–43): useful output from force × height (work, from the next topic), total input from the efficiency, then energy per second and wasted energy per second.
- **Car engine** (p. 43): conservation gives the useful kinetic energy, then the efficiency.

## 6. Practice question types, with CCEA evidence

- **Identify** forms of energy in a list (2–3; Summer 2024 and 2025 Foundation Q1; March 2026 Foundation Q1: power, weight, pressure wrongly ticked; heat missed).
- **Name** the energy changes in devices (2–3; loudspeakers and microphones written backwards in the Summer 2023 and 2025 Foundation reports; the solar panel's input is light).
- **Name** the useful and wasted outputs (2; Summer 2024 Higher Q8(a): light and sound from a television; March 2026 Higher Q6(a): heat wasted by a bulb).
- **State** the Principle of Conservation of Energy (1–2; Summer 2024 Higher Q8(b)(ii); 2024 March Higher Q6(a), two marks; 2022 November Higher Q9(i) asks for the principle's name from its statement).
- **Calculate** efficiency, or the total input from an efficiency (3; Summer 2024 Higher Q8(b)(i): input 240 kJ from 0.8 and 192 kJ; March 2026 Higher Q6(b): input 1200 J from 0.4 and 480 J, where the report says many substituted the wrong value). The 2021 Summer scheme allows symbols for the energies but the useful energy must be on top.

## 7. Definitions and wording

- **Conservation of energy** (p. 40): the specification's sentence (energy can change form, but the total does not change), which the glossary also gives.
- **Efficiency** (p. 42): how well a device turns energy into the intended form; equation as in the specification (1.4.12). The glossary's definition (the fraction of the total input energy transferred usefully) is a better sentence to learn.
- **Forms list**: the book adds nuclear energy to the specification's list (1.4.1 names chemical, heat, electrical, sound, light, magnetic, strain, kinetic and gravitational potential "such as"); harmless, but a "name the forms in the specification" card should use the specification's nine.
- **Abbreviations**: the book's "ueo/tei" and CCEA's "uoe ÷ tie" are both just shorthand; she should write the words first.

## 8. Higher-tier-only content in the book

None (all Foundation). Efficiency problems that need the input found from the efficiency, or the output found by conservation first, appear on the Higher paper.

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Efficiency has no unit and is always less than 1 (p. 42).
- The useful output can never exceed the input (p. 42).
- Find any missing output by conservation before calculating efficiency (p. 43).

## 10. GAPS: what our first note must teach, ranked by exam weight

1. **Forms of energy against things that are not forms** (power, force, weight, pressure, friction; resources such as coal). A sort interaction; cite the reports.
2. **Energy changes in common devices**, with the reversible pairs (loudspeaker against microphone; motor against generator) taught side by side.
3. **The conservation principle** in the specification's sentence, then a missing-output diagram.
4. **Efficiency both ways**: from useful and total; total from efficiency and useful (the March 2026 trap); as a decimal or a percentage.
5. **The joule** (1 J lifts a 100 g apple 1 m), linked forward to work and mgh.
