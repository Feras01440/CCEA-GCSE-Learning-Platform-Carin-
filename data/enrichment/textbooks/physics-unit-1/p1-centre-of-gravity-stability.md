# p1-centre-of-gravity-stability: textbook dossier

**Book.** CCEA's Higher Tier eGuide to Physics Unit 1 of Double Award Science, © CCEA 2023 (git-ignored under `docs/sources/textbooks/`). Page numbers are the book's printed numbers; the PDF page is the printed number plus 4.
**Ours.** **No note and no bundle yet.** The slug exists in our taxonomy and the P1 deck (`data/decks/science/P1.json`) has 8 flashcards for it; `packs/science/content/p1/` has no folder. This dossier is a briefing for the first author.
**Spec.** P1 1.2.20, 1.2.21, 1.2.22, 1.2.23 (all Foundation).
**Verdict.** **Thinner** (nothing authored). The book gives a correct definition with a useful tip (the word "point" is required), the three shapes CCEA names, three stability rules, a three-car picture of toppling and a model six-mark answer on a double-decker bus. It does not describe how to find a centre of gravity (1.2.20 says "investigate"), it never explains toppling through moments in plain words for Foundation, and its advice to avoid "centre of mass" is stricter than CCEA's own marking.

## 1. Book chapter and pages

Section 1.2: learning outcomes p. 15; "Centre of Gravity" p. 28 (definition, exam tip, regular shapes, a figure of three shapes); "Stability" pp. 28–29 (three rules, three cars tilting, a model six-mark answer); the toppling tray in Test Yourself 1.2.4 Q3 p. 31 (answer p. 72); the snooker cue's weight acting at its centre of mass p. 27.

## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. Definition: the single point at which the whole weight of a body can be taken to act (p. 28).
2. Tip: write the whole definition and do not drop the word "point"; say "centre of gravity" rather than "centre of mass" (p. 28).
3. Regular shapes have it at their geometric centre; a metre rule at its 50 cm mark; a figure of a rectangular block, a disc and a ring (p. 28).
4. Stability depends on the height of the centre of gravity and the width of the base: higher is less stable, lower more stable, wider more stable (p. 28).
5. Three cars tilted further and further: while the weight's line passes through the base, its moment turns the car back; once the line passes outside, its moment tips the car over (pp. 28–29).
6. A model answer to a six-mark question about a tilted double-decker bus (p. 29).

## 3. Explanations and devices worth recreating (our own words)

- **The weight's line against the base** (pp. 28–29). The three-car sequence is the right picture: the decision is where the vertical line from the centre of gravity meets the ground, inside or outside the base. CCEA's Summer 2024 Higher Q5(b)(ii) (2 marks: the object, then a reason) wanted the reason to be how high the centre of gravity sits, and the report says many could pick the less stable object but not justify the choice.
- **A checklist for the six-marker** (p. 29). The bus question lists what to cover (the meaning, the effect of height, another factor, why passengers may not stand upstairs, why it falls past a certain angle), and the model answer gives one sentence for each. A good scaffold for our Your turn, built on our own context.
- **The ring's centre is in empty space** (p. 28). The figure places the ring's centre of gravity in the middle of the hole. The Summer 2023 report says many Foundation candidates placed it wrongly.
- **Overhang by moments** (p. 31, tray and teapot). How far can a load hang beyond a shelf edge before the tray tips? Take moments about the edge. This is 1.2.23 (when the weight has a turning effect) as a calculation.

## 5. Worked examples, calculations and data tasks

- **The bus** (p. 29): a written model answer with five points (definition; lower centre of gravity more stable; wider base more stable; standing upstairs raises the centre of gravity; past a certain angle the weight acts outside the base and turns the bus over).
- **The tray** (p. 31): mark the tray's weight with an arrow; then moments about the shelf edge give the furthest the teapot can sit (answer p. 72).

## 6. Practice question types, with CCEA evidence

- **Define** the centre of gravity (2; 2023 March Higher Q9(a), Summer 2024 Higher Q5(a), 2024 November Higher Q10(i), Summer 2025 Unit 7 Higher Booklet B Q4(a)). Summer 2024 scheme (Q5(a), 2 marks): one mark for saying it is a point, which gates the second, for saying the object's entire weight can be taken to act there (`docs/sources/papers/science/2024-Summer/P1-H-Physics-MS-47741.txt`). The Summer 2025 Unit 7 report says few wrote that the weight acts there, and many wrote "gravity" for weight.
- **Mark** the centre of gravity on given shapes (1 each; 2018 November Higher Q5, Summer 2024 Higher Q5(b)(i); the ring in Summer 2023 Foundation Q5(a)).
- **Choose** the most or least stable object and **explain** (2–3; 2018 November Higher Q5, Summer 2024 Higher Q5(b)(ii), 2025 November Higher Q1: widest base, lowest centre of gravity). Summer 2025 Unit 7 Foundation Booklet B Q1(a): "area" or "surface area" given as the reason scored nothing.
- **Toppling with moments**: a crane and its counterweight (2024 March Higher Q2), which links to `p1-moments`.

## 7. Definitions and wording

- **Centre of gravity** (p. 28): the book's definition matches the specification (1.2.20), the glossary and the 2024 scheme. The tip about "point" is supported by the scheme's threshold.
- **Centre of mass** (p. 28 tip): the book says always to write "centre of gravity". CCEA's 2022 Summer Unit 7 Higher Booklet B scheme (Q4(a), naming the point through which a beam's weight acts, 1 mark) credits either name, gravity or mass (`docs/sources/papers/science/2022-Summer/U7-H-Physics-BkB-MS-21616.txt`). The scheme wins for marking: both names earn the mark. Teach "centre of gravity" as the specification's term, without telling her the other is wrong. The book itself uses "centre of mass" in its own exercises (pp. 27, 31).
- **Stability rules** (p. 28): match 1.2.22.

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Include the word "point" in the definition (p. 28).
- A ring's centre of gravity is not on the ring (p. 28 figure).
- Stability depends on the height of the centre of gravity and the width of the base, nothing else (p. 28).

## 10. GAPS: what our first note must teach, ranked by exam weight

1. **The definition in CCEA's words** with both mark points (the point; all the weight acts there), and the "gravity for weight" non-example (cite the 2025 Unit 7 report).
2. **Toppling explained through the weight's line and its moment.** See it as a three-stage tilt on our own object; Your turn: which of two objects topples first and why (height of the centre of gravity or base width, never "area").
3. **The three shapes**, ring included, as a mark-the-X item.
4. **Locating a flat shape's centre of gravity** (1.2.20, "investigate"): hang it from a pin, hang a plumb line from the same pin, draw the line; repeat from another hole; the crossing is the centre of gravity. The book omits this entirely.
5. **When the weight has a turning effect** (1.2.23): only when its line does not pass through the pivot; the overhang calculation as a Higher-flavoured Your turn.
6. **A six-mark answer scaffold** on a new context.
