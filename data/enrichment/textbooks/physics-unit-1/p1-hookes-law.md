# p1-hookes-law: textbook dossier

**Book.** CCEA's Higher Tier eGuide to Physics Unit 1 of Double Award Science, © CCEA 2023 (git-ignored under `docs/sources/textbooks/`). Page numbers are the book's printed numbers; the PDF page is the printed number plus 4.
**Ours.** **No note and no bundle yet.** The slug exists in our taxonomy and the P1 deck (`data/decks/science/P1.json`) has 9 flashcards for it; `packs/science/content/p1/` has no folder. This dossier is a briefing for the first author.
**Spec.** P1 1.2.12, 1.2.13 and Prescribed Practical P2 (all Foundation).
**Verdict.** **Thinner** (nothing authored), and the book is thin too: one and a half pages, a correct statement of the law, F = ke with flexible units, and two good length-versus-extension calculations. It has **no force–extension graph at all**, so 1.2.13 (gradient equals k) and the limit of proportionality as a point on a graph are untaught, and the practical is deferred to a separate guide. Most CCEA Hooke's-law marks come from exactly those graphs.

---

## 1. Book chapter and pages

Section 1.2: learning outcomes p. 14; "Hooke's Law" pp. 22–23 (the law, F = ke, a pointer to the Practical eGuide, two worked examples).

## 2. The book's teaching sequence

1. A short history: a seventeenth-century scientist studying how forces change the shape of a spring (p. 22).
2. The law in words: extension directly proportional to force, provided the limit of proportionality is not passed (p. 22).
3. The equation F = ke, with the extension allowed in mm, cm or m and the spring constant in the matching N/mm, N/cm or N/m (p. 22).
4. The practical is "a prescribed practical" and is sent to another guide (p. 22).
5. Two worked examples on springs whose lengths, not extensions, are given (pp. 22–23).

## 3. Explanations and devices worth recreating (our own words)

- **Lengths are not extensions** (pp. 22–23). Both worked examples give a natural or total length, so the first step is always to subtract. The second goes further: two loads give two total lengths, so the extra load divided by the extra length gives k without knowing the natural length, and the natural length is then found backwards. CCEA's Summer 2025 Higher Q1(b)(i) asks for a total length from an extension read off a graph (extension plus original length: `docs/sources/papers/science/2025-Summer/P1-H-Physics-MS-67250.txt`).
- **Units follow the extension** (p. 22). If the extension is in centimetres, k comes out in N/cm. CCEA schemes award the unit of k as its own mark (2022 March Higher Q4(b) and Summer 2023 Higher Q4(c): N/cm) and the Summer 2025 report says some candidates lost it.
- **"Show that" with a given answer** (pp. 22–23). Both examples end with a show-that; a good shape for a Your turn because she can check herself.

## 4. Diagrams and photographs

None. The book has no spring diagram, no apparatus and no graph for this topic. Every figure our note needs must be designed from the specification and CCEA's papers: a hanging spring with a ruler and a pointer; a force (y) against extension (x) graph, straight through the origin then curving after a marked limit; three springs' lines on one grid for comparing k.

## 5. Worked examples, calculations and data tasks

- **Exercise spring** (pp. 22–23): natural length given, a small force and its extension give k in N/cm; then show the force for a stated total length (subtract the natural length first).
- **Toy spring, two loads** (p. 23): two forces and two total lengths; k from the differences; the unstretched length from either load; then show the total length for a third force.

No exercise uses a graph, and none crosses the limit of proportionality.

## 6. Practice question types, with CCEA evidence

- **State** Hooke's law in words (2; 2022 March Higher Q4(a), Summer 2025 Higher Q1(a), Summer 2026 Higher Q1(a)). The 2022 scheme accepts force proportional to extension as the first mark; the second is "up to (provided … not exceeded) the limit of proportionality". "Elastic limit" is not accepted (Summer 2023 and Summer 2025 reports).
- **Name** the point where the line stops being straight (1; Summer 2023 Higher Q4(a): limit of proportionality).
- **Choose**, from three lines, which spring has the lowest k, and say why (2; Summer 2023 Higher Q4(b), 2025 November Higher Q7(a): smallest gradient).
- **Calculate** k from a graph or from one force and extension, with its unit (4–5; 2019 November Higher Q6(a), 2022 March Higher Q4(b), Summer 2023 Higher Q4(c), Summer 2025 Higher Q1(b)(ii)).
- **Share a load**: a force shared equally by several springs (Summer 2023 Higher Q4(c): many did not divide the 60 N among five springs).
- **Total length** from an extension (Summer 2025 Higher Q1(b)(i)).
- **Unit 7, Practical P2**: the method, the table and the graph.

## 7. Definitions and wording

- **Hooke's law** (p. 22): the book's statement is the specification's (P2) and the glossary's, word for word in meaning. Teach it as the one sentence to learn.
- **Spring constant**: the book defines k only through F = ke. The specification's 1.2.13 (the gradient of force against extension is numerically k) is in the book's learning-outcome list (p. 14) and nowhere else.
- **Limit of proportionality against elastic limit**: the book never mentions the elastic limit, which is good, but it also never explains the limit of proportionality as a point on a graph. CCEA's reports say candidates write "elastic limit" and may think the two are the same point.

## 8. Higher-tier-only content in the book

None (all Foundation).

## 9. Pitfalls and "remember" notes in the book

- Subtract the natural length before using F = ke (pp. 22–23).
- Keep the unit of k matched to the unit of the extension (p. 22).

## 10. GAPS: what our first note must teach, ranked by exam weight

1. **The force–extension graph.** Explain why a straight line through the origin means proportional; show the gradient as k (rise in force over run in extension) on our own numbers; mark the limit of proportionality where the line bends; Your turn: three springs, pick the smallest k and say why (smallest gradient).
2. **The law in CCEA's words**, with the elastic-limit trap as a non-example (cite the 2023 and 2025 reports).
3. **Length against extension**, both directions (extension from lengths; total length from an extension), on our own numbers.
4. **k with its unit**, as a separate step (N/cm or N/m to match the extension).
5. **Practical P2**: hang, measure the original length, add loads, measure at eye level, subtract, repeat, plot force up the side and extension along the bottom; and the shared-load case (several identical springs side by side share the force equally).

## 11. Anything in our material the book suggests is wrong or misleading

No note yet. The 9 deck cards agree with the book and the specification. The deck's trap card says examiners accept only "limit of proportionality"; that is right for the limit, and the 2022 March scheme shows the law itself may be stated either way round (force proportional to extension, or extension to force).
