# Why this platform

You already have the specification, the past papers, the mark schemes and Corbettmaths. That is a good set. Every CCEA student has the same set, and most of what is sold as "revision" is written for AQA or Edexcel and graded 9–1, which is not your exam. So the fair question is what this adds that the pile on your desk does not. Here is the honest answer.

**It knows your papers.** Not "GCSE season" — your units, on their dates, at their times: M4 at 9.15, M8 with the non-calculator paper first and the calculator paper at 10.45, Further Maths Unit 1, each science unit and the 30-minute Booklet B straight after it. Every review, every plan and every countdown runs backwards from those dates, and it changes mode in the last two weeks before each paper. It also knows the rules nobody explains clearly: the 40% terminal rule, one resit before cash-in, and that November 2027 is resit-only for Maths.

**It speaks UMS.** An A in Maths is 320 out of 400 UMS; A\* was 394 in 2025 and 393 in 2026, and because full UMS is reached below 100 raw marks, an A\* means near-full UMS on both M4 and M8. When you sit a past paper here, your raw mark becomes UMS and a grade, with the gap shown in raw marks on your unit. No calculator for CCEA Maths exists anywhere else.

**It starts from where marks are actually lost.** The Chief Examiners publish a report after every series saying which questions separated candidates and what went wrong: bounds done with 45 instead of 47.5, the median from a histogram, circle-theorem reasons without the key words, algebraic fractions cancelled across a sum, quadratics "solved" instead of formed, "hence" ignored. Those reports have been turned into the order the topics are built in, the wrong options in the diagnostics, the find-the-mistake items and the trap list on every topic sheet. You get the pattern of eight years of papers without having to read eight years of reports.

**It will not let you just read.** Each topic begins with five quick questions whose wrong answers are the real mistakes, so you know in two minutes where the gap is. Then a one-screen sheet: what the statement demands, what is on the formula sheet and what you must know, what is not on the spec, how it is examined. Then a worked example where every step is yours to type, a twin, and original questions in CCEA's layout with mark schemes in CCEA's own M/A/MA language, so you see exactly where the method mark is and why a right answer with the wrong method can score zero.

**It remembers for you.** Anything you get wrong, or right without confidence, comes back in two days and again a week later, then on a schedule tuned to your paper date. Most nights that is about ten minutes, chosen for you before you open it. No points, no leaderboards, no streak you can lose — just a map of every statement and what you will still remember on exam morning.

**It is honest about what it is.** The questions are original and every one shows what was checked before it was published. Where the platform does not yet have deep content, it says so and points you to the right Corbettmaths video by number, the right Bitesize page, the right CCEA fact file or the NI Maths Tutor solution. It links to the official papers; it never copies them.

Will it make you achieve the highest marks? Nothing can promise that. What the evidence says is that testing yourself beats re-reading, spaced review beats cramming, mixed practice beats topic-by-topic, and feedback that tells you why and what to do next beats a tick or a cross — and that these work best when someone runs them for you, consistently, aimed at your exam. That is what this is built to do, four evenings a week, and it will show you the numbers so you can judge for yourself within three weeks.
