## 2. The honest verdict

**Does it teach properly?** On the one reviewed topic, yes, and well: the explanation is in plain words with a reason on every line, the See it works the note's own numbers with the marks CCEA gives, the Your turn is answerable from what she just saw, the re-teach after a miss is written for the option she chose and shows the consequence with a figure ("Put x = 1 into both: 5 ≠ 4"), and the missed check comes back on new numbers before the recap. That is better than Seneca's "weak spots" (same format again) and Khan's hints (one step, no diagnosis), and it is CCEA-true in a way no other app is. On the other 132 topics the structure is the same, but the reviewers found the same three faults in every unit and no reviewer has yet confirmed one of them. So: proven on 1, plausible on 132, and the fixers are on it today.

**Is it rich?** In words and checks, more than rich enough: 7–13 See its and 7–13 Your turns per topic, two to five examiner findings per topic, the scheme's mark codes on working lines, videos beside (never instead of) our own steps. Richness is not the risk; **density per minute** is. A 37-minute first topic sits under a Today card that says "Ten minutes on a new topic is enough", and Unit 7's notes are 10,000 words. "Length by need" is the rule you set and I would keep it, but the hero should offer the section as the unit of an evening ("Section 1 · 4 min"), and the deck's "n of N" should not grow as twins are appended (55 became 62 during my walk: a small trust wobble on a surface built on trust).

**Is it up to standard?** The teaching structure is at the standard; the marking is not yet proven at it (every unit review: not confirmed on marking; pass 1 shipped this morning; gate 2 open); the review record that the standard requires exists on one topic; the lints are clean on the migrated units and loud on the classic ones.

**How is the experience?** Calm, clear, no manipulation, one thing per screen, a visible track, a close that tells the truth. The craft is at the floor you set on the surfaces I opened (type, spacing, the hare's places, the fern/ink answer states, no red). Typed answers behave as promised on the case I tried: "30 Nm" on FM2 moments' first Your turn is paid ("Yes."), a wrong "3" gets the re-teach that quotes step 1 of the See it, then "The answer 30 · You chose 3 · Recorded. It comes back before the recap on new numbers". Where it is behind the best: (1) the phone See it overflows: on U7 planning's first See it (6 steps) the card's content grows to 1,066 px inside a 650 px body, the last three steps appear below the fold, and in my run the card had not scrolled 300 ms after each Continue, so the step she just asked for is out of sight until she scrolls — against your own acceptance ("one card at 390×844, no vertical scroll outside a figure"); (2) one manipulable object (the tap-to-strike) in 55 cards where Brilliant has one on most screens; (3) the unit page is a plain list of 29 rows with no sign of where she is (Khan and Seneca show it per topic); (4) the first thing she reads on day one is "Nothing back tonight."; (5) two of the trial's "still open" audit items are still open: no Previous control on the phone deck, and a miss is final in Read.

**Better than the best apps:** CCEA-specific depth with cited schemes and examiner reports on every section; marks on each working line; the re-teach per wrong option with a figure; the twin on new numbers before the recap; no hearts, streaks or confetti; works offline with no account.
**Equal:** one-exercise-per-screen cadence and track (Duolingo); explain → worked example → problems (Math Academy, Brilliant); optional recall; honest minutes (DeepStash's header, adopted).
**Worse:** interaction density per screen (Brilliant); a mastery picture per unit and across the plan (Khan, Seneca); reliability of typed-answer marking (Khan's numeric marking never refuses a right form); lesson length per sitting (Brilliant 10–15 min, Duolingo 3–5); drawings per card (the art direction asks for one on every card; the trial has one on about a third).
**Missing:** practice inside the lesson (your answer 1 defers it); Papers exam mode; a "this is wrong" button on a card that files a finding; search; a visible plan across weeks.

**The three or four things a real student notices first:** the See it scroll on her phone; a check that asks the case she just watched; a right answer marked wrong (or a guess paid) — and, in B2, a feedback line that prints the engine's own key-word list ("cheap|less expensive|…") instead of a sentence; and, on the second evening, that the unit page does not show her what she has done.

## 3. Why work slowed and repeated, and what the lead must change now

I read the stall-lessons memory and tested its five causes against the log and git. Four hold exactly; one is understated. In order of weight:

1. **Shipping was the last step, not a regular one.** Builds 8 → 9 took fourteen days while 65+ content commits landed (git: 39 commits on 7 Oct, 25 on 8 Oct, none built). The owner judged build 8 for two weeks and concluded, rightly, "no improvement in the preview".
2. **The engine gate grew while it was open.** Opened 27 Sep as "verifier confirms"; three "not confirmed" rounds; every new author case was added to the same gate (the log shows "→ gate" on 7 Oct at 22:37, 22:49, 22:49, 22:55, 22:56, 22:58, 23:01, 23:03, 23:08, 23:15, 23:16, 23:18, 23:20, 23:21, 23:26, 23:30, 23:33 — seventeen additions in one hour). A gate that accepts cases cannot close.
3. **The standard was raised four times without a build between** (teach-first 24 Sep; v3 See it 27 Sep; v3.1 option notes the same evening; same-case lint 8 Oct). Each raise re-touched ~100 published topics: 117 topics committed three or more times, 1,559 withdrawn ids. Each raise was right; the sequencing was wrong.
4. **Twenty agents inside a five-hour window.** 7 Oct: 20 agents, "~1%/min of the window", cut at 23:33; 8 Oct: resumed 05:42, cut again ~06:45. Two cuts in eight hours killed every task mid-flight twice; the 7 Oct scratchpad wipe had already cost hours of rebuilt tooling. The log records the lead seeing the cut coming ("USAGE 23:27: 5-hour 82%… the cut is near; the owner ordered no waiting") and continuing: the owner's order was to keep working, not to keep twenty agents; the lead should have proposed the smaller number with the arithmetic.
5. **The main session (builds, Playwright) was absent from 29 Sep and the fork did not take over builds** until 9 Oct. This is understated in the memory: the server dying with every app restart (24 Sep, 27 Sep, 9 Oct 01:49) was known for three weeks and never supervised.

- *Holding reports for test reruns.* 9 Oct 01:27 build 9 cut → specs one file at a time → corrections → reruns → the owner reported at 02:57, ninety minutes later, and build 10 cut at 02:51 on top of it. - *Progress reported as commits and probe counts.* The 7–8 Oct log is a commit ledger; the owner's rule since 9 Oct is "what she can open".

## 4. The quality-consistency system (run from now on)

**4.1 Definition of done per topic** (one checklist; the author ticks it, the reviewer re-ticks it, the lint enforces what it can; it lives at the end of `author-topic.md` and in `REVIEWER.md` as the same text):
1. Sources read in full: spec statement, every paper and scheme for the topic 2018–2026, the examiner reports, the textbook chapter; listed in the note's `sources`.
2. Structure v3.1: every section explains → See it (2–6 steps, each with its reason ≤40 words and its mark chip) → Your turn last; first check real; ≤2 recall prompts in the close; `lesson-v2 --unit <u> --see --teach-fatal` exits 0.
3. New numbers everywhere: no Your turn, twin, worked example, find-the-mistake or question shares its case with the See it or another item (`--see` same-case 0; an overlap scan of worked examples against questions 0).
4. Every choice gate and twin carries option notes; no explanation names an option by its place.
5. Marking proved through the engine: every typed part probed with right answers in six student phrasings, three wrong answers and two near misses; awards in a table in the STATE; a refused right answer is engine-pending, never a note.
6. Tariffs and `marksTypicallyEarned` match the cited scheme line; every citation checked against the report text; "show that" pays through working only.
7. Figures: no figure prints an answer; SVG well-formed; labels ≥3.5% of the figure; dark mode checked; `figure-leaks` 0.
8. Nothing copied: `shingles` 0 for the topic; British English; no CCEA text.
9. Sizes within limits; MARKING/GATE/SIZE/CHARACTER lines 0 for the topic; withdrawn chains resolve.
10. Read as the student: the author opens Slides and Read at 390×844 and 1280×800 (the `/lab/` gallery once it lands) and reads every card once; the hero's minutes are the model's.
11. Credit-only edits in place; anything that changes what is right is withdraw-and-replace with the record.
12. The reviewer's `teach-show-check` pass record written only when 1–11 hold; its `detail` names the widths and what was read.

**4.2 The spec–content contract.** Tests never hard-code a note's shape. The e2e suite derives: the deck size from the title card's promise line or `sectionsOf(note)`; the gate order and ids from the manifest and the note; the "ready" set from `manifest.topics[].ready`; classic-page topics from `ready === false`. One helper (`e2e/content.ts`, in the e2e agent's brief) is the only place content is read. A content change can then only break a test by breaking the product.

**4.3 Continuous integration** (the ci agent's `.github/workflows/`, today): on every push and pull request — `tsc`, `vitest run`, `tsx pipeline/build-content.mts --strict`, `lesson-v2.mjs` with the fatal flags turned on for **every migrated unit** (today only FM1 fails on an answer-shown gate; the other eight warn), `figure-leaks`, `shingles`, `svg-draws`, `withdrawn`; then `next build` and the service worker; then Playwright on the export (chromium, both projects, `trace: retain-on-failure`, the HTML report as an artefact); then a readiness summary and the metrics line printed into the job summary; a preview deployment per commit (§5). Locally the same thing is `npm run verify` (one command, ten minutes, a one-screen summary). A red CI blocks nothing by machine (the owner pushes), but the lead never cuts a build from a red commit.

**4.4 The review-before-switch rule and the policy to return to.** The "structure" policy is a test-build setting (the constant in `src/lib/slides/readiness.ts`). The rule: a unit returns to "reviewed" when every one of its ready topics carries a pass record; the policy constant flips back to "reviewed" when every Year 12 unit has, and from then on a topic switches on only by review. Until then the manifest's `readyVia` tells the truth and the hero may say "test lesson" in small type. A `fail` record takes a topic off at once under both policies (already so).

**4.5 The metrics line** (printed by the lead daily from one script, `scripts/qa/metrics.mjs`, and kept in `docs/plan/builds/`):
`unit | topics | v3.1 | structure-pass | reviewed | probes-ok | lint-lines | same-case | open-findings B/M/m | specs`
e.g. `FM2 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 0 | 0/16 | 0 | 16 | 2/12/9 | green`. The line is the only progress report the owner needs between builds; it cannot be rounded up.

**4.6 The test cadence with the student.** Every build is a test candidate: the owner opens it the same day; the student sits with it once a week (or after each build that changes what she sees), on her phone, for twenty minutes, on two topics she chooses from her plan; the owner writes her words in `docs/plan/review/<date>-student-test.md` within the hour (the 13 Sep and 24 Sep files are the model) and files each finding as an issue (§5). Her words outrank the lints.

**4.7 One planned pass after each test.** Findings from a test become one pass with a written scope (which units, which classes, which surfaces), one brief, and a build at the end; nothing else touches published topics until the next test. Defects (a wrong award, a wrong number, a figure that misleads) are exempt and fixed at once.

**4.8 Risk register** (the ten ways this programme gets worse, each with its early sign and its guard):
1. *A standard raise re-touches everything.* Sign: withdrawn ids per topic rising; commits per topic >1 a week. Guard: §7's rule; the metrics line's `v3.1` column counts the new standard only for new topics until the planned pass.
2. *The engine pays a wrong answer or refuses a right one in a build she uses.* Sign: a corpus-guard count changes; a student finding says "it marked me wrong". Guard: the corpus guard and mutation battery in CI; gates as lists; engine-pending recorded, never worked around.
3. *Specs rot silently.* Sign: the suite is green while content changes, or red for a week. Guard: CI on every commit; the content contract; a red spec file owned by name within a day.
4. *The reviewer passes never get written and "structure" becomes permanent.* Sign: `reviewed` flat for a week. Guard: the metrics line; one reviewer per unit per week; the §4.4 rule.
5. *Teaching that answers its own checks, and worked examples that are the items.* Sign: `same-case` and the overlap scan above 0 on a new topic. Guard: both fatal for new topics; items 3 and 10 of the definition of done.
6. *Usage cuts truncate work.* Sign: 5-hour >80% with agents mid-task. Guard: ≤8 agents; launch only with ≥60% of a window; STATE every 20 minutes in the repo's scratchpad; resume by STATE.
7. *She does not see the work.* Sign: three days without a build; a report with no link. Guard: a build a day; the preview URL per commit; progress = the link.
8. *Rulings are lost or re-argued across compactions and sessions.* Sign: an author asks a question the log already answered. Guard: every ruling lands in `author-topic.md`, `STANDARDS.md` or a new `docs/dev/RULINGS.md` index the day it is made; the LEAD-LOG is history, not the rulebook.
9. *Scope creeps before the core is solid* (companion features, new subjects, exam mode). Sign: a build brief without a feature case. Guard: the feature-case gate; the §6 order.
10. *A permission refusal or a dead server stalls the critical path for days.* Sign: a "BLOCKED" line without an owner ask in the same hour. Guard: surface with the exact one-line command at once; one commit per engine class; agents never write outside the repo; the export server supervised.

## 7. Upgrades without collapse

**A change to the standard (a v3.x).** It is written as a dated section of `author-topic.md` with a shape number, a lint that detects it and a count of topics that meet it; it applies to new topics from that day; published topics are revisited in **one planned pass with a written scope and an end date**, never continuously; a build is cut between any two raises, and the student has seen the first before the second is declared. The metrics line carries the shape count so the owner can see the raise spread without re-reading topics. No raise is declared during a fixer or reviewer wave.

**Engine changes.** One commit per class, each with: the failing fixture first, the fix, the corpus guard unchanged (or its change listed with awards before and after), the mutation battery green. Gates are lists written once and closed inside one window; the verifier re-checks the list, not the engine. A refused right answer in the field is a fixture before it is a fix. Nothing in a pack ever works around the engine.

**Content schema evolution.** Additive fields first (as `optionNotes` and `kind` were); a shape number on the note; a migration script per shape change that reports counts; the strict build refuses a note whose declared shape its lint does not confirm; withdrawn records never change; frozen surfaces (an item's text and award) change only by withdraw-and-replace. The schema export (`schema:export`) is regenerated in the same commit.

**How we know a release is better than the last one — the three measures, compared build to build in the build note:**
1. **Teaching integrity:** topics ready *and reviewed* (not merely ready), and the same-case / overlap counts (must not rise).
2. **Marking integrity:** the corpus guard (right answers paid, wrong answers refused, over the fixture set), the engine-pending count (must fall), and the student's own "marked me wrong" findings (must fall).
3. **Her experience:** the student test's findings by class (blockers must be 0 before the next build is called better), the phone no-scroll check over the corpus, and the specs' and axe's green count.
If any of the three is worse than the last build, the build note says so in its first line.
