# Benchmarks: every surface and every teaching method against the best example

Decision 11 of the platform programme (DIRECTIVE-2.md, 23 Sep 2026): before any build, a one-page comparison per surface and per teaching method against the best-in-class example. Written by the research-plan agent on 23 Sep 2026. Every build agent reads the page for its surface before it starts; the page's "We take" column is its brief's floor and its "Acceptance" list is the test.

## How to read this, and what the sources are worth

Each page has the same shape: what we have today, in the code as verified on 22–23 Sep (docs/plan/review/2026-09-22-research-application.md holds the file and line for every claim); a four-column table (what they do better, what we do better, what we take, how it fits her exams); the sources; and the acceptance checks for the agent that builds it. "Better" and "behind" mean it: where a benchmark beats us, the page says so, and where we merely match it, the page says "equal".

Sources are of three kinds and are marked as such. **Read**: a page fetched and read by an agent on the date given (research 04, 05, 07 and the quality bar were fetched on 1 and 19 Sep 2026; the Kinnu App Store listing, Duolingo's character-animation post, Photomath's own explainer, the Headspace design case study and Anki's studying manual were fetched on 23 Sep 2026). **Snippet**: a fact taken from a search result's summary and not from the page itself. **Study**: a peer-reviewed or evidence-review finding, cited through docs/research/06-learning-science-what-works.md, which carries the DOI or URL. No copyrighted text is reproduced; where a phrase is quoted it is a few words in quotation marks with its source.

Three rules stand over every page, and no benchmark overrides them: docs/plan/emotional-design.md (no shame, no loss anxiety, no variable rewards, no XP, no leaderboards, no confetti; the word "Wrong" never appears), the companion's containment rule (nothing signed inside an answer field's container; `e2e/companion.spec.ts`), and CCEA truth underneath everything (mark language, tariffs, command words; nothing copied). Duolingo's craft is borrowed; Duolingo's manipulation is not (DIRECTIVE-2.md, "How this sits with what we already hold").

The platforms, and what each is the best example of: **Duolingo** (the lesson screen, the character system, the path, the habit loop and its costs), **Kinnu** (two-minute tiles that end in a question, the Knowledge Bank, spacing explained to the learner, calm "study aesthetic" design), **Brilliant** (one idea per screen, an interaction every 30–90 seconds, a diagram that reacts to a wrong answer, predict-then-reveal), **Khan Academy** (mastery states that decay, mastery challenges, hints that reveal one step), **Seneca** (re-presenting a missed idea in a different format, very low friction), **Anki** (the review screen, FSRS, four buttons with the next interval shown), **Headway** (one thing chosen for you each day, 15-minute summaries, a habit framed as a gift), **Headspace** (a character system built for calm), **BBC Bitesize** (the only free CCEA-correct guide pages; the baseline she already has), **Corbettmaths** (the canonical worked example and practice ladder; CCEA checklists by video number), **Dr Frost** (the finest skill decomposition, question generators, self-marking), **Sparx** (bookwork capture, a support video on every question, the XP cautionary tale), **Quizlet** (Learn mode rounds, test-date scheduling, the flashcard everyone knows), **Photomath** (a step list where every step opens into its reason, animated steps, several methods for one problem), with **Mathigon**, **Quantum Country**, **Execute Program** and **DeepStash** where research 07 and the quality bar already studied them.

Rowan's first Letter while it is owed, then the `today-open` line; "Ten minutes is enough tonight" after a three-day gap. (`src/components/home/TodayTiles.tsx`; application table A6, A11, R07-5, R07-11.) The audit of 23 Sep found Rowan silent on Today from the second open (a context-hold race in `TodayTiles.tsx:112-114` lets a requirement-free line fire once and burn its cooldown) and the `topic-open` line absent on every first visit measured (docs/plan/review/2026-09-22-platform-audit.md, must-fix 2).

(`src/components/items/StepRevealNote.tsx`, `scripts/qa/lesson-v2.mjs`; application A2, C4, R06-2.1, R07-1, R07-6; depth-stats.) ## 4. Slides (decision 9): the second way into every topic — against Duolingo's lesson screen, Kinnu's tiles and Brilliant's lessons

**What we have today.** Nothing: one way in, the gated note. The material for Slides already exists in every bundle: the hero, sections with a gate each, figures, callouts, prompts, the recap and the pointer, all in `note.blocks.json`; the depth standard measured the notes as nearly card-ready (paragraph and callout blocks at a median of 58 words, 283 of 2,093 over 75; 104 of 1,474 headings over 8 words; 27 visuals back to back; a gate at most every 5 cards): an S note is 20–26 cards at about 11–13 minutes and an H note 30–33 cards, so the renderer offers an H note in two parts with the break after the first "See it done"; the `role` the standard now requires on every heading titles the cards and finds the break (docs/plan/review/2026-09-22-depth-standard.md §3 "Slides-readiness", §6.6).

**The format, as decided.** A card-based lesson generated from the same `note.blocks.json` (authors write once): one idea, one figure or one interaction per card; a gate every few cards; progress by cards; swipe or tap; 6–12 minutes; the recap and the pointer as the last cards. The hero offers Slides and Read; Slides is the default on a first visit on a phone. Prototype on `fm1/algebraic-fractions-simplify`, judge against Duolingo, Kinnu and Brilliant with the five questions, then roll out. Practice, worked examples and exam-style stay as they are (DIRECTIVE-2.md decision 9).

| They do better | We do better | We take | How it fits her exams |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Duolingo**: one exercise per screen, a thin progress bar at the top, one primary button ("Check", then "Continue"), a feedback band that changes the whole bottom of the screen with colour and sound, a character reacting at the moment of the answer, and a session of three to five minutes that ends on a summary (research 07 §2.2 and the Duolingo animation post, *read* 1 Sep / 23 Sep). The state change on an answer is unmistakable; the critique names this as where we are behind (04-critique §1, practice row). | The content of a card is CCEA-true and original; the gate is retrieval, not recognition; the sequence is a lesson with a recap, not a drill; there are no hearts, so a wrong card costs nothing but a return. | The whole screen as the unit (no chrome but the progress track and one button); the answer state that changes the card's edge and verdict word (04-critique R4: 2 px outcome border, 4 px rule, verdict at h2); a "Continue" that is the only way on after a gate; the session summary as our close card. Not the sound, not the character mid-card (containment rule), not the hearts. | A card holds one idea of one CCEA statement; the gate on it is the retrieval that makes the idea hers before the next; the last cards are the recap ("You can now…") and the pointer to how the paper asks it. |
| **Kinnu**: a tile is a two-minute read then questions that must be answered correctly before moving on; "read or listen"; the design has "breathing room" (App Store listing, *read* 23 Sep). | Our questions can be typed numbers and expressions, and a miss teaches (reteach) instead of blocking. | The tile length as the card budget: at most ~70 words of prose per card (a 120-word section becomes two cards), one figure or one interaction per card; "must answer to move on" only at gate cards. Listening is not taken (no first-party audio; KaTeX prose does not read aloud). | 6–12 minutes a topic on a phone at 180 words a minute: the median note (700 words, 7 gates) is 4 min reading + 5 min gates ≈ 9 min. Longer notes split at "Pause here" into two sittings. |
| **Brilliant**: every screen has a diagram and most screens ask her to do something to it; a wrong answer re-runs the diagram (research 07 §2.1; ustwo case study, *read* 1 Sep). | Our figures are ours (796 in the notes), drawn for the sentence beside them, with the examiner's trap in them. | The rule that a figure card is never a picture with a caption: it carries the gate about it ("which bar has the greater area?") or the "predict" tap (page 6 / A14). The wrong-answer figure (A15) lands here first. | A figure card for a CCEA figure type (histogram, circle theorem, force diagram) is the exam's own object; the gate on it rehearses the exam's own question. |

| **Headway**: a summary is a stack of cards with one idea each and a stated length; "daily insights" are single cards (features page, *snippet* 23 Sep). | — | The stated length on the first card ("About 9 minutes · 22 cards") and the honest count of cards done. | — |

**The generator, from `note.blocks.json` (so authors write once).** Split rules: a `hero` block → the title card (display title, lede, minutes, card count, "Start"); each `h` → a section card (number and heading, the first paragraph); each `p` → a prose card, split at sentence boundaries so no card exceeds ~70 words; a `callout` of kind `why` or `notonspec` → its own card with its label; `figure` / `photo` / `video` / `sim` → a media card, never alone: it carries the next gate if one follows within two blocks, else a "look for…" line taken from the caption; `gate` → a gate card (one control, "Check", "Continue" after the reveal, the note's own "Yes." / "Not quite." and `explain`); `prompt` → a recall card (question, tap to reveal, Again/Good/Easy as the flashcards); `pause` → a "Pause here" card with "Stop for tonight" and "Carry on"; the "You can now" heading and its paragraph → the recap card; the closing panel → the pointer card ("In the exam"); then the hand-off card ("Practise this topic", "Worked examples", "Back to Read"). Gate ids and prompt ids are the same as in Read, so `recordAttempt` and the flow table see the same items and a topic half-done in Slides resumes in Read at the same section.

**Added from the research pass (23 Sep 2026).** The Duolingo lesson, documented: one question per screen with a grey progress bar filling green; select, then Check; a correct answer gets a chime and a green banner with CONTINUE, a wrong one a red banner showing the correct solution and GOT IT, with a gentle sound and no blame; mistakes are repeated at the end of the lesson; about fifteen items in three to five minutes, rising from multiple choice to word banks to typing, ending on an easier item; you cannot go back to earlier questions (UsabilityGeek 2017 and Blake Crosley, *read*, 3rd-party). "Explain My Answer" appears after right and wrong answers and has been free since January 2026 (Duolingo blog, *read*). Two design consequences for Slides: a gate card is followed by its explanation on both outcomes (our `explain` already exists for both), and a missed gate is offered again as the last card before the recap (the "mistakes repeated at the end" pattern, which is also H8's retry in a better place). Unlike Duolingo, Slides lets her swipe back to re-read a card; only a gate's recorded answer is fixed. The evidence for cards over long reading is the segmenting effect, not paging as such: Rey et al. 2019 (56 studies) found small-to-medium gains in retention and transfer for learner-paced segments at the cost of more time, strongest for learners with more prior knowledge; Noetel et al. 2022 (29 reviews) list segmenting among eleven robust principles; a 2024/25 microlearning review is positive but weak; and scrolling versus pages is mixed (Sanchez & Wiley 2009 against a 2025 replication finding no difference) (*snippet/read*). So the Slides format earns its place through one idea per card and a gate every few cards, and the judgement in 3.4 must test that, not the swipe.

**Sources.** Research 07 §2.1–2.2 (Brilliant, Duolingo; read 1 Sep 2026); Duolingo character animation post (read 23 Sep 2026); Kinnu App Store listing (read 23 Sep 2026); Photomath 101 (read 23 Sep 2026); Headway features (snippet 23 Sep 2026); research 06 §13 on segmenting and learner-paced segments (Mayer; Brame 2016; study) and §2 on retrieval after every unit of reading; 04-critique R4 (the state change on a marked answer).

**Acceptance (slides agent).** Generated only: no bundle is edited and the card list is a pure function of `note.blocks.json` with a unit test on the prototype topic's exact card count; one card on screen at 390×844 with no vertical scroll except inside a figure; prose per card ≤ 70 words (a lint over all 180 notes reports the distribution); a gate card blocks advance until answered and records through `recordAttempt` with the Read gate's id; progress "n of N" and a 3 px track; swipe, tap and arrow keys all advance; under `prefers-reduced-motion` `getAnimations().length === 0`; the five questions on the title card, a prose card, a figure card, a gate card and the recap card; the hero shows both buttons and remembers the choice per device; the first-visit default on a phone is Slides; the prototype judged against Duolingo, Kinnu and Brilliant in a written note before roll-out; e2e in `e2e/learn.spec.ts` or a new `e2e/slides.spec.ts`.

## 5. The gate — against Mathigon's blanks, Kinnu's "answer to continue", Brilliant's one question per screen and Execute Program's reviews

**What we have today.** A gate every ≤120 words: blank, choice or number; nothing after it renders until it is answered; "Yes." or "Not quite." with the authored explanation; the first answer is recorded as an attempt and becomes a review card; a wrong gate opens the next stretch at once and cannot be re-answered (`StepRevealNote.tsx:172`; application R06-2.1, H8).

| They do better | We do better | We take | How it fits her exams |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mathigon**: the blank sits inside the sentence; the page reacts to the commit; a wrong fill can be tried again (research 07 §2.4). | Our gate is a real question with a real answer spec, marked by the engine (numbers, blanks, choices), and it comes back in the review inbox with its figure. | One retry after "Not quite." with the explanation shown, the first answer kept as the record (H8). | The retry is where the correction lands on a formed expectation (research 06 §9 hypercorrection; study). |
| **Kinnu**: the question must be answered correctly before the next tile opens (App Store listing, *read* 23 Sep). | We do not lock: a wrong answer teaches and opens the next stretch (the 13 Sep review's teach-first rule). Better for a first-timer. | Nothing. | — |
