# Making Cairn better: an adviser's review

26 September 2026, written by the cloud session after the engine re-verification, for the owner. It is a second pair of eyes on the whole platform, measured against how the best modern learning platforms are built and judged. It does not replace the programme (`docs/plan/2026-09-22-programme.md`) or any studied case. Where it proposes something new, the owner's rule stands: a case first, then the build.

The numbers are this branch's, on 26 September: 198 published topics, 2,588 questions, 4,392 marked parts, 489 worked examples, 385 find-the-mistake items, 1,723 gates, 818 prompts and 170 videos.

---

## 1. The verdict in five lines

1. **The foundations are rare for a platform this young.** The marking engine is exam-true and, since today, verified by 13,988 probes. The learning design is argued from evidence and from her own words. Accessibility is tested in the browser, and nothing about her leaves the device.
2. **The biggest risk is not a missing feature: it is correctness and completeness at scale.**
   - A third of marked parts (1,404 text answers) are marked by key words that nobody has measured.
   - Year 12 still lacks 12 topics. Four of them are Unit 7, which is a quarter of her Double Award.
3. **The second risk is fragility.**
   - Nothing checks a change automatically when it is pushed.
   - The programme's memory lives on one laptop.
   - Her progress lives in one browser, with no reminder to back it up.
   - Her access depends on that laptop being on.
4. **The teach-first decision is the hinge.** Until the eight answers are in, every new Year 12 topic is written to a structure that will then be migrated. Decided now, the remaining topics are written once.
5. **The next month should buy certainty, not surface.**
   - Decide v3 and pilot it on FM1 with her.
   - Finish Year 12 to v3.
   - Make every change prove itself automatically.

   Practice modes, the flashcards restyle and motion come after.

## 2. What is already excellent, and must be protected

- **Exam-true marking, now with a verifier.**
  - The engine marks numbers, algebra, demanded forms, units, equations, tables and steps, and pays follow-through.
  - It diagnoses 3,697 probed common errors, many sourced to CCEA examiners' reports.
  - `npm run content:check` now fails if any published right answer is refused, or any known wrong answer is paid in full.
  - Against the engine of 24 September: 13 refused right answers are now paid, overpaid errors fell from 47 to 1, and there are no regressions.
- **Learning design from evidence and from her.** Each of these has a test that proves it:
  - teach before test (her finding of 13 September);
  - worked examples that fade;
  - retrieval that is spaced (FSRS) and short (her finding of 24 September);
  - sooner returns for confident misses;
  - distractors tagged with the misconception they catch;
  - right answers spread over A, B and C (her finding of 24 September).
- **Accessibility treated as a test, not a promise.**
  - Contrast is measured in Playwright: 3:1 on interactive edges (WCAG 1.4.11) and 4.5:1 on the accent control.
  - There is a keyboard focus model, and reduced motion is honoured.
  - Maths is output as MathML for screen readers.
  - Nothing is red on a miss.
- **Calm by design.** There is no streak pressure, no leaderboard and no nagging. The close card says what comes back and when.
- **Privacy by construction.**
  - No account, no analytics, and nothing sent anywhere.
  - An offline copy of every topic and paper.
- **A process that studies before it builds.** Audits carry IDs, cases carry sources, and acceptance is written as Playwright checks. It is slower, and it is why the quality is what it is.

## 3. The ten moves that matter most, in order

### 1. Decide teach-first v3 this weekend, and write the rest of Year 12 to it once

- **Why.**
  - Twelve Year 12 topics are still to be written.
  - The case says new topics are written to v3 from the start, so every topic written before the decision is written twice.
  - Section 5 gives the eight questions with a recommendation for each.
- **Cost:** an hour of the owner's time.
- **Gain:**
  - No second pass on those notes.
  - A settled brief for Unit 7.
  - A migration that can start on FM1.

### 2. Make every change prove itself: continuous checks on GitHub

- **Why.**
  - The overnight engine work of 24 to 25 September shipped two regressions, and a test suite that no longer matched the note. Nothing stopped it.
  - A workflow on each push would have caught both, in minutes. It runs the typecheck, the 2,088 unit tests and `content:check` with the marking guard.
  - Playwright can run in the same place nightly or on request. There it is alone by construction, which retires the "never under a build" hazard.
- **Cost:**
  - One workflow file.
  - The free Actions minutes cover the fast checks.
  - The whole Playwright suite ran here in 14.7 minutes (244 tests, alone). Nightly runs plus the fast checks on each push should fit GitHub's free allowance for a private repository. The account's plan decides.
- **Who decides:** the owner, since it uses the account's minutes.

### 3. Finish Year 12, Unit 7 first

- **Why.**
  - Unit 7 (practical skills) is 25% of the Double Award:
    - Booklet A: 7.5% for the three practical tasks;
    - Booklet B: 17.5% for the written papers, set in practical contexts.
  - That is more than B2, C2 or P2, which are 14% each. Unit 7 has 0 of its 4 topics, and it needs a brief first.
  - The examiners' reports of 2024 and 2025 name the same kinds of loss:
    - apparatus named and spelt wrongly;
    - the unit and direction of a moment ("very poorly answered");
    - reliability confused with accuracy;
    - the control variable;
    - a total gas volume found by adding up a table's values.

    A practice bank and a Slides deck drill exactly this.
  - Then B2's two topics, C2's two and P2's space block, then B2 E.
- **Cost:** 12 topics written to v3; Unit 7's brief first.

### 4. Measure the text marker the way the guard measures the rest

- **Why.**
  - 1,333 text parts and 71 extended answers, 32% of all marked parts, are marked by key words and patterns.
  - The guard checks that the printed answer is accepted. It cannot tell whether "the active site changes shape" is paid and "the enzyme dies" is refused.
  - Exam boards standardise their examiners on seeded scripts. Do the same offline:
    1. For each text part, write 8 to 12 answers: right in other words, right with a slip, partial, and the common wrong ones.
    2. Label them against the mark scheme with one model, and check the labels with a second.
    3. Have a person spot-check a sample.
    4. Report the marker's agreement per part, and repair the parts below a threshold.
  - This runs at authoring time, so nothing changes for privacy or offline use.
- **Cost:** about an agent-day per unit. The guard's harness already exists.

### 5. Put a qualified person in the loop

- **Why.**
  - "Would a Chief Examiner sign it?" is today answered by agents reading agents. That catches much, but not the errors agents share.
  - Four to six hours from a CCEA teacher of maths and of science would be the strongest trust signal the platform can buy: one topic per unit, plus 50 randomly drawn auto-marked items.
  - The platforms she would compare it with (Sparx, Dr Frost, Save My Exams) are written or reviewed by teachers.
- **Cost:** a tutor's fee.
- **Who decides:** the owner.

### 6. Make her access and her data robust

- **What is wrong.**
  - **Opening it on the phone by the laptop's address** (http://192.168.…:3200):
    - The browser treats a plain-http address other than localhost as insecure, so the service worker never registers. There is no offline copy and no update, whatever `docs/dev/deploy.md` says.
    - If the laptop's address changes, the browser sees a new site and her progress appears empty. It is still there, under the old address.
  - **Her progress lives only in the browser.**
    - The app never asks for persistent storage (`navigator.storage.persist()`).
    - On an iPhone, Safari can clear a site's stored data after seven days of use without a visit, unless the site is on the Home Screen.
  - **The backup is a manual export with no reminder** (programme 10.3, not built).
- **The moves.** None of them sends data anywhere.
  1. A private HTTPS address. The static export deploys to Vercel or Cloudflare Pages as it is, behind access protection, so the phone can install it.
  2. A request for persistent storage after the first sitting.
  3. The fortnightly backup line.
- **Who decides:** the owner, since it is about where she uses it.

### 7. Put the programme's memory in the repository

- **Why.**
  - The queue, the standards, the agent briefs and every checkpoint lived in a temp folder on one machine.
  - When the 5-hour limit stopped the agents, the cloud could not resume B2 D, FM3 or C2 D.
  - `docs/plan/QUEUE.md` §5 lists the files. Copying them into `docs/agents/` makes the programme resumable from anywhere, and its decisions reviewable in history.
- **Cost:** minutes.

### 8. Close the loop on her learning, not only on features

- **Why.**
  - The platform records every attempt with its time, but nothing turns that into what a sit-down needs.
  - Modern platforms show the supporting adult what was learnt, what is slipping and what to do next.
- **Proposal:** a case first, for a one-page "Fortnight" view.
  - Built on her device, and shown to her first.
  - The two of you look at it together.
  - It shows:
    - topics finished;
    - retention on delayed reviews (the cairn's own rule: right in a mixed set two or more days later);
    - the gates and questions missed twice, which is the content-repair list;
    - review debt;
    - minutes per week.
  - With one learner the numbers are small. They are for conversation, not grading.

### 9. Keep a deck to a sitting's length

- **Why.**
  - The trial deck is 38 cards and about 21 minutes by its own count, 5 min 41 s of it the video.
  - Slides drops Read's stopping points.
  - On a phone at night, the best lesson apps keep a unit to a few minutes and make stopping safe. The run already resumes where she left.
- **Proposal:** a small case. A "good place to stop" at the end of each section, and the minutes left shown on the track.

### 10. Hold the scope

- Eleven streams and 94 items is a programme for a team.
- Her evenings are the scarce resource, and her grades come from content, marking and review.
- Until Year 12 is complete at v3, do not start:
  - the icons-and-motion pass;
  - the flashcards restyle;
  - new practice modes.

  Each is approved. Each is better built on finished content.

## 5. The eight teach-first questions: a recommendation for each

The case is `docs/plan/review/2026-09-24-teach-first-case.md`, section 12. Seven of these agree with the case. Question 8 differs.

1. **Where the questions proper run:**
   - now: the Practice stage, which the close card's first button already reaches;
   - for Year 12, after the migration: inside Slides, as the case says.

   Build that renderer once the content it serves is settled.
2. **After a miss:** the twin comes before the recap only.
   - A second try that is spaced helps retention more than an immediate repeat, and it keeps the section's rhythm.
   - An immediate twin doubles the authoring for the smallest gain.
3. **Recall cards:** at most two, with Skip, and each answerable in about twelve words.
   - Her "like writing an essay" was about length.
   - Two short cards hold what the paper needs where there is no formula sheet.
4. **The video:** never a section's only See it. Our own steps with the marks come first, with the video beside them.
   - Our steps work offline and carry the marks on each line.
   - They show CCEA's method, and they take honest minutes.
5. **Skip to your turn:** on return visits only. The first pass is the lesson.
6. **The name on screen:** "Your turn". A teacher's phrase that says "you can do this now", where "Check" says "test".
7. **The pace:** the trial topic and FM1 first, then the second sit-down, then the rest. Not the whole 220 agent-hours at once.
   - It is the owner's own rule: a trial before any roll-out.
   - The budget is tight.
   - Her reaction to v3 over a whole unit is the evidence the other 124 notes need.
8. **The first check of a topic:** a real one, answerable from the first See it, rather than an interface warm-up.
   - She read the warm-up as being tested before being taught.
   - An easy real check gives the same early success, and teaches something.
   - The See it card's own Continue teaches the interface.

## 7. What not to do

- **A runtime AI tutor or chat, in the next two months.**
  - It breaks offline use and on-device privacy.
  - It can be confidently wrong in maths.
  - The Department for Education's product safety expectations for generative AI in education (England, 2025) set a bar that is a project in itself: filtering, monitoring and data protection.
  - Use models where they are already strong and safe: authoring, second readings and the text-marking guard.
- **Accounts or cloud sync.** The export and a private address cover her needs.
- **Streaks, points or leaderboards.** The calm design is an asset.
- **Slides and Read v2 on every Year 12 topic before that topic's v3 pass.** It would be done twice. Roll it out unit by unit, after each unit's pass.
- **Holding the 17 October date without the decision.** "Slides and Read on every Year 12 topic" needs about a week of three to four authors after the pilot, so the date holds only if the eight answers come this weekend.

## 9. How we will know it is working

- **Her.** At each sit-down: the review's four questions, plus "did you learn it, and did you want to come back?"
- **Delayed retention.** The share of reviews right two or more days later, by unit, rising.
- **Marking trust.**
  - The guard at 0 unexplained failures.
  - The text guard's agreement at 95% or more on every Year 12 text part.
  - No "it marked me wrong when I was right" in her reports.
- **Completion.** Every Year 12 topic published at v3; Year 11 by the end of November.
- **Reliability.**
  - Every push checked.
  - No build handed to her without green checks.
  - A backup in the last fortnight.
