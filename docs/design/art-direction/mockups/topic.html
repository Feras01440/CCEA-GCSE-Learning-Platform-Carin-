A bar chart shows how many by how tall. A histogram fixes it by letting the area of each bar carry the count.

1 · Read and check · 2 min

On an ordinary bar chart the height of a bar is the count, which works only while every bar is the same width. Widen one group and its bar towers over the others for no honest reason. A histogram makes the area of each bar the count instead, so the height becomes a rate and the eye compares areas.

The height of a bar is therefore a rate: values per unit of the variable, called frequency density. So frequency density = frequency ÷ class width, and everything in this topic — drawing the bars, reading them back, estimating the median — comes from area of bar = frequency.

Why area and not height

A bar's area is height × width. If the height is values per unit, multiplying by the width in units gives values — the frequency itself. That is why the area of every bar reads as a count, whatever width the class happens to be.
