Total qualification UMS = 400 (180 + 220). Question counts on Summer 2025 papers: M1 28 questions; M4 22 questions; M8 P1 14 questions (50 marks); M8 P2 14 questions (50 marks). Front-page instructions: black ink only, HB pencil for drawings; "You should have a calculator, ruler, compasses and a protractor" (calculator papers) / "ruler, compasses and a protractor" (non-calculator); "All working must be clearly shown… Marks may be awarded for partially correct solutions."

Scheduling rule (Spec §4.5): M1–M4 are timetabled at the same time (so a candidate can sit only one); M5–M8 are timetabled concurrently on a different day, with Paper 2 (calculator) immediately after Paper 1 (non-calculator).

### 3.2 Tier / pathway combinations and grades available (Spec §4.5)

| Combination | Final GCSE grades available |
|---|---|
| M1 + M5 | D – G |
| M2 + M6 | C\* – G |
| M3 + M7 | B – E |
| M4 + M8 | A\* – D |

Rules: "Students take two units, one from M1, M2, M3 or M4 and one from M5, M6, M7 or M8. To receive an award, one of these must be a completion test." The spec's §4.5 sentence says candidates "may enter any one of Units M1, M2, M3 or M4 together with any one of Units M5, M6 or M8" (M7 is evidently an omission in the spec text). Mixed-tier combinations are permitted by CCEA's general rules ("students may take different units at different tiers of entry"; a tiered unit may be re-sat once at either tier) — CCEA Guidance Notes: Entry, Re-sit and Aggregation Rules (local copy) and the Nov–March Q&A (Q7: a cashed-in student "may also wish to change their tier or pathway of entry").

Foundation UMS cap: a Foundation unit cannot score above one UMS below the notional grade-b threshold (M2 max 131 vs b = 132; M6 max 160 vs b = 161); M1 max 107 (c = 108), M5 max 131 (c\* = 148 not reachable; c = 132). Higher M3 max 143 (a = 144), M7 max 175 (a = 176).

Subject-level UMS boundaries (fixed for the life of the spec, out of 400): **A 320, B 292, C\* 268, C 240, D 200, E 160, F 120, G 80**. A\* is set each series: **394 (Summer 2025), 393 (Summer 2026)**.

### 3.3 Terminal rule, resits, cash-in (Spec §4.1; CCEA guidance notes; Nov–March Q&A)

- Terminal (40%) rule: at least 40% of the assessment must be taken in the series in which the qualification is cashed in (so the completion test, 55%, or the M1–M4 unit, 45%, either satisfies it).
- Each unit may be re-sat once before cash-in; the better UMS counts, unless the unit is used to satisfy the terminal rule (then the most recent counts).
- After cash-in the qualification may be retaken; up to two further attempts at each unit, only the higher of the last two counting (or the last if used for the terminal requirement).
- Unit results remain valid until CCEA withdraws the specification.
- Cash-in code G9602; entry codes GMC1–GMC8 in CCEA documents (GMC11…GMC82 on timetables).

### 3.4 Functional Mathematics (Spec §4.4–4.5)
Assessed within M1 (Level 1) and M2 (Level 1 or Level 2) via a raw-mark threshold set each series; Level 2 is reported as an endorsement on the certificate. The M1 paper front page states "Functional Mathematics is assessed in this unit" (specimen).

## 5. Calculators (Spec §4.6)

- Calculators **must** be used in M1, M2, M3, M4 and in Paper 2 of each completion test (M5–M8). Calculators **must not** be used in Paper 1 of each completion test.
- Minimum functions: +, −, ×, ÷, √, x^y and single memory. Higher Tier: trigonometric and relevant statistical functions; Foundation: relevant statistical functions.
- Prohibited: symbolic algebra/differentiation/integration, language translators, communication, stored formulae/text; no lids/cases with printed instructions.
- Examiner reports repeatedly note candidates sitting calculator papers without a calculator, or in the wrong mode (fraction display e.g. "5 25/…" instead of 7.84).

## 6. Formulae: what is given vs what must be memorised

CCEA prints a **Formula Sheet on page 2 of every paper** (specimens 2017 and Summer 2025 papers verified by rendering page 2 of M1, M2, M3, M4, M6 P1, M7 P1, M8 P1, M8 P2). This is a long-standing CCEA arrangement; it is *not* the DfE/Ofqual "2025–2027 formulae sheet" concession for English boards (which does not apply to CCEA). No CCEA-specific change to formula provision for 2025–2027 was found in any circular, addendum or report.

### 6.1 Foundation Tier sheet (M1, M2, M5, M6 — identical)
1. Area of trapezium = ½(a + b)h (with diagram)
2. Volume of prism = area of cross section × length (with diagram)

### 6.2 Higher Tier sheet (M3, M4, M7, M8 — identical on all Higher papers checked)
1. Volume of prism = area of cross section × length
2. Area of trapezium = ½(a + b)h
3. Volume of sphere = 4/3 πr³
4. Surface area of sphere = 4πr²
5. Volume of cone = ⅓ πr²h
6. Curved surface area of cone = πrl
7. Quadratic formula: x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] / 2a for ax² + bx + c = 0, a ≠ 0
8. Sine Rule: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C
9. Cosine Rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A
10. Area of triangle = ½ ab sin C

Local images: `docs/sources/maths/formula-sheets/*.png` and `docs/sources/maths/formula-sheet-M*-Summer2025-p2.png`.

### 6.3 Must be memorised (not on the sheet — inferred from spec content and examiner comments)
Foundation and Higher: circumference = πd = 2πr and area = πr² of a circle (examiners: "wrote both down but used neither correctly"); areas of triangle, rectangle, parallelogram, kite, rhombus; surface area/volume of cubes, cuboids, cylinders (as prisms); Pythagoras' theorem; speed = distance ÷ time, density = mass ÷ volume, pressure = force ÷ area; percentage change, simple and compound interest multipliers; mean from (grouped) frequency tables; angle sums (triangle 180°, quadrilateral 360°, polygon (n−2)×180°, exterior angles sum 360°); midpoint and length of a line segment; gradient = rise/run and y = mx + c; nth term of linear sequences; index laws; probability rules (P(not A) = 1 − P(A); add for mutually exclusive, multiply for independent); relative frequency; conversions (1 litre = 1000 cm³ = 1000 ml, 1 cm = 10 mm, 1 kg = 1000 g), common imperial ↔ metric approximations (5 miles ≈ 8 km).
Higher additionally: trig ratios sin/cos/tan (SOHCAHTOA), arc length and sector area as fractions of the circle, surface area of cylinder (2πrh + 2πr²), gradients of parallel (equal) and perpendicular (product −1) lines, equation of a circle x² + y² = r², laws of indices for fractional/negative powers, surd rules and rationalising, bounds, cumulative frequency / quartiles / IQR, frequency density = frequency ÷ class width, stratified sampling proportion, circle theorems (angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference, angles in same segment, angle in a semicircle, cyclic quadrilateral opposite angles, tangent–radius, alternate segment, equal tangents), similar-shape scale factors (k, k², k³), direct/inverse proportion forms (y = kx, y = kx², y = k/x …), compound-interest/growth-decay formula A = P(1 ± r/100)^n.

## 7. Subject content — full learning outcomes by unit (Spec §3, Version 2)

Transcribed from the specification PDF text. A few typeset formulae were lost in text extraction and are reconstructed in standard notation (flagged "[reconstructed]").

### 7.1 Unit M1: Foundation Tier — targets D, E, F, G and Functional Mathematics Level 1

**Number and algebra** — students should be able to:
- use the 4 operations applied to positive and negative integers, including efficient written methods;
- order positive and negative integers, decimals and fractions;
- use symbols =, ≠, <, >, ≤ and ≥;
- use calculators effectively and efficiently;
- understand and use conventional notation for the priority of operations, including brackets, powers, roots and reciprocals;
- recognise and use relationships between operations, including inverse operations;
- use index notation for squares, cubes and powers of 10;
- use the concepts and vocabulary of factor, multiple, common factor, common multiple and prime;
- use the terms square, positive and negative square root, cube and cube root;
- understand place value and decimal places;
- read, write and compare decimals up to three decimal places;
- add, subtract, multiply and divide decimals up to 3 decimal places;
- round to a specified or appropriate degree of accuracy, number of decimal places or 1 significant figure, including a given power of 10;
- use correct decimal notation when working with money;
- understand and use equivalent fractions;
- write a simple fraction as a terminating decimal;

## 11. Chief Examiner's reports — what candidates find hardest

Reports are organised by unit (M1, M2, M3, M4, M51, M52, M61, M62, M71, M72, M81, M82) with a "Unit Overview" then question-by-question commentary. Findings below are extracted from the Summer 2025 and Summer 2024 reports in full, plus the unit overviews of November 2025, November 2024, Summer 2023 and November 2023.

### 11.1 Summer 2025 (report published 20/07/2026 re-issue)

**M1 (Foundation, calc)** — Q28 compound area of a "badge" made from triangles and rectangle was "inaccessible to many… frequently left blank"; Q16 volume of a cuboid ("many summed the three dimensions", 140 common) and depth when 60 litres poured in (only a handful converted 60 l → 60 000 cm³); Q12 writing expressions h + 5 and 2h + 5 ("candidates continue to struggle… very few"); Q10(c) ordering 0.6, 0.605, 0.61 ("large majority unable"); Q22 comparing fractions/decimals — 2½ read as 2 × ½ = 1; Q23 percentage increase — answers 108% and 92% "seen more frequently than the correct 8%"; Q26 solving 3(y − 7) = 18 — brackets expanded wrongly, y = 8.33 common; Q27 mean from a frequency table — divided by 4 (number of rows) not 20, and range taken from frequencies; Q19 collecting terms with negatives (5c + −2d) and expanding 5(2t + 5); Q20 base angles of isosceles triangles; Q13 measuring angles (wrong protractor scale), cm → mm (×10); Q24 multi-step money problem with fractions of 200 notes; Q25 45% then ⅗ of the remainder; money notation (£441 for £4.41; 1 d.p. money answers); poor written explanations throughout.

**M2 (Foundation, calc)** — Overview: "candidates thinking that the mixed number 2½ is somehow equal to one"; converting fractions to (rounded) decimals/percentages to find a fraction of an amount (⅔ → 28.57%); answering the question they expected rather than the one asked; AO3 questions 8(b), 21, 28 often blank; "most candidates struggled to find the volume of a cylinder" (Q26: 90% scored 0; radius 12 used instead of 6; circumference formula used). Q28 Pythagoras to find a length then an area — "fewer than 5% correct"; Q21 compound area (most challenging); Q25 forming 4a − 2 = 24 (rarely seen); Q24 midpoint reversal; Q19 solving 3(y − 7) = 18 ("very few applied an algebraic method"); Q18 expression in pence (2 − 3x, £ vs p); Q15 percentage increase (108%); Q23 percentage change of a perimeter (97.5% vs 2.5%); Q22 HCF (listing factors incompletely); Q14 2½ = 1 misconception.

**M3 (Higher, calc)** — Q2 depth of liquid in container: "three quarters of the entry calculated the volume of the container and could proceed no further" (0 marks); Q10 forming an expression in pence/3x: >70% scored 0; Q12 adding algebraic fractions (only ⅓ found common denominator; >½ could not start); Q14 compound-area problem (most challenging; "more challenging than anticipated"); Q16 equation of a line through the midpoint (only top 10%); Q19 surface area of cylinder confused with volume; pressure = force ÷ area not understood; Q21 Pythagoras then area (>½ no marks); Q22 reverse percentage in an unusual wording; Q24(c) IQR from cumulative frequency (values in £1000s); Q25 factorising with 4a as common factor (only the very best) and quadratic (30%); Q26 arc length with 260° reflex angle (circumference ÷ 260 seen); mixed number 2½ read as 1 even at this tier.

**M4 (Higher, calc)** — Overview list of "most problems": Q13 bounds (50 kg to nearest 5 kg → 47.5 not 45; whole-number answer); Q15(a) "show that" — setting up a quadratic from the area of a right-angled triangle (full marks or zero; many tried to solve instead); Q17(b) circle theorems needing two steps (isosceles triangle + alternate segment theorem); Q18 reasoning on sample representation (30% of 142); Q20 factorising and simplifying an algebraic fraction (grouping method, difference of two squares with terms reversed); Q21 adding algebraic fractions with a subtraction in the numerator ("most could not deal with the subtraction of more than one term"); Q22 histograms — drawing from a table (frequency density not found; own scales; axes unlabelled), **estimating the median from a histogram "continues to cause problems for the vast majority"**, stratified sampling (many blank). Also: brackets in algebra, rounding/accuracy, "if multiple solutions are left… the worst solution is marked". ### 11.4 Cross-series "hardest topics" ranking

**Higher (M3/M4/M7/M8)** — recurring every series:
1. Setting up (then solving) quadratic equations from geometry — Pythagoras, areas of triangle/trapezium/square, surface area of a cuboid (5–7% full marks typical).
2. Circle theorems with written reasons (alternate segment, isosceles from equal tangents, cyclic quadrilateral misapplied) (≈6% full marks on multi-step parts).
3. Histograms: drawing from a table (frequency density), estimating the median from a histogram, and sampling/stratified sampling (majority score 0).
4. Algebraic fractions: adding/subtracting with a subtraction in the numerator; simplifying via factorising (grouping, DOTS); solving equations with algebra in the denominator (9–18% full marks).
5. Equation of a perpendicular line when the given line must be rearranged; using perpendicular gradients to find a coordinate.
6. Bounds (nearest 5 → ±2.5; in formulae; with time conversions).
7. Proof/reasoning questions (sphere vs cube surface area; sample representation; "show that").
8. Changing the subject when the subject appears twice.
9. Combined transformations (reflection in y = x then enlargement with a given centre); describing enlargements/rotations completely.
10. Direct and inverse proportion with algebra (y ∝ x², y ∝ 1/x).
11. Surds — equations, simplification, rationalising; rational vs irrational.
12. Coordinate geometry of the circle: tangent, radius, equation of circle/chord.
13. 3D trigonometry (space diagonal, angle to base); multi-step sine rule/cosine rule/½ab sin C problems.
14. Standard form combined with percentage change and units.
15. Simultaneous equations when both equations must be multiplied; graphical solutions (line must be drawn).
16. Mensuration: cone/sphere/hemisphere/frustum, cylinder surface area (confused with volume), sector arc with reflex angle, radius from volume, cone-sector apex angle, pressure = force/area.
17. Cumulative frequency (scale reading; IQR in £1000s; comparing with correct vocabulary), IQR from a list.
18. Reverse percentages (28% subtracted instead of ÷1.28) and compound interest with different rates.
19. Population estimation from a sample.
20. Inequalities (keeping the sign; forming from words), index laws with negative/fractional powers and algebraic quotients.

**Foundation (M1/M2/M5/M6)**:
1. Fractions of amounts (⅓ ≠ 30%/33%; ⅔ ≠ 28.57%; mixed number 2½ read as 1); comparing fractions/decimals.
2. Percentage increase/decrease and percentage of an amount without a calculator (108%/92% answers; 6% of 700; 2% of 7200); compound vs simple interest.
3. Ratio in non-"share" form (3 : 5 given one part; 14 : 6 of 20; 1 : 4 after subtracting) and unit-rate proportion.
4. Algebra: writing expressions from words (h + 5, 2h + 5, 3x pence), expanding brackets, solving 5(2y + 3) = 79 or 3(y − 7) = 18, forming equations (1% did), substituting into p = ne/s (163), working backwards through a formula, inequalities, simultaneous equations (graphical/elimination), nth term.
5. Area/volume: perimeter vs area confusion; compound areas (badge; grass); volume of cuboid (dimensions added) and cylinder (90% zero); litres ↔ cm³; trapezium-prism length; tins/boxes rounding up; m² read as 7².
6. Pythagoras (M2: >80% zero; <5% on area follow-up).
7. Statistics: mean from frequency table (divide by 4/6 not total frequency), estimated mean from grouped table (75% zero), median with even n, range vs average, comparing distributions with named statistics, Venn diagrams (<10%), pie chart totals/fractions, conversion graphs (reading, scaling, drawing with negatives), scatter "relationship" vs "correlation".
8. Measures: unit conversions (ml/l, cm/mm, kg/g, km/miles, gallons/litres), time intervals, average speed for a whole journey, map scales 1 : 25 000, reading scales/gauges, imperial equivalents, "continuous nature of measure".
9. Geometry: circumference in context (ribbon round tables — 4%), angle chases and polygon angle sums (540; exterior 360 ÷ n), isosceles base angles, plans/elevations, kite identification, transformations described fully (reflection in x-axis/y = 0, rotation about origin, single transformation only), enlargement scale factor and centre, constructions (angle bisector, loci regions), scale drawings, bearings.
10. Probability: combined events on a scale (1/12), sample-space denominators (36), multiply not add, relative frequency tables and estimates, words vs fractions, "over half" explanations, prime/square/cube/triangular numbers, binary.
11. Number: place value/rounding (43.812 → 44), ordering decimals, money notation (£1.4, 0.80p), BIDMAS on calculator, estimation instructions ignored, index laws (M6/M7).
12. Quality of written communication: explanations "vague and unconvincing"; must use mathematical statements (75% of 360 = 270).

### 11.5 Common procedural errors flagged by examiners (all tiers)
- Leaving multiple attempts without indicating the final one ("the worst solution is marked"); embedded answers not written on the answer line.
- Not showing calculator inputs on calculator papers (e.g. 0.70 × 800 = 560).
- Premature rounding (⅔ → 0.29; trig intermediate values) losing accuracy marks; not rounding to the accuracy demanded; not rounding up/down in context.
- Using ratio notation for probabilities; word answers when a fraction is demanded.
- Ignoring bold instructions ("estimate", "form an equation", "use a scale drawing", "single transformation", "give your answer in pence", "not to measure").
- Adding rather than multiplying (probabilities of independent events, dimensions for volume).
- Trial and improvement where an algebraic method is required (0 marks for simultaneous equations/forming equations).
- Illegible digits (1/7, 0/6) and freehand lines; papers are scanned — use dark pencil for constructions.
- Entry level mismatch: examiners repeatedly note candidates who should have sat M3 rather than M4, M6/M8 rather than M7, etc.
