# 09 — Northern Ireland GCSE student needs and context (CCEA Maths, Further Maths, Double Award Science)

Research date: 1 September 2026. All figures below were taken directly from the linked primary documents (CCEA, JCQ, Department of Education NI, NISRA, school prospectuses) or from named secondary sources. Where a source could not be reached, this is stated.

Access notes: `ccea.org.uk` returns HTTP 403 to generic fetchers; all CCEA pages/PDFs were retrieved with a browser user-agent and parsed locally (PDF text extraction, spreadsheet-to-CSV). Reddit is blocked by the tooling policy in this environment and Mumsnet's search is JavaScript-only, so those communities are represented indirectly (via The Student Room threads, the DE youth survey of 4,496 NI pupils, tutor/parent-facing sites). The web-search budget was exhausted mid-task; later evidence was gathered by direct fetch of known URLs.

## 1. Executive summary

- **Cohort size and shape.** Roughly 24,000–24,500 CCEA GCSE Mathematics candidates certify each summer, about 4,500 sit CCEA GCSE Further Mathematics (the only qualification of its kind in the UK), and about 8,500–8,800 sit Double Award Science. 96.8% of NI GCSE entries are A*–G graded CCEA-style qualifications; only 3.2% are 9–1 (JCQ NI 2026).
- **Top grades are hard-won and very unequally distributed.** In summer 2026, 27.2% of CCEA Maths candidates got A*–A (6.2% A*); in Further Maths 58.7% got A*–A (22.2% A*); in Double Award Science 14.0% got A*A or better (5.1% A*A*). The grammar/non-grammar gap is huge: 51.9% A*–A in Maths in grammar schools vs 11.0% in secondary schools (2026).
- **The A\* in Maths is a "near-perfect UMS" grade.** The subject-level A* boundary is derived each series and sat at 394/400 UMS (2025) and 393/400 (2026); grade A is a fixed 320/400. Students on forums are visibly confused about what raw percentage this means ("some say 79%, others 90+"). Further Maths A* was 184/200 (2025) and 186/200 (2026).
- **Timeline reality.** CCEA Maths is modular: one "gateway" unit (M1–M4, 45%) and one "completion" unit (M5–M8, 55%). Schools typically sit the gateway unit at the end of Year 11 (summer) or in the **November** of Year 12 (there is no January series any more), and the completion unit in the summer of Year 12. Further Maths is usually a Year 12 course for pupils who finished GCSE Maths a year early, or run alongside Maths over two years. From **November 2027 the November series becomes resit-only** for GCSE Maths and English; **March 2028 removes Science from the March series**.
- **What students and parents say is missing.** "I'm doing ccea and really struggling to find any resources" (TSR, 2025); a tutor-resource company says parents ring "confused about which tests their child will be sitting" and notes "very few actual past papers" because the spec only began in 2017; parents specifically hunt for CCEA-familiar tutors because AQA/Edexcel materials do not match. The DE youth survey (4,496 pupils) describes a "permanent state of assessment", burnout, and modular exam windows crowding out sport and music.
- **Tutoring in NI costs £30–£65/hour** for a qualified GCSE maths tutor in Belfast (typical £45–£55; online £38–£50; budget platforms from £20). Ten sessions run £280–£550, which is the realistic price anchor a gift platform competes against.
- **Reform is coming but not until 2029+.** The DE Policy Framework (3 March 2026) keeps **A*–G grading** (9–1 was rejected by 58.7% of consultees and 74% of pupils), keeps **Maths, English and Single/Double Award Science modular**, makes other GCSEs linear with at most two papers, cuts content and coursework, and creates a new 3-unit modular A level replacing AS. Earliest first teaching is **September 2029**, so the current 2017 specifications remain live for every student sitting GCSEs in 2027, 2028 and 2029. No CCEA on-screen/digital GCSE exam plan was found.

Maths unit UMS maxima: M1 107, M2 131, M3 143, M4 180 (45% units); M5 131, M6 160, M7 175, M8 220 (55% units). A* is awarded only at subject level from the M4+M8 total, and its UMS threshold is re-derived every series (CCEA grade boundary notes).

Raw marks for grade A on the top-tier papers were: M4 45/100 (summer 2025 and 2026); M8 40/100 (2025) and 49/100 (2026). ## 3. How CCEA grades map to 9–1

CCEA Regulation's own comparison (https://ccea.org.uk/regulation/qualifications-regulation/gcse-grading/how-do-you-compare-gcse-grades): from summer 2019, A* aligns with 9; A covers 8 and 7; B aligns with 6; the added C* aligns with 5; C aligns with 4; D–G align with 3–1. The bottom of 7/4/1 are pegged to the bottom of A/C/G. So an NI A*–A rate is comparable to an English 7+ rate, and JCQ reports NI figures as "A/7 and above" and "C/4 and above".

Practical implications for the platform: an NI "A" is a wide band (7–8 equivalent) and the A* is a single 9-equivalent top band; the C* grade has no lettered equivalent in England; only 3.2% of NI entries (2026) are 9–1, mostly from English/Welsh boards used by a minority of schools (JCQ NI 2026 notes to editors). The DE consultation proposed switching to 9–1; this was rejected (see section 9).

## 4. Further Mathematics and progression to A level Mathematics

- CCEA describes GCSE Further Mathematics as unique: "There are no other GCSE specifications in the UK comparable to this one. The specification exceeds the level of challenge offered by GCSE Mathematics qualifications in England and our GCSE Mathematics (Higher Tier)." Units: Unit 1 Pure Mathematics (compulsory, 50%), plus two of Unit 2 Mechanics, Unit 3 Statistics, Unit 4 Discrete and Decision Mathematics (25% each). It "extends students' base in mathematics from which they can progress to higher studies in mathematics; and/or studies such as science, geography, technology or business" (https://ccea.org.uk/key-stage-4/gcse/subjects/gcse-further-mathematics-2017).
- Content includes matrices, logarithms, differentiation, integration, kinematics, Newton's laws, moments, probability, bivariate analysis (Student Room thread listing the course content and describing it as "like a bridge between GCSE and A-Level": https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=2612370).
- School positions on whether it is required:
  - Belfast Royal Academy: "We do not require GCSE Further Mathematics to study A-level," while offering "an accelerated course of study for some students at GCSE to complete both GCSE Mathematics and GCSE Further Mathematics in two years" and claiming "one of the largest A-level Further Mathematics classes in Northern Ireland" (https://www.belfastroyalacademy.com/school-curriculum/subjects/mathematics).
  - Campbell College (GCSE options booklet, Sept 2022): Further Maths is selected by an extra examination in Year 10; it "is helpful, though not essential, preparation for A Level Mathematics". Sixth-form entry for A level Mathematics: "A grade in GCSE Mathematics, with at least 340 UMS points and an A Grade in each of the M4 and M8 papers"; for A level Further Mathematics: "A* grades in both GCSE Mathematics and GCSE Further Mathematics"; A level sciences from Double Award require "at least an A in the relevant Double Award Units" and Higher Tier physics units (https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1665586525/campbellcollegecouk/asgxjqwch4sw2spyegfa/GCSECoursesandOptionsBookletSept22.pdf).
  - Mount Lourdes Grammar: progression to A level Maths requires "Higher Level Maths A*/A" and "Students must have studied GCSE Modules M4 & M8"; a selected Year 10 cohort finishes GCSE Maths at the end of Year 11 and studies Further Maths in Year 12 (https://www.mountlourdes.com/maths).
  - Wellington College Belfast: pupils choose whether to study Further Maths towards the end of Key Stage 3 (https://wellingtoncollegebelfast.org/academic-life/mathematics/).
- Take-up: about 4,500 candidates a year (18–19% of the Maths cohort); three-quarters from grammar schools (section 2.1). NI Maths Tutor's testimonial captures the typical accelerated route: "The M4 course was a huge help while completing my Maths GCSE a year early. I'll definitely be using the Further Maths courses next year" (https://www.nimathstutor.co.uk/).

Implication: for an ambitious 15–16-year-old, the meaningful targets are M4+M8 with an A (ideally A*), and if on the Further Maths track, an A/A* there too. A level Maths entry requirements in several grammar schools are written in terms of M4/M8 and UMS, not just letters.

## 5. Year 11 / Year 12 timelines and the modular rules

### 5.1 Structure (GCSE Mathematics 2017 spec)

- Eight units; students take one from M1–M4 (Foundation M1, M2; Higher M3, M4; each 45%, one calculator paper, 1h45 for M1/M2, 2h for M3/M4) and one from M5–M8 (Foundation M5, M6; Higher M7, M8; each 55%, two papers, non-calculator then calculator). M1/M5 target D–G; M2/M6 C*–F (max C*); M3/M7 B–E; M4/M8 A*–C (https://ccea.org.uk/key-stage-4/gcse/subjects/gcse-mathematics-2017 ; https://ontargetresources.co.uk/ccea-gcse-maths-exams-explained/).
- Unit availability when the spec launched: M1–M4 "Summer from 2018, January from 2019"; M5–M8 "Summer from 2019, January from 2020" (Teacher Guidance 2019, https://ccea.org.uk/downloads/docs/Support/Teacher%20Guidance/2019/Teacher%20Guidance.pdf). The January series was replaced by a **November** series from 2020/21: CCEA's boundary archive lists "January 2020" then "November 2021" onward (https://ccea.org.uk/key-stage-4/gcse/subjects/gcse-mathematics-2017/grade-boundaries), and the timetable page lists November 2024, 2025 and 2026 series (https://ccea.org.uk/key-stage-4/gcse/timetables). There is no January GCSE series for this cohort.
- Resit/terminal rules (Guidance March 2020 and the 2026 Q&A): at least 40% of the assessment must be taken in the cash-in series (so at least one unit must be sat in the series where the grade is claimed); a unit may be resat once before cash-in; if a whole qualification is retaken, up to two further attempts per unit, and only the higher of the last two results counts (https://ccea.org.uk/document/24166).

### 5.2 What schools actually do

- Mount Lourdes: Year 11 (45%) sit M2, M3 or M4; Year 12 (55%) sit M6, M7 or M8 (https://www.mountlourdes.com/maths).
- Campbell College: "Each subject is usually studied over two years with the final examinations at the end of year 12, though some offer the ability to take a module of the course at the end of year 11" (Campbell booklet above).
- On Target Resources: "It is up to the school which examinations students will be entered into. They will base this decision initially on tests carried out in Year 10... It is possible that you will start out doing the foundation syllabus but then be moved up to the higher syllabus" (https://ontargetresources.co.uk/ccea-gcse-maths-exams-explained/). Their M8 page notes M8 "can be sat in January or June and makes up 55%" (legacy wording; now November/summer) (https://ontargetresources.co.uk/m8-ccea-gcse-maths-curriculum/).
- Accelerated route: GCSE Maths completed at end of Year 11, Further Maths in Year 12 (Mount Lourdes; TSR 2014 parent; NI Maths Tutor testimonial).
- DE's own description: "Many pupils in Northern Ireland currently face high-stakes CCEA GCSE exams throughout Years 11 and 12"; there is "a mixed pattern of entry involving both Year 11 and Year 12 pupils" which "may disadvantage Year 11 pupils and lead to a pattern of unnecessary early exam entry, followed by a series of unnecessary resits". In summer 2025 CCEA recorded **26,919 resits across single-award GCSEs, with 39.9% improving**; in Maths 33.4% of resitters improved, in English Language 50.7% (DE consultation report, Feb 2026, https://www.education-ni.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-02/Consultation%20Report%20-%20The%20Future%20of%20CCEA%20GCSE%20AS%20and%20A%20Level%20Qualifications_0.pdf).
- CCEA's "expected cohort entry scenarios" (Q&A, Aug 2026) treat the standard Maths cohort as starting September 2026 and cashing in Summer 2028, sitting units in November 2026, Summer 2027 and Summer 2028; a one-year cohort starts Sept 2026 and cashes in Summer 2027 (https://ccea.org.uk/document/24166).

### 5.4 Double Award Science structure

Seven units: B1, C1, P1 (11% each; available November, February/March and Summer; available in the first year of teaching), B2, C2, P2 (14% each; Summer only), Unit 7 Practical Skills (Booklet A practical 7.5% + Booklet B written exam 17.5% = 25%, in the final year); 18 prescribed practicals; 40% terminal rule; Foundation/Higher tiers; CCEA states its science GCSEs are "the only ones to directly examine practical work" (https://ccea.org.uk/key-stage-4/gcse/subjects/gcse-science-double-award-2017 ; spec PDF https://ccea.org.uk/downloads/docs/Specifications/GCSE/GCSE%20Science%20Double%20Award%20%282017%29/GCSE%20Science%20Double%20Award%20%282017%29-specification-Standard_0.pdf).

### 7.2 The CCEA-specific resource gap (what exists, what does not)

- Official: CCEA past papers/mark schemes (spec from 2017, so roughly eight summer series plus November series per unit), BBC Bitesize "bespoke support materials" for CCEA GCSE Maths, CCEA Paper Builder and Topic Tracker (teacher-facing tools) (https://ccea.org.uk/key-stage-4/gcse/subjects/gcse-mathematics-2017).
- Free third-party: Corbettmaths CCEA practice papers, Set A complete for M1–M8 (one paper for M1–M4, two for M5–M8) and Set B only M1–M4, "based on the topics on the GCSE Maths specifications" and uploaded 2021–2024 (https://corbettmaths.com/2021/11/18/ccea-practice-papers/); Keady Maths topic-sorted CCEA past-paper questions for M2–M8 with mark schemes, no Further Maths (https://keadymaths.com/gcseppq/); Revision Maths hosts CCEA past papers (https://revisionmaths.com/gcse-maths/gcse-maths-past-papers/ccea-gcse-maths-past-papers); Save My Exams lists CCEA past papers for Maths, Further Maths and Double Award but its revision notes are for other boards (https://www.savemyexams.com/gcse/boards/ccea/past-papers/).
- Paid NI-specific: On Target Resources practice papers for M1–M8; NI Maths Tutor video courses for M4, Further Maths units 2 and 3, AS bridging, and past-paper video solutions "designed specifically for CCEA students" (https://www.nimathstutor.co.uk/); Colourpoint textbook "Further Mathematics for CCEA GCSE" (https://colourpointeducational.com/further-mathematics-for-ccea-gcse-9781780731919/).
- Missing (inferred from the above): a topic-by-topic question bank tagged to M4/M8 and to Further Maths units 1–4; clear raw-to-UMS-to-grade explanations; anything for Double Award's practical-skills unit; a personal plan tied to the November/summer series structure and the 40% terminal rule; motivation/pacing that respects the "permanent assessment" complaint.

### 7.3 Common complaints summarised

1. Thin, scattered CCEA-specific resources; English-board content only approximately matches.
2. Confusion about which units a pupil sits and what tier caps mean (T2/T5 = C max; only M4+M8 can yield A*).
3. Opaque A* threshold (derived UMS, near-maximum) and UMS vs raw marks.
4. Assessment overload across four years and modular windows crowding out life; resit culture.
5. Grading-scale confusion for universities/employers outside NI (three scales in the UK).
6. Attainment gaps by school type, FSM and (for A* in Maths) gender that a good tool could help narrow.

## 9. CCEA and DE changes announced for 2026–2030 that the platform must anticipate

### 9.1 Exam-series changes (definite; CCEA)

Circular S/IF/35/26, August 2026 (https://ccea.org.uk/downloads/docs/Circulars/S/2026/August/S-IF-35-26-August.pdf) and the Q&A "November and March Examination Series Changes (from 2026/27)" (https://ccea.org.uk/document/24166 ; landing page https://ccea.org.uk/examiner-centre-support/examinations-support/202627-assessment-updates):

- November 2026 runs as normal (any Maths unit may be sat or resat, first time or not).
- From **November 2027**, the November series for GCSE Mathematics and English Language is a **qualification resit series only**: units may be entered only alongside a cash-in by candidates who have already cashed in. A Year 12 pupil who has not certified cannot use November 2027 for a first sitting or a tier change.
- **March 2027 is the last March series with Single/Double Award Science**; from March 2028 Science moves to two series a year (November and Summer). Pupils starting Science in September 2026 have five series instead of six.
- Resit rule restated: one resit per unit before cash-in; 40% terminal requirement; on a full retake up to two further attempts per unit, higher of the last two counts.
- CCEA is also consulting (via the baseline survey) on whether the November timetable should stay in November.

### 9.2 English Language interim specification (definite; affects the same cohort)

A new interim GCSE English Language specification is first taught from September 2026 (GEX units; unit GEN3 removed), with legacy-spec resits ending November 2027; the Summer 2027 timetable v2 adds GEX1/GEX3 papers (https://ccea.org.uk/key-stage-4/gcse/subjects/gcse-english-language-2017 ; https://ccea.org.uk/document/24212).

### 9.3 Subject withdrawals (definite; CCEA, August 2026)

GCSE Economics, German, Engineering and Manufacturing, Leisure Travel and Tourism, Learning for Life and Work, and Contemporary Crafts will be withdrawn: final cohort starts September 2028, final award summer 2030 plus one resit; A level Professional Business Services final cohort September 2029, final award 2031 (https://ccea.org.uk/news/2026/august/ccea-announces-changes-gcse-and-level-subject-offer ; coverage https://www.theukpulse.co.uk/news/uk/northern-ireland-exams-board-to-axe-several-gcse-and-a-level-subjects-by-2030-31). Maths, Further Maths and the sciences are unaffected.

### 9.4 DE qualifications reform (definite policy; specifications not yet written)

Timeline: consultation 18 Sept–13 Nov 2025 (https://www.education-ni.gov.uk/news/consultation-future-ccea-gcses-levels-and-levels-launched ; https://consultations.nidirect.gov.uk/de/the-future-of-ccea-gcses-as-levels-and-a-levels/) → consultation report 18 Feb 2026, then citing "September 2028" phased introduction (https://www.education-ni.gov.uk/news/report-published-following-consultation-future-ccea-gcse-and-level-qualifications) → Policy Framework and ministerial statement 3 March 2026 stating "earliest changes to teaching expected from September 2029" (https://www.education-ni.gov.uk/news/education-minister-unveils-reforms-gcses-and-levels-northern-ireland ; framework PDF https://www.education-ni.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-03/TransformEd-Qualifications-Framework_0.pdf ; page https://www.education-ni.gov.uk/publications/policy-framework-general-qualifications-northern-ireland).

Policy Framework decisions for CCEA GCSEs:
- Linear structure for most GCSEs, **except GCSE English Language, Mathematics, Single and Double Award Science, which stay modular** (end-of-course entry still allowed); modular unit assessments "may be taken at any exam series over the two years".
- Most GCSEs limited to two written papers; content reduced; NEA (coursework/controlled assessment) used sparingly.
- **Grades stay on the nine-point alphabetical A*–G scale** (the 9–1 proposal drew 23.2% support vs 58.7% opposition; pupils 16.3% support; DE: "will not be pursued at this time").
- Science: prescribed experiments completed during the course; practical skills assessed through exam questions (ungraded practical assessment).
- A level: new two-year modular A level with three units; one unit (30%) may be taken at the end of Year 13, the other two (70%) in Year 14; AS discontinued once new specs arrive; A*–E.
- A Regulatory Framework to follow will set "the timing of exam series, resit opportunities, and tiering".

What this means for the platform: the 2017 Maths/Further Maths/Double Award specs remain the live target for pupils sitting in 2027, 2028 and 2029 (and their resits). Revised specs cannot be first-taught before September 2029, i.e. first awards summer 2031 at the earliest; the platform should be architected so that unit/tier structures and grade scales are data, not code, but there is no need to build for 9–1.

### 9.5 CCEA reform groundwork (open now)

- "Reform of Qualifications: GCSE Baseline Survey 2026" for teachers, learners and stakeholders, 26 Aug–21 Oct 2026, asking "whether the content, assessment and support materials reflect the needs of learners" (https://ccea.org.uk/news/2026/august/ccea-launches-survey-gather-baseline-evidence-ahead-gcse-reform).
- Northern Ireland Curriculum 2028 consultation (Years 1–10), 16 June–30 Sept 2026 (https://ccea.org.uk/news/2026/june/have-your-say-new-northern-ireland-curriculum-2028) — changes what Key Stage 3 pupils bring into GCSE from 2028.

### 9.6 Digital / on-screen assessment and grading mechanics

- No CCEA plan for on-screen GCSE exams was found in the timetable, circular, policy framework or consultation documents. The consultation report discusses technology only in relation to AI and the authentication of non-examined assessment. CCEA's digital assessment activity is at Key Stages 1–3 (adaptive/diagnostic assessments listed in its site navigation). Treat digital exams as out of scope through 2030.
- England's formula-sheet and equation-sheet decisions for 2026–2027 apply to Ofqual-regulated boards, not CCEA (https://www.atomlearning.com/blog/gcse-specification-changes).
- A* boundaries remain derived per series (394 → 393 in Maths; 184 → 186 in Further Maths; Double Award A*A* 544 → 541), so any grade predictor must present A* as a band, not a fixed mark.

## 10. What a birthday-gift study platform for a hard-working 15–16-year-old could uniquely offer

Design principles drawn from the evidence: it must be CCEA-exact (unit codes, tiers, UMS), it must model the pupil's actual entry pattern across November/summer series, and it must feel like a serious personal gift rather than a generic app.

1. **A personal exam map.** On first use, the pupil (or the gift-giver) enters the school's plan: which gateway unit and when (e.g. M4 summer 2027 or November 2026), M8 summer 2028, Further Maths units, Double Award units. The app renders real CCEA dates (section 6), the 40% terminal rule, the one-resit-before-cash-in rule, and the November 2027 resit-only cut-off, with countdowns. Nothing on the market does this; parents phone tutors to ask which tests their child sits.
2. **A UMS and A\*-band calculator.** Convert practice-paper raw marks into UMS using CCEA's fixed unit UMS and recent raw boundaries, and show the A* band as "near-maximum UMS on both units" with the 2025/2026 thresholds. Directly answers the "79% or 90%?" confusion.
3. **A CCEA-tagged question bank.** Every past-paper question (M3/M4, M7/M8, Further Maths units 1–4, Double Award units) tagged by topic, tier, calculator/non-calculator, marks, series, and mark-scheme method marks, with "exam-style but new" questions to stretch beyond the eight-year paper archive. Include Further Maths bridging topics (matrices, logs, calculus, mechanics, statistics) because the school route to A level Maths runs through M4/M8 grades and often Further Maths.
4. **Practical-skills preparation for Double Award.** The 18 prescribed practicals, Booklet A/B format, and the 30-minute practical theory papers are unique to CCEA and unsupported by English-board apps.
5. **Serious, motivating, bounded routines.** A "15 minutes a day" retrieval schedule with spaced repetition and streaks, plus timed mock windows aligned to the November and May/June series — explicitly designed against burnout (the youth survey's "permanent state of assessment"). Let the pupil set the goal in the language schools use ("A in M4 and M8 with 340+ UMS", "A* in Further Maths").
6. **The gift layer.** A personal welcome note from the giver, a birthday-dated start, a milestone card at each real results day (4 Feb 2027, 15 Apr 2027, 19 Aug 2027, and so on), and an optional parent/giver progress view — motivating without gamified fluff.
7. **Bridge-to-A-level mode.** After M8/Further Maths, unlock AS-preparation content (CCEA A level Maths), mirroring the "GCSE to AS bridging" products NI tutors sell.
8. **Price anchoring.** Position against £45–£55/hour tuition: a year's subscription at the price of one or two tutoring hours reads as a generous, rational present.
9. **Future-proofing.** Keep grade scales, unit structures and series calendars as configuration so the 2029+ revised specifications (still A*–G; Maths/Science still modular; fewer papers; less content) can be added without a rebuild.
