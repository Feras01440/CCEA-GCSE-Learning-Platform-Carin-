## 2. Unit structure, durations, marks, weightings

Source: Specification §2 "Specification at a Glance" and §4; raw/UMS marks from the grade-boundary PDFs and 2025 papers.

| Unit | Status | Assessment | Duration | Raw marks | UMS max | Weighting | Formula sheet in booklet? | Calculator |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unit 1: Pure Mathematics** (GFM11) | **Mandatory** | External written exam, single question-and-answer booklet | **2 hours** | **100** | 100 | **50%** | Yes (page 2) | Yes (all units "With calculator") |
| **Unit 2: Mechanics** (GFM21) | Optional | External written exam, Q&A booklet | **1 hour** | **50** | 50 | **25%** | Yes | Yes |
| **Unit 3: Statistics** (GFM31) | Optional | External written exam, Q&A booklet | **1 hour** | **50** | 50 | **25%** | Yes + Normal Probability Table (page 3) | Yes |
| **Unit 4: Discrete and Decision Mathematics** (GFM41) | Optional | External written exam, Q&A booklet | **1 hour** | **50** | 50 | **25%** | **No** formula sheet | Yes |

- Students must complete **Unit 1 plus two of Units 2, 3, 4** (total UMS 200).
- **Terminal rule:** at least 40% of the assessment (by unit weighting) must be taken in the series in which the final grade is requested (Spec §4.1).
- **Resits:** each unit may be resat once before cash-in; the better result counts unless the unit is needed for the 40% terminal rule, in which case the more recent mark counts (Spec §4.1).
- **Paper structure observed in Summer 2025 papers:** Unit 1 — "Answer all fourteen questions", total 100; Unit 2 — six questions, 50; Unit 3 — seven questions, 50; Unit 4 — five questions, 50. (The 2019 SAMs Unit 1 had 15 questions.) Instructions: black ink; "Where rounding is necessary give answers correct to 2 decimal places unless stated otherwise"; "All working must be clearly shown… Marks may be awarded for partially correct solutions."
- 2022 only: exceptional COVID arrangements allowed Unit 1 + one optional unit (CER 2022).

### Assessment objectives (Spec §4.2–4.3)

| AO | Description | Each unit | Overall |
|---|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Use and apply standard techniques (recall facts/terminology; use notation; carry out routine multi-step procedures) | 35–45% | **40%** |
| AO2 | Reason, interpret and communicate mathematically (deductions/inferences; chains of reasoning; arguments and proofs; assess validity) | 25–35% | **30%** |
| AO3 | Solve problems in mathematics and other contexts (translate problems into processes; connect areas of maths; interpret in context; evaluate methods/assumptions) | 25–35% | **30%** |

### Grading and UMS (Spec §4.4; grade-boundary PDFs)

- Results per unit reported on a uniform mark scale; grades by aggregating UMS. Grade scale A\*–G (with C\*); U if below G.
- Subject-level UMS boundaries (fixed for life of spec, max 200): A 160, B 146, C\* 134, C 120, D 100, E 80, F 60, G 40. **A\* is set each series at subject level only**: 184 (2025), 185 (2024), 186 (2026).
- Unit-level UMS boundaries (fixed): Unit 1 (max 100): a 80, b 73, c\* 67, c 60, d 50, e 40, f 30, g 20. Units 2–4 (max 50): a 40, b 37, c\* 34, c 30, d 25, e 20, f 15, g 10.

## 3. Who takes it / prior attainment / entry numbers

- **Target students (Spec §1.2):** those who "require knowledge of mathematics beyond GCSE Higher Tier Mathematics and who are capable of working beyond the limits of the GCSE Mathematics specification"; above-average ability wanting to study AS/A-level maths, or A-levels needing maths beyond Higher Tier.
- **Prior attainment (Spec §1.3):** "should ideally have covered all of the content in the CCEA GCSE Mathematics specification at Higher Tier, including all of the content of units M4 and M8."
- CCEA's Student Guide says the spec "goes beyond the level of challenge posed by the GCSE 9 to 1 mathematics qualifications in England and our revised GCSE Mathematics (Higher Tier)"; the Snapshot adds a CCEA claim that "independent academic research found this specification to be the most challenging qualification aimed at this age group worldwide" (marketing claim, not verified independently).
- The 2018 CER notes many candidates were **Year 11** pupils sitting Unit 1 early ("these would have been year 11 pupils, they lacked the algebra skills that would come from having completed their GCSE Maths"), i.e. common delivery is Unit 1 in Y11 and two option units in Y12, or the whole course in Y12 alongside GCSE Maths.

**Entries and outcomes (CCEA provisional results CSVs, single-award GCSE by specification G2337):**

| Series | Total entries | Grammar | Secondary | FE/Other | Cum. % A\* | Cum. % A | Cum. % B | Cum. % C\* | Cum. % C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Summer 2025 | **4,463** (F 2,055 / M 2,408) | 3,483 (78%) | 925 | 55 | 22.0 | 59.6 | 79.2 | 90.3 | 95.0 |
| Summer 2024 | **4,549** (F 2,177 / M 2,372) | 3,566 | 941 | 42 | 21.8 | 58.3 | 77.2 | 88.8 | 94.7 |

For comparison CCEA GCSE Mathematics Summer 2025: 23,645 entries; A\* 7.0%, A 27.4%, C 73.4% — so Further Maths is taken by roughly one-fifth of the CCEA GCSE Maths cohort, overwhelmingly in grammar schools.
Sources: https://ccea.org.uk/downloads/docs/ccea-asset/Statistics/CCEA%20GCSE%20Results%20-%20Summer%202025.zip (CSV "Provisional CCEA single award GCSE results by specification, gender and centre type, summer 2025") and https://ccea.org.uk/downloads/docs/ccea-asset/Results/CCEA%20GCSE%20Results%20-%20Summer%202024.zip ; index page https://ccea.org.uk/examiner-centre-support/qualification-reports-and-results-statistics/202425-results-statistics

**Option-unit popularity:** Chief Examiner reports state Unit 4 entries are tiny — no meaningful report could be written in 2019, 2024 and 2025; **zero** candidates sat Unit 4 in 2022; **four** candidates sat it in 2023. In 2022 the most popular combination was Unit 1 + Unit 2, then Unit 1 + Unit 3. In practice nearly all centres teach Units 1, 2 and 3.

## 4. Full subject content, unit by unit (verbatim learning outcomes from Specification §3, with elaborations from the CCEA Teacher Guidance, Dec 2024)

Teacher Guidance URL: https://ccea.org.uk/downloads/docs/Support/Teacher%20Guidance/2024/GCSE%20Further%20Mathematics%20Teacher%20Guidance.pdf (issued via circular S/IF/2/25, January 2025: https://ccea.org.uk/downloads/docs/Circulars/S/2025/January/S-IF-2-25-January.pdf)

### 4.1 Unit 1: Pure Mathematics (mandatory, 2 h, 100 marks, 50%)

Spec §3.1: "students investigate algebra, trigonometry, differentiation, integration, logarithms, matrices and quadratic inequalities."

| Content area | Learning outcomes (Spec) | Elaboration / limits (Teacher Guidance 2024) |
|---|---|---|
| Algebraic fractions | add, subtract, multiply and divide rational algebraic fractions with linear and quadratic numerators and/or denominators | e.g. simplify a product/quotient of quadratics over linears; factorise everything first |
| Algebraic manipulation | manipulate algebraic expressions, including the expansion of three linear brackets | e.g. expand (2x+1)(x−3)(1−3x); "can be assessed in a variety of ways" |
| Completing the square | complete the square where the coefficient of x² will always be 1; apply completing the square to solving quadratic equations and identifying minimum turning points | e.g. rewrite f(x)=x²+5x+1 as (x+a)²+b; use it to find the minimum and where it occurs; solve by completing the square giving answers in surd form |
| Simultaneous equations | form and solve three equations in three unknowns | Equations are linear; may be given or formed from context and results interpreted; full working required — "Sole use of a calculator will not gain any marks" |
| Quadratic inequalities | solve quadratic inequalities, restricted to quadratic expressions that factorise | Graphical method recommended; may have to form the inequality from a context |
| Trigonometric graphs and equations | sketch the graphs of sin θ, cos θ and tan θ where the range of θ is a subset of −360° ≤ θ ≤ 360°; solve simple trigonometric equations that lead to a maximum of two solutions in a given range | Only sin, cos, tan graphs; **no transformations** (sin 2θ, cos(θ−30°) not asked); example equation cos(½θ − 30°) = −0.7 in −360° ≤ θ ≤ 360°; various ranges used; **sine rule and cosine rule not asked** |
| Differentiation | differentiate expressions restricted to integer powers of x; apply differentiation to gradients; equations of tangents and normals at points on a curve; simple optimisation problems; elementary curve sketching of a quadratic or cubic function | First and second derivatives may be asked; **fractional indices excluded** (positive or negative integers only); **no 3-D optimisation and no problems involving π**; cubics for sketching have x as a common factor or are a product of 3 linear factors, e.g. y = x³ + 2x² − 5x or y = (x−1)(x+2)(2x−3) |
| Integration | demonstrate knowledge that integration is the inverse process to differentiation; integrate expressions restricted to integer powers of x (n ≠ −1); form and evaluate definite integrals; apply integration to finding the area under a curve | Area between a curve, the x-axis and two ordinates x = a and x = b; **excluding combinations of positive and negative areas** |
| Logarithms | demonstrate understanding of logarithms as a natural evolution from indices; solve problems using the laws of logarithms and log/log graphs in context; solve indicial equations using logarithms | Understand 8 = 2³ ⇔ log₂8 = 3; **excluding change-of-base rule**; draw log/log graphs from a table of data (resulting graph a straight line, used to find constants in y = kxⁿ); example indicial equation 3^(2x−1) = 5^(1+x); powers are constants or linear in x |
| Matrices | add, subtract and multiply matrices; find the inverse of 2 × 2 matrices; solve matrix equations; use matrices to solve 2 × 2 simultaneous equations | Non-square matrices may be added/subtracted; for multiplication dimensions do not exceed 3 rows or 3 columns **but 3 × 3 matrices are not included**; matrix equations of the form A + X = B or AX = B (or combinations); **XA = B excluded** |

Formula sheet printed in the Unit 1 booklet (transcribed from the Summer 2025 paper and the SAMs): quadratic formula x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac))/2a; differentiation "If y = axⁿ then dy/dx = naxⁿ⁻¹"; integration ∫axⁿ dx = axⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + c (n ≠ −1); logarithms "If aˣ = n then x = logₐ n".

### 4.2 Unit 2: Mechanics (optional, 1 h, 50 marks, 25%)

Spec §3.2: "students explore kinematics, vectors, forces, Newton's Laws of Motion and moments."

| Content area | Learning outcomes (Spec) | Elaboration / limits (Teacher Guidance 2024) |
|---|---|---|
| Kinematics | draw, interpret and use displacement/time graphs and velocity/time graphs; use constant acceleration formulae | Graphs may show two journeys (one vehicle meeting/overtaking another); vertical and horizontal motion; s/t graphs may include negative displacements, v/t graphs only positive velocity; average speed and average velocity can be asked |
| Vectors | demonstrate understanding of the definition of vector and scalar quantities; calculate the magnitude and direction of a vector; use i and j vectors in calculations | Including the angle between xi + yj and either i or j |
| Forces | demonstrate understanding that force is a vector; identify all forces acting on a body; resolve forces into components; find the resultant of a set of forces; demonstrate understanding of and apply the concept of equilibrium | Resolving and equilibrium problems involve a maximum of four separate forces |
| Newton's laws of motion | apply F = ma to: a body in horizontal or vertical motion; a body on an inclined plane; two connected bodies in rectilinear motion. (F = μR will not be tested — if included, friction will be given as a value or as a value per unit mass) | Straight-line motion only; external forces on an inclined plane act parallel to the plane; **questions may involve the magnitude of the resultant force exerted by a string on a pulley**; connected particles: both horizontal, both vertical, or one of each |
| Moments | demonstrate understanding of the Principle of Moments and equilibrium of a rigid body (restricted to a horizontal uniform rod supported by one or two pivots) | Uniform rods only; **no hinge or ladder questions** |

Not in the spec (do not teach for this exam): projectiles, friction coefficient μ, momentum/impulse, work/energy/power, circular motion, non-uniform rods, ladders.

Formula sheet printed in the Unit 2 booklet (2025 paper/SAMs): quadratic formula; vectors — magnitude of xi + yj is √(x²+y²), angle between xi + yj and i is tan⁻¹(y/x); uniform acceleration v = u + at, v² = u² + 2as, s = ut + ½at², s = ½(u+v)t (u initial velocity, v final velocity, s change in displacement, t time, a acceleration); Newton's Second Law F = ma (F resultant force, m mass, a acceleration). g is used as 10 m/s² in mark schemes (e.g. SAMs).

### 4.3 Unit 3: Statistics (optional, 1 h, 50 marks, 25%)

Spec §3.3: "students investigate central tendency and dispersion, probability, the binomial and normal distributions and bivariate analysis."

| Content area | Learning outcomes (Spec) | Elaboration / limits (Teacher Guidance 2024) |
|---|---|---|
| Central tendency and dispersion | calculate the mean and standard deviation from data or estimates of these from grouped data; calculate the mean and standard deviation for combined sets of data; demonstrate knowledge of the effect on the mean and standard deviation of a linear transformation on a set of data | **Median and interquartile range will not be asked**; combined sets example (6 boys + 4 girls, overall mean given, find girls' mean); if y = ax + b then ȳ = ax̄ + b and σ_y = aσ_x; also recompute mean/SD after items are removed or added |
| Probability | calculate combined probabilities using the addition rule, including events that may not be mutually exclusive; calculate and interpret conditional probabilities using expected frequencies, two-way tables, tree diagrams and Venn diagrams; use the most appropriate method to solve complex problems, including construction and use of Venn and tree diagrams | Understand and use P(A ∪ B), P(A ∩ B), P(A \| B); conditional probability can also be tested through the binomial and/or normal distributions |
| Binomial distribution | use Pascal's triangle to expand (p + q)ⁿ where n ≤ 8; understand and use the binomial expansion to calculate probabilities in real-life contexts | e.g. coin tossed 7 times, P(head)=0.4, find P(1 or 2 heads) and P(at least 2 heads); "the full derivation of binomial probabilities is required" |
| Normal distribution | recognise that the distribution of many real-world variables takes the shape of a bell curve; calculate a single probability from the normal distribution using tables and z = (x − μ)/σ where the mean and standard deviation are given | Probabilities of the form P(z > 1), P(z < −0.5) **but not P(1 < z < 2)**; only find a probability from a z-value, **not a z-value from a probability**; a good diagram is encouraged |
| Bivariate analysis | calculate and interpret Spearman's Rank Correlation Coefficient; draw the line of best fit by eye passing through (x̄, ȳ); calculate and use the equation of the line of best fit | Plotting of original data will be given; −1 ≤ r ≤ 1 and "for this specification, values −0.4 ≤ r ≤ 0.4 will be taken as weak or no correlation"; the line of best fit must always go through both means |

Not in the spec: median/IQR/box plots, histograms, product-moment correlation, regression by least squares, hypothesis tests, inverse normal, P(a<z<b).

Formula sheet in the Unit 3 booklet (2025 paper): Mean = Σfx/Σf; Standard deviation = √(Σfx²/Σf − (x̄)²) where x̄ is the mean; P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B); P(A \| B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B); Spearman's r = 1 − 6Σd²/(n(n² − 1)). Plus a full **Normal Probability Table** (Φ(z) for z = 0.00 to 3.99 with "ADD" difference columns) on page 3.

### 4.4 Unit 4: Discrete and Decision Mathematics (optional, 1 h, 50 marks, 25%)

Spec §3.4: "students explore counting, logic, linear programming, time series and critical path analysis."

| Content area | Learning outcomes (Spec) | Elaboration / limits (Teacher Guidance 2024) |
|---|---|---|
| Counting | demonstrate understanding of and use the addition and multiplication principles to count events in series and parallel respectively; calculate the number of ways of arranging r objects from n objects; calculate the number of ways of choosing r objects from n objects | Calculators may be used for nCr and nPr |
| Logic | demonstrate understanding of the concept of Boolean variables, including forming compound expressions using logical operators AND, OR and NOT; use truth tables to prove the equivalence of propositional statements (involving no more than three variables) | — |
| Linear programming | model real-life scenarios as linear programming problems; use graphical methods to maximise or minimise an expression involving up to five inequalities in one or two variables (solutions may be real numbers or restricted to integers) | — |
| Time series | demonstrate understanding of why we smooth data; calculate appropriate moving averages (using three, four or five points); draw a trend line and use it to make predictions (the plotting of original data will be given) | The correct trend line will always be a straight line |
| Critical path analysis | demonstrate understanding of how an activity network represents a project (using activities on an arc); construct an activity network from a precedence table; identify the critical path by finding the earliest and latest event times; calculate float times; perform basic scheduling using Gantt charts | Scheduling can be performed by inspection |

Not in the spec: graph algorithms (Dijkstra, Kruskal/Prim, route inspection), simplex, sorting/bin-packing algorithms, game theory. **No formula sheet** for Unit 4.

### 8.1 Unit 1 Pure — hardest topics (ranked by how often reports flag them)

| Rank | Topic | Evidence across reports |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Algebraic fractions / algebraic manipulation** | Flagged every year (2018 Q8, 2019 Q12, 2022 Q9, 2023 Q9, 2024 Q8, 2025 Q7). Errors: not factorising all quadratics first ("tying themselves up in knots with cubic numerators"), not using (x+3)(x−3) as LCD, illegal cancelling across sums, sign errors when subtracting a bracketed product, dividing by a constant before factorising, not simplifying fully. |
| 2 | **Tangents and normals / applying differentiation in an unfamiliar way** | 2019 Q11 ("candidates need more practice in determining the equation of a normal"), 2022 Q8(ii), 2024 Q9 (many set curve = line instead of differentiating; used given gradient wrongly), 2025 Q8(ii) (substitute y into an equation involving dy/dx — "poorly answered by the majority"), 2025 Q13 (set dy/dx = 0 "out of habit" instead of dy/dx = 5). |
| 3 | **Laws of logarithms — simplifying/combining expressions** | 2019 Q9(a) (few could get y = x^(3/2)), 2022 Q5(a) ("one of the most challenging"), 2023 Q7(a) ("only the best candidates scoring any marks"), 2024 Q6(a) (forgot to cube the 2 in (2x)³), 2025 Q9(a). By contrast standard log *equations* (3^(2x−1) = 5^(1+x)) are answered well. |
| 4 | **Optimisation** (final question) | 2018 Q12, 2019 Q14 (many did not realise calculus needed; failed to show maximum), 2022 Q13 (derivative of −900/x wrong; answer 30 mm instead of substituting back for 60 mm), 2024 Q13, 2025 Q14 (negative powers when differentiating; solving; second derivative for nature). |
| 5 | **Forming three simultaneous equations from context** | Solving is fine; forming is poor: 2018 Q10, 2022 Q11 ("£8 dearer", "50% greater"), 2023 Q11 (percentages; working backwards from given equations gains no marks), 2024 Q11 (thousands; forgetting to scale answers back ×1000), 2025 Q12(iv) currency conversion with a 40% decrease "the most poorly answered part of the entire paper"; calculator-only solutions get no marks. |
| 6 | **Integration to recover a curve from its gradient function** | 2023 Q2 (constant omitted or wrong; some used limits), 2024 Q1(ii), 2025 Q6 ("poorly answered" — saw the word "gradient" and differentiated). Also 2019 Q13 (integrating with an unknown constant k; area ratio) was "the most difficult question on the whole paper". |
| 7 | **Trig graphs and equations** | Sketches too straight, missing scales/asymptotes; 2025 Q2(a) tan graph "particularly problematic" (asymptote drawn at 180°, curves crossing asymptotes, sine-like shapes). Equations: finding the second solution in range (2022 Q4(b)(i) majority scored 1 mark), CAST errors, multiplying instead of dividing when isolating x, extra out-of-range answers. |
| 8 | **Completing the square then "hence" find the minimum** | 2019 Q2, 2023 Q4(ii), 2025 Q3(ii): candidates differentiate instead — zero marks because the required method was not used; also sign errors in (x+a)²+b. |
| 9 | **Quadratic inequalities** | Failing to move all terms to one side (2018 Q4), losing the inequality sign, wrong region without a sketch (2019 Q6, 2024 Q3), negative x² coefficient (2022 Q7), not relating solution back to context — excluding negative values (2023 Q5). |
| 10 | **Curve sketching of cubics** | Differentiating when asked for intercepts (2018 Q9, 2019 Q8, 2024 Q10), extra (0,0) intercept (2023 Q12), sketches inconsistent with computed turning points, not labelling intercepts/turning points. |
| 11 | **Log/log graphs (y = kxⁿ)** | Generally well done but recurring mark losses: values not to 3 dp, axes unlabelled, log values on wrong axis, inappropriate scales, inverting the gradient, giving log A instead of A, not checking answers lie within data range, rounding a context answer down instead of up. |
| 12 | **Matrices** | Usually strong. Errors: squaring elements instead of multiplying (2019 Q1, 2023 Q8, 2024 Q7), determinant arithmetic, multiplying inverse in wrong order, not using the matrix method when instructed (2025 Q5(ii) — algebraic solutions "received no marks"), unclear reasoning when det = 0 (2023 Q6(ii)). |

General Unit 1 advice repeated by examiners: show full working (calculator-only answers score nothing when wrong); use brackets; simplify before substituting in definite integrals; obey "hence", "use matrices", bold text and stated decimal places (2 dp default, 3 dp for log tables); check answers make sense in context; do not work backwards in "show that" questions.

### 8.2 Unit 2 Mechanics — hardest topics

| Rank | Topic | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Force diagrams** | Every report: missing arrows/labels, omitted normal reaction, weight of rod not at the centre, mass written instead of weight (no g), extra reactions at rod ends, friction in wrong direction, tensions labelled differently. 2025: "only around 50% achieved full marks for correctly labelling the forces". |
| 2 | **Force on a pulley in connected-particle problems = 2T (resultant of the two tensions)** | 2022 Q5(iv) "a large number… didn't know", 2025 Q4(iv) "the least well-answered question on the paper" — examiners point to Teacher Guidance p.10. |

| 4 | **Resolving forces / equilibrium on inclined planes** | sin/cos swapped; vertical equilibrium equation with missing terms (R = 17 sin 33° type errors; R = 50 without resolving); mixing magnitude and angle; not realising a = 0 in equilibrium parts; using weight instead of mass in F = ma. |

### 8.4 Unit 4 Discrete & Decision — very little evidence
Only the 2023 report (four candidates) comments: confusion whether to add or multiply in counting; selecting the wrong vertex for maximum profit in linear programming; moving averages must be given to the stated significant figures; expressing a Boolean result "in English phrases" was hard; critical-path late times occasionally wrong; in scheduling, activities started before all predecessors finished. Candidates who take it tend to perform well.

## 9. Implications for a learning platform (derived from the above)

1. Cover Units 1, 2 and 3 fully; treat Unit 4 as optional/low-priority content (tiny uptake).
2. Drill the "method-locked" question types where the wrong (even if mathematically valid) method scores zero: "hence" after completing the square; "using a matrix method"; "show that" without working backwards; forming equations rather than solving given ones.
3. Build explicit practice on: algebraic fractions (factorise-first routine), normals/tangents from dy/dx, log-law simplification, integrating a gradient function with constant, optimisation with negative powers, forming 3-variable equations from worded contexts, tan/cos/sin sketches with asymptotes and scales.
4. Mechanics: force-diagram checklist (label, arrow, weight = mg at centre, R perpendicular, friction opposing motion, no extra reactions); pulley force = 2T; equate i and j coefficients.
5. Statistics: conditional-probability denominators, pooled SD via Σx², linear scaling rules, binomial "at least" with brackets, z-table diagram habit, Spearman tie-handling and interpretation thresholds.
6. Presentation rules in every worked solution: 2 dp default, 3 dp for log tables, brackets, units, answers on the answer line, sanity-check against context.

## 10. Open questions / caveats

- No revised Further Mathematics specification has been announced (the subject page still lists the 2017 spec; the "2026/27 assessment updates" page only concerns English, Mathematics and Science series changes). Worth re-checking each autumn.
- The GCSE formulae booklet PDF's Pure and Mechanics pages are images; the formulae listed here were transcribed from the identical sheets printed in the 2025 papers/SAMs.
- Session times on the 2026/2027 timetables were read from a multi-column PDF layout; dates are certain, but minute-level times should be re-verified against the PDF before being shown to students.
- Unit 4 entry numbers are not published separately (reports only say "very small"; 4 in 2023; 0 in 2022).
- No Chief Examiner reports exist for 2020 and 2021; 2021 papers in the feed were used under alternative awarding arrangements.
- SaveMyExams and other third-party sites host copies of papers, but the CCEA URLs above are the authoritative sources.
