### A1 — worked example `we.science.c2.c2-reactivity-series.02`: the step marks are one short of the stem's tariff

**Current.** The stem advertises `(a) … [2]`, `(b) Write the balanced symbol equation, including
state symbols. [3]`, `(c) … [2]` — seven marks. The steps carry only six mark codes:

| step | `earns` | text |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `["P1","P2"]` | "Sodium is one of the top three, so it reacts with COLD water: sodium hydroxide + hydrogen." |
| 2 | `[]` | "Formulae first: Na, H2O, NaOH, H2." |
| 3 | `["P3"]` | "…Doubling the sodium and the water fixes it: 2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2." |
| 4 | `["P4"]` | "State symbols: 2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g)." |
| 5 | `["P5","P6"]` | "A sodium atom and a potassium atom each have one outer electron…" |

**Why it is wrong.** Part (b) is worth [3] but only P3 (balancing) and P4 (state symbols) sit

`src/components/items/WorkedExampleAsQuestion.tsx:459`, so the shortfall also lowers the twin.

**Fix.** Give step 2 the formulae mark and renumber downstream:

- step 2 `earns: ["P3"]`
- step 3 `earns: ["P4"]`
- step 4 `earns: ["P5"]`
- step 5 `earns: ["P6","P7"]`

Seven codes for the stem's 2 + 3 + 2. (The other three worked examples in the two bundles
already balance: RS.01 5/5, DX.01 6/6, DX.02 5/5.)

### A2 — `q.science.c2.c2-reactivity-series.0004` part `main`: the key-word group never mentions steam, so three wrong answers earn the mark and three correct ones do not

**Current.**

```json
"keyWords": [{"any":["provides","produces","supplies","source","generates","makes","boils","evaporates"],"marks":1}]
```

**Why it is wrong.** The group tests only the verb, never its object. Any answer containing
"produces", "makes" or "supplies" scores the mark whatever it claims is produced — including the
two answers the item's own mark scheme rejects (`reject: ["to absorb the heat","to give out heat","to dry the gas"]`)
and the answer the Summer 2025 examiner callout names as the commonest loss. Meanwhile three
natural correct answers score nothing because they use a verb that is not on the list.

**Fix (verified).**

```json
"keyWords": [{"any":["steam"],"marks":1,
```

### A3 — `q.…0010` part `main`: the reversed explanation scores 3/3, and so does "gains … negative ion"

**Current.**

```json
"keyWords": [
  {"any":["one outer electron","one electron in its outer shell","single outer electron"],"marks":1},
  {"any":["more easily","easier","readily","more quickly"],"marks":1},
  {"any":["positive ion","ion"],"marks":1}]
```

**Why it is wrong.** The groups are presence-based and none of them is tied to potassium, so the
pairing can be swapped wholesale and still collect every mark. Marked now:

- "Calcium has one outer electron and potassium has two, so calcium loses its outer electron more easily and forms its positive ion more readily." → **3/3**
- "Calcium has a single outer electron so calcium loses it more easily than potassium and forms its positive ion more readily than potassium does." → **3/3**
- "Potassium has one outer electron and gains it more easily, forming a negative ion more readily." → **3/3** (bare `"ion"` in group 3 matches "negative ion")

**Fix (verified).**

```json
"keyWords": [
  {"any":["one outer electron","one electron in its outer shell","single outer electron"],"marks":1,
   "reject":["calcium has one","calcium has a single","calcium has only one"]},
  {"any":["more easily","easier","readily","more quickly"],"marks":1,
   "reject":["calcium loses its outer electron more easily","calcium loses it more easily","calcium loses them more easily","calcium loses its electron more easily"]},
  {"any":["positive ion"],"marks":1,"reject":["negative ion","gain"]}]
```

| response | now | fixed |
|---|---|---|
| accepted answer | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| worked solution | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| "A potassium atom has a single outer electron, so it loses it more easily than calcium loses its two and forms its positive ion more readily." | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| "Potassium has one electron in its outer shell and calcium has two, so potassium gives its electron up more easily and becomes a positive ion faster." | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| "Potassium has one outer electron so it loses electrons more easily and forms positive ions more readily." | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| swapped (calcium for potassium) | **3/3** | 1/3 |
| swapped, second wording | **3/3** | 1/3 |
| "gains it more easily … negative ion" | **3/3** | 2/3 |
| "Potassium is higher up the series so it is more reactive." | 0/3 | 0/3 |

Dropping bare `"ion"` from group 3 is what stops the negative-ion answer; the accepted answer,
the worked solution and all three paraphrases still say "positive ion", so nothing correct is lost.

### A4 — `q.…0002` part `main`: three correct pairs of observations under-earn because every key word is a fixed multi-word phrase

### A5 — `q.…0001` part `main`: the enforced reject list cancels a correct justified answer, and the common-error regex has no guard

**Current.** `"keyWords": [{"any":["potassium"],"marks":1,"reject":["copper","iron","zinc"]}]`
and `commonErrors[0].pattern.regex = "copper|zinc|\\biron\\b"`.

**Why it is wrong.** "Potassium, because it is above copper, iron and zinc in the reactivity
series." scores **0/1** — the reject words fire even though the metal named is right — and the
regex then matches the same answer, so the feedback reads "That metal is lower in the series."
The reject list adds nothing the key word does not already do: an answer that says only "copper"
has no "potassium" in it and scores 0 anyway. (The `scheme[0].reject` list is display-only and can
stay as documentation; it is `answer.keyWords[0].reject` that is enforced.)

**Fix (verified).**

```json
"keyWords": [{"any":["potassium"],"marks":1}]
```
```json
"commonErrors[0].pattern.regex": "^(?![\\s\\S]*potassium)[\\s\\S]*\\b(copper|zinc|iron)\\b"
```

| response | now | fixed |
|---|---|---|
| "potassium" | 1/1 | 1/1 |
| "Potassium, because it is above copper, iron and zinc in the reactivity series." | **0/1** | 1/1 |
| "copper" / "zinc" / "iron is the most reactive" | 0/1 | 0/1 |

`matchesCommonError` on the justified answer: `true` → `false`; on "copper": `true` → `true`.

### A6 — `q.…0011` part `a(i)`: the common-error regex fires on an answer that *does* name the sparks

**Current.** `commonErrors[0].pattern.regex = "glow|red"`.

**Why it is wrong.** The pattern has no word boundary and no guard, so it matches any answer
containing "red" or "glow". "It throws orange sparks and glows red hot." — a true description of
iron in air — draws the feedback "Glowing red is copper. Iron throws orange sparks — both 2025
papers recorded these two being swapped", which contradicts what the learner wrote. (The 0/1 on
that answer is the listing rule doing its designed job on a two-clause hedge, not the regex.)

**Fix (verified).**

```json
"commonErrors[0].pattern.regex": "^(?![\\s\\S]*spark)[\\s\\S]*(glow|\\bred\\b|white light)"
```

`matchesCommonError("It throws orange sparks and glows red hot.")`: `true` → `false`;
`matchesCommonError("it glows red")`: `true` → `true`.

### A8 — `ftm.science.c2.c2-reactivity-series.01` has two wrong lines, but the learner is asked to tap one

**Current.**

```
studentWorking[0] "magnesium + steam → magnesium hydroxide + hydrogen"   <- mistakeLine: 1
studentWorking[1] "The damp mineral wool absorbs the heat from the burner."
studentWorking[2] "The hydrogen is collected over water."
correction[0] "magnesium + steam → magnesium oxide + hydrogen"
correction[1] "Heating the damp mineral wool boils the water in it, which supplies the steam."
correction[2] "The hydrogen is collected over water because it is insoluble."
marksEarnedAsWritten ["P2"]
```

**Why it is wrong.** `FindTheMistake.tsx:79` scores the interaction as `tapped[0] === item.mistakeLine`
and then asks her to "Write line 1 correctly", so exactly one line may be wrong. Line 2 is also
wrong, and the item says so itself: `whatWentWrong` ends "Line 2 then gives the wool the wrong
job", and `feedback` says "the mineral-wool mark goes". A learner who taps line 2 — a genuinely
wrong line — is told she is wrong. Two further problems follow from the three-line correction:
`correction[2]` rewrites line 3, which `whatWentWrong` calls correct ("the collection is right");
and `fixMatches` accepts **any** correction line, so typing "The hydrogen is collected over water
because it is insoluble." is accepted as the fix for line 1 (verified: `match=true how=line` for
all three correction entries).

**Fix.** Leave line 1 as the single mistake and make line 2 correct:

```json
"studentWorking": [
  "magnesium + steam → magnesium hydroxide + hydrogen",
  "Heating the damp mineral wool boils the water in it, which supplies the steam.",
  "The hydrogen is collected over water because it is insoluble."],
"mistakeLine": 1,
"correction": ["magnesium + steam → magnesium oxide + hydrogen"],
"marksEarnedAsWritten": ["P2","P3"]
```

and drop the last sentence of `whatWentWrong` ("Line 2 then gives the wool the wrong job: it is
damp so that heating it makes the steam."), opening `feedback` with "Two of the three marks:
hydrogen is correct and so is the mineral wool." The mineral-wool misconception is already
carried by `q.…0004` and by post-diagnostic item 03, so nothing is lost.

### B3 — `q.…0010` parts `a` and `b`: the reject lists cancel correct answers on a question that invites citing the table

**Current.**
`a`: `{"any":["magnesium"],"marks":1,"reject":["silver","copper"]}` with
`commonErrors[0].pattern.regex = "silver|copper"`.
`b`: `{"any":["silver"],"marks":1,"reject":["magnesium","zinc"]}`.

**Why it is wrong.** Both stems say "**Use the table** to name…", so naming the solutions in the
row is the natural way to answer — and both reject lists then cancel the mark:

- (a) "Magnesium, because it reacts with the zinc sulfate, copper(II) sulfate and silver nitrate solutions." → **0/1**, and the regex also matches it, so the feedback reads "Read along each row and count the reactions", which is exactly what she did.
- (b) "Silver, because it did not react with magnesium sulfate, zinc sulfate or copper(II) sulfate." → **0/1**, feedback `"magnesium" cancels the mark it sits with. Leave it out.`

The reject lists protect nothing: an answer naming the wrong metal has no "magnesium" / "silver"
in it and scores 0 on the key word alone. The enforced list is `answer.keyWords[0].reject`; the
`scheme[0].reject` list is display-only and may stay. (Note also that (a)'s enforced list is
`["silver","copper"]` while its scheme reject is `["silver","copper","zinc"]` — the two disagree.)

**Fix (verified).**

```json
"a.answer.keyWords": [{"any":["magnesium"],"marks":1}]
"a.commonErrors[0].pattern.regex": "^(?![\\s\\S]*magnesium)[\\s\\S]*\\b(silver|copper|zinc)\\b"
"b.answer.keyWords": [{"any":["silver"],"marks":1}]
```

### B8 — `q.…0010` part `d`: `MgNO32` is accepted as `Mg(NO3)2` (engine-level)

**Current.** `balancedLatex: "Mg + 2AgNO3 -> Mg(NO3)2 + 2Ag"`, which is correct and balanced
(Mg 1/1, Ag 2/2, N 2/2, O 6/6).

**Why it matters.** `normaliseEquation` in `src/components/items/equation-marking.ts:92` strips
brackets that wrap an atom (`s.replace(/\(([^()+\-*/=^]+)\)/g, "$1")`), a rule written for
physics symbol strings. It also strips the brackets in a chemical formula, so
`"Mg + 2AgNO3 → MgNO32 + 2Ag"` marks **3/3**. This is the only part in either bundle with a
bracketed group in a product, so this is where it shows. `Mg + 2AgNO3 → MgN2O6 + 2Ag` is
correctly refused, and `Mg(NO₃)₂` with Unicode subscripts is correctly accepted.

**Fix.** Engine-level, not fixable in the bundle: gate the atom-bracket strip on
`opts.implicitMultiply` (which is set only for `kindOf: "physics"`) so chemistry keeps its
brackets. Flagged here for the reviewer to route.

### B9 — a `$…$`-wrapped equation is refused (engine-level, affects both bundles)

`markAnswer("$\\ce{Mg(s) + H2O(g) -> MgO(s) + H2(g)}$", …)` → **0/3** "That is not the expected
equation", while the same line without the dollars → **3/3**. `normaliseEquation` unwraps `\ce{}`,
`\text{}`, `\mathrm{}` and strips `{}` and `\` but never strips `$`. Both notes print their
equations in exactly the `$\ce{…}$` form (`c2-reactivity-series` note block 6 and 15,
`c2-displacement-and-extraction` note block 17 and 21), so a learner who copies the form she was
taught fails. **Fix:** engine-level — add `.replace(/\$/g, "")` to `normaliseEquation`. Flagged,
not fixed.
