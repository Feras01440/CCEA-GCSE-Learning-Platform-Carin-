### we.science.b1.b1-competition-food-webs.01 [verified] paper B1 calc
STEM: Ladybirds were removed from a wheat field by a pesticide. Use the food web to explain what happened to the number of aphids and to the wheat yield. [4]
  FIG: svg — A hedgerow food web with wheat and hawthorn as producers, aphid, wood mouse and rabbit as primary consumers, ladybird and blue tit as secondary consumers, and kestrel and fox at the top.
  1. The ladybird eats the aphid, so removing ladybirds removes one of the aphid's predators.   — Start by naming the feeding relationship from the web. An explanation that never mentions who eats whom is not using the web.  (P1)
  2. Fewer aphids are eaten, so the number of aphids increases.   — Say which way the number moves, and why. 'The aphids change' earns nothing.  (P2)
  3. Aphids feed on the wheat, so more aphids means more wheat is eaten.   — Follow the arrow down a level. The web tells you the aphid's food without you having to know anything about aphids.  (P3)
  4. So the wheat yield falls.   — Finish with the answer to the question actually asked. A chain of reasoning that stops one step short of the yield loses the last mark.  (P4)
  FINAL: Ladybirds eat aphids, so removing the ladybirds removes one of the aphid's predators and fewer aphids are eaten. The number of aphids therefore increases. Aphids feed on the wheat, so more aphids means more wheat is eaten and the wheat yield falls.
  TWIN: Foxes were removed from the area around the hedgerow. ## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.b1.b1-competition-food-webs (pre) [verified]
- 01 (Name the Sun as the energy source): What is the source of energy for almost every ecosystem on Earth?
    ✔ the Sun — The Sun. Green plants capture its light energy by photosynthesis and make it available to everything else.
    ✘ the leaves of the trees [sci.foodweb.sun-not-named-as-energy-source] — The leaves capture the energy; they are not its source. March 2026 examiners reported parts of a tree given instead of the Sun.
    ✘ the soil [sci.foodweb.sun-not-named-as-energy-source] — The soil supplies water and minerals, not energy.
    ✘ the producers [sci.foodweb.sun-not-named-as-energy-source] — Producers are how the energy enters the web, but the energy itself comes from the Sun.
- 02 (Read the direction of an arrow): An arrow in a food chain points from a grass plant to a rabbit. What does it show?
    ✔ energy and substances passing from the grass into the rabbit — The arrow shows consumption and the transfer of energy and substances. Read it as 'is eaten by'.
    ✘ the rabbit eating the grass, so the arrow points at what is eaten [sci.foodweb.arrow-direction-reversed] — The arrow points at the eater, not at the food. Drawn the other way round it would say the grass eats the rabbit.
    ✘ the grass and the rabbit competing for space [sci.foodweb.arrow-direction-reversed] — Food-web arrows never show competition; they show feeding.
    ✘ the rabbit returning nutrients to the grass [sci.foodweb.arrow-direction-reversed] — That happens through decomposition, and it is not what a food-chain arrow shows.
- 03 (Separate the plant and animal resource lists): Which of these do plants NOT compete for?
    ✔ food — Plants make their own food by photosynthesis, so they never compete for it. They compete for water, light, space and minerals.
    ✘ light [sci.foodweb.plants-compete-for-food] — Light is one of the four: a tall plant shading a short one is competition for light.
    ✘ minerals [sci.foodweb.plants-compete-for-food] — Minerals are one of the four. Roots growing through the same soil compete for nitrate.
    ✘ space [sci.foodweb.plants-compete-for-food] — Space is one of the four: room for roots below and leaves above.
- 04 (Call competition competition): Grey squirrels were introduced to a wood and the red squirrel population fell. Which sentence would score?
    ✔ The greys competed with the reds for food and nesting sites, so the reds had less of both. — Competition for a named resource. That is the relationship the mark scheme rewards.
    ✘ The greys hunted and ate the red squirrels. [sci.foodweb.competitor-called-predator] — Greys do not eat reds. March 2026 examiners reported exactly this answer, and it earns nothing.
    ✘ The reds were at a lower trophic level than the greys. [sci.foodweb.trophic-level-miscounted] — Both squirrels eat the same food, so they are at the same trophic level. The issue is competition, not levels.
    ✘ The greys are better adapted, so the reds died out naturally. [sci.foodweb.competitor-called-predator] — Close, but it never names what they are better at. Say what resource is being competed for.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.science.b1.b1-competition-food-webs.01 [verified] sci.foodweb.competitor-called-predator · earned none · ccea-cer:science:2026-march:B1H:Q1
STEM: Sean is asked: 'Red squirrels have become rare in a wood since grey squirrels arrived. Explain why, and name the source of energy for the wood. [3]'
His answer:
  ✘ 1. The grey squirrels hunt the red squirrels and eat them.
    2. That is why there are fewer reds now.
    3. The source of energy for the wood is the leaves of the trees.
  WRONG: Grey squirrels do not eat red squirrels. The two species **compete** for the same food and the same nest sites, and the greys are larger and can eat a wider range of food, so the reds are left with less. Line 3 has the second error: the **leaves capture** the energy, but the **Sun** is where it comes from.
  FIX: The grey squirrels compete with the red squirrels for food and for nest sites. / The greys can eat a wider range of food, so the reds get less and fewer survive and breed. / The source of energy for the wood is the Sun.
  FEEDBACK: Nothing scored from three, which is worth knowing because neither idea is hard. Two habits fix it. First, before writing 'eats', check whether the two organisms actually appear in the same food chain — if they eat the same things, the word you want is **compete**. Second, when a question asks for a source of energy, the answer in an ecosystem question is almost always the **Sun**; the plant is what captures it.

## RETRIEVAL PROMPTS
- rp.science.b1.b1-competition-food-webs.01 [verified] (qa) What is the source of energy for most ecosystems, and what captures it?  →  The Sun. Green plants — the producers — capture it by photosynthesis and make it available to consumers.
- rp.science.b1.b1-competition-food-webs.02 [verified] (definition) What does an arrow in a food chain represent?  →  Consumption, and the transfer of energy and substances such as carbon and nitrogen. It runs from the organism that is eaten to the organism that eats it.
- rp.science.b1.b1-competition-food-webs.03 [verified] (qa) What do plants compete for, and what do animals compete for?  →  Plants: water, light, space, minerals. Animals: water, food, territory, mates, and they must avoid predators. Plants never compete for food.
- rp.science.b1.b1-competition-food-webs.04 [verified] (trap) Two species decline when a third arrives, but none of them eats the others. What is the relationship called?  →  Competition — they need the same limited resource. Calling a competitor a predator is the error examiners report on the squirrel question.
- rp.science.b1.b1-competition-food-webs.05 [verified] (trap) Which trophic level holds the producers, and what can never be at that level?  →  Level 1 holds the producers, which are green plants. No animal can be at Level 1.
