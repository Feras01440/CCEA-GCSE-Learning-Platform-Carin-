## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.01 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: The velocity/time graph shows a train leaving a station on a straight track.

(i) Calculate the acceleration of the train during the first 20 seconds.
(ii) Calculate the total distance travelled by the train in the 80 seconds.
  FIG: svg — A velocity/time graph for a train: a straight line from (0, 0) to (20, 24), a horizontal line to (50, 24), then a straight line down to (80, 0). The shaded area beneath is split by dashed lines into a triangle of 240 m, a rectangle of 720 m and a triangle of 360 m.
  1. Acceleration $= \dfrac{24 - 0}{20 - 0} = 1.2$ m/s²   — The gradient of a velocity/time graph is the acceleration, because the rise is a change in velocity and the run is the time it took. Metres per second, per second.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because the gradient of a velocity/time graph is the acceleration | ✘Because the gradient of a velocity/time graph is the distance | ✘Because the gradient of a velocity/time graph is the velocity
  2. Triangle: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 20 \times 24 = 240$ m   — Distance is the area under the graph. Split the shape at every joint first: here a triangle while the train speeds up, a rectangle at steady speed, a triangle while it slows.  (MW1)
  3. Rectangle: $30 \times 24 = 720$ m   — The middle stage lasts 50 - 20 = 30 s at a steady 24 m/s, so its area is a plain rectangle.  (MW1)
  4. Triangle: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 30 \times 24 = 360$ m   — The last stage runs from 50 s to 80 s, so its base is 30 s and its height is the 24 m/s the train is losing.  (MW1)
  5. Total distance $= 240 + 720 + 360 = 1320$ m   — Add the three pieces. The whole shape is a trapezium, so $\tfrac{1}{2}(80 + 30) \times 24$ gives the same $1320$ m in one line: use whichever you can see faster, and use the other to check.  (W1)
  FINAL: (i) $1.2$ m/s²  (ii) $1320$ m
  TWIN: A lift's velocity/time graph rises in a straight line from rest to 6 m/s in 4 seconds, stays at 6 m/s for 11 seconds, then falls in a straight line to rest in 3 seconds. Calculate the total distance it travels.  → numeric 87 m tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction

### we.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.02 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: A tram is travelling at 18 m/s along a straight track when it begins to brake, 600 m from its terminus.
It decelerates uniformly at 0.6 m/s² for 10 seconds, reaching a velocity of $V$ m/s.
It then travels at $V$ m/s for 25 seconds.
Finally it decelerates uniformly, coming to rest exactly at the terminus.

(i) Calculate the value of $V$.
(ii) Calculate how far the tram travels in the first 35 seconds.
(iii) Calculate the whole time the tram takes to cover the 600 m.
  1. $V = 18 - 0.6 \times 10 = 12$   — Deceleration means the velocity is falling, so subtract. Reading the gradient off the graph gives the same thing: a drop of 0.6 m/s in every second for 10 seconds.  (MW1,W1)
  2. Braking stage: $\tfrac{1}{2}(18 + 12) \times 10 = 150$ m   — That stage is a trapezium: parallel sides 18 m/s and 12 m/s, width 10 s. Half the sum of the parallel sides, times the width.  (MW1)
  3. Steady stage: $12 \times 25 = 300$ m, so the first 35 s cover $150 + 300 = 450$ m   — A rectangle this time. The two stages together are the 10 + 25 seconds the part asks about.  (MW1,W1)
  4. Distance still to run $= 600 - 450 = 150$ m   — This is the step that makes the last part possible. The tram has 600 m in total, so whatever is left after the first two stages must be covered by the final triangle.  (MW1)
  5. $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times t = 150$, so $t = 25$ s   — The last stage is a triangle of height 12 m/s and unknown base $t$. Writing its area and setting it equal to the distance left turns the geometry into an equation with one unknown.  (MW1,W1)
  6. Total time $= 10 + 25 + 25 = 60$ s   — The question asks for the whole journey, so add the 35 seconds already gone to the 25 seconds just found. Handing in the 25 on its own is the loss the examiners reported in Summer 2022.  (W1)
  FINAL: (i) $12$  (ii) $450$ m  (iii) $60$ s
  TWIN: A tram travelling at 20 m/s decelerates uniformly at 0.5 m/s² for 8 seconds. Calculate its velocity at the end of the 8 seconds.  → numeric 16 m/s tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction

### we.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.03 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: A bus passes a stationary taxi at a constant 12 m/s and keeps that velocity.
8 seconds later the taxi sets off, accelerating uniformly for 12 seconds to reach 20 m/s, and then holds 20 m/s until it draws level with the bus.

(i) Calculate the time, measured from the moment the taxi starts moving, until it draws level with the bus.
(ii) Calculate the distance the taxi has travelled in that time.
  FIG: svg — A velocity/time graph with two journeys: the bus as a horizontal line at 12 m/s, and the taxi at rest until 8 s, rising in a straight line to 20 m/s at 20 s, then horizontal, with a dashed line at 35 s where the areas are equal.
  1. Let $T$ be the time measured from the moment the bus passed the taxi.   — One clock for both vehicles. Starting the taxi's clock at zero is what wrecks this question: every distance then refers to a different moment.  (MW1)
  2. Bus: distance $= 12T$   — The bus keeps a steady velocity, so its area is a rectangle of height 12 and width $T$.  (MW1)
  3. Taxi, first 12 s of motion: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 20 = 120$ m   — The taxi's graph is a triangle while it speeds up. It reaches 20 m/s at $T = 8 + 12 = 20$ s on the shared clock.  (MW1)
  4. Taxi, after that: $20(T - 20)$, so the taxi has gone $120 + 20(T - 20)$   — A rectangle whose width is however long the taxi has been at full speed. Both distances are now expressions in the same $T$, which is exactly what the examiners said the successful candidates wrote down.  (MW1)
  5. $12T = 120 + 20T - 400$, so $8T = 280$ and $T = 35$ s   — Level means equal distances from the same starting line, so set the two expressions equal and solve. The equation is linear, so no formula is needed.  (MW1,W1)
  6. Time after the taxi starts $= 35 - 8 = 27$ s   — Read the question again. $T$ is measured from the bus passing, and the part asks for the time from when the taxi started, so take off the 8-second head start.  (W1)
  7. Distance $= 12 \times 35 = 420$ m   — The two vehicles are level, so either expression gives the distance. The bus's rectangle is the quicker one, and the taxi's areas can be used to check it.  (MW1,W1)
  FINAL: (i) $27$ s  (ii) $420$ m
  TWIN: A cyclist travelling at a constant 9 m/s passes a stationary scooter. 5 seconds later the scooter sets off, accelerating uniformly for 10 seconds to 18 m/s and then holding that velocity. Calculate the time, measured from the moment the cyclist passed, until the scooter draws level.  → numeric 20 s tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.pre (pre) [verified]
- 01 (Prerequisite: area of a triangle): From an earlier topic: a triangle has base 12 and height 9. What is its area?
    ✔ 54 — Half the base times the height: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 9 = 54$.
    ✘ 108 [fm.kin.half-omitted-in-area] — That is the rectangle around the triangle. The one half is missing.
    ✘ 21 [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — That adds the base and the height. Area multiplies them.
    ✘ 1.33 [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — That divides the two lengths, which gives a ratio rather than an area.
- 02 (Prerequisite: area of a trapezium): From an earlier topic: a trapezium has parallel sides 6 and 10 and a width of 8 between them. What is its area?
    ✔ 64 — Half the sum of the parallel sides, times the width: $\tfrac{1}{2}(6 + 10) \times 8 = 64$.
    ✘ 128 [fm.kin.half-omitted-in-area] — The one half has been left out, so the answer is exactly twice the area.
    ✘ 80 [fm.kin.area-as-rectangle] — That is a rectangle at the longer side. A trapezium uses the average of the two parallel sides.
    ✘ 48 [fm.kin.area-as-rectangle] — That is a rectangle at the shorter side, which is too small.
- 03 (Prerequisite: gradient of a straight line): From an earlier topic: a straight line runs from $(0, 0)$ to $(8, 16)$. What is its gradient?
    ✔ 2 — Rise 16 over run 8 gives 2.
    ✘ 0.5 [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — The fraction is upside down: that is run over rise.
    ✘ 128 [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — That multiplies the two, which gives an area rather than a gradient.
    ✘ 8 [fm.kin.gradient-not-taken-from-graph] — That is the run on its own. Divide the rise by it.
- 04 (Prerequisite: solve a linear equation with a bracket): From an earlier topic: solve $8x = 60 + 12(x - 10)$.
    ✔ $x = 15$ — Expanding gives $8x = 12x - 60$, so $4x = 60$ and $x = 15$.
    ✘ $x = 30$ [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — The bracket has been expanded as $12x - 10$. Every term inside is multiplied by 12.
    ✘ $x = -15$ [fm.kin.head-start-ignored] — A sign has slipped when the $12x$ crossed the equals sign. Take $8x$ from both sides, not the other way round.
    ✘ $x = 3$ [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — That comes from $20x = 60$, which adds the two $x$ terms instead of subtracting.

### dx.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (Name what the gradient of a velocity/time graph gives): What does the gradient of a velocity/time graph give?
    ✔ The acceleration — Change in velocity divided by time: metres per second, per second.
    ✘ The distance travelled [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — Distance is the area underneath, not the slope. Mixing these two is the commonest loss on this topic.
    ✘ The velocity [fm.kin.gradient-not-taken-from-graph] — Velocity is the height of the graph. The slope says how fast that height is changing.
    ✘ The displacement [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — Displacement, like distance, comes from the area under the graph.
- 02 (Name what the area under a velocity/time graph gives): What does the area between a velocity/time graph and the time axis give?
    ✔ The distance travelled — Velocity multiplied by time is a distance, and that is exactly what an area on these axes measures.
    ✘ The acceleration [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — Acceleration is the gradient. The area has units of metres per second times seconds, which is metres.
    ✘ The average velocity [fm.kin.average-speed-formula] — The area gives the distance. Dividing it by the total time would then give the average speed.
    ✘ The final velocity [fm.kin.gradient-not-taken-from-graph] — The final velocity is the height of the graph at the end, which is a reading rather than an area.
- 03 (Read a horizontal section on velocity/time axes): A velocity/time graph is horizontal at 14 m/s for 20 seconds. What is happening?
    ✔ The body keeps a steady 14 m/s and covers 280 m — Flat means the velocity is not changing, so the acceleration is zero. The area is the rectangle 14 x 20.
    ✘ The body is at rest for 20 seconds [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — That is what a flat section means on displacement/time axes. Here the height is the velocity, and 14 m/s is not rest.
    ✘ The body covers no distance, because the graph is flat [fm.kin.area-as-rectangle] — The graph is flat but the area underneath is not zero: the rectangle is 280 m tall by wide.
    ✘ The body is accelerating steadily [fm.kin.gradient-not-taken-from-graph] — Steady acceleration would be a sloping straight line. A horizontal line has gradient zero, so the acceleration is zero.
- 04 (Read a falling section as a deceleration): A velocity/time graph falls in a straight line from 24 m/s to rest in 8 seconds. What is the deceleration?
    ✔ 3 m/s² — The gradient is -3 m/s², and a deceleration of 3 m/s² is the same statement said the other way round.
    ✘ -3 m/s², because deceleration is always negative [fm.kin.deceleration-sign] — The gradient is -3. A question that asks for the deceleration wants its size, so write 3 m/s² and say it is a deceleration.
    ✘ 192 m/s² [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — 24 x 8 is an area, which would be a distance if the graph were a rectangle. An acceleration is a gradient: divide instead.
    ✘ 0.33 m/s² [fm.kin.gradient-not-taken-from-graph] — That is the run divided by the rise. Acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the time, so 24 divided by 8.
- 05 (Choose the right area formula for a sloping stage): On a velocity/time graph a straight section rises from 8 m/s to 20 m/s over 15 seconds. Which calculation gives the distance covered in that section?
    ✔ Half of (8 + 20), times 15 — A trapezium: half the sum of the two parallel sides, times the width. That is 210 m.
    ✘ 20 times 15 [fm.kin.area-as-rectangle] — That treats the section as a rectangle at the highest velocity, which counts the empty triangle above the line as distance travelled.
    ✘ (8 + 20) times 15 [fm.kin.half-omitted-in-area] — The one half has gone. That doubles the trapezium, giving 420 m instead of 210 m.
    ✘ (20 − 8) divided by 15 [fm.kin.gradient-and-area-swapped] — That is the gradient, so it gives the acceleration in m/s². The distance is an area.
- 06 (Handle a head start in a catch-up problem): A van passes a parked car at a steady velocity. 6 seconds later the car sets off. Working with one clock that starts when the van passes, you find the car draws level at $T = 33$ s. The question asks for the time from the moment the car started. What do you write?
    ✔ 27 s — The car's own clock started 6 seconds later, so take the head start off: 33 - 6 = 27 s.
    ✘ 33 s [fm.kin.head-start-ignored] — That is the time on the shared clock, measured from the van passing. The Summer 2023 examiners reported this exact step as the one candidates missed.
    ✘ 39 s [fm.kin.head-start-ignored] — The head start is taken off, not added: the car has been moving for less time than the van, not more.
    ✘ 6 s [fm.kin.stage-time-not-used] — That is the head start on its own. It is the correction, not the answer.
- 07 (Refuse one formula across a whole multi-stage journey): A car speeds up for 8 s, holds that velocity for 12 s, then brakes to rest, covering 700 m in all. A student writes $700 = \tfrac{1}{2}(30 + 0)t$ for the whole journey. What is wrong with that?
    ✔ The acceleration is not the same throughout, so one constant-acceleration formula cannot cover the whole journey — Constant-acceleration formulae apply to one stage at a time. Across the three stages, use the area under the graph.
    ✘ Nothing, as long as the arithmetic is right [fm.kin.one-formula-whole-journey] — The formula assumes one steady acceleration from start to finish, and this journey has three different ones. The Summer 2022 examiners saw this attempted across a whole journey and it earned nothing.
    ✘ The 700 should be halved first [fm.kin.half-omitted-in-area] — The distance is not the problem: the formula is being applied to a journey whose acceleration changes twice.
    ✘ The formula should have been $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ [fm.kin.one-formula-whole-journey] — Swapping to another constant-acceleration formula does not help: every one of them assumes a single steady acceleration.

### q.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.0010 [verified] practice · diff 2 · untiered · FM2 calc · 2 marks · (main)calculate2 · Deceleration from a graph

  FIX: Parallel sides 9 and 3, width 30 / Distance = half of (9 + 3) × 30 = 180 m
  FEEDBACK: Naming the trapezium and reading its three measurements off the graph is the method mark (M), and it is safely earned here. The working mark (W) goes with the halving. A quick check that catches this every time: the answer must lie between the rectangle at 3 m/s, which is 90 m, and the rectangle at 9 m/s, which is 270 m.

## RETRIEVAL PROMPTS
- rp.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.01 [verified] (definition) What does the gradient of a velocity/time graph give?  →  The acceleration: the change in velocity divided by the time it took.
- rp.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.02 [verified] (definition) What does the area under a velocity/time graph give?  →  The distance travelled. Metres per second times seconds gives metres.
- rp.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.03 [verified] (procedure) How do you find the distance from a multi-stage velocity/time graph?  →  Split the area at every joint into triangles, rectangles and trapezia, work out each one, and add them. Or treat the whole shape as a single trapezium where it is one.
- rp.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.04 [verified] (formula) Write the area of a trapezium under a velocity/time graph.  →  Half the sum of the two parallel velocities, multiplied by the time the stage lasts.
- rp.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.05 [verified] (trap) Why is the greatest velocity times the total time usually wrong for the distance?  →  Because it counts the empty space above the sloping parts as distance travelled. It is only right when the graph is one rectangle all the way across.
- rp.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.06 [verified] (procedure) How do you handle a catch-up question on a velocity/time graph?  →  Use one clock for both bodies, write each distance as an expression in that single time, set the two expressions equal and solve.
- rp.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.07 [verified] (trap) You solve a catch-up problem and get T = 33 s. What must you check before writing it down?  →  Which clock the question uses. If T was measured from the first vehicle and the question asks about the second, take off the head start.
- rp.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.08 [verified] (procedure) A journey finishes with a braking stage whose time is unknown. How do you find it?  →  Work out the distance still to be covered, write the area of that last stage in terms of the unknown time, set the two equal and solve.
- rp.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.09 [verified] (trap) Can one constant-acceleration formula be used across a whole multi-stage journey?  →  No. Each formula assumes a single steady acceleration, so it applies to one stage at a time. Across stages, use the area under the graph.
- rp.fm.u2.velocity-time-graphs.10 [verified] (qa) How do you get an average speed from a velocity/time graph?  →  Find the total distance as the area under the graph, then divide by the total time.
