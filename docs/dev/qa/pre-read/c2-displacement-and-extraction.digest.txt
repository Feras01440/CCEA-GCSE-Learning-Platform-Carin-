### we.science.c2.c2-displacement-and-extraction.01 [verified] paper C2 calc
STEM: A clean strip of zinc is left standing in blue copper(II) sulfate solution for ten minutes.
(a) Name the type of reaction taking place. [1]
(b) Give two observations. [2]
(c) Write the balanced symbol equation. [3]
  FIG: svg — Two test tubes side by side: a strip of zinc standing in blue copper sulfate solution, where the blue fades and a brown coating grows, and a strip of copper in colourless zinc sulfate solution, where nothing changes.
  1. Zinc is above copper in the reactivity series, so the zinc takes the sulfate and the copper is pushed out. That is a displacement reaction.   — Name it before you describe it. Summer 2023 and Summer 2024 both record candidates who described the change perfectly but never wrote the word displacement.  (P1)
  2. First observation: the blue solution fades to colourless.   — Copper(II) ions are what make the solution blue; as they leave the solution the blue drains away. Zinc sulfate solution is colourless.  (P2)
  3. Second observation: a brown solid coats the zinc strip.   — Name the colour. CCEA has recorded the colour of copper as poorly known in two separate series, and 'a solid forms' on its own is not the mark.  (P3)  WHY: ✔Because the mark is for the colour, which identifies the metal | ✘Because a solid always has to be described by its mass | ✘Because copper is the only brown element
  4. Formulae: Zn, CuSO4, ZnSO4, Cu.   — The sulfate is a spectator, so it travels straight across unchanged. Write all four formulae before balancing.  (P4)
  5. Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu is already balanced: one zinc, one copper, one sulfate on each side.   — Check each element before you decide nothing is needed. A one-to-one swap needs no balancing numbers.  (P5,P6)
  FINAL: (a) Displacement. (b) The blue solution fades to colourless, and a brown solid forms on the zinc. (c) Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu.

### we.science.c2.c2-displacement-and-extraction.02 [verified] paper C2 calc
STEM: Iron is extracted from haematite, an ore containing iron(III) oxide, $\ce{Fe2O3}$.
(a) Use the Data Leaflet to calculate the relative formula mass of iron(III) oxide. [2]
(b) In the thermite reaction $\ce{2Al + Fe2O3 -> 2Fe + Al2O3}$, calculate the mass of iron produced from 24.0 g of iron(III) oxide. Give your answer to one decimal place. [3]
  1. From the Data Leaflet, Fe = 56 and O = 16.   — Take both values off the leaflet rather than from memory: the leaflet is the only version CCEA marks against.
  2. Fe2O3 has two irons and three oxygens: 2 × 56 + 3 × 16 = 112 + 48 = 160.   — Multiply each relative atomic mass by the number of atoms of that element. Missing the 2 in front of the iron is the error Summer 2025's report names.  (P1,P2)  WHY: ✔Because the small 2 tells you there are two iron atoms in the formula | ✘Because iron is always counted twice in an oxide | ✘Because the 3 on the oxygen also applies to the iron
  3. In the equation, one Fe2O3 gives two Fe, so 160 of oxide gives 2 × 56 = 112 of iron.   — Read the ratio off the balanced equation, not off the formula. The 2 in front of Fe is what doubles the iron.  (P3)
  4. Mass of iron = 24.0 × 112 ÷ 160.   — Set it out as one line before you touch the calculator; a written line is what earns the method mark when the arithmetic slips.  (P4)
  5. = 16.8 g, to one decimal place as the question asks.   — The accuracy is instructed in the stem, so write it to one decimal place. Summer 2025's report records marks lost for ignoring that instruction.  (P5)
  FINAL: (a) 160. (b) 16.8 g of iron.
  TWIN: Use the Data Leaflet to calculate the relative formula mass of aluminium oxide, Al2O3.  → numeric 102 tol={"type":"exact"} forms=decimal

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.c2.c2-displacement-and-extraction.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Prerequisite from the previous topic — you are not expected to have met displacement yet): Which metal is higher in the reactivity series, zinc or copper?
    ✔ zinc — Zinc is above copper. Everything in this lesson follows from comparisons like that one.
    ✘ copper [sci.metals.reactivity-order-not-recalled] — The order runs potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium, zinc, iron, copper, so copper is last of the eight. The previous topic builds that list if it is not fixed yet.
    ✘ they are equally reactive [sci.metals.reactivity-order-not-recalled] — No two metals in the series sit at the same height; that is what makes a series useful.
- p2 (Prerequisite from C1 §1.5 — reading a formula that contains a group of atoms): In the formula $\ce{CuSO4}$, which part is the sulfate?
    ✔ SO₄ — The sulfate is the SO₄ part, and in this lesson it travels from one metal to another without changing.
    ✘ Cu [sci.formulae.subscript-case-errors] — Cu is copper, the metal. The sulfate is the SO₄ group that follows it.
    ✘ O₄ [sci.formulae.subscript-case-errors] — The sulfur belongs to the group too: sulfate is SO₄ as one unit, not oxygen on its own.
- p3 (Prerequisite from C1 §1.5 — what balancing means): When an equation is balanced, what has to be the same on both sides of the arrow?
    ✔ the number of atoms of each element — Atoms are neither made nor destroyed, so each element must be counted equal on both sides.
    ✘ the number of substances [sci.equations.balancing-omitted] — Two reactants can easily give three products. It is the atoms of each element that must match, not the number of formulae.
    ✘ the number of big numbers in front [sci.equations.balancing-omitted] — Those numbers are the tool, not the target. You change them until each element's atom count matches.

### dx.science.c2.c2-displacement-and-extraction.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (Name the reaction type): A strip of zinc is placed in copper(II) sulfate solution and a brown solid forms on it. What type of reaction is this?
    ✔ displacement — Displacement: the more reactive metal has taken the place of the less reactive one.
    ✘ neutralisation [sci.metals.displacement-not-identified] — Neutralisation is an acid and a base making a salt and water. There is no acid here.
    ✘ thermal decomposition [sci.metals.displacement-not-identified] — Thermal decomposition needs heat to break one compound into two. Here two substances swap partners at room temperature.
    ✘ combustion [sci.metals.displacement-not-identified] — Combustion is burning in oxygen. There is no oxygen involved in this tube.
- 02 (Predict the colour change in a displacement): Zinc is added to blue copper(II) sulfate solution. What happens to the colour of the solution?
    ✔ the blue fades to colourless — The blue belongs to the copper(II) ions; as they leave the solution the colour drains away, and zinc sulfate solution is colourless.
    ✘ it stays blue [sci.metals.displacement-colour-unknown] — It cannot stay blue: the copper(II) ions that make it blue are being turned into copper metal.
    ✘ it turns brown [sci.metals.displacement-colour-unknown] — Brown is the solid copper appearing on the zinc, not the solution. The solution loses its colour.
    ✘ it turns green [sci.metals.displacement-colour-unknown] — Green is not one of the colours in this reaction. Zinc sulfate solution is colourless.
- 03 (Decide whether a displacement happens): Which of these mixtures will not react?
    ✔ copper added to zinc sulfate solution — Copper is below zinc, so it cannot push zinc out of its solution. Nothing happens.
    ✘ magnesium added to copper(II) sulfate solution [sci.metals.reactivity-order-not-recalled] — Magnesium is well above copper, so this one does react and copper is displaced.
    ✘ zinc added to silver nitrate solution [sci.metals.reactivity-order-not-recalled] — Zinc is above silver, so the zinc displaces silver from the solution.
    ✘ magnesium added to zinc sulfate solution [sci.metals.reactivity-order-not-recalled] — Magnesium is above zinc, so this reaction happens and zinc is displaced.
- 04 (Write the products of a displacement): Zinc is added to silver nitrate solution. What are the products?
    ✔ zinc nitrate and silver — The nitrate is a spectator: it stays with whichever metal is in solution, and that is now the zinc.
    ✘ zinc chloride and silver [sci.metals.displacement-products-wrong] — There is no chlorine anywhere in this mixture. Summer 2024 examiners recorded exactly this answer. The salt keeps its nitrate.
    ✘ zinc and silver nitrate [sci.metals.displacement-products-wrong] — Those are the reactants written out again. Summer 2024's report names that as a common response.
    ✘ zinc oxide and silver nitrate [sci.metals.displacement-products-wrong] — No oxygen is involved. The zinc takes the nitrate and the silver comes out as the metal.
- 05 (Link the extraction method to the reactivity series): Why is aluminium extracted from its ore by electrolysis rather than by heating with carbon?
    ✔ aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot remove its oxygen — Position decides the method: above carbon means electrolysis.
    ✘ electrolysis is the cheaper method [sci.extraction.method-not-linked-to-series] — Electrolysis is much more expensive, because of the electrical energy it needs. It is used because carbon will not work.
    ✘ aluminium ore does not contain oxygen [sci.extraction.method-not-linked-to-series] — Bauxite contains aluminium oxide, so there is oxygen to remove. The question is what can remove it.
    ✘ aluminium melts at too low a temperature for a furnace [sci.extraction.method-not-linked-to-series] — Melting points are not what decides the method. Reactivity is.
- 06 (Explain the thermite reaction from the series): In the thermite reaction, aluminium powder is mixed with iron(III) oxide. Why does the aluminium take the oxygen?
    ✔ aluminium is more reactive than iron — That single comparison is the whole answer, and Summer 2023 examiners reported few candidates making it.
    ✘ aluminium is a powder, so it has a larger surface area [sci.metals.aluminium-reactivity-not-explained] — Surface area changes how fast a reaction goes, not whether it can happen at all.
    ✘ iron(III) oxide is unstable at room temperature [sci.metals.aluminium-reactivity-not-explained] — Iron(III) oxide is very stable, which is why the mixture needs a fuse to start it.
    ✘ aluminium has a lower melting point than iron [sci.metals.aluminium-reactivity-not-explained] — True, but not the reason. The reason is that aluminium sits above iron in the reactivity series.

      SOLUTION: Put a small, clean piece of each metal into a separate test tube holding the same volume of one salt solution, and repeat until every metal has met every solution. Keep the volume and the concentration of the solution and the size of the metal the same each time so that the only thing changed is which metal is used. Watch each tube: a reaction shows that the metal added is more reactive than the metal in the solution, and the evidence is a coating of a new metal on the strip, a change in the colour of the solution, or a rise in temperature. A tube in which nothing changes shows that the added metal is less reactive, so no displacement happens. The metal that reacts with every other solution is the most reactive, the metal that reacts with none of them is the least, and the rest fall into place in between. That order also decides how each metal is taken out of its ore. A metal above carbon in the series, such as aluminium, holds its oxygen too tightly for carbon to remove it, so it must be extracted by electrolysis. A metal below carbon, such as iron, can be reduced by heating its ore with carbon, which takes the oxygen away and leaves the metal.
