(i) Write down the distance she has travelled and her displacement from the start.
(ii) Define a vector quantity, and give one example from this journey.
(iii) Define a scalar quantity, and give one example from this journey.
  FIG: svg — A walk of 3 kilometres east then 4 kilometres north, drawn as two dashed arrows, with a solid arrow straight from the start to the finish labelled 5 kilometres. The path is 7 kilometres long but the finish is only 5 kilometres from the start.
  1. Distance $= 3 + 4 = 7$ km.   — Distance adds up the whole path, whichever way each piece went, because it has no direction attached to it.  (W1)  WHY: ✔Because distance measures the path travelled, with no direction attached | ✘Because distance is always the shortest route between two points | ✘Because distance and displacement are two words for the same measure
  2. Displacement $= 5$ km on a bearing of $036.87^{\circ}$, since $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5$.   — Displacement is one straight step from the start to the finish, so it needs both a size and a direction to be stated in full.  (MW1)

  4. A scalar quantity has magnitude only. The distance she rode is one.   — Magnitude means the size of the quantity. A scalar stops there; nothing about direction is part of it.  (W1,W1)
  FINAL: (i) distance $7$ km, displacement $5$ km north-east of the start  (ii) magnitude and direction, for example displacement  (iii) magnitude only, for example distance

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u2.vector-and-scalar-quantities.pre (pre) [verified]
- 01 (Prerequisite: the unit of force): From an earlier topic: which unit is a force measured in?
    ✔ The newton — Forces are measured in newtons, written N.
    ✘ The kilogram — Kilograms measure mass. A force is measured in newtons.
    ✘ The joule — Joules measure energy. A force is measured in newtons.
    ✘ The metre per second — That is a speed. A force is measured in newtons.
- 02 (Prerequisite: bearings): From an earlier topic: a bearing is measured from which direction, and which way round?
    ✔ From north, clockwise — Bearings start at north and turn clockwise, written with three figures.
    ✘ From north, anticlockwise — The turn is clockwise, the way the hands of a clock move.
    ✘ From east, clockwise — East is where an angle measured from the positive x-axis starts, which is a different convention.
    ✘ From south, clockwise — North is the zero of a bearing.
- 03 (Prerequisite: Pythagoras): From an earlier topic: a right-angled triangle has shorter sides 3 and 4. How long is the hypotenuse?
    ✔ $5$ — $3^2 + 4^2 = 25$, and $\sqrt{25} = 5$.
    ✘ $7$ — That adds the two sides. Pythagoras squares them first.
    ✘ $25$ — That is the square of the hypotenuse. The last step is a square root.
    ✘ $12$ — That multiplies the two sides, which gives twice the area rather than a length.

### dx.fm.u2.vector-and-scalar-quantities.post (post) [verified]
- 04 (The definition of a vector quantity): Which of these is the definition of a vector quantity?
    ✔ A quantity with magnitude and direction — Both halves are needed, and this is the wording CCEA credits.
    ✘ A quantity with magnitude only [fm.vectors.definitions-swapped] — That is a scalar. A vector adds a direction to the size.
    ✘ A quantity such as force [fm.vectors.definition-by-example] — That is an example rather than a definition, and CCEA refuses an example offered in place of one.
    ✘ A quantity measured in newtons [fm.vectors.definition-by-example] — A unit does not decide it: displacement is a vector and is measured in metres.
- 05 (Weight as a vector): Is weight a vector or a scalar, and why?
    ✔ A vector, because it is a force and it acts downwards — Weight is the pull of gravity on a mass, so it has a size in newtons and a direction.
    ✘ A scalar, because it is just a number of newtons [fm.vectors.weight-scalar] — This is the commonest error on this question. Weight is a force, and every force has a direction.
    ✘ A scalar, because it is the same as mass [fm.vectors.weight-scalar] — Mass and weight are different measures: kilograms against newtons, and mass has no direction while weight does.
    ✘ A vector, because it is measured on a balance [fm.vectors.definition-by-example] — The instrument does not decide it. Weight is a vector because it is a force with a direction.
- 06 (Speed against velocity): Which statement is correct?
    ✔ Speed is a scalar and velocity is a vector — Velocity is a speed with a direction attached, so it is the vector of the pair.
    ✘ Speed is a vector and velocity is a scalar [fm.vectors.speed-velocity-classification] — It is the other way round. Velocity carries the direction.
    ✘ Both are vectors [fm.vectors.speed-velocity-classification] — Speed answers only how fast, so it has no direction.
    ✘ Both are scalars [fm.vectors.speed-velocity-classification] — Velocity names a direction as well as a size, so it is a vector.
- 07 (Distance against displacement): A runner does one full lap of a 400 m track and stops where she started. What are her distance and her displacement?
    ✔ Distance $400$ m, displacement $0$ m — The path is 400 m long, and the finish is the start, so there is no displacement.
    ✘ Distance $400$ m, displacement $400$ m [fm.kin.speed-vs-velocity] — Displacement is measured from start to finish in a straight line, and here those are the same point.
    ✘ Distance $0$ m, displacement $400$ m [fm.kin.speed-vs-velocity] — The two are the other way round. She really did cover 400 m of track.
    ✘ Distance $0$ m, displacement $0$ m [fm.kin.speed-vs-velocity] — Distance counts the whole path, so a lap of the track is 400 m however it ends.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u2.vector-and-scalar-quantities.01 [verified] fm.vectors.weight-scalar · earned W1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2025-summer:FM2:Q1
STEM: Cathal is classifying four measures for the opening question of a Unit 2 paper. His answers:
    1. Distance: scalar
    2. Velocity: vector
  ✘ 3. Weight: scalar
    4. Tension: vector
  WRONG: Line 3 calls weight a scalar. Weight is the pull of gravity on a mass, so it is a force: it has a size in newtons and it acts downwards. The confusion comes from mass, which really is a scalar and is often called weight in ordinary speech.
  FIX: Distance: scalar / Velocity: vector / Weight: vector / Tension: vector
  FEEDBACK: Three of the four are right, which on a two-mark table earns the first mark. Weight is the one CCEA reports most often as misclassified, so it is worth a second look every time: if a measure is a force, it is a vector.
