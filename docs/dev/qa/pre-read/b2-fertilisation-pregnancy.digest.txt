### we.science.b2.b2-fertilisation-pregnancy.02 [verified] paper B2 calc
STEM: Explain how the placenta is adapted for the diffusion of substances between the mother and the foetus. [3]
  FIG: svg — The placenta against the uterus wall with villi reaching into it, the umbilical cord running to the foetus, and the foetus inside the amnion; arrows show dissolved nutrients and oxygen crossing to the foetus and carbon dioxide and urea crossing back.
  1. Recognise the question: this is an exchange surface, like the alveolus and the root hair.   — The same four points answer all of them: a large surface area, a thin barrier, a good blood supply, and a difference in concentration that is kept up.  ()
  2. The villi give a large surface area, so more substances diffuse at once.   — A feature on its own is not a marking point. Each mark is the feature plus what it does.  (P1)  WHY: ✔Because a bare 'large surface area' does not say what it achieves | ✘Because the villi are the only part that matters | ✘Because surface area is always worth two marks
  3. The barrier between the two blood supplies is thin, so the diffusion distance is short.   — Thin is only half the point; the short distance is the other half.  (P2)
  4. Both sides have a good blood supply, which keeps the difference in concentration up.   — Blood flow carries the arriving substance away, so the difference never evens out and diffusion keeps going.  (P3)
  5. Check: the answer never says the two bloods mix.   — They come close but never join — that is why diffusion is needed at all.  ()
  FINAL: The villi give a large surface area, so more diffusion happens at once; the barrier is thin, so the distance to diffuse is short; and the good blood supply on both sides keeps the difference in concentration up.
  TWIN: Name the part of the placenta that gives it its large surface area.  → text accepted=["the villi","villi"] keyWords=[{"any":["villi","villus"],"marks":1}]

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.b2.b2-fertilisation-pregnancy.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Recall the site of fertilisation (previous topic)): Before we start, three things you have already met. In which part of the female reproductive system does fertilisation take place?
    ✔ the oviduct — The oviduct. Everything in this topic happens along the route from there to the uterus.
    ✘ the uterus [sci.fert.site-not-oviduct] — The uterus is the destination, not the place the nuclei fuse.
    ✘ the ovary [sci.fert.site-not-oviduct] — The ovary releases the ovum; fertilisation happens after it has left.
    ✘ the cervix — The cervix is the ring of muscle at the base of the uterus.
- p2 (Recall cell structures (B1)): Which structure in a cell releases energy by respiration?
    ✔ the mitochondria — The mitochondria. A sperm cell is packed with them, and you will see why shortly.
    ✘ the nucleus — The nucleus holds the chromosomes.
    ✘ the cell membrane [sci.cells.membrane-described-as-protection] — The membrane controls what enters and leaves the cell.
    ✘ the cytoplasm — Most reactions happen in the cytoplasm, but respiration that needs oxygen happens inside the mitochondria.
- p3 (Recall the levels of organisation (B1)): A group of cells of the same kind working together is called a…
    ✔ tissue — A tissue. Differentiation, later in this lesson, is how a ball of identical cells ends up with tissues in it.
    ✘ organ [sci.cells.organisation-order-reversed] — An organ is made of several tissues.
    ✘ organ system [sci.cells.organisation-order-reversed] — An organ system is a group of organs.
    ✘ organism [sci.cells.organisation-order-reversed] — An organism is the whole living thing.

### dx.science.b2.b2-fertilisation-pregnancy (post) [verified]
- d1 (Tell a zygote from an embryo): A single cell is formed when a sperm nucleus and an egg nucleus fuse. What is that cell called?
    ✔ a zygote — A zygote — one cell, and diploid.
    ✘ an embryo [sci.fert.zygote-embryo-confused] — An embryo is many cells. This one has not divided yet.
    ✘ a gamete [sci.fert.zygote-embryo-confused] — The gametes were the sperm and the ovum, before they fused.
    ✘ a foetus [sci.fert.zygote-embryo-confused] — A foetus is much later, once the organs have formed.
- d2 (Tell implantation from differentiation): A ball of cells sinks into the thickened lining of the uterus. What is this called?
    ✔ implantation — Implantation: a change of place.
    ✘ differentiation [sci.fert.implantation-differentiation-confused] — Differentiation is a change of job — the cells becoming different from one another. It comes after implantation.
    ✘ fertilisation [sci.fert.site-not-oviduct] — Fertilisation happened days earlier, in the oviduct.
    ✘ ovulation — Ovulation is the release of the ovum from the ovary.
- d3 (Tell differentiation from implantation): The cells of a ball of cells begin to become different from one another, forming tissues. What is this called?
    ✔ differentiation — Differentiation: a change of job.
    ✘ implantation [sci.fert.implantation-differentiation-confused] — Implantation is the ball settling into the lining — a change of place, not of job.
    ✘ mitosis [sci.fert.implantation-differentiation-confused] — Mitosis makes more cells, all identical. Differentiation is what makes them different.
    ✘ fertilisation [sci.fert.zygote-embryo-confused] — Fertilisation is the fusing of two nuclei.
- d4 (Recall how gametes are formed): Which type of cell division makes sperm?
    ✔ meiosis — Meiosis. It halves the chromosome number, which is why the sperm nucleus is haploid.
    ✘ mitosis [sci.fert.sperm-formed-by-mitosis] — Mitosis makes identical diploid cells. If gametes were made by mitosis the chromosome number would double at every fertilisation.
    ✘ differentiation [sci.fert.implantation-differentiation-confused] — Differentiation is cells becoming specialised, not a type of division.
    ✘ fertilisation [sci.fert.zygote-embryo-confused] — Fertilisation joins two cells rather than dividing one.
- d5 (Know which way each substance crosses): Which pair of substances passes from the mother's blood to the foetus across the placenta?
    ✔ oxygen and dissolved nutrients — Oxygen and dissolved nutrients go in; carbon dioxide and urea come back.
    ✘ carbon dioxide and urea [sci.fert.placenta-direction-reversed] — Those are the two wastes, and they travel the other way.
    ✘ oxygen and urea [sci.fert.placenta-direction-reversed] — Oxygen goes in, but urea is a waste from the foetus and comes out.
    ✘ blood cells and antibodies [sci.fert.placenta-blood-mixes] — The two blood supplies never mix, so blood cells do not cross.
- d6 (Give the job of the amniotic fluid): What is the function of the amniotic fluid?
    ✔ it cushions the foetus — It cushions the foetus against knocks.
    ✘ it supplies the foetus with food [sci.fert.amniotic-fluid-feeds-foetus] — Food reaches the foetus through the placenta and the umbilical cord.
    ✘ it supplies the foetus with oxygen [sci.fert.amniotic-fluid-feeds-foetus] — Oxygen also crosses the placenta; the fluid carries none of it.
    ✘ it carries waste away from the foetus [sci.fert.amniotic-fluid-feeds-foetus] — Waste leaves through the umbilical cord and the placenta.
- d7 (Continue a doubling pattern): A zygote has divided 6 times by mitosis. How many cells are there?
    ✔ 64 — 64. Each division doubles the count: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64.
    ✘ 12 [sci.mitosis.doubling-counted-as-adding] — That doubles the number of divisions rather than the number of cells.
    ✘ 32 [sci.mitosis.doubling-counted-as-adding] — That is one doubling short. Count the divisions again.
    ✘ 36 [sci.mitosis.doubling-counted-as-adding] — That squares the number of divisions. It is two that is multiplied by itself each time.
