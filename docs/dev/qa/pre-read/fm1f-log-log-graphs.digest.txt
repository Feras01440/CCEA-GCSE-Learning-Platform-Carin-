### we.fm.u1.log-log-graphs.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: A log-log graph of $\log F$ against $\log h$ is a straight line through $(0, 0.602)$ and $(1.398, 2.699)$, where $F = k h^{n}$.
Find $n$ and $k$, and write down the relationship between $F$ and $h$.
  FIG: svg — A log-log line with a dashed right-angled triangle between its two end points, both legs labelled, and the intercept marked log k.
  1. Write the two forms side by side: $\log F = n \log h + \log k$ and $y = mx + c$.   — This line costs nothing and it decides everything that follows: it is what tells you the gradient is the power and the intercept is the logarithm of the constant.  (MW1)
  2. Gradient $= \frac{2.699 - 0.602}{1.398 - 0} = \frac{2.097}{1.398} = 1.5$, so $n = 1.5$.   — The change in the vertical goes on top. Writing the fraction out before dividing is what stops it going in upside down.  (M2)  WHY: ✔Because the gradient sits where $m$ sits, and the comparison puts $n$ there | ✘Because $n$ is always the larger of the two constants | ✘Because the gradient of any log-log line is $1$
  3. The line meets the vertical axis at $0.602$, so $\log k = 0.602$.   — Name what you have read before you use it. Calling it log k rather than k is the whole defence against the slip the reports name most often.  (M3)
  4. Un-log it: $k = 10^{0.602} = 4.0$.   — A separate line for a separate step. Folded into the line above, this is the step that quietly goes missing.  (W1)
  5. So the relationship is $F = 4.0 h^{1.5}$.   — Answer in the form the question named. Leaving it as a log equation answers a different question, and so does giving the two constants without assembling them.  (W2)
  FINAL: $n = 1.5$, $k = 4.0$ and $F = 4.0 h^{1.5}$
  TWIN: A log-log graph of $\log P$ against $\log d$ is a straight line of gradient $2$ meeting the vertical axis at $0.699$, where $P = k d^{n}$.
Find $k$, correct to one decimal place.  → numeric 5 tol={"type":"dp","places":1} forms=decimal/fraction

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.log-log-graphs.pre (pre) [draft]
- p1 (Gradient from two points): Three quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new idea, so answer from what you already know.
What is the gradient of the straight line through $(1, 5)$ and $(4, 11)$?
    ✔ $2$ — The change in the vertical is $6$ and in the horizontal is $3$, so the gradient is $2$.
    ✘ $0.5$ — That is the fraction the other way up. The vertical change belongs on top.
    ✘ $6$ — That is the vertical change on its own. It still has to be divided by the horizontal change.
- p2 (The intercept of a straight line): In $y = mx + c$, what does $c$ tell you?
    ✔ Where the line crosses the vertical axis — At $x = 0$ the equation leaves $y = c$, which is the intercept.
    ✘ How steep the line is — That is $m$, the gradient. The constant $c$ fixes the height.
    ✘ Where the line crosses the horizontal axis — That crossing is where $y = 0$, which is a different value.
- p3 (Product and power laws): Using the laws of logarithms, what is $\log(k x^{n})$?
    ✔ $\log k + n \log x$ — The product law splits it, then the power law brings $n$ down in front.
    ✘ $\log k \times n \log x$ — A product inside a logarithm becomes a sum of logarithms, not a product of them.
    ✘ $n \log k + \log x$ — The power belongs to $x$, so it is $\log x$ that $n$ multiplies.

### dx.fm.u1.log-log-graphs.post (post) [draft]
- d1 (The linear form of a power law): Taking logarithms of $y = kx^{n}$ gives which straight line?
    ✔ $\log y = n \log x + \log k$ — Product law then power law, and the result sits beside $y = mx + c$.
    ✘ $\log y = k \log x + \log n$ [fm.logs.gradient-intercept-swapped] — The two constants have changed places. The power comes down as the gradient.
    ✘ $\log y = \log n + \log k + \log x$ [fm.logs.power-as-multiplier-inside] — The power does not become a separate logarithm; it multiplies $\log x$.
- d2 (Un-logging the intercept): A log-log line has gradient $1.5$ and meets the vertical axis at $0.602$. What is $k$?
    ✔ $4.0$ — The intercept is $\log k$, so $k = 10^{0.602} = 4.0$.
    ✘ $0.602$ [fm.logs.log-a-given-as-a] — That is the intercept itself, which is the logarithm of $k$. It still has to be un-logged.
    ✘ $1.5$ [fm.logs.gradient-intercept-swapped] — That is the gradient, which gives the power rather than the constant.
- d3 (The gradient, the right way up): A log-log line rises by $2.097$ as $\log h$ increases by $1.398$. What is $n$?
    ✔ $1.5$ — The vertical change goes on top: $\frac{2.097}{1.398} = 1.5$.
    ✘ $0.667$ [fm.logs.gradient-inverted] — That is the fraction upside down, which gives the reciprocal of the power.
    ✘ $2.097$ [fm.logs.gradient-inverted] — That is the vertical change alone. A gradient is always a division.
- d4 (The accuracy this topic is marked to): A table of logarithms is being completed for a log-log graph. To how many decimal places should the values be given?
    ✔ Three — Three decimal places throughout, in the table and on the plot.
    ✘ Two [fm.logs.decimal-places-instruction-ignored] — Two places is the accuracy the reports name as a lost mark almost every year on this topic.
    ✘ As many as the calculator shows [fm.logs.decimal-places-instruction-ignored] — The paper asks for three, and a full calculator display is not what the answer line is marked against.
- d5 (What the finished relationship takes): With $F = 4.0 h^{1.5}$, what goes into the relationship when a depth of $12.25$ cm is predicted?
    ✔ $12.25$, the depth itself — The logarithms were only there to straighten the graph. The relationship takes the measurement.
    ✘ $\log 12.25$, the logarithm of the depth [fm.logs.log-values-in-original-equation] — Putting a logarithm into the power law mixes the two forms, and gives $4.54$ instead of $171.5$.
    ✘ $0.602$, the intercept [fm.logs.log-values-in-original-equation] — The intercept is already used: it became the constant in front. The depth is what varies.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u1.log-log-graphs.01 [draft] fm.logs.log-a-given-as-a · earned MW1,M2 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2018-summer:FM1:Q11
STEM: Orla drew a log-log graph for $F = k h^{n}$ and read the gradient as 1.5 and the intercept as 0.602. Her working:
    1. log F = n log h + log k
    2. comparing with y = mx + c
    3. gradient = n, so n = 1.5
  ✘ 4. intercept = k, so k = 0.602
    5. F = 0.602 h^1.5
  WRONG: Lines 1 to 3 are exactly right, and line 5 is honest work on the value line 4 handed over.
Line 4 names the intercept as $k$. The comparison in line 2 put $\log k$ in the intercept position, not $k$, so the number read off the axis is the logarithm of the constant.
Un-logging it gives $k = 10^{0.602} = 4.0$.
  FIX: intercept = log k, so log k = 0.602 / k = 10^0.602 = 4.0 / F = 4.0 h^1.5
  FEEDBACK: The comparison and the gradient are both right, so the method marks stand and the rest is marked on her own value. The fix is one extra line, written every time: the intercept is log k, so k is ten to that power. Examiners have named this step in three separate series, which is why it is worth a line of its own.

## RETRIEVAL PROMPTS
- rp.fm.u1.log-log-graphs.01 [draft] (formula) What straight line does $y = kx^{n}$ become when logarithms are taken?  →  $\log y = n \log x + \log k$, so the gradient is $n$ and the intercept is $\log k$.
- rp.fm.u1.log-log-graphs.02 [draft] (procedure) Give the three steps that turn $y = kx^{n}$ into a straight line.  →  Take logarithms of both sides; use the product law to split $\log(kx^{n})$ into $\log k + \log x^{n}$; use the power law to write $\log x^{n}$ as $n \log x$.
- rp.fm.u1.log-log-graphs.03 [draft] (trap) The log-log line meets the vertical axis at $0.602$. What is $k$?  →  The intercept is $\log k$, so $k = 10^{0.602} = 4.0$ to one decimal place. The intercept is never the constant itself.
- rp.fm.u1.log-log-graphs.04 [draft] (trap) Which way up is the gradient of a log-log line written?  →  The change in $\log y$ on top, the change in $\log x$ underneath, taken between two points far apart on the line.
- rp.fm.u1.log-log-graphs.05 [draft] (qa) To how many decimal places are the log values given, and what are the axes labelled?  →  Three decimal places throughout, and the axes are labelled $\log x$ and $\log y$, not $x$ and $y$.
- rp.fm.u1.log-log-graphs.06 [draft] (trap) Why should a prediction be checked against the range of the data?  →  The line was fitted only to the readings you plotted, so outside that range the relationship has not been tested and the answer is a guess.
- rp.fm.u1.log-log-graphs.07 [draft] (trap) Once $k$ and $n$ are found, what goes into the relationship to make a prediction?  →  The measurement itself, not its logarithm. The logarithms were only used to straighten the graph.
