### we.fm.u1.logarithms-from-indices.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: Work out $\log_{3} 81$, showing your working.
  FIG: svg — The index statement 3 to the power 4 equals 81 beside its logarithm form.
  1. Call the answer $x$, so $\log_{3} 81 = x$.   — Naming the unknown is what lets you write the index form on the next line. Without it there is nothing to rearrange.  (M1)
  2. Write the index form: $3^{x} = 81$.   — This is the line the formula sheet gives you, and it is where the method mark sits in every logarithm question of this kind.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because $a^{x} = n$ and $x = \log_{a} n$ are the same statement | ✘Because $3$ and $81$ can be divided | ✘Because every logarithm is worked out on a calculator
  3. Count in powers of $3$: $3^{1} = 3$, $3^{2} = 9$, $3^{3} = 27$, $3^{4} = 81$.   — Counting up is quicker than guessing, and it makes the check obvious: the list stops the moment it reaches $81$.  (M2)
  4. The list reaches $81$ at the index $4$, so $x = 4$.   — Check by going back: $3^{4} = 81$, which is the number in the question.  (W1)
  FINAL: $\log_{3} 81 = 4$
  TWIN: Work out $\log_{2} 64$.  → numeric 6 tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.0005} forms=decimal/fraction

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.logarithms-from-indices.pre (pre) [draft]
- p1 (Evaluate a power): Three quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new idea, so answer from what you already know.
What is $2^{5}$?
    ✔ $32$ — Five twos multiplied together give $32$.
    ✘ $10$ — That is $2 \times 5$. An index tells you how many times to multiply, not what to multiply by.
    ✘ $25$ — That is $5^{2}$. The base is the big number at the bottom, so it is $2$ that is raised here.
- p2 (The zero index): What is $3^{0}$?
    ✔ $1$ — Any number except zero raised to the power zero is $1$. That fact becomes one of the two anchors of this lesson.
    ✘ $0$ — A power of zero does not make the answer zero. Dividing $3^{1}$ by $3$ gives $3^{0} = 1$.
    ✘ $3$ — That is $3^{1}$. Stepping the index down one more divides by $3$ again and gives $1$.
- p3 (Negative indices): What is $2^{-3}$?
    ✔ $\frac{1}{8}$ — A negative index means a reciprocal: $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{2^{3}} = \frac{1}{8}$.
    ✘ $-8$ — The minus sign belongs to the index, not to the answer. It turns the power upside down instead of making it negative.
    ✘ $8$ — That is $2^{3}$. The negative index sends it to the bottom of a fraction.

### dx.fm.u1.logarithms-from-indices.post (post) [draft]
- d1 (Evaluate a logarithm from a known power): What is $\log_{2} 64$?
    ✔ $6$ — $2^{6} = 64$, and the logarithm is that index.
    ✘ $8$ [fm.logs.base-and-index-swapped] — That solves $x^{2} = 64$. The base is $2$, so the equation to solve is $2^{x} = 64$.
    ✘ $32$ [fm.logs.unknown-base-divided] — That is $64 \div 2$. A logarithm counts the multiplications, so it is the index rather than a quotient.
- d2 (The anchor values): What is $\log_{9} 1$?
    ✔ $0$ — $9^{0} = 1$, so the logarithm of $1$ is $0$ to every base.
    ✘ $1$ [fm.logs.log-of-one-given-as-one] — That is $\log_{9} 9$. The two anchors are $\log_{a} a = 1$ and $\log_{a} 1 = 0$.
    ✘ $9$ [fm.logs.base-and-index-swapped] — That is the base itself. The logarithm is the index on the base, and here that index is $0$.
- d3 (Recover the number from the index form): If $\log_{4} x = 3$, what is $x$?
    ✔ $64$ — $4^{3} = 64$, which is the index form of the statement.
    ✘ $81$ [fm.logs.base-and-index-swapped] — That is $3^{4}$. The base $4$ is the number being raised, and $3$ is the index.
    ✘ $12$ [fm.logs.unknown-base-divided] — That is $4 \times 3$. The $3$ is an index, so it becomes a power rather than a multiplier.
- d4 (A logarithm below 1): What is $\log_{10} 0.01$?
    ✔ $-2$ — $0.01 = \frac{1}{100} = 10^{-2}$, so the logarithm is $-2$.
    ✘ $2$ [fm.logs.negative-index-sign-dropped] — $10^{2} = 100$, not $0.01$. Numbers below $1$ have negative logarithms.
    ✘ $0.01$ [fm.logs.base-and-index-swapped] — That is the number inside the logarithm repeated. The answer is the index that turns $10$ into it.

## RETRIEVAL PROMPTS
- rp.fm.u1.logarithms-from-indices.01 [draft] (formula) Write the link between an index statement and a logarithm.  →  $a^{x} = n$ is the same statement as $x = \log_{a} n$: the logarithm is the index.
- rp.fm.u1.logarithms-from-indices.02 [draft] (procedure) How do you work out a logarithm such as $\log_{5} 625$ without a calculator?  →  Set it equal to $x$, write the index form $5^{x} = 625$, then count powers of $5$ until you reach $625$; the index you stop at is the logarithm.
- rp.fm.u1.logarithms-from-indices.03 [draft] (qa) What are $\log_{a} a$ and $\log_{a} 1$?  →  $\log_{a} a = 1$ because $a^{1} = a$, and $\log_{a} 1 = 0$ because $a^{0} = 1$.
- rp.fm.u1.logarithms-from-indices.04 [draft] (trap) Why is the logarithm of a number smaller than $1$ negative?  →  A number below $1$ is a reciprocal of a power, and a reciprocal carries a negative index, so the logarithm is negative.
- rp.fm.u1.logarithms-from-indices.05 [draft] (procedure) A question gives $\log_{x} 216 = 3$. What is the first line you write, and what do you do next?  →  Write the index form $x^{3} = 216$, then take the cube root, which gives $x = 6$. Dividing $216$ by $3$ is not the way to undo an index.
