## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.fm.u2.vector-magnitude-and-direction.01 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: A force $\mathbf{F} = (24\mathbf{i} + 7\mathbf{j})$ N acts on a body, where $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ are perpendicular unit vectors, with $mathbf{i}$ along the x-axis.

(i) Calculate the magnitude of $\mathbf{F}$.
(ii) Calculate the angle $\mathbf{F}$ makes with the positive x-axis. Round to 2 decimal places.
(iii) Calculate the angle $\mathbf{F}$ makes with $\mathbf{j}$. Round to 2 decimal places.
  FIG: svg — A single arrow from the origin to the point 24 across and 7 up, with dashed lines showing the two components, the arrow labelled 25 and the angle it makes with the i direction marked as theta.
  1. $|\mathbf{F}| = \sqrt{24^2 + 7^2} = \sqrt{625} = 25$ N   — The two components are at right angles, so the arrow is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle and Pythagoras gives its length. The formula is on the Unit 2 sheet.  (MW1,W1)  WHY: ✔Because the i and j directions are perpendicular, so the components form a right-angled triangle | ✘Because the magnitude of a vector is always the sum of its components | ✘Because the larger component is the length of the arrow
  2. $\theta = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{7}{24} = 16.26^{\circ}$   — The angle with i sits between the arrow and the x-axis, and the side opposite it is the j component while the side beside it is the i component. Opposite over adjacent is the tangent.  (MW1,W1)
  3. $\tan^{-1}\dfrac{24}{7} = 73.74^{\circ}$   — Measuring from j swaps which side is opposite and which is adjacent, so the fraction turns over. Nothing else changes.  (MW1)
  4. Check: $16.26 + 73.74 = 90$   — The two axes are at right angles, so the two angles must add to $90^{\circ}$. It is a five-second check that catches an upside-down fraction.  (W1)
  FINAL: (i) $25$ N  (ii) $16.26^{\circ}$  (iii) $73.74^{\circ}$
  TWIN: A force is $(9\mathbf{i} + 40\mathbf{j})$ N. Calculate its magnitude, in newtons.  → numeric 41 N tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u2.vector-magnitude-and-direction.pre (pre) [verified]
- 01 (Prerequisite: Pythagoras): From an earlier topic: a right-angled triangle has shorter sides 5 and 12. How long is the hypotenuse?
    ✔ $13$ — $25 + 144 = 169$, and $\sqrt{169} = 13$.
    ✘ $17$ — That adds the two sides. Pythagoras squares them first, then roots the total.
    ✘ $169$ — That is the square of the hypotenuse. One more step is needed.
    ✘ $60$ — That multiplies the sides, which gives twice the area rather than a length.
- 02 (Prerequisite: naming the sides of a right-angled triangle): From an earlier topic: in a right-angled triangle, which pair of sides does the tangent of an angle use?
    ✔ Opposite and adjacent — $\tan \theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$.
    ✘ Opposite and hypotenuse — That pair gives the sine.
    ✘ Adjacent and hypotenuse — That pair gives the cosine.
    ✘ The two shorter sides and the hypotenuse — All three together is Pythagoras, which gives a length rather than an angle.
- 03 (Prerequisite: inverse tangent on a calculator): From an earlier topic: $\tan \theta = 1$. What is $\theta$, in degrees?
    ✔ $45$ — $\tan 45^{\circ} = 1$, so $\tan^{-1} 1 = 45^{\circ}$.
    ✘ $90$ — The tangent is not defined at $90^{\circ}$; it grows without limit as the angle approaches it.
    ✘ $1$ — That is the value of the tangent, not the angle. Use the inverse key.
    ✘ $0$ — $\tan 0^{\circ} = 0$, not 1.

### dx.fm.u2.vector-magnitude-and-direction.post (post) [verified]
- 04 (The magnitude formula): What is the magnitude of $(6\mathbf{i} + 8\mathbf{j})$?
    ✔ $10$ — $\sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10$.
    ✘ $100$ [fm.vectors.magnitude-root-omitted] — That is the sum of the squares. The square root is the last step.
    ✘ $14$ [fm.vectors.magnitude-components-added] — That adds the components. They are at right angles, so they cannot simply be added.
    ✘ $8$ [fm.vectors.magnitude-components-added] — That is the larger component. The arrow is longer than either of its components.
- 05 (Which ratio the angle with i uses): Which expression gives the angle between $x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j}$ and $\mathbf{i}$?
    ✔ $\tan^{-1}\dfrac{y}{x}$ — This is the form printed on the Unit 2 formula sheet.
    ✘ $\tan^{-1}\dfrac{x}{y}$ [fm.vectors.direction-ratio-inverted] — That is the angle with $\mathbf{j}$. The two answers add to $90^{\circ}$, so it is a quick one to spot.
    ✘ $\sin^{-1}\dfrac{y}{x}$ [fm.vectors.direction-ratio-inverted] — Sine compares a side with the hypotenuse, and $x$ is not the hypotenuse here.
    ✘ $\tan^{-1}(xy)$ [fm.vectors.direction-ratio-inverted] — The tangent is a ratio of the two sides, so they are divided rather than multiplied.
- 06 (The two angles add to a right angle): A vector makes an angle of $28^{\circ}$ with $\mathbf{i}$. What angle does it make with $\mathbf{j}$?
    ✔ $62^{\circ}$ — The axes are at right angles, so the two angles add to $90^{\circ}$.
    ✘ $28^{\circ}$ [fm.vectors.direction-ratio-inverted] — That would need the vector to lie at $45^{\circ}$, halfway between the axes.
    ✘ $152^{\circ}$ [fm.vectors.direction-ratio-inverted] — That subtracts from $180^{\circ}$, which is the angle on a straight line rather than between the axes.
    ✘ $332^{\circ}$ [fm.vectors.direction-ratio-inverted] — That subtracts from a full turn, which is not what the second axis is.
- 07 (Speed as the magnitude of a velocity): A body has velocity $(3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j})$ m/s. What is its speed?
    ✔ $5$ m/s — Speed is the magnitude of the velocity: $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$.
    ✘ $(3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j})$ m/s [fm.kin.speed-vs-velocity] — That is the velocity itself. A speed is a single number, because it carries no direction.
    ✘ $7$ m/s [fm.vectors.magnitude-components-added] — That adds the components rather than using Pythagoras on them.
    ✘ $25$ m/s [fm.vectors.magnitude-root-omitted] — That is the sum of the squares, with the root not yet taken.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u2.vector-magnitude-and-direction.01 [verified] fm.vectors.direction-ratio-inverted · earned MW1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2025-summer:FM2:Q2
STEM: Séamus is finding the angle that $(5\mathbf{i} + 12\mathbf{j})$ N makes with the positive x-axis. His working:
    1. x = 5, y = 12
  ✘ 2. angle = tan⁻¹(x ÷ y)
    3. angle = tan⁻¹(5 ÷ 12)
    4. angle = 22.62°
  WRONG: Line 2 has the fraction upside down. The Unit 2 sheet prints the angle with $\mathbf{i}$ as $\tan^{-1}\dfrac{y}{x}$, because the $\mathbf{j}$ component is opposite that angle and the $\mathbf{i}$ component is beside it. What line 4 gives, $22.62^{\circ}$, is the angle measured from $\mathbf{j}$ instead.
  FIX: x = 5, y = 12 / angle = tan⁻¹(y ÷ x) / angle = tan⁻¹(12 ÷ 5) / angle = 67.38°
  FEEDBACK: Choosing an inverse tangent is right, and that earns the method mark (M). The check that saves this one takes five seconds: the two angles must add to $90^{\circ}$, and $22.62 + 67.38 = 90$, so the answer written down is the other one of the pair. Sketching the triangle and marking which angle you want first makes it hard to go wrong.
