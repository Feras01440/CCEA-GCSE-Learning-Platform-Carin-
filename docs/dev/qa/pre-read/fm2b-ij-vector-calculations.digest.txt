## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.fm.u2.ij-vector-calculations.01 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: Throughout, $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ are perpendicular unit vectors, with $mathbf{i}$ along the x-axis.

$\mathbf{u} = (5\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j})$ and $\mathbf{v} = (-2\mathbf{i} + 7\mathbf{j})$.

(i) Find $\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}$.
(ii) Find $2\mathbf{u} - 3\mathbf{v}$.
(iii) Calculate $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}|$.
  FIG: svg — A table: adding pairs i terms with i terms and j terms with j terms; subtracting works the same way one column at a time; multiplying by a scalar multiplies every component; and equal vectors give equal i parts and equal j parts, which is two equations.
  1. $\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v} = (5 - 2)\mathbf{i} + (-3 + 7)\mathbf{j} = 3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$   — The $\mathbf{i}$ direction and the $\mathbf{j}$ direction are independent, so nothing crosses between the two columns. Add the $\mathbf{i}$ terms, then add the $\mathbf{j}$ terms.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because a step across and a step up are independent, so each direction is totalled on its own | ✘Because the components must be added and then combined into a single number | ✘Because i and j can be cancelled once both vectors are written down
  2. $2\mathbf{u} = 10\mathbf{i} - 6\mathbf{j}$ and $3\mathbf{v} = -6\mathbf{i} + 21\mathbf{j}$   — A scalar multiplies every component, not just the first one. Write both scaled vectors down before subtracting: it is the step where signs go astray.  (MW1)
  3. $2\mathbf{u} - 3\mathbf{v} = (10 - (-6))\mathbf{i} + (-6 - 21)\mathbf{j} = 16\mathbf{i} - 27\mathbf{j}$   — Subtracting a negative $\mathbf{i}$ component adds to it, which is why the answer's $\mathbf{i}$ term is 16 and not 4. Brackets round each scaled vector keep that visible.  (MW1,W1)
  4. $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5$   — A magnitude is asked for as a single number, so this is the one part of the question whose answer has no $\mathbf{i}$ or $\mathbf{j}$ in it.  (MW1,W1)
  FINAL: (i) $3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$  (ii) $16\mathbf{i} - 27\mathbf{j}$  (iii) $5$
  TWIN: $\mathbf{a} = (7\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j})$ and $\mathbf{b} = (-3\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j})$. Find $\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b}$. Write the vector in i and j form, typed as plain letters (for example 3i + 4j). The two terms may be given in either order.  → algebraic i + 11j (equivalent)

### we.fm.u2.ij-vector-calculations.02 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: Throughout, $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ are perpendicular unit vectors, with $mathbf{i}$ along the x-axis.

Three forces act on a body: $\mathbf{a} = (p\mathbf{i} + 2q\mathbf{j})$ N, $\mathbf{b} = (3q\mathbf{i} - p\mathbf{j})$ N and $\mathbf{c} = (18\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j})$ N.

Given that $3\mathbf{a} - 2\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{c}$, calculate the values of $p$ and $q$.
  FIG: svg — A six-row table of the route through a vector equation with unknowns: substitute with brackets, expand every term, collect the i terms and the j terms separately, equate the i coefficients, equate the j coefficients, then solve the pair of scalar equations.
  1. $3(p\mathbf{i} + 2q\mathbf{j}) - 2(3q\mathbf{i} - p\mathbf{j}) = 18\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j}$   — Substitute with brackets round every vector. The brackets are what stop the $-2$ reaching only the first term inside the second one.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because the scalar outside multiplies every term inside the bracket, signs included | ✘Because brackets are only a presentation choice here | ✘Because the minus sign applies only to the i term
  2. $3p\mathbf{i} + 6q\mathbf{j} - 6q\mathbf{i} + 2p\mathbf{j} = 18\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j}$   — Expand each bracket fully. $-2 \times -p\mathbf{j}$ is $+2p\mathbf{j}$: two negatives give a plus.  (MW1)
  3. Equating $\mathbf{i}$: $3p - 6q = 18$, so $p - 2q = 6$.   — Two vectors are equal only when both components match, so one vector equation is two scalar equations. Drop the $\mathbf{i}$ once it has done its job of telling you which terms to gather.  (MW1)
  4. Equating $\mathbf{j}$: $6q + 2p = 2$, so $p + 3q = 1$.   — The same gathering for the $\mathbf{j}$ terms. These two equations carry no $\mathbf{i}$ and no $\mathbf{j}$: they are ordinary simultaneous equations now.  (MW1)
  5. Subtracting: $5q = -5$, so $q = -1$, and then $p = 4$.   — Solve the pair by elimination, then substitute back into either equation to find the other unknown. Check both in the equation you did not use.  (W1)
  FINAL: $p = 4$, $q = -1$
  TWIN: $\mathbf{e} = (x\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j})$ and $\mathbf{f} = (3\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j})$. Given that $\mathbf{e} + \mathbf{f} = (8\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j})$, find the values of $x$ and $y$. Write the answer as $x = \ldots$, $y = \ldots$  → algebraic (5, -3) (equivalent)

### we.fm.u2.ij-vector-calculations.03 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: Throughout, $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ are perpendicular unit vectors, with $mathbf{i}$ along the x-axis, and distances are in metres.

Relative to an origin $O$, the point $A$ has position vector $(3\mathbf{i} + 8\mathbf{j})$ m and the point $B$ has position vector $(11\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j})$ m.

(i) Find the displacement vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$.
(ii) Calculate the distance $AB$.
  FIG: svg — Two points A at 3 across and 8 up and B at 11 across and 2 up, each joined to the origin by a dashed position vector a and b, with a solid arrow from A to B labelled AB.
  1. $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$   — To get from $A$ to $B$ you can go back to the origin and out again, which is $-\mathbf{a}$ then $+\mathbf{b}$. So the displacement is the finish's position vector minus the start's.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because the journey from A to B is minus a, then plus b | ✘Because A comes first in the name, so a comes first in the subtraction | ✘Because a displacement is always the larger vector minus the smaller
  2. $= (11\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j}) - (3\mathbf{i} + 8\mathbf{j}) = 8\mathbf{i} - 6\mathbf{j}$ m   — Subtract one column at a time, brackets round each vector. This part asks for a vector, so the answer keeps its $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$.  (W1)
  3. $AB = \sqrt{8^2 + (-6)^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$ m   — A distance is a length, so it is the magnitude of the displacement. Squaring removes the minus sign, which is why the direction of travel does not matter here.  (MW1,W1)
  FINAL: (i) $8\mathbf{i} - 6\mathbf{j}$ m  (ii) $10$ m
  TWIN: The point $C$ has position vector $(\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j})$ m and the point $D$ has position vector $(13\mathbf{i} + 9\mathbf{j})$ m. Find the displacement vector $\overrightarrow{CD}$. Write the vector in i and j form, typed as plain letters (for example 3i + 4j). The two terms may be given in either order.  → algebraic 12i + 5j (equivalent)

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u2.ij-vector-calculations.pre (pre) [verified]
- 01 (Prerequisite: expanding a bracket with a negative outside): From an earlier topic: expand $-2(3x - 5)$.
    ✔ $-6x + 10$ — $-2 \times 3x = -6x$ and $-2 \times -5 = +10$.
    ✘ $-6x - 10$ — The second term is two negatives multiplied, which gives a plus.
    ✘ $6x - 10$ — The first term takes the minus sign too: every term inside is multiplied.
    ✘ $-6x - 5$ — The $-5$ has not been multiplied at all. The $-2$ reaches both terms.
- 02 (Prerequisite: solving simultaneous equations): From an earlier topic: solve $x + 2y = 7$ and $x - y = 1$.
    ✔ $x = 3$, $y = 2$ — Subtracting gives $3y = 6$, so $y = 2$ and then $x = 3$.
    ✘ $x = 2$, $y = 3$ — The two values are the right pair but the wrong way round; check them in the second equation.
    ✘ $x = 5$, $y = 1$ — That satisfies the second equation but not the first.
    ✘ $x = 1$, $y = 3$ — That satisfies the first equation but not the second. Both must hold.
- 03 (Prerequisite: collecting like terms): From an earlier topic: simplify $4a + 3b - 6a + b$.
    ✔ $-2a + 4b$ — $4a - 6a = -2a$ and $3b + b = 4b$; the two letters stay apart.
    ✘ $2a + 4b$ — $4 - 6$ is $-2$, not $2$.
    ✘ $2ab$ — Unlike terms cannot be combined into one term.
    ✘ $-2a + 3b$ — The lone $b$ counts as $1b$, so the $b$ terms total $4b$.
- 04 (Prerequisite: Pythagoras): From an earlier topic: a right-angled triangle has shorter sides 9 and 12. How long is the hypotenuse?
    ✔ $15$ — $81 + 144 = 225$ and $\sqrt{225} = 15$.
    ✘ $21$ — That adds the two sides. Pythagoras squares them first.
    ✘ $225$ — That is the square of the hypotenuse; the root is the last step.
    ✘ $108$ — That multiplies the sides, which is twice the area rather than a length.

### dx.fm.u2.ij-vector-calculations.post (post) [verified]
- 05 (Adding vectors in component form): What is $(5\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j}) + (-2\mathbf{i} + 7\mathbf{j})$?
    ✔ $3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$ — $5 - 2 = 3$ and $-3 + 7 = 4$; the two directions are totalled separately.
    ✘ $7\mathbf{i} - 10\mathbf{j}$ [fm.vectors.subtract-instead-of-add] — That subtracts the second vector instead of adding it.
    ✘ $3\mathbf{i} - 10\mathbf{j}$ [fm.vectors.component-sign-dropped] — The i terms were added and the j terms subtracted. One operation applies to both columns.
    ✘ $7$ [fm.vectors.magnitude-when-vector-asked] — That combines everything into one number. The question asks for a vector, so it keeps its i and j.
- 06 (Scalar multiples): What is $3(4\mathbf{i} - 5\mathbf{j})$?
    ✔ $12\mathbf{i} - 15\mathbf{j}$ — Every component is multiplied by 3, sign included.
    ✘ $12\mathbf{i} - 5\mathbf{j}$ [fm.vectors.scalar-multiple-partial] — Only the i component has been multiplied. The scalar reaches both.
    ✘ $12\mathbf{i} + 15\mathbf{j}$ [fm.vectors.component-sign-dropped] — The j component is negative, and multiplying by a positive 3 keeps it negative.
    ✘ $7\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j}$ [fm.vectors.scalar-multiple-partial] — That adds 3 to each component. A scalar multiple multiplies.
- 07 (Equal vectors give two equations): $x\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j} = 8\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j}$. What follows?
    ✔ $x = 8$ and $y = 5$ — Equal vectors have equal i parts and equal j parts, which is two separate equations.
    ✘ $x + 5 = 8 + y$ [fm.vectors.coefficients-not-equated] — That mixes the two directions into one equation. They are independent, so they are equated separately.
    ✘ $x = 5$ and $y = 8$ [fm.vectors.coefficients-not-equated] — The i part is equated with the i part: $x$ with 8, and 5 with $y$.
    ✘ $x\mathbf{i} = 8\mathbf{i}$, so $x\mathbf{i} - 8\mathbf{i} = 0\mathbf{i}$ [fm.vectors.i-j-left-in-equations] — The i is carried into the scalar equation, where it has no job left to do. Drop it once the terms are gathered.
- 08 (Displacement from position vectors): $A$ has position vector $(3\mathbf{i} + 8\mathbf{j})$ and $B$ has position vector $(11\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j})$. What is $\overrightarrow{AB}$?
    ✔ $8\mathbf{i} - 6\mathbf{j}$ — $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$: the finish minus the start.
    ✘ $-8\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j}$ [fm.vectors.position-difference-reversed] — That is $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$, the journey from B to A.
    ✘ $14\mathbf{i} + 10\mathbf{j}$ [fm.vectors.subtract-instead-of-add] — That adds the two position vectors, which lands somewhere neither point is.
    ✘ $10$ [fm.vectors.magnitude-when-vector-asked] — That is the distance $AB$. The question asks for the vector, so the i and j stay.
- 09 (Expanding with a negative scalar): What is $-3 \times -3x$?
    ✔ $9x$ — Two negatives multiply to a positive, and $3 \times 3 = 9$.
    ✘ $-9x$ [fm.vectors.component-sign-dropped] — One of the minus signs has been dropped. Two of them cancel.
    ✘ $-6x$ [fm.vectors.component-sign-dropped] — That adds the two threes instead of multiplying them, and keeps a minus.
    ✘ $6x$ [fm.vectors.component-sign-dropped] — The threes are multiplied, not added, so the coefficient is 9.
- 10 (Vector answers against scalar answers): A part asks for the resultant force in vector form. Which answer can earn the marks?
    ✔ $(6\mathbf{i} + 8\mathbf{j})$ N — A vector answer keeps both components, so the reader knows the size and the direction.
    ✘ $10$ N [fm.vectors.magnitude-when-vector-asked] — That is the magnitude of the resultant. It answers a different question from the one asked.
    ✘ $53.13^{\circ}$ [fm.vectors.magnitude-when-vector-asked] — That is the direction on its own, and it is not what was asked for either.
    ✘ $14$ N [fm.vectors.magnitude-components-added] — That adds the components into one number, which is neither the vector nor its magnitude.
- 11 (Newton's Second Law in vectors): A force of $(15\mathbf{i} - 20\mathbf{j})$ N acts on a mass of 5 kg. What is the acceleration?
    ✔ $(3\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{j})$ m/s² — $\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{F} \div m$, and the division reaches both components.
    ✘ $(75\mathbf{i} - 100\mathbf{j})$ m/s² [fm.vectors.scalar-multiple-partial] — That multiplies by the mass. $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$, so the mass divides the force here.
    ✘ $(3\mathbf{i} - 20\mathbf{j})$ m/s² [fm.vectors.scalar-multiple-partial] — Only the i component has been divided. Every component is divided by the mass.
    ✘ $5$ m/s² [fm.vectors.magnitude-when-vector-asked] — That gives a single number where a vector was asked for; the magnitude of the acceleration is not the same thing.
- 12 (Constant acceleration in vector form): A body starts from rest with acceleration $(2\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j})$ m/s². What is its displacement after 2 seconds?
    ✔ $(4\mathbf{i} + 12\mathbf{j})$ m — $\mathbf{s} = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}t^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times (2\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j})$.
    ✘ $(2\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j})$ m [fm.kin.t-not-squared] — The $t$ has not been squared: $\tfrac{1}{2}at^2$ with $t = 2$ multiplies by 2, not by 1.
    ✘ $(8\mathbf{i} + 24\mathbf{j})$ m [fm.kin.t-not-squared] — The one half has been left out of $\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}t^2$.
    ✘ $(4\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j})$ m [fm.vectors.scalar-multiple-partial] — Only the i component has been worked through. Every component gets the same treatment.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u2.ij-vector-calculations.01 [verified] fm.vectors.subtract-instead-of-add · earned MW1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2022-summer:FM2:Q2
STEM: Niamh is finding the resultant of two forces $(6\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j})$ N and $(-5\mathbf{i} + 9\mathbf{j})$ N. Her working:
    1. Resultant = the vector sum of the forces
    2. i: 6 - 5 = 11
  ✘ 3. j: -2 - 9 = -11
    4. Resultant = 11i - 11j N
  WRONG: Line 3 subtracts the second force's $\mathbf{j}$ component where line 1 says the forces are added. The $\mathbf{i}$ line is right: -5 is already negative, so adding it takes 5 away and gives 1. The $\mathbf{j}$ line should add 9 to -2 and give 7.
  FIX: Resultant = the vector sum of the forces / i: 6 - 5 = 1 / j: -2 + 9 = 7 / Resultant = i + 7j N
  FEEDBACK: Naming the resultant as the vector sum and keeping the two columns apart is the method, and it earns the method mark (M). One operation applies to the whole vector, so if the i line adds, the j line adds too. Writing the two vectors one above the other before you start makes a stray sign very hard to miss.

### ftm.fm.u2.ij-vector-calculations.02 [verified] fm.vectors.i-j-left-in-equations · earned MW1,MW1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2024-summer:FM2:Q2
STEM: Dara is solving $x\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j} = 8\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j}$ for $x$ and $y$. His working:
    1. Two vectors are equal, so their components match
    2. x i + 5 j - 8 i - y j = 0
    3. x i - 8 i = 0 and 5 j - y j = 0, so x i = 8 i and 5 j = y j
  ✘ 4. x i = 8 i, so x = 8 i and y = 5 j
  WRONG: Line 4 keeps the $\mathbf{i}$ and the $\mathbf{j}$ inside the answers.   FIX: Two vectors are equal, so their components match / Equating the i coefficients: x = 8 / Equating the j coefficients: 5 = y / x = 8 and y = 5
  FEEDBACK: The reasoning is right the whole way down, and equating the two pairs of coefficients earns the method marks (M). $x$ is a number of newtons, or metres, and a number never carries an $\mathbf{i}$. Examiners report this every year, so it is worth one last look before the answer line.
