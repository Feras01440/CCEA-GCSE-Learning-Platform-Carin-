## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.science.c2.c2-measuring-rates.01 [verified] paper C2 calc
STEM: The graph shows the volume of carbon dioxide collected when 2.0 g of marble chips reacted with 40 cm³ of dilute hydrochloric acid.
(a) Use the graph to find the total volume of gas produced. [1]
(b) Use the graph to find the time at which the reaction finished. [1]
(c) Calculate the mean rate of reaction for the whole reaction, in cm³/s. Give your answer to two decimal places. [3]
  FIG: svg — A curve of the volume of carbon dioxide against time: steep at the start, bending over and running flat from fifty seconds onwards.
  1. Follow the curve to the right until it stops rising. It runs flat at 54 cm³, so the total volume of gas is 54 cm³.   — Read the total off the flat part of the curve. Adding the volumes in the table counts the same gas over and over, and Summer 2025's report names it as a real error.  (P1)  WHY: ✔Because the syringe reading is a running total already, so the last one is the whole lot | ✘Because each reading is the gas made since the last reading | ✘Because the table stops before the reaction does
  2. The curve becomes flat at 50 s, so the reaction finished at 50 s.   — Flat means nothing more is happening. The table runs on to 60 s, but the reaction was over before that.  (P2)
  3. rate = change in volume ÷ time taken   — Write the rule before the numbers. The rule is what tells you which number goes on top.  (P3)
  4. rate = 54 ÷ 50   — Substitute the time the reaction took, 50 s, not the last time in the table. Dividing by 60 s gives 0.9 cm³/s and loses the answer mark.  (P4)
  5. rate = 1.08 cm³/s   — Two decimal places because the question said so, and the unit is the volume unit over seconds.  (P5)
  FINAL: (a) 54 cm³. (b) 50 s. (c) rate = change ÷ time = 54 ÷ 50 = 1.08 cm³/s.
  TWIN: The same reaction is followed on a balance instead. After 200 s the flask has lost 3.60 g and the reading has stopped falling. Calculate the mean rate of reaction in g/s, to three decimal places.  → numeric 0.018 grams per second tol={"type":"dp","places":3} forms=decimal

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.c2.c2-measuring-rates.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Prerequisite from C1 — reading units; you are not expected to have met rates yet): Which of these is a unit of volume?
    ✔ cm³ — Cubic centimetres. Every gas-syringe reading in this lesson is in cm³.
    ✘ g [sci.quant.units-not-converted] — Grams measure mass. Both turn up in this topic, so it is worth separating them before you start.
    ✘ s [sci.quant.units-not-converted] — Seconds measure time. The rate unit puts a volume over a time, so you need to be able to tell them apart.
- p2 (Prerequisite from mathematics — a rate is an amount divided by a time): A car travels 90 km in 3 hours. What is its average speed?
    ✔ 30 km/h — 90 ÷ 3 = 30. Every rate in chemistry is built the same way: the change divided by the time.
    ✘ 270 km/h [sci.quant.divided-instead-of-multiplied] — That multiplies. A rate divides the amount by the time, so 90 ÷ 3 = 30 km/h.
    ✘ 0.033 km/h [sci.rates.rate-inverted] — That is the time divided by the distance, which is the fraction upside down. Section 1 of the lesson is about which number goes on top.
- p3 (Prerequisite from C1 §1.8 — reactions of acids with carbonates): Marble chips are calcium carbonate. Which gas is given off when they react with dilute hydrochloric acid?
    ✔ carbon dioxide — Acid plus a carbonate always gives a salt, water and carbon dioxide. That gas is what every method in this lesson is following.
    ✘ hydrogen [sci.metals.magnesium-steam-products] — Hydrogen comes from an acid with a metal. With a carbonate the gas is carbon dioxide, and limewater turning milky is the test.
    ✘ oxygen [sci.gases.oxygen-test-lit-splint] — Oxygen comes from hydrogen peroxide with a catalyst, which is one of the other reactions on this specification. A carbonate gives carbon dioxide.

### dx.science.c2.c2-measuring-rates.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (Read a total from a set of syringe readings): A gas syringe reads 0, 22, 36, 46, 52, 54 and 54 cm³ at 10 s intervals. What total volume of gas was produced?
    ✔ 54 cm³ — The readings are a running total, so the last one is the whole amount. On a graph it is the height of the flat stretch.
    ✘ 264 cm³ [sci.rates.total-volume-summed] — That adds every reading together, counting the same gas six times over. Summer 2025's Booklet B report names this exact error.
    ✘ 18 cm³ [sci.rates.start-value-not-subtracted] — That is the gas made after the first 20 s only. The total is everything collected from the start.
    ✘ 22 cm³ [sci.practical.curve-read-from-wrong-line] — That is the reading after 10 s, not the total. Follow the readings to the end, where they stop changing.
- 02 (Apply rate = change ÷ time): A reaction produces 54 cm³ of gas and the curve goes flat at 50 s. What is the mean rate of reaction?
    ✔ 1.08 cm³/s — 54 ÷ 50 = 1.08. Volume on top, time underneath, unit cm³/s.
    ✘ 0.93 cm³/s [sci.rates.rate-inverted] — That is the time divided by the volume, the fraction upside down. Rate is always the change on top.
    ✘ 0.9 cm³/s [sci.rates.time-not-divided] — That divides by the last time printed in the table. The reaction was over before then, so use the time it actually took.
    ✘ 2700 cm³/s [sci.quant.divided-instead-of-multiplied] — That multiplies instead of dividing. A rate is an amount per second, so the time goes underneath.
- 03 (Name the apparatus for a method): Which apparatus would you use to measure the volume of carbon dioxide given off in a reaction?
    ✔ a gas syringe — A gas syringe, or an inverted measuring cylinder over water. Keep the word gas: 'syringe' alone lost marks in Summer 2025.
    ✘ a top-pan balance [sci.practical.apparatus-names-unknown] — The balance follows the mass lost, which is the other method. It never tells you a volume.
    ✘ a thermometer [sci.practical.apparatus-names-unknown] — A thermometer is for the temperature-change experiments in the energy topic, not for measuring a gas.
    ✘ a conical flask [sci.practical.apparatus-names-unknown] — The reaction happens in the conical flask, but the flask does not measure anything. The gas syringe does.
- 04 (Apply rate = 1 ÷ time): In the disappearing-cross experiment the cross was hidden after 32 s. Using rate = 1 ÷ time, what is the rate?
    ✔ 0.031 s⁻¹ — 1 ÷ 32 = 0.031 s⁻¹. A longer time gives a smaller rate, which is what you want it to do.
    ✘ 32 s⁻¹ [sci.rates.rate-inverted] — That is the time itself, not one divided by it. The rule turns the time upside down on purpose.
    ✘ 1.875 s⁻¹ [sci.quant.units-not-converted] — That turns the time into minutes first, so the answer is per minute. The unit asked for is s⁻¹, so keep the time in seconds.
    ✘ 0.031 s [sci.practical.gradient-unit] — The number is right but the unit is not. One divided by a time in seconds gives a rate per second, written s⁻¹, and CCEA marks that unit separately.
- 05 (Sketch the effect of a change on a rate curve): A reaction between 2.0 g of marble chips and excess acid gives 54 cm³ of gas. It is repeated with the same 2.0 g crushed to a powder. What does the new curve do?
    ✔ it is steeper and levels off at 54 cm³ — Steeper because it is faster, and the same height because the same mass of carbonate can only make the same amount of gas.
    ✘ it is steeper and levels off above 54 cm³ [sci.rates.sketch-wrong-endpoint] — A faster reaction makes the same gas sooner, not more of it. Summer 2025 recorded 2 of 3 marks on exactly this sketch because the line did not level off at the original value.
    ✘ it is less steep and levels off at 54 cm³ [sci.practical.curve-read-from-wrong-line] — Powder has a much larger surface area, so the reaction is faster and the curve is steeper, not shallower.
    ✘ it is steeper and never levels off [sci.rates.sketch-wrong-endpoint] — Every curve levels off once a reactant runs out. With 2.0 g of carbonate that happens at the same height as before.
- 06 (Head a results table properly): A results table has the headings 'Time' and 'Volume of gas'. What is missing?
    ✔ the units, / s and / cm³ — Units belong in the heading, and the axis labels are copied straight from there. The missing x-axis label was the commonest lost mark in Summer 2025's Booklet B.
    ✘ a column for the rate [sci.practical.axis-unit-omitted] — The rate is worked out afterwards from the readings; it is not something you record at the bench. The units are what the headings are missing.
    ✘ nothing, the table is complete [sci.practical.axis-unit-omitted] — A heading without a unit does not say what the numbers are. Write 'Time / s' and 'Volume of gas / cm³'.
    ✘ a title above the table [sci.practical.axis-unit-omitted] — A title is never a mark. The unit in each heading is.
