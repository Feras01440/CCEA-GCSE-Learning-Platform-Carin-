## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.fm.u1.differentiation-integer-powers.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$ for the curve $y = 5x^{3} + 7x - 9 - \frac{4}{x^{2}}$
  FIG: svg — A table of the four terms of y = 5x cubed minus 4 over x squared plus 7x minus 9, each rewritten and then differentiated.
  1. Rewrite the fraction as a power: $-\frac{4}{x^{2}} = -4x^{-2}$.   — The rule is written for powers of x, so anything under a line has to come up as a negative index first. Nothing has changed except the way it is written.  (MW1)
  2. Differentiate $5x^{3}$: multiply by 3 and step the power down to give $15x^{2}$.   — Take one term at a time and finish it before looking at the next. Most lost marks in this topic come from doing four terms at once in your head.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because 5 times 3 is 15 and 3 steps down to 2 | ✘Because 5 plus 3 is 8 | ✘Because the power always becomes 2
  3. Differentiate $-4x^{-2}$: $-4 \times -2 = 8$ and the index steps down from $-2$ to $-3$, giving $8x^{-3}$.   — Two negatives meet here, one in the coefficient and one in the power, and they do different jobs. The product of the signs sets the new coefficient; the index only ever moves down.  (W1)
  4. Differentiate $7x$ to get $7$, and differentiate $-9$ to get $0$.   — A straight line contributes its own gradient, and a constant contributes nothing, because a horizontal graph never rises.  (W1)
  5. Collect the four pieces: $\frac{dy}{dx} = 15x^{2} + 7 + \frac{8}{x^{3}}$.   — Putting the negative power back over the line is optional, and it is how CCEA prints the answer, so it is the safer habit.  (W2)
  6. Differentiate that answer again: $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = 30x - \frac{24}{x^{4}}$.   — Start from the first derivative, never from y. Here 15x squared gives 30x, 8x to the minus 3 gives minus 24x to the minus 4, and the constant 7 disappears.  (MW2)
  FINAL: $\frac{dy}{dx} = 15x^{2} + 7 + \frac{8}{x^{3}}$ and $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = 30x - \frac{24}{x^{4}}$
  TWIN: Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for the curve $y = 3x^{5} + 6x - 2 - \frac{10}{x^{1}}$  → algebraic 15x^{4} + 6 + \frac{10}{x^{2}} (equivalent)

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.differentiation-integer-powers.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Move a power from under the line to a negative index): Three quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new method, so answer from what you already know.
Write $\frac{3}{x^{2}}$ using a single power of $x$.
    ✔ $3x^{-2}$ — A power under the line changes sign when it comes up. That single rewrite is what lets the power rule reach the term.
    ✘ $3x^{2}$ [fm.calc.index-moved-to-numerator] — Moving a term to the top line also changes the sign of its index, so the 2 becomes $-2$.
    ✘ $-3x^{2}$ [fm.calc.index-moved-to-numerator] — The sign that changes belongs to the index, not to the coefficient. The 3 stays positive.
- p2 (Multiply two negative numbers): What is $-4 \times -2$?
    ✔ $8$ — Two negatives multiply to a positive. This exact product appears when a negative coefficient meets a negative power.
    ✘ $-8$ [fm.calc.negative-power-differentiation] — A negative times a negative is positive. Carrying a stray minus sign here changes the whole derivative.
    ✘ $-6$ [fm.calc.negative-power-differentiation] — That is $-4 + -2$. The rule multiplies the coefficient by the power.
- p3 (Step down from a negative number): Which number is one **less** than $-2$?
    ✔ $-3$ — Down from $-2$ is $-3$. The index in a derivative always steps down, whichever side of zero it starts on.
    ✘ $-1$ [fm.calc.negative-power-differentiation] — Moving towards zero is going up. One less than $-2$ is $-3$.
    ✘ $2$ [fm.calc.negative-power-differentiation] — That is $-2$ with the sign removed. Stepping down keeps the sign and makes the number smaller.

### dx.fm.u1.differentiation-integer-powers.post (post) [verified]
- q1 (Apply the power rule to a single positive power): Differentiate $y = 6x^{4}$.
    ✔ $24x^{3}$ — Multiply by 4 to get 24, then step the power down to 3. Both moves, in order.
    ✘ $24x^{4}$ [fm.calc.power-not-reduced] — The coefficient is right. The power still has to step down by one, from 4 to 3.
    ✘ $6x^{3}$ [fm.calc.coefficient-not-multiplied] — The power stepped down correctly. The coefficient also has to be multiplied by the power it came from, so 6 becomes 24.
- q2 (Differentiate a quadratic, constant included): Differentiate $y = 9x^{2} - 6x + 4$.
    ✔ $18x - 6$ — The constant goes to zero and the term in x leaves its own coefficient behind.
    ✘ $18x - 2$ [fm.calc.constant-term-kept] — The $+ 4$ has no slope, so it differentiates to zero rather than being copied down.
    ✘ $18x$ [fm.calc.linear-term-derivative-omitted] — The $-6x$ differentiates to $-6$, not to nothing. Only a plain number disappears.
- q3 (Differentiate a term with a negative power): Differentiate $y = \frac{9}{x^{1}}$.
    ✔ $-\frac{9}{x^{2}}$ — Written as $9x^{-1}$, it differentiates to $-9x^{-2}$, which is $-\frac{9}{x^{2}}$.
    ✘ $9$ [fm.calc.index-moved-to-numerator] — Bringing the $x$ to the top line without changing the sign of its index turns the term into $9x$, which is a different expression.
    ✘ $\frac{9}{x^{2}}$ [fm.calc.negative-power-differentiation] — The index is right. Multiplying 9 by $-1$ makes the coefficient negative.
- q4 (Differentiate twice): $y = 6x^{4} - 3x^{2}$. Find $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$.
    ✔ $72x^{2} - 6$ — Differentiate to $24x^{3} - 6x$, then differentiate that. The constant that appears on the way disappears at the next step.
    ✘ $24x^{3} - 6x$ [fm.calc.second-derivative-stops-at-first] — That is $\frac{dy}{dx}$. The second derivative asks you to differentiate it once more.
    ✘ $72x^{3} - 6x$ [fm.calc.power-not-reduced] — The coefficients are right for the second step. The powers still have to step down, so $x^{3}$ becomes $x^{2}$.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u1.differentiation-integer-powers.01 [verified] fm.calc.negative-power-differentiation · earned MW1,M1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2025-summer:FM1:Q8
STEM: Orla was asked to find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for $y = 4x^{3} + 7 - \frac{6}{x^{2}}$. Her working:
    1. y = 4x^3 + 7 - 6/x^2
    2. y = 4x^3 - 6x^-2 + 7
    3. 4x^3 differentiates to 12x^2
  ✘ 4. -6x^-2 differentiates to -12x^-3
    5. dy/dx = 12x^2 - 12/x^3
  WRONG: Line 2 is exactly right: the fraction is rewritten as a negative index before anything else happens, which is the step most candidates miss.
Line 4 has the index right as well. It is the coefficient that slipped: $-6$ is multiplied by the power $-2$, and a negative times a negative is positive, so the term is $+12x^{-3}$.
Because the rest of the line looks orderly, nothing on the page announces the error.
  FIX: -6x^-2 differentiates to (-6) x (-2) x^-3 = +12x^-3 / dy/dx = 12x^2 + 12/x^3
  FEEDBACK: The rewrite and the index are both right, so the method marks stand. Write the two signs down separately before you multiply them: the coefficient carries one, the power carries the other, and only their product reaches the answer. The finished derivative is $\frac{dy}{dx} = 12x^{2} + \frac{12}{x^{3}}$.

## RETRIEVAL PROMPTS
- rp.fm.u1.differentiation-integer-powers.01 [verified] (formula) State the rule for differentiating $ax^{n}$.  →  Multiply the coefficient by the power and step the power down by one: $nax^{n-1}$.
- rp.fm.u1.differentiation-integer-powers.02 [verified] (procedure) What must you do to a term such as $\frac{4}{x^{2}}$ before differentiating it?  →  Rewrite it as $4x^{-2}$, so that the power rule can reach it.
- rp.fm.u1.differentiation-integer-powers.03 [verified] (trap) A term has index $-2$. What is the index after differentiating?  →  $-3$: the index always steps down, whichever side of zero it starts on.
- rp.fm.u1.differentiation-integer-powers.04 [verified] (qa) What is the derivative of a constant, and why?  →  Zero, because the graph of a constant is a horizontal line and a horizontal line has no slope.
- rp.fm.u1.differentiation-integer-powers.05 [verified] (definition) What does $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$ mean, and how do you find it?  →  It is the derivative of the derivative: differentiate $y$, then differentiate that answer again.
