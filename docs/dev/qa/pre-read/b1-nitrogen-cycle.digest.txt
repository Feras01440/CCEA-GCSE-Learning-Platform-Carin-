## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.science.b1.b1-nitrogen-cycle.01 [verified] paper B1 calc
STEM: A nitrogen cycle diagram has four arrows lettered W, X, Y and Z.
W: nitrogen gas → nitrates.  X: protein in dead material → ammonium compounds.
Y: ammonium compounds → nitrates.  Z: nitrates → nitrogen gas.
Name the process at each arrow. [4]
  FIG: svg — The nitrogen cycle as seven boxes joined by eight labelled arrows, each arrow carrying its process and the bacteria that do it.
  1. W is nitrogen fixation.   — Anything that takes nitrogen out of the air is fixation. This is one of the two arrows Summer 2025's candidates did know.  (P1)
  2. X is decomposition.   — Dead material breaking down is decomposition, every time. This is one of the two middle arrows the report says were not identified.  (P2)
  3. Y is nitrification.   — Ammonium turning into nitrate is nitrification — the name tells you the product.  (P3)
  4. Z is denitrification.   — Nitrate turning back into nitrogen gas is denitrification. De- means taking away, and what is taken away is the nitrate.  (P4)
  FINAL: W nitrogen fixation; X decomposition; Y nitrification; Z denitrification.
  TWIN: The same diagram is relettered. Name the process that turns urea in animal urine into ammonium compounds, and the process that returns nitrogen to the air.  → text accepted=["urea is turned into ammonium compounds by decomposition, and nitrogen is returned to the air by denitrification"] keyWords=[{"any":["decomposition","decay"],"marks":1},{"any":["denitrification"],"marks":1}]

### we.science.b1.b1-nitrogen-cycle.02 [verified] paper B1 calc
STEM: One corner of a field floods every winter. Soil nitrate there is 11 mg per kg, while the well-drained part of the same field has 42 mg per kg.
Explain why the waterlogged corner has less nitrate. [3]
  FIG: svg — Two soil columns, one well drained with air in the spaces between grains and one waterlogged with those spaces full of water.
  1. The waterlogged soil has its air spaces full of water, so there is little or no oxygen — it is anaerobic.   — Start with the condition, not the bacteria. Every later point depends on the oxygen having gone.  (P1)
  2. Denitrifying bacteria are anaerobic, so they multiply and turn nitrates into nitrogen gas, which is lost to the air.   — Name the bacteria AND what they do to the nitrate. Naming the process alone is what March 2026's report says lost marks.  (P2)
  3. Nitrifying and nitrogen-fixing bacteria are aerobic, so they are less active and less nitrate is made.   — The second half of the answer: nitrate is not only being destroyed, it is also not being replaced.  (P3)
  FINAL: In the waterlogged corner the air spaces in the soil are full of water, so there is little or no oxygen and conditions are anaerobic. Denitrifying bacteria are anaerobic, so they multiply and convert nitrates into nitrogen gas, which escapes into the air. At the same time the nitrifying and nitrogen-fixing bacteria are aerobic and become far less active, so less new nitrate is made. Both effects together leave only 11 mg per kg instead of 42.
  TWIN: A farmer digs drainage channels across the flooded corner. Explain how this would raise the soil nitrate again.  → text accepted=["draining lets air back into the spaces between the soil grains so there is oxygen again, the anaerobic denitrifying bacteria become less active so less nitrate is lost as nitrogen gas, and the aerobic nitrifying and nitrogen-fixing bacteria become more active so more nitrate is made"] keyWords=[{"any":["oxygen","air spaces"],"marks":1},{"any":["denitrifying"],"marks":1},{"any":["nitrifying","nitrogen-fixing"],"marks":1}]

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.b1.b1-nitrogen-cycle (pre) [verified]
- 01 (Name the fixation arrow): Which process changes nitrogen gas from the air into nitrates?
    ✔ nitrogen fixation — Fixation: taking the gas out of the air and locking it into a compound plants can use.
    ✘ denitrification [sci.nitrogen.fixation-reversed] — Denitrification runs the other way: nitrates back into nitrogen gas.
    ✘ nitrification [sci.nitrogen.nitrifying-denitrifying-swapped] — Nitrification starts from ammonium compounds in the soil, not from the gas in the air.
    ✘ decomposition [sci.nitrogen.middle-processes-unknown] — Decomposition starts from dead material and produces ammonium compounds.
- 02 (Bacteria, not process): A question asks for the TYPE OF BACTERIA that turn ammonium compounds into nitrates. Which answer scores?
    ✔ nitrifying bacteria — The type of bacteria, as asked. March 2026 examiners reported marks lost for giving the process name instead.
    ✘ nitrification [sci.nitrogen.process-named-not-bacteria] — That is the process. The question asked which bacteria carry it out: nitrifying bacteria.
    ✘ denitrifying bacteria [sci.nitrogen.nitrifying-denitrifying-swapped] — Denitrifying bacteria destroy nitrate. Nitrifying bacteria make it.
    ✘ nitrogen-fixing bacteria [sci.nitrogen.fixation-reversed] — Nitrogen-fixing bacteria work on nitrogen gas from the air, not on ammonium compounds.
- 03 (The middle of the cycle): Which two processes were the ones Summer 2025's candidates could NOT identify on a cycle diagram?
    ✔ decomposition and denitrification — The two middle arrows. Fixation and nitrification were widely known; these two were not.
    ✘ nitrogen fixation and nitrification [sci.nitrogen.middle-processes-unknown] — Those were the two most candidates did get. The marks were lost on the other two.
    ✘ photosynthesis and respiration [sci.nitrogen.middle-processes-unknown] — Those belong to the carbon cycle, not the nitrogen cycle.
    ✘ absorption and feeding [sci.nitrogen.middle-processes-unknown] — Those move nitrogen between organisms, but they are not the four microbial processes the outcome lists.
- 04 (The waterlogging chain): Why does a waterlogged soil contain less nitrate?
    ✔ there is no oxygen, so anaerobic denitrifying bacteria thrive and convert nitrate to nitrogen gas — The chain the mark scheme wants: no air spaces, no oxygen, denitrifying bacteria multiply, nitrate leaves as gas.
    ✘ the nitrifying bacteria run in reverse and turn nitrate back into ammonium [sci.nitrogen.nitrifying-denitrifying-swapped] — Nitrifying bacteria only work one way. It is the denitrifying bacteria that remove nitrate.
    ✘ the plants take up more nitrate when the soil is wet [sci.nitrogen.waterlogged-soil-effect] — Waterlogged roots cannot respire properly, so uptake falls rather than rises. The loss is caused by bacteria.
    ✘ nitrogen gas dissolves in the water and blocks the bacteria [sci.nitrogen.waterlogged-soil-effect] — Nothing is blocked. What changes is which bacteria can respire: the anaerobic ones take over.
- 05 (Nitrate is for protein): What do plants use absorbed nitrates for?
    ✔ to make amino acids and then proteins — Amino acids and proteins. March 2026 examiners accepted either word here.
    ✘ as a source of energy [sci.nitrogen.nitrate-use-wrong] — Energy comes from glucose, released by respiration. Nitrate supplies the nitrogen atoms proteins need.
    ✘ as a raw material for photosynthesis [sci.nitrogen.nitrate-use-wrong] — Photosynthesis needs carbon dioxide and water. Nitrate is used afterwards, to turn glucose into amino acids.
    ✘ to make cellulose for the cell wall [sci.nitrogen.nitrate-use-wrong] — Cellulose is made from glucose and contains no nitrogen at all.
- 06 (Roots, not leaves): How do plants obtain the nitrogen they need?
    ✔ they absorb nitrates from the soil through their roots — Through the roots, as nitrate ions in solution. The gas in the air is no use to them.
    ✘ they absorb nitrogen gas from the air through their leaves [sci.nitrogen.plants-absorb-nitrogen-gas] — Nitrogen gas is far too unreactive for a plant to use. That is the whole reason nitrogen-fixing bacteria matter.
    ✘ they make nitrogen during photosynthesis [sci.nitrogen.plants-absorb-nitrogen-gas] — Photosynthesis makes glucose from carbon dioxide and water. No nitrogen is involved.
    ✘ they take it directly from dead material with their roots [sci.nitrogen.ammonium-step-omitted] — Dead material has to be decomposed to ammonium and then nitrified to nitrate before a root can absorb it.
- 07 (Legumes and root nodules): A field is sown with clover for one season. What happens to the soil nitrate, and why?
    ✔ it rises, because nitrogen-fixing bacteria in the clover's root nodules make nitrates — Clover is a legume. Its root nodules house nitrogen-fixing bacteria, which is why farmers use it to restore a field.
    ✘ it falls, because the clover uses it all up [sci.nitrogen.legume-role] — Clover takes up nitrate like any plant, but its nodule bacteria add far more than it takes.
    ✘ it rises, because clover roots break the soil up and let rain wash nitrate in [sci.nitrogen.legume-role] — Rain carries nitrate away, not in. The rise comes from the bacteria in the root nodules.
    ✘ it stays the same, because clover is not a legume [sci.nitrogen.legume-role] — Clover is a legume, along with peas, beans, lentils and soya. Summer 2023 examiners reported that many candidates did not recognise the crop as one.

### ftm.science.b1.b1-nitrogen-cycle.01 [verified] sci.nitrogen.process-named-not-bacteria · earned none · ccea-cer:science:2026-march:B1H:Q7
STEM: Ruairí is asked: 'Name the types of bacteria at arrows P, Q and R on the nitrogen cycle. P: nitrogen gas → nitrate. Q: ammonium → nitrate. R: nitrate → nitrogen gas. [3]'
His answer:

    2. Q: nitrification
    3. R: denitrification
  WRONG: Every one of these is a **process**, and the question asked for the **types of bacteria**. The three answers should be **nitrogen-fixing**, **nitrifying** and **denitrifying** bacteria. March 2026's Chief Examiner named this exact slip: *some candidates lost marks because they gave the names of the processes rather than the names of the types of bacteria as asked*.
  FIX: P: nitrogen-fixing bacteria / Q: nitrifying bacteria / R: denitrifying bacteria
  FEEDBACK: Nothing from three, and the biology behind every answer was right. The fix is a reading habit, not a revision one: underline the noun in the question — *process* or *bacteria* — before you write. The pattern is regular, so it costs nothing to learn: the process ends in **-ation** or **-ition**, the bacteria end in **-ing**.

### ftm.science.b1.b1-nitrogen-cycle.02 [verified] sci.nitrogen.nitrifying-denitrifying-swapped · earned P1 · ccea-cer:science:2026-march:B1H:Q7
