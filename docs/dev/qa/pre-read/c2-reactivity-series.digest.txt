### we.science.c2.c2-reactivity-series.01 [verified] paper C2 calc
STEM: The diagram shows the apparatus used to react magnesium with steam.
(a) Complete the word equation: magnesium + steam → ............ + ............ [2]
(b) State the purpose of the damp mineral wool. [1]
(c) Give two observations made inside the boiling tube during the reaction. [2]
  FIG: svg — A boiling tube on its side holding damp mineral wool at the closed end and a coil of magnesium ribbon in the middle, heated by two burners, with a delivery tube leading into an upturned tube of water.
  1. Steam is hot water vapour, so this is a metal reacting with water — but at steam temperature.   — Decide which of the two water reactions you are in before you write anything. Cold water and steam give different products.
  2. magnesium + steam → magnesium oxide + hydrogen   — Steam gives the metal OXIDE and hydrogen. Magnesium hydroxide belongs to the cold-water reaction, and magnesium does not react with cold water at all.  (P1,P2)  WHY: ✔Because steam reactions give the oxide, and cold-water reactions give the hydroxide | ✘Because magnesium is too reactive to make a hydroxide | ✘Because hydroxides only form with Group 1 metals
  3. The damp mineral wool is there to make the steam: heating it boils the water it is soaked in.   — Say what it produces, not what it absorbs. 'To absorb the heat' and 'to give out heat' were both recorded as wrong in Summer 2025.  (P3)
  4. First observation: a bright white light where the ribbon burns.   — An observation is something you can see or feel. Name the light, not the chemistry.  (P4)
  5. Second observation: a white solid is left where the magnesium ribbon was.   — Two observations means two different ones. A colour and a state beat two ways of saying the same thing.  (P5)
  FINAL: (a) magnesium + steam → magnesium oxide + hydrogen. (b) Heating the damp mineral wool boils the water in it, which supplies the steam. (c) A bright white light, and a white solid left where the ribbon was.
  TWIN: Zinc is heated in a stream of steam in the same apparatus. Complete the word equation: zinc + steam → ............ + ............  → text accepted=["zinc oxide and hydrogen","zinc oxide + hydrogen"] keyWords=[{"any":["zinc oxide"],"marks":1,"reject":["zinc hydroxide"]},{"any":["hydrogen"],"marks":1,"reject":["oxygen"]}]

### we.science.c2.c2-reactivity-series.02 [verified] paper C2 calc
STEM: A small piece of sodium is dropped onto cold water in a trough.
(a) Name the two products of the reaction. [2]
(b) Write the balanced symbol equation, including state symbols. [3]
(c) Explain, in terms of electrons, why potassium reacts with cold water more vigorously than sodium. [2]
  1. Sodium is one of the top three, so it reacts with COLD water: sodium hydroxide + hydrogen.   — Cold water gives the hydroxide. Sodium oxide is the reaction with air, and CCEA lists it as a recurring error.  (P1,P2)
  2. Formulae first: Na, H2O, NaOH, H2.   — Write every formula before you balance. Hydrogen is diatomic, so the gas is H2, never H.
  3. Na + H2O → NaOH + H2 has two hydrogens on the left and three on the right, so it is not balanced. Doubling the sodium and the water fixes it: 2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2.   — Balance by changing the numbers in front, never the formulae. Changing H2 to H would be a formula change and scores nothing.  (P3)  WHY: ✔Because a balancing number in front is the only thing you are allowed to change | ✘Because H2O can be written as HO if it helps the balance | ✘Because the arrow lets you add an extra hydrogen
  4. State symbols: 2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g).   — Sodium is a solid, the water is a liquid, the hydroxide dissolves so it is aqueous, and hydrogen leaves as a gas.  (P4)
  5. A sodium atom and a potassium atom each have one outer electron, but potassium's is further from the nucleus, so potassium loses it more easily and forms its positive ion more readily.   — The mark is for the electron, not for the word 'vigorous'. Reactivity is the tendency to form the positive ion.  (P5,P6)
  FINAL: (a) Sodium hydroxide and hydrogen. (b) 2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g). (c) Both atoms have one outer electron, but potassium loses its outer electron more easily, so it forms its positive ion more readily and reacts more vigorously.
  TWIN: Calcium is added to cold water. Name the two products of the reaction.  → text accepted=["calcium hydroxide and hydrogen"] keyWords=[{"any":["calcium hydroxide"],"marks":1,"reject":["calcium oxide"]},{"any":["hydrogen"],"marks":1,"reject":["oxygen"]}]

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.c2.c2-reactivity-series.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Prerequisite from C1 §1.5 — you are not expected to have met this topic yet): In a word equation, what goes on the right-hand side of the arrow?
    ✔ the products — Reactants on the left, products on the right. That is all you need before starting this lesson.
    ✘ the reactants [sci.formulae.equation-given-for-formula] — Those are on the left. The arrow means 'turns into', so what comes out is written after it. The lesson below uses word equations from the second section on.
    ✘ the catalyst [sci.formulae.equation-given-for-formula] — A catalyst is written above the arrow when it is shown at all. The right-hand side is for the products.
- p2 (Prerequisite from C1 §1.5 — the diatomic elements): Which of these is the correct formula for hydrogen gas?
    ✔ H₂ — Hydrogen travels as a molecule of two atoms, so every equation in this lesson ends in H₂.
    ✘ H [sci.formulae.diatomic-forgotten] — A single H is one atom, not the gas. Hydrogen is diatomic, so the gas is H₂ — worth checking before you start, because every reaction here makes it.
    ✘ H₂O [sci.formulae.diatomic-forgotten] — That is water. The gas given off in this topic is hydrogen, H₂.
- p3 (Prerequisite from C1 §1.1 — group number and outer electrons): Sodium is in Group 1 of the Periodic Table. How many electrons does a sodium atom have in its outer shell?
    ✔ one — The group number is the number of outer electrons for Groups 1 to 7, and that single electron is why sodium is so reactive.
    ✘ two [sci.metals.potassium-loses-one-electron] — Two outer electrons would put it in Group 2. Section 1 of the lesson turns this fact into the whole reactivity series, so it is worth fixing first.
    ✘ eight [sci.metals.potassium-loses-one-electron] — Eight is a full outer shell, which is what the Group 0 gases have. Sodium has one.

### dx.science.c2.c2-reactivity-series.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (Recall the order of the reactivity series): Which of these four metals is the most reactive?
    ✔ potassium — Potassium is at the top of the series: it forms its positive ion most readily of the eight.
    ✘ zinc [sci.metals.reactivity-order-not-recalled] — Zinc sits below magnesium and aluminium. It reacts with steam, but not with cold water.
    ✘ iron [sci.metals.reactivity-order-not-recalled] — Iron is second from the bottom of the eight. Only copper is less reactive.
    ✘ copper [sci.metals.reactivity-order-not-recalled] — Copper is the least reactive of the eight: it will not react with water or with steam.
- 02 (Choose the products of a metal with steam): Magnesium is heated in a stream of steam. What are the products?
    ✔ magnesium oxide and hydrogen — Steam gives the metal oxide and hydrogen.
    ✘ magnesium hydroxide and hydrogen [sci.metals.magnesium-steam-products] — The hydroxide comes from cold water, and magnesium does not react with cold water. Steam gives the oxide.
    ✘ magnesium oxide and water [sci.metals.magnesium-steam-products] — Water is a reactant here, not a product. The gas made is hydrogen, which is why it can be collected.
    ✘ magnesium hydroxide and oxygen [sci.metals.magnesium-steam-products] — Both halves need changing: the product is the oxide, and the gas is hydrogen, not oxygen.
- 03 (State the purpose of a piece of apparatus): In the magnesium and steam experiment, what is the damp mineral wool for?
    ✔ heating it boils the water in it, which supplies the steam — That is the whole job: it is the steam generator.
    ✘ it absorbs the heat so the tube does not crack [sci.metals.mineral-wool-purpose] — Summer 2025 examiners recorded exactly this answer as a loss. The wool is the source of the steam, not a heat shield.
    ✘ it dries the hydrogen before it is collected [sci.metals.mineral-wool-purpose] — The wool is damp on purpose. Drying agents such as anhydrous calcium chloride do the opposite job, and they sit elsewhere.
    ✘ it stops the magnesium ribbon sliding down the tube [sci.metals.mineral-wool-purpose] — The wool is at the closed end, away from the ribbon. It is heated to make steam.
- 04 (Separate the cold-water metals from the rest): Which metal does not react with cold water?
    ✔ iron — Iron needs steam. Only potassium, sodium and calcium react with cold water.
    ✘ potassium [sci.metals.reactivity-order-not-recalled] — Potassium reacts with cold water fiercely, melting and skidding across the surface.
    ✘ sodium [sci.metals.reactivity-order-not-recalled] — Sodium reacts with cold water, giving sodium hydroxide and hydrogen.
    ✘ calcium [sci.metals.reactivity-order-not-recalled] — Calcium is the third of the cold-water three, and it is the one CCEA usually puts in a trough.
- 05 (Match an observation in air to its metal): A metal is heated in air and gives off orange sparks. Which metal is it?
    ✔ iron — Iron sparks orange. Fine iron wool is the usual demonstration.
    ✘ copper [sci.metals.air-observations-swapped] — Copper glows red without sparking. Examiners in both 2025 papers recorded iron and copper being swapped here.
    ✘ magnesium [sci.metals.air-observations-swapped] — Magnesium burns with a bright white light, which is hard to look at directly.
    ✘ zinc [sci.metals.air-observations-swapped] — Zinc is not one of the three CCEA names for this table; the sparks belong to iron.
- 06 (Give the physical property behind a collection method): Hydrogen from a metal and steam is collected over water. Which property of hydrogen makes that possible?
    ✔ it is insoluble in water — Insoluble, so it collects instead of dissolving away. That is the one-word answer CCEA prints.
    ✘ it is less dense than air [sci.gases.insolubility-reason-not-given] — That is why hydrogen can also be collected by upward delivery. Over water, the property that matters is that it does not dissolve.
    ✘ it burns with a squeaky pop [sci.gases.insolubility-reason-not-given] — That is the test for hydrogen, not a reason for the collection method. The question asks for a physical property.
    ✘ it is denser than water [sci.gases.insolubility-reason-not-given] — Hydrogen is far less dense than water; and density is not what decides whether a gas can be collected over it.

      SOLUTION: Drop a small piece of each metal into cold water. Only one reacts: bubbles of gas rise from it, it slowly disappears and a colourless solution with a white solid in it is left. That sample is the calcium, and the products are calcium hydroxide and hydrogen. Take the other two and heat each in turn in a stream of steam, made by heating damp mineral wool at the closed end of a boiling tube. One of them reacts, glowing and leaving a white solid, and hydrogen is given off, which can be collected over water because it is insoluble. ## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.science.c2.c2-reactivity-series.01 [verified] sci.metals.magnesium-steam-products · earned P2 · ccea-cer:science:2025-summer:C2F:Q1
STEM: Órlaith is asked: 'Magnesium is heated in a stream of steam. Complete the word equation and state the purpose of the damp mineral wool. [3]'
Her answer:
  ✘ 1. magnesium + steam → magnesium hydroxide + hydrogen
    2. The damp mineral wool absorbs the heat from the burner.
    3. The hydrogen is collected over water.
  WRONG: The gas is right and the collection is right, but the solid product is not. Cold water gives a metal hydroxide; steam gives the metal oxide. Magnesium does not react with cold water at all, so magnesium hydroxide cannot be the product here. Line 2 then gives the wool the wrong job: it is damp so that heating it makes the steam.
  FIX: magnesium + steam → magnesium oxide + hydrogen / Heating the damp mineral wool boils the water in it, which supplies the steam. / The hydrogen is collected over water because it is insoluble.
  FEEDBACK: One of the three marks: hydrogen is correct. The oxide mark goes because the hydroxide belongs to the cold-water reaction, and the mineral-wool mark goes because absorbing heat is not what a damp piece of wool is doing in that tube.
