### we.science.p2.p2-echoes-ultrasound-sonar-radar.01 [verified] paper P2 calc
STEM: A ship sends a sonar pulse straight down. The echo returns 0.12 s later. Sound travels through sea water at 1500 m/s.
Calculate the depth of the water. [4]
  FIG: svg — A ship sending a sonar pulse straight down to the seabed and receiving the echo back, with the depth marked and the two journeys labelled.
  1. distance to the reflector = (speed × time) ÷ 2   — Write the rule before any number. The ÷ 2 belongs in the equation line, not as an afterthought at the end.  (P1)
  2. total distance = 1500 × 0.12 = 180 m   — Speed times time always gives the distance the wave actually covered — and the timer ran for the whole journey.  (P2)
  3. depth = 180 ÷ 2 = 90 m   — Halve it, because that total is the trip down plus the trip back up.  (P3)  WHY: ✔Because the pulse travelled down to the seabed and back up to the ship | ✘Because the echo is half as loud as the pulse | ✘Because sound travels half as fast on the way back
  4. depth = 90 m   — Finish with the unit. A sanity check: the answer must be smaller than the total distance, never larger.  (P4)
  FINAL: depth = 90 m
  TWIN: A walker shouts at a cliff and hears the echo 1.5 s later. Sound travels at 340 m/s in air.
Calculate the distance to the cliff. [4]  → numeric 255 m tol={"type":"exact"} forms=decimal

### we.science.p2.p2-echoes-ultrasound-sonar-radar.02 [verified] paper P2 calc
STEM: A diver knows the water below her boat is 62 m deep. A sonar pulse sent from the boat returns 0.08 s later.
Calculate the speed of sound in the water. [3]
  1. total distance = 2 × 62 = 124 m   — Going the other way round, the factor of two is a doubling, not a halving: the pulse covered the depth twice.  (P1)  WHY: ✔Because the 0.08 s covers the journey down and the journey back up | ✘Because the speed of sound doubles in water | ✘Because two echoes were received
  2. speed = distance ÷ time = 124 ÷ 0.08   — Now an ordinary speed calculation, with the corrected distance in it.  (P2)
  3. speed = 1550 m/s   — Finish with the unit. Sound in sea water is around 1500 m/s, so this is believable.  (P3)
  FINAL: speed = 1550 m/s

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.p2.p2-echoes-ultrasound-sonar-radar.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Prerequisite: distance, speed and time): Before we start, from P1: rearranged to give a distance, what does speed = distance ÷ time become?
    ✔ distance = speed × time — That rearrangement is the engine of every echo calculation.
    ✘ distance = speed ÷ time — Dividing by the time would make a long journey come out short. Multiply.
    ✘ distance = time ÷ speed — That is upside down. Distance = speed × time.
- p2 (Prerequisite: the two wave families): From the last topic: sound is which kind of wave?
    ✔ longitudinal — Longitudinal, and so is ultrasound, which is only sound too high to hear.
    ✘ transverse [sci.waves.sound-called-transverse] — Water waves and electromagnetic waves are transverse. Sound is longitudinal — the waves topic covers this first.
- p3 (Prerequisite: the milli prefix): How many milliseconds are there in one second?
    ✔ 1000 — 1000. Echo times often arrive in milliseconds and have to be converted before substituting.
    ✘ 100 [sci.waves.prefix-not-converted] — Milli means a thousandth, so there are 1000 milliseconds in a second.
    ✘ 60 [sci.waves.prefix-not-converted] — 60 is seconds in a minute. Milli means a thousandth.

### dx.science.p2.p2-echoes-ultrasound-sonar-radar.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (The definition of ultrasound): What is ultrasound?
    ✔ sound with a frequency above 20 000 Hz — That is the specification's wording, and the number is what carries the mark.
    ✘ ultraviolet light [sci.em.ultrasound-for-ultraviolet] — Those are two different things. Ultraviolet is electromagnetic; ultrasound is sound. Examiners have reported the two words being swapped.
    ✘ a transverse wave that can travel through a vacuum [sci.waves.sound-called-transverse] — Ultrasound is sound, so it is longitudinal and it needs particles to travel through. Only electromagnetic waves cross a vacuum.
- 02 (The echo calculation): A sonar pulse returns from the seabed 0.2 s after it was sent. Sound travels at 1500 m/s in the water. How deep is the water?
    ✔ 150 m — (1500 × 0.2) ÷ 2. The pulse covers the depth twice, so halve the total.
    ✘ 300 m [sci.waves.echo-distance-not-halved] — That is the total path of the pulse, down and back. The seabed is half as deep.
    ✘ 75 m [sci.waves.echo-distance-not-halved] — That halves twice. Halve the total distance once, and only once.
    ✘ 7500 m [sci.waves.equation-rearranged-wrongly] — Speed divided by time is not a distance. Multiply the speed by the time, then halve.
- 03 (The reason behind the halving): Why is the time halved in an echo calculation?
    ✔ because the pulse travels to the reflector and back, covering twice the distance — That is the whole reason, and it is what the scheme wants written down.
    ✘ because the echo is only half as loud as the pulse that was sent out [sci.waves.echo-distance-not-halved] — Loudness does not come into it. The halving is about the distance the pulse covered.
    ✘ because sound travels more slowly on the way back [sci.waves.echo-distance-not-halved] — The speed is the same in both directions. What is doubled is the distance, not the time each metre takes.
- 04 (Ultrasound against ultraviolet): A student writes that ultrasound is part of the electromagnetic spectrum. What has gone wrong?
    ✔ ultrasound is sound, so it is longitudinal and needs a medium; the electromagnetic one is ultraviolet — The two words look alike and mean quite different things. Examiners have seen ultrasound written where ultraviolet was wanted.
    ✘ nothing: ultrasound and ultraviolet are two names for the same wave [sci.em.ultrasound-for-ultraviolet] — They are different families. Ultrasound is a sound wave; ultraviolet is an electromagnetic wave.
    ✘ ultrasound is electromagnetic, but it sits outside the seven named regions [sci.em.ultrasound-for-ultraviolet] — Ultrasound is not electromagnetic at all. It is sound above the range of human hearing.
- 05 (Speed from an echo): A clap is echoed back from a wall 85 m away after 0.5 s. What is the speed of sound?
    ✔ 340 m/s — The sound covered 2 × 85 m in 0.5 s, so the speed is that distance divided by the time.
    ✘ 170 m/s [sci.waves.echo-distance-not-doubled] — Only one leg of the journey was counted. Double the distance before dividing by the time.
    ✘ 42.5 m/s [sci.waves.equation-rearranged-wrongly] — Distance multiplied by time is not a speed. Speed = distance ÷ time.
    ✘ 680 m/s [sci.waves.echo-distance-not-halved] — That doubles the distance twice over. One doubling is enough: out and back.
- 06 (The kilo prefix): An ultrasound probe works at 40 kHz. What is this frequency in Hz?
    ✔ 40 000 Hz — Kilo means a thousand, so multiply by 1000 before substituting into any equation.
    ✘ 40 Hz [sci.waves.prefix-not-converted] — The prefix was dropped altogether. Summer 2025 examiners reported the kilo prefix causing trouble on an ultrasound question.
    ✘ 4 000 Hz [sci.waves.prefix-not-converted] — Kilo is a thousand, not a hundred.
    ✘ 40 000 000 Hz [sci.waves.prefix-not-converted] — That is a million, which is the mega prefix. Kilo is a thousand.
