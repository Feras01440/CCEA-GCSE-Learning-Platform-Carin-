## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.b2.b2-reproductive-systems.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Recall the levels of organisation (B1)): Before we start, three things you have already met in B1. Which level of organisation is a group of organs working together?
    ✔ an organ system — An organ system. The male and female reproductive systems are exactly that.
    ✘ a tissue [sci.cells.organisation-order-reversed] — A tissue is a group of similar cells. Organs are built from tissues.
    ✘ an organ [sci.cells.organisation-order-reversed] — An organ is one structure made of several tissues. A group of organs working together is the next level up.
    ✘ an organism [sci.cells.organisation-order-reversed] — An organism is the whole living thing, made of several organ systems.
- p2 (Recall cell structures (B1)): Which part of a cell holds the chromosomes?
    ✔ the nucleus — The nucleus. Later in this unit you will meet a cell whose nucleus carries only half a set.
    ✘ the cytoplasm — The cytoplasm is where most reactions happen, but the chromosomes are kept in the nucleus.
    ✘ the cell membrane [sci.cells.membrane-described-as-protection] — The membrane controls what enters and leaves the cell.
    ✘ the mitochondria — Mitochondria release energy by respiration. You will meet them again in the sperm cell.
- p3 (Recall excretion (B1)): Which organ makes urine?
    ✔ the kidney — The kidney. Keep that in mind: nothing in the reproductive system makes urine.
    ✘ the bladder [sci.excretion.bladder-for-kidney] — The bladder stores urine; it does not make it.
    ✘ the liver — The liver makes urea, which the kidney then puts into urine, but the urine itself is made in the kidney.
    ✘ the ureter — The ureter is the tube that carries urine from the kidney to the bladder.

### dx.science.b2.b2-reproductive-systems (post) [verified]
- d1 (Name the site of sperm production): Where are sperm made?
    ✔ in the testes — The testes. They also make testosterone.
    ✘ in the prostate gland [sci.repro.male-parts-answered-with-urine] — The prostate gland adds fluid to sperm that have already been made.
    ✘ in the sperm tube [sci.repro.male-path-order-wrong] — The sperm tube carries sperm; it does not make them.
    ✘ in the scrotum [sci.repro.scrotum-holds-nothing] — The scrotum is the sac. What it holds is the testes, and those are where the sperm are made.
- d2 (Tell the urethra from the ureter): Which tube carries both semen and, at other times, urine?
    ✔ the urethra — The urethra. It is the only tube in the body that does both jobs.
    ✘ the ureter [sci.repro.urethra-ureter-confused] — The ureter runs from the kidney to the bladder and carries urine only. The two words are nearly the same, so the examiner reads the spelling closely.
    ✘ the sperm tube [sci.repro.male-path-order-wrong] — The sperm tube carries sperm only, and only as far as the prostate gland.
    ✘ the oviduct [sci.repro.ovary-oviduct-swapped] — The oviduct is part of the female system.
- d3 (Name the site of fertilisation): In which part of the female reproductive system does fertilisation take place?
    ✔ the oviduct — The oviduct. The ovum is fertilised on its way from the ovary to the uterus.
    ✘ the uterus [sci.repro.ovary-oviduct-swapped] — The uterus is where the ball of cells implants afterwards, not where the nuclei fuse.
    ✘ the ovary [sci.repro.ovary-oviduct-swapped] — The ovary releases the ovum. Fertilisation happens after the ovum has left it.
    ✘ the cervix [sci.repro.cervix-vagina-confused] — The cervix is the ring of muscle at the base of the uterus.
- d4 (Give the function of the prostate gland): What does the prostate gland do?
    ✔ it adds fluid to the sperm — It adds fluid to the sperm. Sperm and that fluid together are semen.
    ✘ it makes urine [sci.repro.male-parts-answered-with-urine] — Urine is made in the kidney. The reports say answers here drift towards urine more than anything else.
    ✘ it makes sperm [sci.repro.male-path-order-wrong] — Sperm are made in the testes. By the time the sperm reach the prostate gland they are complete.
    ✘ it stores sperm until they are needed [sci.repro.male-parts-answered-with-urine] — Storage is not the job the specification gives it. It adds fluid.
- d5 (Explain the position of the testes): Why are the testes held in the scrotum, outside the body?
    ✔ because it is cooler there than inside the body — Cooler. Sperm are made best a little below body temperature.
    ✘ because there is more room outside the body [sci.repro.scrotum-holds-nothing] — Room is not the reason. Temperature is.
    ✘ because sperm could not leave the body otherwise [sci.repro.scrotum-holds-nothing] — Sperm leave along the sperm tube and the urethra, whatever the position of the testes.
    ✘ because the scrotum makes the sperm [sci.repro.scrotum-holds-nothing] — The scrotum is a sac of skin; it makes nothing. The testes inside it make the sperm.
- d6 (Name the cervix): Which structure is the ring of muscle between the uterus and the vagina?
    ✔ the cervix — The cervix, the neck of the uterus.
    ✘ the vagina [sci.repro.cervix-vagina-confused] — The vagina is the passage below the cervix, not the ring of muscle itself.
    ✘ the oviduct [sci.repro.ovary-oviduct-swapped] — The oviducts join the uterus at the top, not the bottom.
    ✘ the urethra [sci.repro.urethra-ureter-confused] — The urethra carries urine out of the body and is not part of the female reproductive system.
- d7 (Use a scale to find a real length): A diagram is printed at half life size. A structure measures 30 mm on the diagram. How long is it really?
    ✔ 60 mm — 60 mm. At half life size the drawing is half the real length, so the real length is twice the drawing.
    ✘ 15 mm [sci.quant.magnification-inverted] — That is the drawn length multiplied by the magnification. Half life size means the real thing is the bigger one, so divide.
    ✘ 30 mm [sci.quant.units-not-converted] — That is the length on the paper. The scale has not been used at all.
    ✘ 300 mm [sci.quant.units-not-converted] — That is the drawn length multiplied by ten, which is a unit change rather than the scale.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.science.b2.b2-reproductive-systems.01 [verified] sci.repro.male-parts-answered-with-urine · earned P1,P3 · ccea-cer:science:2024-summer:B2H:Q1
STEM: Órlaith is asked to give the function of three parts of the male reproductive system, for one mark each.
Her answers:
    1. testis — makes sperm
  ✘ 2. prostate gland — makes urine
    3. urethra — carries semen out through the penis
  WRONG: Lines 1 and 3 are exactly what the scheme wants. Line 2 gives the prostate gland a job that belongs to the kidney. The prostate gland adds fluid to the sperm; sperm and that fluid together are semen.
  FIX: testis — makes sperm / prostate gland — adds fluid to the sperm to make semen / urethra — carries semen out through the penis
  FEEDBACK: Two of the three marks stand. The examiners' reports say answers about the prostate gland drift towards urine more often than anything else on this question. A quick test before you write: urine is made in the kidney, stored in the bladder and carried out by the urethra, and no other structure here touches it.

- rp.science.b2.b2-reproductive-systems.05 [verified] (qa) Where does fertilisation happen, and where does the ball of cells settle afterwards?  →  Fertilisation happens in the oviduct. The ball of cells settles into the lining of the uterus.
- rp.science.b2.b2-reproductive-systems.06 [verified] (novel-example) Why are the testes held in the scrotum, outside the body?  →  Because it is cooler outside the body, and sperm are made best at a temperature a little below body temperature.
