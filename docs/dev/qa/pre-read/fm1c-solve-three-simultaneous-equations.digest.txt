### we.fm.u1.solve-three-simultaneous-equations.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: Solve the equations below, showing clearly each stage of your solution.
$2x + 3y + z = 13$
$x - y + 2z = 10$
$3x + 2y - z = 7$
  FIG: svg — Two boxes showing the two eliminations: equation 1 plus equation 3 gives 5x + 5y = 20, and twice equation 1 minus equation 2 gives 3x + 7y = 16.
  1. Number the equations $(1)$, $(2)$ and $(3)$, then look down the $z$ column: $1$, $2$, $-1$.   — Choosing the letter before you start is the whole plan. A column holding a 1 and a negative 1 is the cheapest to clear, because those two equations can simply be added.  (M1)
  2. $(1) + (3)$ gives $5x + 5y = 20$, which becomes equation $(4)$.   — Add the like terms in order: x terms, then y terms, then the numbers on the right. Saying that last part out loud is what stops the commonest slip in this topic.  (W1)  WHY: ✔Because $+z$ and $-z$ add to zero | ✘Because equation (3) is the last one | ✘Because both equations contain a 3
  3. $2 \times (1) - (2)$ gives $3x + 7y = 16$, which becomes equation $(5)$.   — The same letter has to go a second time, from a different pair. Doubling equation (1) makes both z terms match, and doubling an equation reaches its right-hand side too.  (M2)
  4. Dividing $(4)$ by $5$ gives $x + y = 4$, and solving that with $(5)$ gives $x = 3$ and $y = 1$.   — This is an ordinary pair now. Making the numbers smaller first costs one line and saves several.  (W2)
  5. Substituting into $(1)$: $2(3) + 3(1) + z = 13$, so $z = 4$.   — Go back to an original equation, because (4) and (5) no longer contain z. Pick the one whose z term is simplest.  (MW1)
  6. Check in $(2)$: $3 - 1 + 2(4) = 10$.   — Equation (2) only ever entered the working through a doubled copy of equation (1), so it still tests the answer. If it agrees, all three values are right.  (W3)
  FINAL: $x = 3$, $y = 1$, $z = 4$
  TWIN: Solve the equations below, showing clearly each stage of your solution.
$x + 2y + 3z = 15$
$3x + y - z = 10$
$x + y + 2z = 9$
Give the first two unknowns as $(x, y)$.  → algebraic (2, 5) (equivalent)

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.solve-three-simultaneous-equations.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Multiply out a bracket, constant term included): Three quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new method, so answer from what you already know.
What is $3(2x - 5)$?
    ✔ $6x - 15$ — Both terms are multiplied by 3, which is exactly what scaling a whole equation does.
    ✘ $6x - 5$ [fm.simeq.multiplier-not-applied-to-constant] — The 5 is multiplied as well. Leaving a constant untouched is the slip that quietly breaks an elimination later on.
    ✘ $5x - 15$ [fm.simeq.multiplier-not-applied-to-constant] — The 3 multiplies the 2 to give $6x$; adding them instead is a different operation.
- p2 (Subtract one equation from another with a negative term): Take $2x - 5y = 1$ away from $2x + 3y = 17$. What is left?
    ✔ $8y = 16$ — $3y - (-5y) = 8y$ and $17 - 1 = 16$. Both sides change together.
    ✘ $-2y = 16$ [fm.simeq.elimination-sign-slip] — Taking away $-5y$ adds $5y$. Two minus signs beside each other turn into a plus.
    ✘ $8y = 18$ [fm.simeq.elimination-sign-slip] — The letters are handled correctly and the right-hand side was added instead of subtracted: $17 - 1 = 16$.
- p3 (Substitute a negative value): If $x = 4$ and $y = -1$, what is $3x + 2y$?
    ✔ $10$ — $12 + (-2) = 10$. Carrying a negative value through a substitution is half of the last step of this topic.
    ✘ $14$ [fm.simeq.elimination-sign-slip] — $2 \times (-1)$ is $-2$, so it comes off the 12 rather than going on.
    ✘ $5$ [fm.simeq.elimination-sign-slip] — Multiply before adding: $3 \times 4 = 12$ and $2 \times (-1) = -2$.

### dx.fm.u1.solve-three-simultaneous-equations.post (post) [verified]
- q1 (Choose the letter to eliminate from the coefficients): In the system $2x + 3y + z = 13$, $x - y + 2z = 10$, $3x + 2y - z = 7$, which letter is cheapest to clear first?
    ✔ $z$, because its coefficients are $1$, $2$ and $-1$ — Small coefficients, and a pair that are already equal and opposite, so equations (1) and (3) can simply be added.
    ✘ $x$, because it always goes first [fm.simeq.different-letter-eliminated] — No letter goes first by rule. Read the columns and choose the one that needs least scaling.
    ✘ $y$, because it has the largest numbers [fm.simeq.different-letter-eliminated] — Large coefficients mean more scaling and more chances to slip. Clearing $y$ would work, but it is the slowest route here.
- q2 (Keep the two new equations in the same pair of unknowns): You have cleared $z$ from equations $(1)$ and $(2)$. What should the second elimination do?
    ✔ Clear $z$ from equations $(1)$ and $(3)$ — Same letter, different pair. Both new equations are then in x and y and can be solved together.
    ✘ Clear $x$ from equations $(2)$ and $(3)$ [fm.simeq.different-letter-eliminated] — That leaves one equation in x and y and one in y and z. Two equations in three unknowns cannot be finished.
    ✘ Solve equation $(3)$ on its own [fm.simeq.different-letter-eliminated] — A single equation in three unknowns has endless solutions. All three equations are needed before anything is pinned down.
- q3 (Scale a whole equation): Doubling $2x + 3y + z = 13$ gives which equation?
    ✔ $4x + 6y + 2z = 26$ — Every term doubles, the right-hand side with them.
    ✘ $4x + 6y + 2z = 13$ [fm.simeq.multiplier-not-applied-to-constant] — The letters were doubled and the $13$ was not, so the equation is no longer true. Both sides always move together.
    ✘ $4x + 3y + z = 26$ [fm.simeq.multiplier-not-applied-to-constant] — Only the first term was doubled. Multiplying an equation reaches every term in it.
- q4 (Recognise that the third value needs an original equation): A candidate reaches $x = 3$ and $y = 1$, then writes $z = 1$. What has gone wrong?
    ✔ The value of $y$ has been copied onto the $z$ line — Equations (4) and (5) no longer contain z, so z has to come from an original equation. Here it is $4$.
    ✘ The value of $x$ is wrong [fm.simeq.stopped-at-the-pair] — $x = 3$ is right. The trouble is the last line, where z has not been found at all.
    ✘ Nothing, the answer is complete [fm.simeq.stopped-at-the-pair] — Substituting into equation (1) gives $z = 4$, so the last line is not the answer yet.
- q5 (Check in the equation that was not used): Which of these is worth the last twenty seconds of a solve?
    ✔ Substituting all three values into all three original equations — Two of them built the answer, so the third is a genuine test, and it catches an arithmetic slip while the marks are still there.
    ✘ Rubbing out the eliminations to leave a tidy page [fm.presentation.calculator-no-method] — The eliminations carry most of the marks. A page holding only three values earns almost nothing.
    ✘ Writing the three values out again underneath [fm.simeq.no-check-in-third-equation] — Copying them out tests nothing. Substituting them into an equation you did not use does.
- q6 (Know what an unsupported answer earns): A calculator gives the three values in seconds. What is that page worth in Further Mathematics?
    ✔ Nothing, because no method is shown — The Teacher Guidance says so directly and the reports repeat it. Use the calculator to check values you have already found on paper.
    ✘ Full marks, because the values are right [fm.presentation.calculator-no-method] — Most of the marks here are for the eliminations, and right values with no working have been reported as earning nothing.
    ✘ Half the marks [fm.presentation.calculator-no-method] — There is no half credit for an unsupported answer here; the instruction to show each stage is printed with the question.

### q.fm.u1.solve-three-simultaneous-equations.0001 [verified] practice · diff 1 · untiered · FM1 calc · 2 marks · (main)find2 · A single elimination step lifted out of a full solve
  (main) [2] Two equations of a system are
$(1)$ $2x + 3y + z = 13$
$(3)$ $3x + 2y - z = 7$
Add them to remove $z$, and find the equation in $x$ and $y$ that is left.
      ANSWER: algebraic 5x + 5y = 20 (equivalent)
      M1 M1: adding the two equations term by term, so that the two $z$ terms cancel
      W1 W1: $5x + 5y = 20$, or any equation equivalent to it such as $x + y = 4$
      SOLUTION: The $z$ terms are $+z$ and $-z$, so adding removes $z$ with no scaling at all. The $x$ terms give $2 + 3 = 5$, the $y$ terms give $3 + 2 = 5$, and the right-hand side gives $13 + 7 = 20$. So the equation is $5x + 5y = 20$. Dividing by $5$ turns it into $x + y = 4$, which is easier to carry forward.
      ERR [fm.simeq.elimination-sign-slip] {"kind":"algebraic","latex":"5x + 5y = 6"} → The letters are added correctly, so the method mark stands. The number on the right was subtracted instead of added: $13 + 7 = 20$. (1)
      ERR [fm.simeq.added-instead-of-subtracted] {"kind":"algebraic","latex":"-x + y + 2z = 6"} → That is equation $(3)$ taken away from equation $(1)$, which leaves $2z$ behind instead of removing $z$. Terms that are equal and opposite are added away, not subtracted away. (0)
      HINTS: The two $z$ terms are $+z$ and $-z$. || Add the $x$ terms, then the $y$ terms, then the two numbers on the right.

### q.fm.u1.solve-three-simultaneous-equations.0002 [verified] practice · diff 2 · untiered · FM1 calc · 2 marks · (main)find2 · A single elimination that needs one equation doubled first
  (main) [2] Two equations of a system are
$(1)$ $2x + 3y + z = 13$
$(2)$ $x - y + 2z = 10$
Double equation $(1)$, subtract equation $(2)$, and find the equation in $x$ and $y$ that is left.
      ANSWER: algebraic 3x + 7y = 16 (equivalent)
      M1 M1: doubling every term of $(1)$, the right-hand side included: $4x + 6y + 2z = 26$
      W1 W1: $3x + 7y = 16$
      SOLUTION: Double every term of $(1)$: $4x + 6y + 2z = 26$. Both equations now carry $2z$, so subtracting $(2)$ removes $z$. The $x$ terms give $4 - 1 = 3$, the $y$ terms give $6 - (-1) = 7$, and the right-hand side gives $26 - 10 = 16$. So the equation is $3x + 7y = 16$.
      ERR [fm.simeq.elimination-sign-slip] {"kind":"algebraic","latex":"3x + 7y = 36"} → The letters are right, so the elimination itself earned its mark. The right-hand side went the other way: $26 - 10 = 16$, not $36$. (1)
      ERR [fm.simeq.multiplier-not-applied-to-constant] {"kind":"algebraic","latex":"3x + 7y = 3"} → Every letter in $(1)$ was doubled but the $13$ was left alone, so the equation being used is no longer true. Doubling an equation doubles both of its sides. (0)
      ERR [fm.simeq.added-instead-of-subtracted] {"kind":"algebraic","latex":"5x + 5y + 4z = 36"} → Both equations carry $+2z$, so adding gives $4z$ and $z$ is still there. Matching signs are subtracted away; opposite signs are added away. (0)
      HINTS: Doubling an equation means doubling the number on the right as well. || Doubled, $(1)$ reads $4x + 6y + 2z = 26$. || Both equations now carry $2z$, so subtracting removes it.

### q.fm.u1.solve-three-simultaneous-equations.0003 [verified] practice · diff 2 · untiered · FM1 calc · 3 marks · (main)solve3 · The pair of equations left after both eliminations
  (main) [3] Removing $z$ twice from a system has left this pair.
$(4)$ $5x + 5y = 20$
$(5)$ $3x + 7y = 16$
Solve them. Give your answer as $(x, y)$.
      ANSWER: algebraic (3, 1) (equivalent)
      M1 M1: dividing $(4)$ by $5$, or scaling either equation so that one letter matches
      MW1 MW1: eliminating and reaching $y = 1$
      W1 W1: $x = 3$ ft
      SOLUTION: Divide $(4)$ by $5$: $x + y = 4$. Multiply that by $3$: $3x + 3y = 12$. Subtract it from $(5)$: $4y = 4$, so $y = 1$. Then $x + 1 = 4$, so $x = 3$. Check in $(5)$: $3(3) + 7(1) = 16$.
      ERR [fm.simeq.values-in-wrong-slots] {"kind":"algebraic","latex":"(1, 3)"} → Both numbers are right and they are the wrong way round. The first slot is $x$, and $x = 1$ does not fit equation $(4)$. (2)
      HINTS: Every term of $(4)$ divides by $5$, giving $x + y = 4$. || Multiply that by $3$ so the $x$ terms match equation $(5)$. || Subtract, find $y$, then go back for $x$.
