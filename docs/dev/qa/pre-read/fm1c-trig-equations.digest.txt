## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.fm.u1.trig-equations.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: Solve the equation $2\sin x = 1.4$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$, giving your answers correct to 2 decimal places.
  FIG: svg — The graph of y = sin x from 0 to 360 degrees with a line at y = 0.7 cutting the curve at 44.43 and 135.57 degrees.
  1. $\sin x = 1.4 \div 2 = 0.7$   — Isolate the ratio before anything else. An inverse sine only makes sense once the equation reads sin x equals a single number, and that number has to lie between -1 and 1.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because $\sin^{-1}$ needs the ratio on its own | ✘Because $1.4$ is too large to be an angle | ✘Because dividing always comes first
  2. Sketch $y = \sin x$ and draw $y = 0.7$ across it. The line cuts the curve $2$ times.   — Counting crossings before solving tells you how many answers to produce, so you cannot stop one short. It costs about fifteen seconds.  (M1)
  3. $\sin^{-1}(0.7) = 44.43^\circ$   — This is the calculator's angle, always the one in the first quadrant for a positive sine. It is the first crossing on the rising part of the curve.  (MW2)
  4. $180 - 44.43 = 135.57^\circ$   — The arch is symmetrical about $x = 90^\circ$, so the second angle is as far past the mirror as the first is short of it. On the CAST diagram this is the S quadrant.  (MW3)
  5. Both $44.43^\circ$ and $135.57^\circ$ lie between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$, so both are kept.   — The range check is the last line of the working, every time. An angle outside the printed range is not a solution of the question that was asked, and an extra one is penalised.  (W1)
  FINAL: $x = 44.43^\circ$ or $x = 135.57^\circ$
  TWIN: Solve the equation $4\cos x + 3 = 0$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$, giving your answers correct to 2 decimal places.  → algebraic x=138.59, x=221.41 (equivalent)

### we.fm.u1.trig-equations.02 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: (a) Solve $\cos x = -0.25$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$.
(b) Hence solve $\cos(2x - 40^\circ) = -0.25$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 180^\circ$.
Give every answer correct to 2 decimal places.
  FIG: svg — A four-row table of the method: widen the range for the bracket, solve for the bracket, add the shift back, then divide by the multiplier.
  1. $\cos^{-1}(-0.25) = 104.48^\circ$, and $360 - 104.48 = 255.52^\circ$.   — Part (a) done properly is most of part (b). Keep the minus sign in the calculator, and use cosine's own rule for the partner.  (MW1,MW2)
  2. As $x$ runs $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$, the bracket runs $-40^\circ$ to $320^\circ$.   — Widen the range before you hunt for angles. Doing it the other way round is how solutions get lost, or invented, at the ends.  (M1)  WHY: ✔Because the bracket, not $x$, is what the cosine is taken of | ✘Because the range is always doubled | ✘Because the answers must be positive
  3. $2x - 40 = 104.48^\circ$ or $255.52^\circ$   — Those are exactly the two angles from part (a), which is what the word "Hence" was promising. Both sit inside the widened range, so neither is discarded.  (MW3)
  4. $2x = 144.48^\circ$ or $295.52^\circ$, so $x = 72.24^\circ$ or $147.76^\circ$.   — Undo in reverse order: the bracket subtracted $40$ last, so add it back first; it multiplied by $2$ first, so divide by it last. Halving before adding gives $92.24^\circ$, which is the reported slip.  (MW4)
  5. Both answers lie between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, so both are kept.   — Check against the range printed in part (b), not the one in part (a). They are different ranges and only one of them is being asked about.  (W1)
  FINAL: (a) $x = 104.48^\circ$ or $x = 255.52^\circ$  (b) $x = 72.24^\circ$ or $x = 147.76^\circ$
  TWIN: Solve $\tan x = 2.5$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$, giving your answers correct to 2 decimal places.  → algebraic x=68.20, x=248.20 (equivalent)

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.trig-equations.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Prerequisite: isolating a quantity in a linear equation): Three quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new work, so answer from what you already know.
Solve $2y = 1.4$.
    ✔ $y = 0.7$ — Divide both sides by $2$. The first step of every equation in this topic is exactly this, with $\sin x$ in place of $y$.
    ✘ $y = 2.8$ [fm.trig.transform-order] — That multiplies where it should divide. Undo a multiplication by dividing.
    ✘ $y = 1.4$ [fm.trig.transform-order] — The $2$ has been ignored rather than undone, so the equation is no longer balanced.
- p2 (Prerequisite: reading an inequality as a range): Which of these numbers is in the range $0^\circ \le x \le 180^\circ$?
    ✔ $150^\circ$ — The two signs are 'less than or equal to', so the ends count and everything between them does too.
    ✘ $210^\circ$ [fm.trig.answer-outside-range] — Past $180^\circ$, so outside. A solution outside the printed range earns nothing.
    ✘ $-30^\circ$ [fm.trig.answer-outside-range] — Below $0^\circ$, so outside at the other end. Ranges in this topic are often negative, so read them carefully.
- p3 (Prerequisite: using the inverse sine key): On a calculator set to degrees, what is $\sin^{-1} 0.5$?
    ✔ $30^\circ$ — That is the calculator's angle. Everything in this lesson is about the angle it does not offer you.
    ✘ $0.01$ [fm.trig.second-solution-missing] — That is $\sin 0.5^\circ$, the sine of the number rather than the angle with that sine. The inverse key runs the other way.
    ✘ $150^\circ$ [fm.trig.second-solution-missing] — That is a genuine second solution, and the calculator does not give it. It comes from the curve, which is what the next few screens are about.

### dx.fm.u1.trig-equations.post (post) [verified]
- q1 (Find the partner of a sine solution): $\sin^{-1} 0.5 = 30^\circ$. What is the other solution of $\sin x = 0.5$ between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$?
    ✔ $150^\circ$ — $180 - 30 = 150$. The sine arch is symmetrical about $90^\circ$.
    ✘ $330^\circ$ [fm.trig.answer-outside-range] — $360$ minus the angle is cosine's rule. Here it lands below the axis, where the sine is $-0.5$.
    ✘ $210^\circ$ [fm.trig.second-solution-missing] — Adding $180$ is tangent's rule. On the sine curve it flips the sign of the value instead of keeping it.
- q2 (Find the partner of a cosine solution): $\cos^{-1} -0.5 = 120^\circ$. What is the other solution between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$?
    ✔ $240^\circ$ — $360 - 120 = 240$. The cosine curve is symmetrical about $180^\circ$.
    ✘ $60^\circ$ [fm.trig.answer-outside-range] — $180$ minus the angle belongs to sine. Its cosine is $0.5$, which is not what was asked for.
    ✘ $300^\circ$ [fm.trig.second-solution-missing] — Adding $180$ belongs to tangent. On the cosine curve it changes the sign of the value.
- q3 (Find the partner of a tangent solution): $\tan x = 1$ has one solution at $45^\circ$. Where is the other, between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$?
    ✔ $225^\circ$ — The tangent curve repeats every $180^\circ$, so the partner is simply $180^\circ$ further on.
    ✘ $135^\circ$ [fm.trig.answer-outside-range] — That is sine's rule, and its tangent is $-1$ rather than $1$.
    ✘ $315^\circ$ [fm.trig.answer-outside-range] — That is cosine's rule, and its tangent is $-1$.
- q4 (Keep the sign of a negative ratio): You are solving $\cos x = -0.75$. What do you type into the calculator?
    ✔ $\cos^{-1}(-0.75)$, minus sign included — It returns $138.59^\circ$, which is already in the second quadrant, exactly where a negative cosine belongs.
    ✘ $\cos^{-1}(0.75)$ and put the minus back later [fm.trig.cast-with-negative-value] — That gives $41.41^\circ$, in the first quadrant, where cosine is positive. Both answers then come out in the wrong quadrants.
    ✘ $-\cos^{-1}(0.75)$ [fm.trig.cast-with-negative-value] — A minus sign in front of the angle is not the same as a minus sign inside the ratio, and it puts the answer outside a range that starts at zero.
- q5 (Apply the printed range): Your two angles for an equation come out as $210^\circ$ and $330^\circ$, and the range printed is $0^\circ \le x \le 180^\circ$. What do you write on the answer line?
    ✔ Neither: there is no solution in this range — Both angles are past $180^\circ$. Saying so plainly is the answer, and it is worth the mark.
    ✘ Both, because the working is correct [fm.trig.answer-outside-range] — An answer outside the printed range is not a solution of the question that was asked, and extra values are penalised.
    ✘ $210^\circ$, because it is the smaller [fm.trig.answer-outside-range] — Smaller is not the same as inside. $210$ is still greater than $180$.
- q6 (Undo a bracket in the right order): The bracket $2x - 40^\circ$ equals $104.48^\circ$. What is $x$?
    ✔ $72.24^\circ$ — Add the $40$ back, then divide by $2$: the reverse of the order the bracket applied them.
    ✘ $92.24^\circ$ [fm.trig.transform-order] — That divides first and adds afterwards. The two operations do not commute, so the order is the whole question here.
    ✘ $248.96^\circ$ [fm.trig.transform-order] — That multiplies by $2$ where it should divide. The bracket already contains $2x$, so the $2$ has to come off.
- q7 (Read the CAST diagram): Which quadrants hold the solutions when $\cos x$ is negative?
    ✔ The second and the third — **C** sits in the fourth and **A** in the first, so cosine is positive there and negative in the other two: quadrants $2$ and $3$.
    ✘ The first and the second [fm.trig.cast-with-negative-value] — Those are the quadrants where sine is positive, which is the A and S pair.
    ✘ The third and the fourth [fm.trig.cast-with-negative-value] — Cosine is positive in the fourth, because that is where the C sits.
