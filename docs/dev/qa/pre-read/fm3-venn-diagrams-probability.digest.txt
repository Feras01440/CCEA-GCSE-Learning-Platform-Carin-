### we.fm.u3.venn-diagrams-probability.01 [verified] paper FM3 calc
STEM: A leisure centre has 45 members. 26 use the weights room, 21 go to classes and 7 do neither. $x$ members do both.
Find $x$, and hence the probability that one member, picked at random, goes to classes only.
  FIG: svg — A two-circle Venn diagram for 45 members with the overlap written as x, the weights-only region as 26 minus x, the classes-only region as 21 minus x and 7 outside both circles.
  1. Weights only $= 26 - x$; classes only $= 21 - x$; outside $= 7$.   — Write every region in terms of the unknown before anything else. The two printed totals each include the overlap, so each loses an $x$.  (M1)
  2. $(26 - x) + x + (21 - x) + 7 = 45$   — The four regions do not overlap, so together they are the whole membership. That sentence is the equation, and forgetting the 7 outside is what makes it come out wrong.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Include the 7 outside the circles, because they are members too | ✘Leave the 7 out, because they are outside the circles | ✘Add the 7 to both circle totals
  3. $54 - x = 45$, so $x = 9$.   — Collect the constants and the single $x$. One $x$ is left because the overlap was added once and subtracted twice.  (W1)
  4. Regions: 17, 9, 12, 7. Their total is 45.   — Put $x$ back and check the total against the 45 the question gave. This is the one check that catches a misplaced region before it spoils every probability.  (MW1)
  5. $P(\text{classes only}) = \dfrac{12}{45} = \dfrac{4}{15}$   — 'Only' is the region outside the overlap, and the denominator is the whole membership. A bare count would earn nothing on the answer line.  (W1)
  FINAL: $x = 9$ and $P(\text{classes only}) = \dfrac{4}{15}$
  TWIN: 32 pupils were asked which instruments they play. 18 play the piano, 15 play the guitar and 4 play neither.
Find the number who play both.  → numeric 5 pupils tol={"type":"exact"} forms=decimal

### we.fm.u3.venn-diagrams-probability.02 [verified] paper FM3 calc
STEM: 75 households were asked which of glass, paper and textiles they recycle. 6 recycle all three, 7 recycle glass and textiles but not paper, 5 recycle paper and textiles but not glass, 11 recycle paper only, 7 recycle textiles only and 9 recycle none of them. $x$ recycle glass and paper but not textiles, and $2x$ recycle glass only.
Find $x$, and hence the probability that a household recycles exactly two of the three materials.
  FIG: svg — A three-circle Venn diagram for 75 households with the glass-only region written 2x and the glass-and-paper-only region written x.
  1. Regions: $2x$, $x$, 6, 7, 5, 11, 7, 9.   — List all eight regions of a three-circle diagram, the outside one included. Eight regions is the number to expect, and a missing one is the commonest reason an equation comes out wrong.  (M1)
  2. $2x + x + 6 + 7 + 5 + 11 + 7 + 9 = 75$   — Set their total equal to the 75 households. The two unknown regions give $3x$, not $2x$, because one of them is twice the other.  (MW1)
  3. $3x + 45 = 75$, so $3x = 30$ and $x = 10$.   — Collect and solve. Checking that $x$ is a whole number is worth a second: a fraction here means a region was misread.  (W1)
  4. Glass only $= 20$, glass and paper only $= 10$. All eight regions total 75.   — Substitute and check the total. 20 + 10 + 7 + 6 + 11 + 5 + 7 + 9 comes to 75, which is what the survey said.  (MW1)
  5. $P(\text{exactly two}) = \dfrac{10 + 7 + 5}{75} = \dfrac{22}{75} = \dfrac{22}{75}$   — 'Exactly two' is the three pair regions and never the centre, and the denominator is all 75 households, including the 9 who recycle nothing.  (W1)
  FINAL: $x = 10$ and $P(\text{exactly two}) = \dfrac{22}{75}$
  TWIN: Using the same completed diagram, calculate the probability that one household, picked at random, recycles glass.  → numeric 0.5733333333333334 tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=fraction/decimal

### dx.fm.u3.venn-diagrams-probability.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Solving a one-step linear equation (from GCSE Mathematics)): Before the lesson, a check on something you already know. Solve $37 - x = 32$.
    ✔ $x = 5$ — Yes. Every unknown-region Venn question ends in an equation of exactly this shape.
    ✘ $x = 69$ — The 32 has been added rather than subtracted. Moving $x$ across gives $37 = 32 + x$, so $x = 5$.
    ✘ $x = -5$ — The sign has flipped. $37 - x = 32$ means $x$ is what takes 37 down to 32, which is 5.
- p2 (The complement of a count (from GCSE Mathematics)): Another check on earlier work. In a group of 40 people, 25 own a bicycle. How many do not?
    ✔ $15$ — Yes: $40 - 25$. The region outside a Venn circle is found the same way.
    ✘ $65$ — The two numbers have been added. The people who do not own one are part of the same 40, so subtract.
    ✘ $25$ — That is the number who do own one. The question asked for the rest of the group.
- p3 (Adding the regions of a Venn diagram (from GCSE Mathematics)): One more from earlier work, before anything new. A two-circle Venn diagram has regions 12, 5 and 9, with 4 outside. How many are in the diagram altogether?
    ✔ $30$ — Yes: $12 + 5 + 9 + 4$. Every region counts, including the one outside the circles.
    ✘ $26$ — The 4 outside the circles has been left out. It belongs to the group just as much as the rest.
    ✘ $21$ — Only the two circles have been added as printed. The regions are separate, so all four are added once each.

### dx.fm.u3.venn-diagrams-probability.post (post) [verified]
- d1 (Taking the centre off a printed pair total): A three-circle Venn diagram has 8 in the centre. The question says 20 chose both tennis and squash. What goes in the tennis-and-squash region that is outside the third circle?
    ✔ $12$ — Yes: $20 - 8$. A printed pair total always includes the people who chose all three.
    ✘ $20$ [fm.prob.only-misread-in-venn] — That would count the 8 in the centre twice, once here and once there. Take the centre off first.
    ✘ $28$ [fm.prob.only-misread-in-venn] — The centre has been added rather than subtracted. The 20 already contains it.
- d2 (Finding the region outside the circles): In a survey of 60 people, the regions inside the two circles come to 47. What goes outside the circles?
    ✔ $13$ — Yes: $60 - 47$. Checking that every region totals the stated group is the habit that saves this question.
    ✘ $0$ [fm.prob.venn-total-not-used] — Nothing says the circles hold everybody. The survey named 60, and only 47 are placed so far.
    ✘ $47$ [fm.prob.venn-total-not-used] — That is the number already inside the circles. The outside region is what is left of the 60.
- d3 (Reading a whole circle rather than its 'only' region): A two-circle Venn diagram for 50 people has 18 in the left circle only, 7 in the overlap and 14 in the right circle only. Pick one person at random. How likely is it that this person is in the left circle?
    ✔ $\dfrac{25}{50}$ — Yes: the whole circle is $18 + 7 = 25$, out of all 50 people.
    ✘ $\dfrac{18}{50}$ [fm.prob.only-misread-in-venn] — That is 'in the left circle only'. Without the word only the overlap counts as well.
    ✘ $\dfrac{25}{39}$ [fm.prob.venn-total-not-used] — The denominator has become the number inside the circles. The 11 outside them are part of the 50 too.
- d4 (Giving a probability rather than a count): A Venn diagram of 80 members shows 9 in the overlap of two circles. Pick one member at random. How likely is it that this member is in the overlap?
    ✔ $\dfrac{9}{80}$ — Yes. A probability is always a count over a total; the answer line never takes a bare count.
    ✘ $9$ [fm.prob.venn-count-given-as-probability] — That is the count in the region. Divide it by the 80 members to turn it into a probability.
    ✘ $\dfrac{80}{9}$ [fm.prob.venn-count-given-as-probability] — The fraction is upside down, and it is larger than 1. The total goes on the bottom.
- d5 (Reading a conditional probability off a diagram): On a Venn diagram of 100 people, 35 are in circle $B$ and 12 of those are also in circle $A$. Given that one person, picked at random, is in $B$, what is the probability that they are also in $A$?
    ✔ $\dfrac{12}{35}$ — Yes. Being told the person is in $B$ shrinks the group to those 35, so 35 is the denominator.
    ✘ $\dfrac{12}{100}$ [fm.condprob.denominator-not-conditioned] — The numerator is right, but the condition has been ignored. The group is no longer all 100.
    ✘ $\dfrac{35}{100}$ [fm.condprob.denominator-not-conditioned] — That is the probability of being in $B$ at all. The question asks about $A$ within $B$.
- d6 (Telling 'exactly two' from 'two or more'): A three-circle Venn diagram has single regions 20, 14 and 9, pair regions 10, 7 and 5, a centre of 6 and 9 outside. How many people are in exactly two of the circles?
    ✔ $22$ — Yes: the three pair regions, $10 + 7 + 5$. The centre is three circles, not two.
    ✘ $28$ [fm.prob.only-misread-in-venn] — The centre has been counted in. Those 6 are in all three circles, so they are not 'exactly two'.
    ✘ $43$ [fm.prob.only-misread-in-venn] — That is one whole circle. 'Exactly two' means the regions where two circles overlap and the third does not.

- rp.fm.u3.venn-diagrams-probability.01 [verified] (procedure) In what order do you fill a three-circle Venn diagram, and why?  →  Centre first, then the three pair regions, then the three single regions, then the region outside the circles.
