## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: Express $\log a + 3\log b - 2\log c$ as a single logarithm.
  FIG: svg — Four lines combining log a plus 3 log b minus 2 log c into a single logarithm, one law at a time.
  1. Deal with the coefficients first: $3\log b = \log b^{3}$ and $2\log c = \log c^{2}$.   — Coefficients always go first. A term with a number in front cannot join a product or a quotient until that number is inside as a power.  (M1)  WHY: ✔Because $n \log a = \log a^{n}$, so the coefficient becomes an index | ✘Because the coefficient can be cancelled from every term | ✘Because a coefficient multiplies the argument
  2. The expression is now $\log a + \log b^{3} - \log c^{2}$.   — Rewriting the whole line before combining anything keeps the three terms visible, so nothing is lost when the laws start to act.  (M2)
  3. Add the first two: $\log a + \log b^{3} = \log(ab^{3})$.   — Added logarithms multiply inside. Doing the addition before the subtraction keeps the subtracted term where it belongs.  (M3)
  4. Subtract the last: $\log(ab^{3}) - \log c^{2} = \log\frac{ab^{3}}{c^{2}}$.   — The subtracted term goes underneath. Putting it on top is the slip the Summer 2025 report names, and it is invisible unless you check which term had the minus.  (W1)
  FINAL: $\log\frac{ab^{3}}{c^{2}}$
  TWIN: Express $2\log p + \log q - 3\log r$ as a single logarithm.  → algebraic \log\frac{p^{2}q}{r^{3}} (equivalent)

### we.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.02 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: Express $3\log 2x$ as a single logarithm.
  FIG: svg — The power law applied to 3 log 2x: the whole of 2x is cubed, giving log 8x cubed.
  1. Write the bracket first: $3\log 2x = \log(2x)^{3}$.   — The bracket is the whole point. Written without it, the cube looks as though it belongs to the x alone, and that is exactly what the examiners report happening.  (M1)
  2. Expand the bracket: $(2x)^{3} = 2^{3} \times x^{3} = 8x^{3}$.   — Every factor inside the bracket is cubed, so the $2$ becomes $8$. Check it on a number if you are unsure: at $x = 1.5$ both $3\log 2x$ and $\log 8x^{3}$ come to $1.431364$.  (M2)  WHY: ✔Because the index applies to everything inside the bracket | ✘Because only the letter can carry an index | ✘Because the coefficient stays outside the bracket
  3. So $3\log 2x = \log 8x^{3}$.   — Compare it with $\log 2x^{3}$, which is what the slip produces: at $x = 1.5$ that comes to $0.829304$, not $1.431364$.  (W1)
  FINAL: $\log 8x^{3}$
  TWIN: Express $2\log 5x$ as a single logarithm.  → algebraic \log 25x^{2} (equivalent)

### we.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.03 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: It is given that $\log 3 = p$ and $\log 5 = q$, with logarithms to base $10$.
Express $\log 7.5$ in terms of $p$ and $q$.
  FIG: svg — A constant rewritten as a logarithm: 1 equals log 10, so a missing logarithm can be built from the ones you are given.
  1. Write $7.5$ using only the numbers you have logarithms for: $7.5 = \frac{15}{2} = \frac{3 \times 5}{2}$.   — Factorising the number is the whole question. Until it is written in threes, fives and twos, none of the three laws has anything to act on.  (M1)
  2. Apply the laws: $\log 7.5 = \log 3 + \log 5 - \log 2 = p + q - \log 2$.   — The product becomes a sum and the division becomes a subtraction. What is left is one logarithm with no letter of its own.  (M2)
  3. Build the missing one: $2 = \frac{10}{5}$, so $\log 2 = \log 10 - \log 5 = 1 - q$.   — To base $10$, $\log 10 = 1$. That is what lets a logarithm you were not given be built from the ones you were.  (M3)  WHY: ✔Because $10^{1} = 10$, so $\log 10 = 1$ | ✘Because every logarithm of a whole number is 1 | ✘Because $\log 2$ cannot be written down at all
  4. Substitute, keeping the bracket: $p + q - (1 - q) = p + q - 1 + q = p + 2q - 1$.   — The bracket changes the sign of both terms inside it. Dropping it leaves $p + q - 1$, which is short of a whole $q$.  (W1)
  FINAL: $p + 2q - 1$
  TWIN: It is given that $\log 2 = m$ and $\log 3 = n$. Express $\log 18$ in terms of $m$ and $n$.  → algebraic m + 2n (equivalent)

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.pre (pre) [draft]
- p1 (A power applied to a bracket): Three quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new method, so answer from what you already know.
What is $(2x)^{3}$?
    ✔ $8x^{3}$ — Everything inside the bracket is cubed, so the $2$ becomes $8$.
    ✘ $2x^{3}$ — Only the $x$ has been cubed. The bracket means the $2$ is cubed as well.
    ✘ $6x^{3}$ — The $2$ has been multiplied by $3$ rather than cubed.
- p2 (Evaluating a logarithm from a power): What is $\log_{10} 100$?
    ✔ $2$ — $10^{2} = 100$, so the logarithm is $2$. That fact is what lets a constant be written as a logarithm.
    ✘ $10$ — That is the base. The logarithm is the index on the base.
    ✘ $100$ — That is the number inside. The answer is the index that reaches it.
- p3 (Turning a decimal into a fraction): Write $7.5$ as a fraction in its lowest terms.
    ✔ $\frac{15}{2}$ — $7.5 = \frac{15}{2}$, and $15 = 3 \times 5$, which is where the given logarithms come in.
    ✘ $\frac{7}{5}$ — The digits have been read as a fraction. $7.5$ is seven and a half.
    ✘ $\frac{75}{100}$ — That is $0.75$. The number here is larger than $7$.

### dx.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.post (post) [draft]
- d1 (The product law): What is $\log 5 + \log 6$ as a single logarithm?
    ✔ $\log 30$ — Adding logarithms multiplies the arguments.
    ✘ $\log 11$ [fm.logs.sum-inside-the-log] — The plus has gone inside unchanged. Added outside means multiplied inside.
    ✘ $\log 5 \times \log 6$ [fm.logs.product-to-sum] — The logarithms themselves are not multiplied. One logarithm of the product is what the law gives.
- d2 (The quotient law): What is $\log 20 - \log 4$ as a single logarithm?
    ✔ $\log 5$ — Subtracting outside divides inside: $\log\frac{20}{4} = \log 5$.
    ✘ $\log 16$ [fm.logs.sum-inside-the-log] — The minus has gone inside unchanged. Subtracted outside means divided inside.
    ✘ $\frac{\log 20}{\log 4}$ [fm.logs.quotient-as-divided-logs] — Dividing the two logarithms is a different calculation, and it is not the logarithm of anything here.
- d3 (The power law with a coefficient inside): What is $4\log 3x$ as a single logarithm?
    ✔ $\log 81x^{4}$ — $(3x)^{4} = 81x^{4}$: the fourth power reaches the $3$ as well.
    ✘ $\log 3x^{4}$ [fm.logs.coefficient-not-raised] — The power reached the $x$ but not the $3$. Write the bracket $(3x)^{4}$ first.
    ✘ $\log 12x$ [fm.logs.power-as-multiplier-inside] — The $4$ has gone inside as a multiplier rather than as a power.
- d4 (Which term goes underneath): Where does the $c$ go when $\log a + \log b - \log c$ is written as a single logarithm?
    ✔ In the denominator, giving $\log\frac{ab}{c}$ — A subtracted logarithm divides, so its argument goes underneath.
    ✘ In the numerator, giving $\log\frac{ac}{b}$ [fm.logs.subtraction-rule-misapplied] — The subtracted term is $c$, so $c$ is the one that divides. Check which term carries the minus.
    ✘ Outside, giving $\frac{\log ab}{c}$ [fm.logs.quotient-as-divided-logs] — The division happens inside one logarithm, not outside it.
- d5 (A constant written as a logarithm): To base $10$, what is $1 + \log x$ as a single logarithm?
    ✔ $\log 10x$ — $1 = \log 10$, and then the product law gives $\log 10x$.
    ✘ $\log(x + 1)$ [fm.logs.sum-inside-the-log] — The $1$ has gone inside as an addition. It first has to become $\log 10$.
    ✘ $\log x$ [fm.logs.power-as-multiplier-inside] — The $1$ cannot simply be dropped. It is $\log 10$, and it multiplies inside.
- d6 (Equal logarithms, equal arguments): An equation has reached $\log 4x = \log 20$. What comes next?
    ✔ $4x = 20$ — Equal logarithms have equal arguments, so the logarithms are dropped together.
    ✘ $4x = \frac{\log 20}{\log 4}$ [fm.logs.quotient-as-divided-logs] — Nothing needs dividing here. Both sides are already single logarithms.
    ✘ It cannot be taken any further without a calculator [fm.logs.cannot-remove-logs] — Once both sides are one logarithm, they can be dropped and the equation is linear.
- d7 (Expanding, the laws backwards): What is $\log(m^{4}n)$ written as separate logarithms?
    ✔ $4\log m + \log n$ — The product splits into a sum and the power drops down in front.
    ✘ $\log 4m + \log n$ [fm.logs.power-as-multiplier-inside] — The index has become a multiplier inside instead of a coefficient outside.
    ✘ $4\log m \times \log n$ [fm.logs.product-to-sum] — A product inside becomes a sum of logarithms, never a product of them.
- d8 (Dropping logarithms from both sides): It is given that $2\log y = 3\log x$. What is the next line?
    ✔ $\log y^{2} = \log x^{3}$ — Both coefficients go inside as powers, and then the logarithms can be dropped.
    ✘ $y = \frac{3\log x}{2}$ [fm.logs.cannot-remove-logs] — A logarithm has been left on one side. Take both coefficients inside first.
    ✘ $2y = 3x$ [fm.logs.cannot-remove-logs] — The logarithms cannot be dropped while coefficients are still outside them.

### q.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.0014 [verified] exam-style · diff 4 · untiered · FM1 calc · 6 marks · (a)express3|(b)solve3 · A single-logarithm part and an equation, the shape a Unit 1 logarithm question takes
  (a) [3] Express $\log a + 2\log b - \log c$ as a single logarithm.
Give your answer as a single logarithm.
      ANSWER: algebraic \log\frac{ab^{2}}{c} (equivalent)
      M1 M1: $2\log b = \log b^{2}$
      M1 M2: $\log(ab^{2})$
      W1 W1: $\log\frac{ab^{2}}{c}$ ft
      SOLUTION: The coefficient becomes a power: $2\log b = \log b^{2}$. Adding: $\log a + \log b^{2} = \log(ab^{2})$. Subtracting: $\log(ab^{2}) - \log c = \log\frac{ab^{2}}{c}$.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.01 [draft] fm.logs.coefficient-not-raised · earned M1,M2 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2024-summer:FM1:Q6
STEM: Aoife was asked to express $3\log 2x$ as a single logarithm. Her working:
    1. 3 log 2x
    2. = log (2x)^3
  ✘ 3. = log 2x^3
  WRONG: Lines 1 and 2 are exactly right, and line 2 is the line that earns most of the marks: the coefficient is inside and the bracket is there.
Line 3 expands the bracket as though the cube belonged to the $x$ alone. $(2x)^{3}$ means $2x \times 2x \times 2x$, so the $2$ is cubed as well: $(2x)^{3} = 8x^{3}$.
Check it on a number. At $x = 1.5$, $3\log 2x = 1.431364$ and $\log 8x^{3} = 1.431364$, while $\log 2x^{3} = 0.829304$.
  FIX: = log (2x)^3 / = log 8x^3
  FEEDBACK: The method is right and the bracket was written, which is where most of the credit sits. What went is the last mark, and it is the one the Summer 2024 report says the majority lost on this exact step. When you expand a bracket with a power, say the coefficient out loud first: two cubed is eight.

### ftm.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.02 [draft] fm.logs.quotient-as-divided-logs · earned M2,M3,W1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2025-summer:FM1:Q9
STEM: Ruairí was asked to solve $\log(x + 5) - \log(x - 1) = \log 3$. His working:
    1. log(x + 5) - log(x - 1) = log 3
  ✘ 2. log(x + 5) / log(x - 1) = log 3
    3. (x + 5) / (x - 1) = 3
    4. x + 5 = 3x - 3
    5. 8 = 2x, so x = 4
  WRONG: Lines 3, 4 and 5 are all correct work, and the final answer happens to be right.
Line 2 is not. It divides one logarithm by the other, which is a different quantity from $\log\frac{x + 5}{x - 1}$. The line that belongs there is $\log\frac{x + 5}{x - 1} = \log 3$: subtracting logarithms divides inside one logarithm, not outside.
A right answer reached through a wrong line still loses the method mark, because the examiner marks the working.
  FIX: log((x + 5) / (x - 1)) = log 3 / (x + 5) / (x - 1) = 3
  FEEDBACK: Everything after line 2 is sound, so most of the marks stand. The quotient law puts the division inside the logarithm, and the difference matters: $\log 100 - \log 4$ is $\log 25$, while $\log 100 \div \log 4$ is about $3.321928$. Write the single logarithm before you drop it.

### ftm.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.03 [draft] fm.logs.subtraction-rule-misapplied · earned M1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2025-summer:FM1:Q9
STEM: Méabh was asked to express $\log a + 2\log b - \log c$ as a single logarithm. Her working:

    2. = log a + log b^2 - log c

Line 3 gathers the wrong pair. The minus sign belongs to $\log c$, so it is $c$ that divides, and $b^{2}$ that multiplies. The line should read $\log(ab^{2}) - \log c$.
The finished single logarithm is $\log\frac{ab^{2}}{c}$, with the subtracted term underneath.
  FIX: = log(a b^2) - log c / = log(a b^2 / c)
  FEEDBACK: The power law was applied correctly and that mark stands. Before you combine, underline the term that carries the minus: that one, and only that one, goes underneath. The Summer 2025 report names this swap directly.

## RETRIEVAL PROMPTS
- rp.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.01 [draft] (formula) Write the three laws of logarithms.  →  $\log a + \log b = \log(ab)$, $\log a - \log b = \log\frac{a}{b}$, and $n\log a = \log a^{n}$.
- rp.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.02 [draft] (trap) What is $\log a + \log b$, and what is it not?  →  It is $\log(ab)$. It is not $\log(a + b)$: the plus outside becomes a times inside.
- rp.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.03 [draft] (trap) What is $\log a - \log b$, and what is it not?  →  It is $\log\frac{a}{b}$. It is not $\frac{\log a}{\log b}$: the division happens inside one logarithm.
- rp.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.04 [draft] (procedure) In what order do you combine several logarithms into one?  →  Coefficients first, turning each into a power; then add the terms to multiply inside; then subtract, putting that argument underneath.
- rp.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.05 [draft] (trap) Write $3\log 2x$ as a single logarithm.  →  $\log(2x)^{3} = \log 8x^{3}$. The cube reaches the $2$ as well as the $x$.
- rp.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.06 [draft] (qa) To base $10$, how do you write the number $2$ as a logarithm?  →  $2 = \log 100$, because $10^{2} = 100$. A constant has to become a logarithm before the laws can combine it.
- rp.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.07 [draft] (procedure) How do you expand $\log\frac{x^{3}}{y}$ into separate logarithms?  →  The division becomes a subtraction and the power drops down: $3\log x - \log y$.
- rp.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.08 [draft] (qa) An equation has reached $\log P = \log Q$. What can you do?  →  Drop both logarithms: $P = Q$. Each side must be a single logarithm first.
- rp.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.09 [draft] (trap) You are told $\log 3 = p$ and $\log 5 = q$ but you need $\log 2$. What do you do?  →  Build it: $2 = \frac{10}{5}$, so $\log 2 = \log 10 - \log 5 = 1 - q$, because $\log 10 = 1$ to base ten.
- rp.fm.u1.laws-of-logarithms.10 [draft] (trap) From $2\log y = 3\log x$, how do you reach $y$ in terms of $x$?  →  Take both coefficients inside: $\log y^{2} = \log x^{3}$, so $y^{2} = x^{3}$ and $y = x^{\frac{3}{2}}$.
