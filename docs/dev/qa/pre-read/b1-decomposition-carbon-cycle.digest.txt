STEM: A bag of leaf litter at the bottom of a pond fell from 60 g to 48 g over 12 weeks. Readings were taken every 4 weeks.

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.b1.b1-decomposition-carbon-cycle (pre) [verified]
- 01 (One arrow out, three arrows in): Which process removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere?
    ✔ photosynthesis — Photosynthesis, and nothing else on this cycle. Green plants take carbon dioxide in and lock the carbon into glucose.
    ✘ respiration [sci.carbon.respiration-in-both-columns] — Respiration always produces carbon dioxide. Summer 2025 examiners reported it written in both columns of the table.
    ✘ combustion [sci.carbon.photosynthesis-adds-co2] — Burning a fuel releases carbon dioxide. It is one of the three that add.
    ✘ decomposition [sci.carbon.photosynthesis-adds-co2] — Decomposers respire as they break dead material down, so decomposition adds carbon dioxide.
- 02 (Extracellular digestion): Where does a saprophytic fungus digest its food?
    ✔ outside its cells, on the dead material — Extracellular digestion. The enzymes go out to the food, because the food cannot come in.
    ✘ inside its cells, after swallowing it [sci.decomposition.enzymes-not-secreted] — A fungus has no mouth and cannot swallow. It secretes enzymes onto the food and absorbs the products.
    ✘ in a stomach at the base of the hypha [sci.decomposition.enzymes-not-secreted] — Fungi have no digestive system at all. Everything happens outside the cell.
    ✘ in the soil, once the material has dissolved by itself [sci.decomposition.enzymes-not-secreted] — Dead leaves do not dissolve on their own. The fungus has to secrete enzymes to break them down.
- 03 (Warm, moist, aerobic): In which conditions would leaf litter decay fastest?
    ✔ warm, moist, plenty of air — All three together. Decomposers are living organisms, so they work fastest when their own conditions are right.
    ✘ cold, moist, plenty of air [sci.decomposition.decay-conditions-wrong] — Cold slows the decomposers' enzymes, so decay is slower. That is why food keeps in a fridge.
    ✘ warm, waterlogged, no air [sci.decomposition.decay-conditions-wrong] — Waterlogging drives the air out, so the decomposers cannot respire aerobically and decay is very slow.
    ✘ hot, dry, plenty of air [sci.decomposition.decay-conditions-wrong] — Without water the decomposers cannot grow and their enzymes cannot work in solution.
- 04 (Saprophyte, not parasite): What is a saprophyte?
    ✔ an organism that feeds on dead material — Dead material — that is what sapro- means. Fungi and bacteria are the saprophytes on this specification.
    ✘ an organism that feeds on a living host and harms it [sci.decomposition.saprophyte-called-parasite] — That is a parasite. A saprophyte feeds on material that is already dead.
    ✘ an organism that makes its own food from light [sci.decomposition.saprophyte-called-parasite] — That is a producer. Fungi have no chlorophyll and cannot photosynthesise.
    ✘ an animal that eats other animals' remains [sci.decomposition.saprophyte-called-parasite] — A scavenger swallows its food. A saprophyte digests it outside its body and absorbs the products.
- 05 (Divide by the whole time): Leaf litter fell from 60 g to 48 g over 12 weeks, with readings taken every 4 weeks. What is the rate in grams per week?
    ✔ 1 — 12 g lost ÷ 12 weeks = 1 g per week.
    ✘ 3 [sci.rate.divided-by-wrong-time] — Divided by the 4-week gap between readings instead of the 12 weeks the experiment ran. March 2026 examiners named this exact error.
    ✘ 12 [sci.rate.divided-by-wrong-time] — That is the total mass lost, not a rate. A rate needs the division by time.
    ✘ 48 [sci.rate.divided-by-wrong-time] — That is the mass left at the end. Start by subtracting to find how much was lost.
- 06 (Answer the question that was asked): A results table shows all three bags losing mass, A fastest and C slowest. The question asks for the overall trend. Which answer scores?
    ✔ In every environment the mass of leaf litter decreased as time increased. — That is the overall trend: one sentence covering all three sets of results.
    ✘ Environment A decomposed fastest because it was warm and had air. [sci.graph.fastest-given-not-trend] — True, but it answers a different question. March 2026 examiners reported candidates giving the fastest conditions when the trend was asked for.
    ✘ Environment C decomposed the slowest. [sci.graph.fastest-given-not-trend] — A single comparison is not a trend. A trend says what happens as one variable changes.
    ✘ The mass changed over the twelve weeks. [sci.graph.trend-not-from-data] — 'Changed' gives no direction. Say that the mass decreased as time increased.

      SOLUTION: The carbon in the leaf came from carbon dioxide in the air, taken in by the oak tree and built into glucose by photosynthesis. Once the leaf falls, saprophytic fungi and bacteria feed on it. They secrete enzymes out onto the leaf, the enzymes digest its large insoluble molecules outside their cells — extracellular digestion — and the small soluble products are absorbed into the decomposers.
