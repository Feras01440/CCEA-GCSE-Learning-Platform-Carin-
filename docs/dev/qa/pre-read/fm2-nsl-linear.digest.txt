### we.fm.u2.newtons-second-law-linear.01 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: A box of mass 7 kg is pulled along a rough horizontal floor by a horizontal force of 40 N. The resistance to motion is 12 N.

Calculate the acceleration of the box.

Take $g = 10$ m/s².
  FIG: svg — A box of mass 7 kilograms on a horizontal floor, with a driving force of 40 newtons to the right, a resistance of 12 newtons to the left, the normal reaction R upwards and the weight of 70 newtons downwards. A separate open arrow above the box marks the acceleration to the right.
  1. For the box, taking the direction of motion as positive:   — Name the body and the direction before any numbers. Examiners report forces on one body confused with forces on a whole system, and this line is what prevents it.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because F = ma applies to one named body, and the forces must be that body's own | ✘Because it is the conventional way to lay out the page | ✘Because the direction of motion is always to the right
  2. Resultant $= 40 - 12 = 28$ N   — $F$ in $F = ma$ is the resultant along the line of motion, not one of the forces. Write the subtraction out so the resistance is visibly taken off.  (MW1)
  3. $28 = 7a$, so $a = 4$ m/s²   — The $m$ is the mass in kilograms. The $70$ N on the diagram is the weight, and it plays no part here because the box is not moving vertically.  (W1)
  FINAL: $a = 4$ m/s²

### we.fm.u2.newtons-second-law-linear.02 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: A lift of total mass 500 kg hangs on a vertical cable. The tension in the cable is 5600 N.

Calculate the acceleration of the lift, and state its direction.

Take $g = 10$ m/s².
  FIG: svg — A lift of mass 500 kilograms hanging on a cable, with the tension of 5600 newtons acting upwards and the weight of 5000 newtons downwards, and an open arrow marking the acceleration upwards.
  1. Weight $= 500 \times 10 = 5000$ N   — The diagram needs the weight in newtons, so convert the mass once and write it on the arrow. This is the only place the two meet.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because a force diagram is drawn in newtons, and a mass in kilograms is not a force | ✘Because the mass must be converted before it can go into F = ma | ✘Because weight and mass are two words for the same quantity
  2. Taking up as positive: $5600 - 5000 = 500a$   — State the positive direction, then write the resultant in that direction. The cable pulls up and gravity pulls down, so the resultant is the difference.  (MW1)
  3. $600 = 500a$, so $a = 1.2$ m/s²   — The answer is positive, and up was positive, so the acceleration is upwards. Say so: a direction is part of the answer here.  (W1)
  FINAL: $a = 1.2$ m/s², directed upwards
  TWIN: A lift of total mass 400 kg hangs on a cable in which the tension is 4400 N. Calculate the magnitude of its acceleration, in m/s².  → numeric 1 m/s² tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u2.newtons-second-law-linear.pre (pre) [verified]
- 01 (Prerequisite: weight from mass): From an earlier topic: a body has a mass of 6 kg. What is its weight, taking $g = 10$ m/s²?
    ✔ $60$ N — Weight $= mg = 6 \times 10 = 60$ N.
    ✘ $6$ N — That is the mass with a newton sign on it. Multiply by $g$ to turn a mass into a force.
    ✘ $0.6$ N — That divides by $g$. Weight is the mass multiplied by $g$.
    ✘ $6$ kg — That is the mass again. A weight is a force, measured in newtons.
- 02 (Prerequisite: rearranging a formula): From an earlier topic: rearrange $F = ma$ to make $a$ the subject.
    ✔ $a = \dfrac{F}{m}$ — $m$ is multiplying $a$, so divide both sides by $m$.
    ✘ $a = \dfrac{m}{F}$ — The fraction is upside down. Dividing both sides by $m$ leaves $F$ on top.
    ✘ $a = F - m$ — $ma$ is a product, so $m$ cannot be removed by subtracting it.
    ✘ $a = Fm$ — Multiplying by $m$ undoes nothing; it is division that clears it.
- 03 (Prerequisite: a rate per unit): From an earlier topic: a rope costs 4 pounds per metre. What do 8 metres cost?
    ✔ £32 — $4 \times 8 = 32$: a rate per unit is multiplied by the number of units.
    ✘ £4 — That is the cost of one metre. Eight metres cost eight times as much.
    ✘ £2 — That divides instead of multiplying.
    ✘ £12 — That adds the two numbers. A rate per unit is multiplied.

### dx.fm.u2.newtons-second-law-linear.post (post) [verified]
- 04 (Mass or weight in F = ma): A box of mass 5 kg, weight 50 N, is pushed by a resultant force of 30 N. What is its acceleration?
    ✔ $6$ m/s² — $30 = 5a$, so $a = 6$ m/s². The $m$ in $F = ma$ is the mass in kilograms.
    ✘ $0.6$ m/s² [fm.connected.multiplies-weight-by-a] — That divides by the weight, 50 N. The weight belongs on the diagram, not in the equation.
    ✘ $150$ m/s² [fm.connected.mass-force-confused] — That multiplies instead of dividing. $F = ma$ rearranges to $a = F \div m$.
    ✘ $25$ m/s² [fm.connected.mass-force-confused] — That subtracts one number from the other. The relationship is a product, not a difference.
- 05 (The resultant, not one force): A body is pushed by 60 N against a resistance of 20 N. Which number goes into $F = ma$?
    ✔ $40$ N — $F$ is the resultant along the line of motion: $60 - 20 = 40$ N.
    ✘ $60$ N [fm.new.resultant-not-formed] — That is the driving force alone. The resistance is still acting, so it comes off first.
    ✘ $80$ N [fm.new.resistance-added] — The resistance opposes the motion, so it is subtracted rather than added.
    ✘ $20$ N [fm.new.resultant-not-formed] — That is the resistance alone, which acts backwards.
- 06 (A resistance given per kilogram): A resistance of 5 N per kg acts on a body of mass 9 kg. What is the resisting force?
    ✔ $45$ N — $5 \times 9 = 45$ N: every kilogram is resisted by 5 N.
    ✘ $5$ N [fm.new.friction-per-kg-not-converted] — That is the force on one kilogram. Multiply by the mass to get the whole force.
    ✘ $1.8$ N [fm.new.friction-per-kg-not-converted] — That divides the mass by the rate. A rate per kilogram is multiplied by the mass.
    ✘ $0.56$ [fm.new.friction-per-kg-not-converted] — That divides the other way round, and a force needs a unit.
- 07 (A lift on a cable): A lift of mass 300 kg hangs on a cable with tension 3300 N. Taking up as positive and $g = 10$ m/s², which equation is right?
    ✔ $3300 - 3000 = 300a$ — The resultant upwards is the tension minus the weight, and the weight is $300 \times 10 = 3000$ N.
    ✘ $3300 = 300a$ [fm.forces.g-omitted-weight] — The weight is still acting. Leaving it out is the commonest slip on a vertical problem.
    ✘ $3300 + 3000 = 300a$ [fm.new.resistance-added] — The weight acts downwards while the tension acts upwards, so they subtract.
    ✘ $3300 - 300 = 300a$ [fm.connected.mass-force-confused] — That subtracts the mass rather than the weight. A force diagram is in newtons.
- 08 (What a 'show that' asks for): A part says 'Show that the mass is 650 kg'. What earns the marks?
    ✔ Forming the equation of motion and solving it for the mass — The forward route is what is being marked: build the equation from the diagram, then solve.
    ✘ Putting 650 into the equation and showing both sides agree [fm.show-that.verify-by-substitution] — That is verification, and CCEA caps it at two marks. It assumes what the part asks you to derive.
    ✘ Writing down 650 kg with its units [fm.show-that.verify-by-substitution] — The answer is already printed in the question, so writing it again shows nothing.
    ✘ Drawing the force diagram on its own [fm.show-that.verify-by-substitution] — The diagram is a good first step, but the equation and the solving are where the marks are.
