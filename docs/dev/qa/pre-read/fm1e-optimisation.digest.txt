## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.fm.u1.optimisation.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: A rectangular flower bed is made against a straight wall. The other three sides are edged with $48$ m of timber. The two sides at right angles to the wall are each $x$ m long.
Find the value of $x$ that gives the greatest possible area, show that it is a maximum, and state that greatest area.
  FIG: svg — A rectangular flower bed against a wall, with two sides of x metres and the side facing the wall completing the 48 metres of timber
  1. The timber covers two sides of $x$ and one side of $y$, so $2x + y = 48$ and $y = 48 - 2x$.   — The wall is the fourth side and costs nothing, which is the only reason this problem has an answer. Reading that from the picture is the first mark.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because the wall replaces one of the four sides | ✘Because the bed is a square | ✘Because x and y are always equal
  2. The area is $A = xy = x(48 - 2x) = -2x^{2} + 48x$.   — Substituting the constraint leaves one letter, which is what differentiation needs. Expand it now: an unsimplified expression is where slips start.  (MW1)
  3. Differentiate: $\frac{dA}{dx} = -4x + 48$.   — Each term separately: multiply by the power, step the power down. The term in x contributes its coefficient, never nothing.  (MW1)
  4. Set the gradient to zero: $-4x + 48 = 0$, so $4x = 48$ and $x = 12$.   — A largest or smallest value sits where the quantity stops changing, so the derivative is zero there.  (M1,W1)
  5. Differentiate again: $\frac{d^{2}A}{dx^{2}} = -4$, which is negative, so $x = 12$ gives a maximum.   — The question is not answered until the turning point has been named. A negative second derivative is the evidence the scheme pays for.  (MW1)
  6. Substitute back: $y = 48 - 2 \times 12 = 24$ m, and $A = 12 \times 24 = 288$ m².   — The question asked for the area, not for x. Finishing at $x = 12$ leaves the last marks on the table, and the unit belongs on the answer line.  (W1)  WHY: ✔Because the question asked for an area, not a length | ✘Because x is always the answer | ✘Because the units are the same either way
  FINAL: $x = 12$ m gives a maximum, because $\frac{d^{2}A}{dx^{2}} = -4$, and the greatest area is $288$ m².
  TWIN: A rectangular pen is built against a barn wall using $36$ m of fencing for the two ends, the far side and one divider parallel to the ends, each end being $x$ m.
The area is $A = -3x^{2} + 36x$. Find the value of $x$ that gives the greatest area.  → numeric 6 tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction

### we.fm.u1.optimisation.02 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: A rectangular vegetable plot of area $54$ m² is fenced round all four sides and has one more fence across it, parallel to the two shorter sides. The plot is $x$ m across.
Find the value of $x$ that uses the least fencing, show that it is a minimum, and state that least length.
  FIG: svg — A rectangular vegetable plot with one extra fence across the middle, 9 metres across and 6 metres deep
  1. The area fixes the depth: $xy = 54$, so $y = \frac{54}{x}$.   — Here the constraint is an area rather than a length, so the substitution produces a fraction instead of a subtraction.  (MW1)
  2. The fencing is two sides of $x$, two sides of $y$ and one more $y$ across the middle: $L = 2x + 3y = 2x + \frac{162}{x}$.   — Count the fences on the picture one at a time. Leaving the middle fence out is the single commonest way this expression goes wrong.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because the extra fence is a third side of length y | ✘Because the extra fence is a third side of length x | ✘Because the extra fence has no length
  3. Write the fraction as a power and differentiate: $L = 2x + 162x^{-1}$, so $\frac{dL}{dx} = 2 - \frac{162}{x^{2}}$.   — The power rule only speaks about powers, so the fraction has to come up as a negative index first. The index steps down from -1 to -2.  (MW1)
  4. Set it to zero: $\frac{162}{x^{2}} = 2$, so $x^{2} = 81$ and $x = 9$.   — Take the square root, and take only the positive one: a length cannot be negative, so the other root is discarded with a word.  (M1,W1)
  5. Second derivative: $\frac{d^{2}L}{dx^{2}} = \frac{324}{x^{3}}$. At $x = 9$ that is $\frac{4}{9}$, which is positive, so this is a minimum.   — This second derivative depends on x, so the value of x has to be put in before the sign can be read. A general statement is not evidence.  (MW1)
  6. Substitute back: $y = \frac{54}{9} = 6$ m, and $L = 2 \times 9 + 3 \times 6 = 36$ m.   — Check the answer against the picture: a plot 9 m by 6 m has an area of 54 m², so the constraint is satisfied and the arithmetic holds.  (W1)
  FINAL: $x = 9$ m gives a minimum, because $\frac{d^{2}L}{dx^{2}} = \frac{4}{9}$ there, and the least fencing is $36$ m.
  TWIN: A rectangular field of area $288$ m² lies beside a river and is fenced on three sides only, the side parallel to the river being $x$ m.
The fencing is $L = x + \frac{576}{x}$ m. Find the value of $x$ that uses the least fencing.  → numeric 24 tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction

### we.fm.u1.optimisation.03 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: A notice is printed on a rectangular card. The printed part has an area of $300$ cm² and is $w$ cm wide. There is a margin of $3$ cm at each side and $4$ cm at the top and at the bottom.
Show that the total area of the card is $A = 8w + 348 + \frac{1800}{w}$ cm², then find the least possible total area.
  FIG: svg — A notice with a printed rectangle of area 300 square centimetres and margins of 3 centimetres at the sides and 4 centimetres at the top and bottom
  1. The printed part is $w$ by $h$ with $wh = 300$, so $h = \frac{300}{w}$.   — The fixed printed area is the constraint. Naming the printed height h before using it keeps the two rectangles apart in your head.  (MW1)
  2. The card is wider by two side margins and taller by two end margins: $(w + 6)$ by $\left(h + 8\right)$.   — Two margins, not one, on each measurement. Halving this is the slip that makes the whole answer plausible and wrong.  (MW1)
  3. Multiply out: $A = wh + 8w + 6h + 48 = 300 + 8w + 6\left(\frac{300}{w}\right) + 48 = 8w + 348 + \frac{1800}{w}$.   — Every line of a show-that earns its own mark, so write the expansion before the tidy version. The printed result is the last line you write, never the first.  (MW1,W1)
  4. Differentiate: $\frac{dA}{dw} = 8 - \frac{1800}{w^{2}}$.   — The constant 348 goes to zero and the fraction is differentiated as 1800w to the power -1, whose index steps down to -2.  (MW1)
  5. Set it to zero: $\frac{1800}{w^{2}} = 8$, so $w^{2} = 225$ and $w = 15$ cm.   — Only the positive root is a width. Leaving the answer as w squared is the last place this question is commonly abandoned.  (M1,W1)
  6. Second derivative: $\frac{d^{2}A}{dw^{2}} = \frac{3600}{w^{3}}$, which at $w = 15$ is $\frac{16}{15}$: positive, so the area is least here.   — Positive means the gradient is climbing through zero, which is a dip, and a dip in area is the smallest card.  (MW1)
  7. The least total area is $A = 348 + 8 \times 15 + \frac{1800}{15} = 588$ cm².   — That is a card $21$ cm by $28$ cm, which multiplies back to $588$, so the answer checks against the picture.  (W1)
  FINAL: $w = 15$ cm gives a minimum, and the least total area of the card is $588$ cm².
  TWIN: A workshop makes $x$ trays a week, and the cost of making each tray is $C = 2x + 15 + \frac{800}{x}$ pounds.
Find the number of trays a week that makes the cost of each tray least.  → numeric 20 tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.optimisation.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Rearrange a product to make one letter the subject): Three checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new method, so answer from what you already know.
A rectangle $x$ m by $y$ m has area $54$ m². Write $y$ in terms of $x$.
    ✔ $y = \frac{54}{x}$ — Dividing both sides of $xy = 54$ by $x$ leaves $y$ on its own. That fraction is what turns an optimisation problem into one letter.
    ✘ $y = 54 - x$ — Subtracting undoes an addition. Here $x$ and $y$ are multiplied, so the opposite operation is division.
    ✘ $y = 54x$ — That would make $y$ grow with $x$. A fixed area means the two measurements work against each other.
- p2 (Differentiate a negative power): Differentiate $y = 5x^{-1}$.
    ✔ $-5x^{-2}$ — Multiply by the power $-1$ and step the index down to $-2$.
    ✘ $5x^{-2}$ — The coefficient is multiplied by the power, and that power is $-1$, so the sign changes.
    ✘ $-5x^{0}$ — The index steps down from $-1$, and down from $-1$ is $-2$.
- p3 (Take a square root): Solve $x^{2} = 81$ for the positive value of $x$.
    ✔ $x = 9$ — Both roots are $9$ and $-9$; a length takes the positive one.
    ✘ $x = 81$ [fm.optim.square-root-not-taken] — That is the value of $x^{2}$. The square root still has to be taken before you have $x$.
    ✘ $x = 40.5$ — Halving undoes doubling, not squaring. The opposite of squaring is the square root.

### dx.fm.u1.optimisation.post (post) [verified]
- d1 (Read a constraint from a picture): A bed against a wall uses $48$ m of edging on three sides, the two ends being $x$ m each. Which constraint is right?
    ✔ $2x + y = 48$ — Two ends and one front, because the wall takes the fourth side.
    ✘ $2x + 2y = 48$ [fm.optim.wall-side-fenced] — That fences all four sides and ignores the wall. It would give a greatest area of $144$ m² instead of $288$ m².
    ✘ $xy = 48$ [fm.optim.constraint-not-read-from-diagram] — That is an area of 48 m², but 48 m of edging is a length. The constraint has to add up the sides.
- d2 (Know the step after solving the derivative): You have solved $\frac{dA}{dx} = 0$ and found a value of $x$. What does the scheme want next?
    ✔ The second derivative, with its value and sign — A stationary value is not yet a maximum. The second derivative is the evidence the mark is for.
    ✘ Nothing more; the value of $x$ is the answer [fm.optim.nature-not-shown] — The question asks for a maximum or a minimum, so the turning point has to be named before the answer is complete.
    ✘ A table of values on either side [fm.optim.nature-not-shown] — A sign check either side is valid mathematics, but where the stem says using calculus the second derivative is the expected evidence.
- d3 (Answer the quantity the question asked for): The bed's area is $A = -2x^{2} + 48x$ and the greatest area is wanted. You have found $x = 12$. What do you write on the answer line?
    ✔ $288$ m² — Substituting $12$ back into $A$ gives $288$, and an area carries square metres.
    ✘ $12$ m [fm.optim.final-quantity-not-evaluated] — That is the width that produces the greatest area, not the area itself. The last step is to substitute it back.
    ✘ $24$ m [fm.optim.final-quantity-not-evaluated] — That is the other side of the bed. Both lengths are useful working, and neither is the area that was asked for.
- d4 (Read the sign of the second derivative): A quantity has $\frac{d^{2}P}{dx^{2}} = \frac{4}{9}$ at its stationary value. What has been shown?
    ✔ That the stationary value is a minimum — A positive second derivative means the gradient is climbing through zero, which is a dip.
    ✘ That the stationary value is a maximum [fm.calc.second-derivative-sign-misread] — The test runs the other way: positive names a minimum and negative names a maximum.
    ✘ Nothing, because the value is a fraction [fm.optim.nature-not-shown] — Only the sign matters, and a fraction has a sign like any other number. This one is positive.
- d5 (Differentiate a sum with a plain term in x and a negative power): Differentiate $L = x + \frac{576}{x}$.
    ✔ $1 - \frac{576}{x^{2}}$ — The plain $x$ contributes $1$, and $576x^{-1}$ gives $-576x^{-2}$.
    ✘ $-\frac{576}{x^{2}}$ [fm.calc.linear-term-derivative-omitted] — The fraction has been handled correctly and the plain $x$ has vanished. The derivative of $x$ is $1$, so that $1$ belongs in the answer.
    ✘ $1 + \frac{576}{x^{2}}$ [fm.calc.negative-power-differentiation] — The index is right. Multiplying $576$ by the power $-1$ makes that term negative.

### q.fm.u1.optimisation.0011 [verified] practice · diff 4 · untiered · FM1 calc · 3 marks · (main)find3 · Two pens against a barn wall
  METHOD LOCK: Using calculus
