## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.b2.b2-osmosis.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Recall the cell membrane's function (B1)): Before we start, three things from B1. Which structure controls what enters and leaves a cell?
    ✔ the cell membrane — The membrane. Everything in this topic happens across it.
    ✘ the cell wall [sci.cells.wall-membrane-confused] — The wall is outside the membrane and gives support. The membrane is the one that chooses what crosses.
    ✘ the vacuole [sci.cells.vacuole-function-unknown] — The vacuole stores cell sap. Controlling entry and exit is the membrane's job.
    ✘ the nucleus [sci.cells.wall-membrane-confused] — The nucleus holds the chromosomes and controls the cell's activities, not what crosses its edge.
- p2 (Compare plant and animal cells (B1)): Which structure does a plant cell have that an animal cell does not?
    ✔ a cell wall — The cell wall — and it turns out to be the reason a plant cell survives being put in pure water.
    ✘ a cell membrane [sci.cells.plant-only-list-wrong] — Both kinds of cell have a membrane. The wall is the plant-only one.
    ✘ mitochondria [sci.cells.plant-only-list-wrong] — Both respire, so both have mitochondria.
    ✘ a nucleus [sci.cells.plant-only-list-wrong] — Both have a nucleus. Bacteria are the ones without.
- p3 (Recall diffusion (B1)): Diffusion moves molecules…
    ✔ from where there are more of them to where there are fewer — Down the concentration gradient, with no energy needed. Osmosis is a special case of exactly this.
    ✘ from where there are fewer of them to where there are more [sci.osmosis.direction-reversed] — That direction needs energy, and it is called active transport. Diffusion goes the other way.
    ✘ only when the cell uses energy from respiration [sci.active-transport.water-by-active-transport] — Diffusion is free: it is just molecules moving about at random and spreading out.
    ✘ only through a cell wall [sci.cells.wall-membrane-confused] — Diffusion happens anywhere molecules are free to move, including in air and in liquids with no cell in sight.

### dx.science.b2.b2-osmosis (post) [verified]
- 01 (State the definition the specification gives): Osmosis is the movement of water molecules through a partially permeable membrane…
    ✔ from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution — That is the specification's wording. Water goes to where there is less of it.
    ✘ from a more concentrated solution to a dilute solution [sci.osmosis.direction-reversed] — That is the direction the sugar would go if it could fit through. Water moves the other way: towards the more concentrated solution.
    ✘ from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution, using energy from respiration [sci.active-transport.water-by-active-transport] — The direction is right, but osmosis needs no energy — it is a kind of diffusion. Energy from respiration belongs to active transport.
    ✘ between two solutions of equal concentration [sci.osmosis.direction-reversed] — Between equal concentrations there is no net movement at all, so nothing would change.
- 02 (Predict the direction of water movement): A carrot cylinder is left in pure water. What happens to its mass, and why?
    ✔ It increases, because water moves into the cells by osmosis — Pure water is the most dilute solution there is, so water moves into the cell sap.
    ✘ It decreases, because water moves out of the cells by osmosis [sci.osmosis.direction-reversed] — Water moves towards the more concentrated solution, and that is the cell sap inside the carrot, not the pure water outside.
    ✘ It increases, because sugar moves into the cells from the water [sci.osmosis.sucrose-crosses-membrane] — There is no sugar in pure water, and sugar molecules are too large to cross the membrane anyway. Only the water moves.
    ✘ It stays the same, because the cell wall stops water entering [sci.osmosis.cell-wall-limits-entry] — The wall limits how much water can enter, which is why the cell does not burst, but water does get in and the mass does rise.
- 03 (Describe a plasmolysed cell): In a plasmolysed plant cell, which structure has moved?
    ✔ the cell membrane, which has pulled away from the cell wall — That is the sentence the scheme wants: the membrane pulls away from the wall.
    ✘ the cell wall, which has pulled away from the membrane [sci.osmosis.wall-moves-not-membrane] — The wall is rigid and stays where it is. It is the membrane, with the shrinking contents, that comes away from it.
    ✘ the nucleus, which has moved into the vacuole [sci.osmosis.turgor-plasmolysis-swapped] — The nucleus is not what the question is about. Look at the outside of the cell contents: the membrane has left the wall.
    ✘ nothing has moved; the whole cell has simply shrunk [sci.osmosis.wall-moves-not-membrane] — The contents shrink, but the wall does not, and the gap that opens between them is the point of the word plasmolysed.
- 04 (Calculate a percentage change in mass): A cylinder starts at 4.50 g and finishes at 4.86 g. What is the percentage change in mass?
    ✔ +8.0% — Change ÷ starting mass × 100 = 0.36 ÷ 4.50 × 100.
    ✘ +7.4% [sci.quant.percentage-wrong-denominator] — That is the change divided by the FINAL mass. A percentage change is always measured against the mass you started with.
    ✘ +0.36% [sci.quant.change-given-not-percentage] — That is the change in grams, not a percentage. Divide it by the starting mass and multiply by 100.
    ✘ −8.0% [sci.quant.change-subtracted-backwards] — Starting mass minus final mass gives the change the wrong way round. The cylinder gained mass here, so the answer is positive.
- 05 (Give the role of the cell wall): Why does a plant cell in pure water become turgid rather than bursting?
    ✔ The strong cell wall limits how much water can enter — Once the wall is pushed out as far as it will go, no more water can get in. That is the wall's job in this topic.
    ✘ The cell membrane stops any more water entering [sci.osmosis.cell-wall-limits-entry] — The membrane is partially permeable and lets water through freely. It is the wall outside it that limits the entry of water.
    ✘ The vacuole stores the extra water safely [sci.osmosis.cell-wall-limits-entry] — The vacuole does fill up, but it is the wall that stops the cell expanding any further and bursting.
    ✘ Plant cells do not take in water by osmosis [sci.osmosis.direction-reversed] — They do, and that is why they become turgid. What the wall changes is the ending, not the process.
- 06 (Contrast an animal cell with a plant cell): A red blood cell is placed in pure water. What happens to it?
    ✔ Water enters by osmosis and the cell bursts — An animal cell has no cell wall, so nothing limits the water entering and the membrane splits.
    ✘ Water enters by osmosis and the cell becomes turgid [sci.osmosis.animal-cell-becomes-turgid] — Turgid is a plant-cell word: it needs a wall for the contents to press against. An animal cell simply swells until it bursts.
    ✘ Water leaves by osmosis and the cell shrinks [sci.osmosis.direction-reversed] — Pure water is more dilute than the cytoplasm, so water moves in, not out. It shrinks in a concentrated solution instead.
    ✘ Nothing happens, because the membrane is not permeable to water [sci.osmosis.cell-wall-limits-entry] — The membrane is partially permeable, and water is exactly the molecule it lets through.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.science.b2.b2-osmosis.01 [verified] sci.quant.percentage-wrong-denominator · earned P1 · ccea-cer:science:2025-summer:U7ABioH:Q1
STEM: Aoife is asked: 'A carrot cylinder of mass 5.00 g had a mass of 4.70 g after 24 hours in sucrose solution. Calculate the percentage change in mass, to one decimal place. [2]'
Her working:
    1. change in mass = 4.70 − 5.00 = −0.30 g
  ✘ 2. percentage change = −0.30 ÷ 4.70 × 100
    3. = -6.4%
  WRONG: Line 1 is right, minus sign and all. Line 2 divides by 4.70 g, the mass at the END. A percentage change is always measured against the value you started with, so the divisor should be 5.00 g.
  FIX: change in mass = 4.70 − 5.00 = −0.30 g / percentage change = −0.30 ÷ 5.00 × 100 / = −6.0%
  FEEDBACK: One mark of the two: the change in mass is correct and carries its minus sign, which the Summer 2025 practical examiners singled out as a mark candidates lose. The second mark goes because of the denominator. Write the divisor down before you reach for the calculator, and say to yourself 'out of the mass I started with'.
