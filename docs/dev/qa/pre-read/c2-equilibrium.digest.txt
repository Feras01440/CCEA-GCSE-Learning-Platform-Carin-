### we.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.01 [verified] paper C2 calc
STEM: Blue crystals of hydrated copper(II) sulfate are heated in a test tube. The solid turns white and droplets of liquid collect at the cool end of the tube.
(a) Name the white solid. [1]
(b) Write the word equation for the reaction, using the sign for a reversible reaction. [2]
(c) Describe what you would see if water were added to the white solid. [2]
  FIG: svg — Two test tubes: heating blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate drives off water and leaves a white solid, and adding water to the white solid turns it blue again and gives out heat.
  1. The white solid is anhydrous copper(II) sulfate.   — Anhydrous means the water has gone. Keep the (II), because copper has more than one oxidation state and the scheme is case- and number-sensitive.  (P1)
  2. hydrated copper(II) sulfate ⇌ anhydrous copper(II) sulfate + water   — Reactant on the left, both products on the right. The water is a product here, not a spectator, because it has left the crystal.  (P2)
  3. Use the ⇌ sign, not →.   — The question asked for the reversible sign, so it is a mark of its own. Summer 2024's report names the missing equilibrium sign as a common loss.  (P3)  WHY: ✔Because the reaction can be driven back by adding water | ✘Because the reaction gives out heat | ✘Because there are two products
  4. Adding water: the white solid turns blue.   — Give both colours. 'It turns blue' without saying what it was is worth less than 'white to blue', and a single colour on its own was not accepted in Summer 2024.  (P4)
  5. The tube also becomes hot: heat is given out.   — A second observation, and a different kind from the first. Say 'heat given out', not just 'heat', and never 'exothermic', which is a deduction rather than something you can see or feel.  (P5)
  FINAL: (a) Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate. (b) hydrated copper(II) sulfate ⇌ anhydrous copper(II) sulfate + water. (c) The white solid turns blue and the tube becomes hot, because heat is given out.

### we.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.02 [verified] paper C2 calc
STEM: A reversible reaction is left to run in a sealed vessel. The graph shows the rate of the forward reaction and the rate of the reverse reaction against time.
(a) Describe what happens to each rate during the first minute. [2]
(b) State what is true of the two rates once equilibrium has been reached. [1]
(c) Explain why the amounts of reactants and products then stay constant. [1]
  FIG: svg — Two curves of rate against time in a closed container: the forward rate falls from its starting value, the reverse rate rises from zero, and from the moment they meet both stay equal.
  1. The forward rate starts at its highest, 10, and falls.   — The forward reaction begins with the most reactant it will ever have, so it starts fastest and slows as the reactants are used up.  (P1)
  2. The reverse rate starts at zero and rises.   — At the start there is no product, so there is nothing to react back. As product builds, the reverse reaction speeds up.  (P2)
  3. At 60 s the two rates meet at 4, and from then on they are equal.   — Equal rates is the definition. Note that the two curves meet but neither falls to zero: both reactions are still going.  (P3)
  4. Because product is being made as fast as it is being used up, the amounts of reactants and products remain constant.   — This is the second half of the definition, and it follows from the first. Summer 2024's report records 'the concentration did not change' being offered in place of the rates, which is this half without the half that explains it.  (P4)
  FINAL: (a) The forward rate starts at its highest, 10, and falls; the reverse rate starts at zero and rises. (b) The two rates are equal. (c) Product is formed as fast as it is used up, so the amounts of reactants and products remain constant.

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Prerequisite from C1 §1.5 — reading an equation; you are not expected to have met equilibrium yet): In a chemical equation, what does the arrow mean?
    ✔ turns into — Reactants turn into products. This lesson changes that arrow into one with two heads, and the change of symbol is the whole idea.
    ✘ is equal to [sci.formulae.equation-given-for-formula] — An equation balances, but the arrow is not an equals sign: it shows a direction, which is exactly what this topic is about.
    ✘ is mixed with [sci.formulae.equation-given-for-formula] — Mixing is what the plus sign shows. The arrow shows the change.
- p2 (Prerequisite from c2-measuring-rates): What is the rate of a reaction?
    ✔ how much change happens in each second — An amount divided by a time. This lesson compares two rates in the same container.
    ✘ how much product is made altogether [sci.rates.rate-as-yield] — That is the total, with no time in it. A rate always has a time underneath.
    ✘ how long the reaction takes [sci.rates.rate-inverted] — That is a time on its own. The rate is the change divided by that time.
- p3 (Prerequisite from C1 — open and closed containers): A test tube is sealed with a bung. What can get in or out?
    ✔ nothing — Nothing at all. Section 4 calls that a closed system, and equilibrium needs one.
    ✘ gases only [sci.equilibrium.closed-system-omitted] — A bung stops the gas as well. That is the point of using one, and it is what makes the reverse reaction possible.
    ✘ heat only [sci.equilibrium.closed-system-omitted] — Heat can still pass through the glass, but no substance can. Closed is about substances.

### dx.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (Define a reversible reaction): What does a reversible reaction mean?
    ✔ the products can react to form the reactants again — Both directions are real, which is why the equation carries a double arrow.
    ✘ the reactants can be returned to their original state [sci.equilibrium.reversible-as-original-state] — 'Original state' is the phrasing Summer 2025 examiners refused. Name the substances: the products react to form the reactants again.
    ✘ the reaction can be stopped at any point [sci.equilibrium.reaction-has-stopped] — Stopping a reaction is not reversing it. Reversible means the products turn back into the reactants.
    ✘ the reaction gives out heat one way and takes it in the other [sci.equilibrium.reversible-as-original-state] — That is true of a reversible reaction, but it is not what the word means. The meaning is about direction, not energy.
- 02 (The copper(II) sulfate demonstration): Blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate is heated. What is seen, and what is formed?
    ✔ blue to white; anhydrous copper(II) sulfate and water — Heating drives the water out of the crystals and leaves the white anhydrous solid.
    ✘ blue to black; copper(II) oxide and water [sci.equilibrium.colour-change-unknown] — Copper(II) oxide is black, but it comes from heating the carbonate. Here the water simply leaves the crystals.
    ✘ white to blue; hydrated copper(II) sulfate [sci.equilibrium.colour-change-unknown] — That is the reverse change, the one you see when water is added. Heating goes blue to white.
    ✘ blue to colourless; copper(II) sulfate solution [sci.equilibrium.colour-change-unknown] — A solution is what you get by adding a lot of water. Heating leaves a dry white powder.
- 03 (Define dynamic equilibrium): A reaction in a sealed vessel has reached dynamic equilibrium. Which statement is true?
    ✔ the forward and reverse reactions are going at the same rate — Equal rates. Both reactions are still running, which is what the word dynamic records.
    ✘ the amounts of reactants and products are equal [sci.equilibrium.rates-not-amounts] — The amounts stay constant, but there is no reason for them to be equal to each other. It is the rates that are equal.
    ✘ both reactions have stopped [sci.equilibrium.reaction-has-stopped] — Nothing has stopped. It only looks still because each reaction undoes the other exactly.
    ✘ the forward reaction has finished and the reverse one has started [sci.equilibrium.reaction-has-stopped] — They run at the same time, not one after the other. From the start both are happening.
- 04 (Explain the closed-system condition): Why must the system be closed for a dynamic equilibrium to be reached?
    ✔ so that no substance can escape and the reverse reaction can keep up — A product that has left the container cannot react back, so the two rates could never become equal.
    ✘ so that the temperature cannot change [sci.equilibrium.closed-system-omitted] — Closed is about substances, not heat. Heat still passes through the glass of a stoppered flask.
    ✘ so that the reaction is safe [sci.equilibrium.closed-system-omitted] — Safety may be a reason for using a bung, but it is not the reason the definition names.
    ✘ so that the forward reaction stops [sci.equilibrium.reaction-has-stopped] — Neither reaction stops at equilibrium. Closing the system lets the reverse reaction catch up with the forward one.
- 05 (Name the three conditions): Which set of conditions can be altered to change the amount of product in a reversible reaction?
    ✔ temperature, pressure and concentration — All three. Temperature is the one nearly everybody names; the marks are usually in the other two.
    ✘ temperature only [sci.equilibrium.only-temperature-named] — Summer 2025 records exactly this: many named temperature and few mentioned concentration or pressure.
    ✘ colour and mass [sci.equilibrium.only-temperature-named] — Colour is something you observe, and mass is conserved. Neither is a condition you set.
    ✘ the size of the container only [sci.equilibrium.only-temperature-named] — Changing the container's size changes the pressure, so say pressure — and add temperature and concentration.
- 06 (Read the equilibrium point from a graph): On the rate-against-time graph for a reversible reaction in a sealed vessel, the two curves meet at 60 s. What is happening at 60 s?
    ✔ the forward and reverse rates have become equal — Both are 4 from then on, and the amounts stop changing.
    ✘ the reaction has finished [sci.equilibrium.reaction-has-stopped] — Neither curve has fallen to zero, so both reactions are still going. They are simply going at the same rate.
    ✘ the amounts of reactant and product have become equal [sci.equilibrium.rates-not-amounts] — The graph plots rates, not amounts. Equal rates make the amounts constant, not equal.
    ✘ the reverse reaction has overtaken the forward one [sci.equilibrium.rates-not-amounts] — The curves meet and then run together; neither goes above the other.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.01 [verified] sci.equilibrium.rates-not-amounts · earned P2 · ccea-cer:science:2024-summer:C2H:Q7
STEM: Ruairí is asked: 'A reversible reaction in a sealed flask has reached a dynamic equilibrium. Finish this sentence in two ways, giving a statement that is true at that point each time: at equilibrium … [2]'
His answer:
  ✘ 1. … the amounts of the reactants and the products are equal,
    2. … and the amounts of reactants and products remain constant.
  WRONG: The second line is exactly right. The first should be about the rates, not the amounts: at equilibrium the forward and reverse reactions are going at the same rate. The amounts stay constant, but there is no reason at all for them to be equal to each other.
  FIX: … the rates of the forward and reverse reactions are equal,
  FEEDBACK: One of the two marks. The amounts line stands; the rates line is the one to fix. Summer 2024 examiners saw the same swap again and again, with the concentration written where the rates belonged. Rates first, amounts second, every time.

- rp.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.01 [verified] (definition) What is a reversible reaction?  →  One that can go in both directions: the reactants form the products, and the products can react to form the reactants again.
- rp.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.02 [verified] (trap) What sign is used in place of the usual arrow in a reversible reaction, and why is it worth a mark?  →  The reversible sign, a double arrow. It records that both reactions happen, so a single arrow is a different claim and earns nothing.

Adding water turns it blue again and gives out heat.
- rp.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.04 [verified] (definition) Define dynamic equilibrium.  →  In a closed system, the rates of the forward and reverse reactions are equal and the amounts of reactants and products remain constant.
- rp.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.05 [verified] (trap) At equilibrium, what is equal and what is constant?  →  The rates of the two reactions are equal; the amounts of reactants and products are constant. The amounts are not equal to each other.
- rp.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.06 [verified] (qa) Name the three conditions that can be altered to change the direction of a reversible reaction.  →  Temperature, pressure and concentration.
- rp.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.07 [verified] (qa) Why must a system be closed before it can reach a dynamic equilibrium?  →  Nothing can enter or escape, so the products stay in the container and can react back. In an open container the product escapes and the reverse reaction never catches up.
- rp.science.c2.c2-equilibrium.08 [verified] (trap) Why is the equilibrium called dynamic rather than still?  →  Both reactions are still going at equilibrium, at the same rate as each other, so nothing has stopped even though nothing appears to change.
