## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.fm.u2.constant-acceleration-formulae.01 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: A jet ski moves in a straight line with constant acceleration. It passes a buoy A at 4 m/s and a buoy B at 16 m/s, 8 seconds later.

(i) Calculate the acceleration of the jet ski.
(ii) Calculate the distance AB.
  FIG: svg — A straight line marked A and B, with 4 m/s at A, 16 m/s at B and the 8 seconds between them shown underneath.
  1. $u = 4$, $v = 16$, $t = 8$, $a = ?$, $s = ?$   — List the five letters first and fill in what you are given. Written out like this, the choice of formula stops being a guess.  (MW1)
  2. $v = u + at$ leaves out $s$, which is the letter not wanted here.   — Two letters are unknown, so pick the formula that misses out the one the part does not ask for. Here that is $s$.  WHY: ✔Because the part asks for a and does not need s | ✘Because v = u + at is the first formula on the sheet | ✘Because s is always left out when t is known
  3. $16 = 4 + 8a$, so $8a = 12$ and $a = 1.5$ m/s²   — Substitute, then rearrange. Writing the substitution line before the rearranged one is what earns the method mark even if the algebra then slips.  (W1)
  4. For $s$: $u = 4$, $v = 16$, $t = 8$, so use $s = \tfrac{1}{2}(u + v)t$.   — This one leaves out $a$. Using it keeps the answer clear of the acceleration just found, so a slip there cannot travel into this part.  (MW1)
  5. $s = \tfrac{1}{2}(4 + 16) \times 8 = 80$ m   — Work out the bracket first, then halve, then multiply. The answer carries its unit: metres, because a displacement is a length.  (W1)
  FINAL: (i) $1.5$ m/s²  (ii) $80$ m
  TWIN: A go-kart starts from rest and accelerates uniformly in a straight line to 12 m/s in 5 seconds. Calculate the distance it travels in those 5 seconds.  → numeric 30 m tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u2.constant-acceleration-formulae.pre (pre) [verified]
- 01 (Prerequisite: make a letter the subject of a formula): From an earlier topic: Rearranged so that $a$ is the subject, $v = u + at$ becomes which of these?
    ✔ $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$ — Take $u$ from both sides, then divide both sides by $t$.
    ✘ $a = \dfrac{v}{t} - u$ [fm.kin.wrong-equation-chosen] — Only the whole of the left-hand side may be divided by $t$, not one term of it. Subtract $u$ first.
    ✘ $a = v - u - t$ [fm.kin.wrong-equation-chosen] — The $t$ is multiplying $a$, so it is undone by dividing, not by subtracting.
    ✘ $a = t(v - u)$ [fm.kin.wrong-equation-chosen] — Multiplying by $t$ undoes a division. Here $a$ was multiplied by $t$, so divide.
- 02 (Prerequisite: solve a linear equation with a bracket): From an earlier topic: Solve $4(U + 26) = 144$.
    ✔ $U = 10$ — Divide both sides by 4 to get $U + 26 = 36$, then take 26 off.
    ✘ $U = 118$ [fm.kin.wrong-equation-chosen] — The 26 has been subtracted before the division by 4. Undo the multiplication that is outside the bracket first.
    ✘ $U \approx 1.38$ [fm.kin.wrong-equation-chosen] — That treats $4(U + 26)$ as $4 \times 26 \times U$. The bracket is a sum, so it cannot be turned into a product.
    ✘ $U = 36$ [fm.kin.wrong-equation-chosen] — That is $U + 26$, which is one step from the answer. Take the 26 off to finish.
- 03 (Prerequisite: substitute with a square and a half): From an earlier topic: Work out $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.8 \times 6^{2}$.
    ✔ 14.4 — $6^{2} = 36$, then $0.8 \times 36 = 28.8$, and half of that is 14.4.
    ✘ 2.4 [fm.kin.t-not-squared] — The 6 has not been squared: that is $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.8 \times 6$.
    ✘ 28.8 [fm.kin.half-omitted-in-area] — The squaring and the multiplication are right, but the half has not been applied.
    ✘ 5.76 [fm.kin.t-not-squared] — That squares $0.8 \times 6$ as a whole. Only the 6 is squared.

### dx.fm.u2.constant-acceleration-formulae.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (Choose the formula that leaves out the letter you do not need): A body has a known starting velocity, a known acceleration and a known final velocity. You need the distance. Which formula should you choose?
    ✔ $v^{2} = u^{2} + 2as$ — You know $u$, $v$ and $a$ and you want $s$. This is the formula that leaves out $t$, the letter you neither know nor need.
    ✘ $v = u + at$ [fm.kin.wrong-equation-chosen] — That one leaves out $s$, which is the very thing you are looking for.
    ✘ $s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^{2}$ [fm.kin.wrong-equation-chosen] — It would work if you knew $t$, but you do not, so it brings in a second unknown.
    ✘ $s = \tfrac{1}{2}(u + v)t$ [fm.kin.wrong-equation-chosen] — Again this needs $t$. Choose the formula whose missing letter is the one you do not have.
- 02 (Turn a deceleration into a signed acceleration): A car travelling in the positive direction decelerates at 3 m/s². What goes in the formula for $a$?
    ✔ $a = -3$ — Slowing down while travelling forwards means the velocity is falling, so the acceleration is negative.
    ✘ $a = 3$ [fm.kin.deceleration-sign] — That would make the car speed up. A deceleration is a negative acceleration once the direction of travel is positive.
    ✘ $a = -\dfrac{1}{3}$ [fm.kin.deceleration-sign] — The size does not change, only the sign. The deceleration is 3 m/s², so $a = -3$.
    ✘ It depends on whether $u$ is positive [fm.kin.deceleration-sign] — Once the direction of travel is taken as positive, slowing down is always a negative acceleration.
- 03 (Substitute into s = ut + half a t squared): A trolley starts at 3 m/s and accelerates uniformly at 0.8 m/s² for 6 seconds. Which line is the correct substitution into $s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^{2}$?
    ✔ $s = 3 \times 6 + \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.8 \times 6^{2}$ — The $t$ inside the second term is squared and the $t$ in the first term is not. That gives 32.4 m.
    ✘ $s = 3 \times 6 + \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.8 \times 6$ [fm.kin.t-not-squared] — The 6 in the second term has not been squared, which is the slip the Summer 2019 examiners singled out on this formula.
    ✘ $s = (3 + \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.8) \times 6^{2}$ [fm.kin.wrong-equation-chosen] — Only the second term carries the $t^{2}$; the first term is $ut$, so it cannot be pulled inside the same bracket.
    ✘ $s = 3 \times 6^{2} + \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.8 \times 6$ [fm.kin.t-not-squared] — The squares are on the wrong terms: $u$ multiplies $t$, and $\tfrac{1}{2}a$ multiplies $t^{2}$.
- 04 (Refuse to assume a middle point is the midpoint): Points P, Q and R lie on a straight line, with PR = 144 m. A particle passes all three. Nothing in the question says where Q is. What is the distance PQ?
    ✔ Not yet known: it has to be worked out from the motion — Q is wherever the velocities put it. Work out the time or the velocity at Q first, then the distance.
    ✘ 72 m, because Q is halfway [fm.kin.midpoint-assumed] — Nothing says Q is halfway. The Summer 2025 examiners reported weaker candidates assuming exactly this and setting the distance to half.
    ✘ 144 m, because P and R are 144 m apart [fm.kin.midpoint-assumed] — That is the whole of PR. PQ is only part of it.
    ✘ It cannot be found at all [fm.kin.wrong-equation-chosen] — It can: the velocity at Q, with $u$ and $a$, is enough to fix it.

### q.fm.u2.constant-acceleration-formulae.0004 [verified] practice · diff 2 · untiered · FM2 calc · 3 marks · (main)calculate3 · A deceleration, no context
  (main) [3] A body travelling in a straight line at 20 m/s decelerates uniformly at 2.5 m/s² until it comes to rest. Calculate the distance it travels while it is slowing down.
      ANSWER: numeric 80 m tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction
      MW1 MW1: a = -2.5 and v = 0 used
      MW1 MW2: 0 = 400 + 2 x (-2.5) x s seen
      W1 W1: 80
      SOLUTION: $u = 20$, $v = 0$, $a = -2.5$. Then $0 = 20^{2} + 2(-2.5)s$, so $5s = 400$ and $s = 80$ m.
      ERR [fm.kin.deceleration-sign] {"kind":"numeric","value":-80,"tolerance":{"type":"absolute","value":0.005}} → A negative distance is the sign that $a$ went in as $+2.5$. The body is slowing while travelling forwards, so $a = -2.5$, and the distance comes out as 80 m. (1)
      HINTS: Slowing down while travelling forwards means $a$ is negative. || Coming to rest means $v = 0$. || You have no time, so use $v^{2} = u^{2} + 2as$.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u2.constant-acceleration-formulae.01 [verified] fm.kin.t-not-squared · earned MW1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2019-summer:FM2:Q2
STEM: Niamh is finding how far a trolley travels. It starts at 3 m/s and accelerates uniformly at 0.8 m/s² for 6 seconds. Her working:
    1. u = 3, a = 0.8, t = 6, s = ?
    2. s = ut + ½at²
  ✘ 3. s = 3 × 6 + ½ × 0.8 × 6
    4. s = 18 + 2.4 = 20.4 m
  WRONG: The list and the choice of formula are both right. Line 3 writes the second term as $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.8 \times 6$ instead of $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.8 \times 6^{2}$: the $t$ inside that term is squared, and only that one is.
  FIX: s = 3 × 6 + ½ × 0.8 × 6² / s = 18 + ½ × 0.8 × 36 / s = 18 + 14.4 = 32.4 m
  FEEDBACK: Listing the letters and naming the formula is exactly the method the scheme rewards, and that method mark (M) is safely earned. The working mark (W) goes with the squared $t$. The Summer 2019 examiners named this as one of the two arithmetic slips they saw most on this formula, the other being a bracket expanded wrongly.

## RETRIEVAL PROMPTS
- rp.fm.u2.constant-acceleration-formulae.01 [verified] (procedure) What is the first thing to write in any constant-acceleration question?  →  The list: s, u, v, a, t, with the values you are given filled in and a question mark beside the one you want.
- rp.fm.u2.constant-acceleration-formulae.02 [verified] (formula) Which constant-acceleration formula leaves out the displacement?  →  v = u + at. It links the two velocities, the acceleration and the time.
- rp.fm.u2.constant-acceleration-formulae.03 [verified] (formula) Which constant-acceleration formula leaves out the time?  →  v squared = u squared + 2as. Use it when no time is given and none is wanted.
- rp.fm.u2.constant-acceleration-formulae.04 [verified] (trap) A body decelerates at 3 m/s². What goes into the formula for a?  →  Minus 3, once the direction of travel has been taken as positive. A deceleration is a negative acceleration.
- rp.fm.u2.constant-acceleration-formulae.05 [verified] (trap) In s = ut + half a t squared, which t is squared?  →  Only the one in the second term. The first term is u multiplied by t, with no square.
- rp.fm.u2.constant-acceleration-formulae.06 [verified] (trap) Three points lie on a line and the question names all three. What must you not assume?  →  That the middle one is halfway. Its position comes from the velocities, not from the picture.
