### we.fm.u2.resolving-forces.01 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: A sledge is pulled along level ground by a rope. The pull along the rope is $42$ N and the rope makes an angle of $28°$ with the ground.

Calculate the component of the pull
(i) parallel to the ground,
(ii) at right angles to the ground.

Round each answer to 2 decimal places.
  FIG: svg — A force of 42 N drawn as an arrow at 28 degrees above a horizontal line. Dashed lines complete a right-angled triangle: the side along the floor is labelled 42 cos 28 degrees and the side at right angles to it is labelled 42 sin 28 degrees.
  1. The pull, the ground and the dashed vertical make a right-angled triangle with the $28°$ angle at the sledge.   — Draw the triangle before choosing a ratio: the angle has to be marked between the force and the line you are resolving along.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because the ratio depends on which side of the angle the direction lies | ✘Because the triangle gives the size of the force | ✘Because the angle must always be measured from the horizontal
  2. Parallel to the ground: $42\cos 28° = 37.08$ N.   — The ground is the side beside the angle, so this component takes the cosine.  (MW1,W1)
  3. At right angles to the ground: $42\sin 28° = 19.72$ N.   — The upward direction is opposite the angle, so this one takes the sine.  (MW1,W1)
  4. Check: $37.08^2 + 19.72^2$ comes back to $42^2$, as it must.   — The two components are the legs of a right-angled triangle whose longest side is the force, so Pythagoras is a free check.  (W1)
  FINAL: (i) $37.08$ N  (ii) $19.72$ N
  TWIN: A rope pulls a crate along level ground with a force of $36$ N at $65°$ to the ground. Calculate the component of the pull parallel to the ground, rounded to 2 decimal places.

### we.fm.u2.resolving-forces.02 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: A box of mass $9$ kg rests on a slope inclined at $24°$ to the horizontal.

Calculate the component of the weight of the box
(i) down the slope,
(ii) at right angles to the slope.

Round each answer to 2 decimal places.
  FIG: svg — A box of mass 9 kg on a slope inclined at 24 degrees. A solid arrow from the box points straight down and is labelled 90 N. Two dashed arrows split it: one down the slope labelled 90 sin 24 degrees and one into the slope labelled 90 cos 24 degrees.
  1. Weight $= 9 \times 10 = 90$ N, acting vertically downwards.   — Turn the mass into a force first: a component of a mass means nothing, and g = 10 on this paper.  (MW1)
  2. The angle between the weight and the line into the slope equals the slope's angle, $24°$.   — The two right angles in the picture force it: tilt the surface by 24° and the normal tilts by 24° as well.  (MW1)
  3. Down the slope: $90\sin 24° = 36.61$ N.   — Down the slope is opposite that angle, so it takes the sine.  (MW1,W1)
  4. Into the slope: $90\cos 24° = 82.22$ N.   — Into the slope sits beside the angle, so it takes the cosine. It is the larger of the two for any slope under 45°.  (MW1,W1)
  FINAL: (i) $36.61$ N  (ii) $82.22$ N
  TWIN: A crate of mass $14$ kg rests on a slope inclined at $18°$ to the horizontal. Calculate the component of its weight acting down the slope, rounded to 2 decimal places.

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u2.resolving-forces.pre (pre) [verified]
- 01 (Prerequisite: which trigonometric ratio): From an earlier topic: in a right-angled triangle, which ratio connects the angle to the side next to it and the longest side?
    ✔ cosine — Cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse, so it reaches the side beside the angle.
    ✘ sine — Sine is opposite over hypotenuse, so it reaches the side across from the angle.
    ✘ tangent — Tangent connects the two shorter sides and leaves the longest one out.
    ✘ Pythagoras — That relates the three sides but uses no angle at all.
- 02 (Prerequisite: evaluating a trigonometric product): From an earlier topic: work out $30\cos 60°$.
    ✔ $15$ — $\cos 60° = 0.5$, so the answer is half of 30.
    ✘ $30$ — That leaves the cosine out altogether.
    ✘ $25.98$ — That is $30\sin 60°$.
    ✘ $0.5$ — That is the cosine on its own, without the 30.
- 03 (Prerequisite: weight from mass): From an earlier topic: taking $g = 10$ m/s², what is the weight of a body of mass $14$ kg?
    ✔ $140$ N — $14 \times 10 = 140$ N.
    ✘ $14$ N — That is the mass with newtons written after it.
    ✘ $1.4$ N — That divides by $g$ instead of multiplying.
    ✘ $137.2$ N — That uses $g = 9.8$, which this unit does not.

### dx.fm.u2.resolving-forces.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (Choosing sine or cosine): A force $F$ acts at an angle $\theta$ to a straight line. Which expression gives its component along that line?
    ✔ $F\cos\theta$ — The line is the side beside the angle, so the cosine gives the part along it.
    ✘ $F\sin\theta$ [fm.forces.sin-cos-swapped] — That is the component at right angles to the line, opposite the angle.
    ✘ $F\tan\theta$ [fm.forces.sin-cos-swapped] — Tangent compares the two components with each other, not either of them with the force.
    ✘ $\dfrac{F}{\cos\theta}$ [fm.forces.sin-cos-swapped] — A component is never bigger than the force it came from, so it is multiplied, not divided.
- 02 (The weight on a slope): A body of weight $W$ rests on a slope inclined at $\alpha$ to the horizontal. Which expression gives the component of $W$ at right angles to the slope?
    ✔ $W\cos\alpha$ — The slope's angle sits between the weight and the line into the surface, so that component takes the cosine.
    ✘ $W\sin\alpha$ [fm.forces.sin-cos-swapped] — That is the component down the slope.
    ✘ $W$ [fm.forces.force-left-unresolved] — The whole weight only presses into the surface when the surface is horizontal.
    ✘ $W\tan\alpha$ [fm.forces.sin-cos-swapped] — Tangent would make the component larger than the weight once the slope passes 45°.
- 03 (An angle given to the vertical): A force of $24$ N acts at $40°$ to the vertical. Which expression gives its horizontal component?
    ✔ $24\sin 40°$ — The angle is measured from the vertical, so the horizontal direction is the one opposite it.
    ✘ $24\cos 40°$ [fm.forces.angle-from-wrong-line] — That would be right if the $40°$ were measured from the horizontal. Read which line the angle is given to.
    ✘ $24\cos 50°$ [fm.forces.angle-from-wrong-line] — This one is the same value by a longer route, but it needs $50°$ from the horizontal, which the question does not give.
    ✘ $24\tan 40°$ [fm.forces.sin-cos-swapped] — Tangent compares the two components and cannot give either from the force.
- 04 (Which angle the question gives): A rope pulls a box up a slope. The rope is at $25°$ to the slope and the slope is at $15°$ to the horizontal. Which angle is used to resolve the pull along the slope?
    ✔ $25°$ — The angle between the rope and the slope is the one that belongs in the cosine.
    ✘ $15°$ [fm.forces.angle-from-wrong-line] — That is the slope's own tilt, which the weight uses, not the rope.
    ✘ $40°$ [fm.forces.angle-from-wrong-line] — That is the rope's angle to the horizontal, which would resolve along the ground instead.
    ✘ $10°$ [fm.forces.angle-from-wrong-line] — Subtracting the two angles measures nothing on the diagram.
- 05 (Combining components that face each other): One pull gives a component of $30$ N to the right and another gives $12$ N to the left. What is the total component to the right?
    ✔ $18$ N — Opposite directions subtract, so $30 - 12 = 18$ N to the right.
    ✘ $42$ N [fm.forces.opposing-component-sign-dropped] — Adding treats the second pull as helping the first, when it works against it.
    ✘ $360$ N [fm.forces.components-added-as-scalars] — Components are added and subtracted, never multiplied.
    ✘ $12$ N [fm.forces.term-missing-from-equation] — That leaves the first component out of the total altogether.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u2.resolving-forces.01 [verified] fm.forces.angle-from-wrong-line · earned MW1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2024-summer:FM2:Q3
STEM: Ciara is resolving a force of $30$ N that acts at $50°$ to the vertical. She wants its horizontal component. Her working:
    1. the angle is $50°$
    2. the component along a line is force times cosine of the angle
  ✘ 3. horizontal component $= 30\cos 50° = 19.28$ N
  WRONG: Line 2 is the right rule, but it needs the angle between the force and the line being resolved along. The $50°$ is measured from the vertical, and the horizontal is at right angles to that, so the horizontal component is the one opposite the angle and takes the sine.
  FIX: the angle is $50°$, measured from the vertical / the horizontal is at right angles to the vertical, so it is opposite the angle / horizontal component $= 30\sin 50° = 22.98$ N
  FEEDBACK: Quoting the rule correctly earns the method mark (M). Before choosing sine or cosine, mark the angle on a sketch and ask which side of it the direction you want lies on: beside the angle takes cosine, across from it takes sine.
