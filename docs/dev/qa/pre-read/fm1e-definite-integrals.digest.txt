### we.fm.u1.definite-integrals.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: Find the value of $\int_{1}^{3} \left(3x^{2} - 4x + 1\right) dx$
  FIG: svg — The curve y = 3x^2 - 4x + 1 with the strip between x = 1 and x = 3 shaded
  1. Integrate each term and simplify as you write it: $\left[x^{3} - 2x^{2} + x\right]_{1}^{3}$.   — Simplifying now, rather than leaving a term over its divisor, is what keeps the substitution arithmetic short. The limits travel with the square bracket.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because the square bracket holds the limits until they are substituted | ✘Because the integral sign is still needed | ✘Because the constant of integration goes inside it
  2. Substitute the top limit: at $x = 3$ the bracket is $12$.   — Top limit first. Working it out on its own line, in its own bracket, is what protects the next step from a sign slip.  (M1)
  3. Substitute the bottom limit: at $x = 1$ the bracket is $0$.   — The same expression, the other value. Nothing is re-integrated here.  (M2)
  4. Subtract, top minus bottom: $12 - \left(0\right) = 12$.   — The order matters. The other way round gives $-12$, the same size with the sign turned over, and the scheme does not accept it.  (W1)
  FINAL: $12$
  TWIN: Find the value of $\int_{0}^{2} \left(6x^{2} + 1\right) dx$  → numeric 18 tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.definite-integrals.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Integrate a single power of x): Two checks on what this lesson is built from. Neither is the new method, so answer from what you already know.
What is $\int 6x^{2}\,dx$?
    ✔ $2x^{3} + c$ — Raise the power to $3$ and divide $6$ by that new power.
    ✘ $6x^{3} + c$ — The power has risen correctly. The coefficient still has to be divided by the new power of $3$.
    ✘ $12x + c$ — That is the derivative. Integrating raises the power rather than lowering it.
- p2 (Subtract a negative number): What is $7 - (-3)$?
    ✔ $10$ — Subtracting a negative adds it on. This exact step appears whenever the lower limit gives a negative value.
    ✘ $4$ — That is $7 - 3$. The number being subtracted is itself negative, so the two signs make a plus.
    ✘ $-10$ — The first number is positive and larger, so the answer is positive.

### dx.fm.u1.definite-integrals.post (post) [verified]
- d1 (Substitute the limits in the right order): A candidate integrates and reaches $\left[x^{2} + 3x\right]_{1}^{4}$. What comes next?
    ✔ $\left(28\right) - \left(4\right)$ — Top limit first, bottom limit second, giving $24$.
    ✘ $\left(4\right) - \left(28\right)$ [fm.int.limits-reversed] — That gives $-24$, the right size with the sign turned over. The number on top of the integral sign is substituted first.
    ✘ $\left(28\right) + \left(4\right)$ [fm.int.limits-reversed] — The two substitutions are subtracted, not added. Adding them would count the strip below the lower limit as well.
- d2 (Evaluate a definite integral): What is the value of $\int_{1}^{2} \left(4x^{3} - 6x\right) dx$?
    ✔ $6$ — The bracket is $x^4 - 3x^2$, worth $4$ at the top limit and $-2$ at the bottom.
    ✘ $-6$ [fm.int.limits-reversed] — Both substitutions are right and the subtraction has gone the wrong way round, which turns the sign over.
    ✘ $22$ [fm.int.limits-into-integrand] — That came from putting the limits into $4x^3 - 6x$ itself. They belong in the integrated expression.
- d3 (Explain why the constant of integration is absent): Why is there no $+ c$ in a definite integral?
    ✔ It appears in both substitutions and cancels in the subtraction — $(F(b) + c) - (F(a) + c)$ leaves $F(b) - F(a)$ whatever $c$ was.
    ✘ Because $c$ is always zero between limits [fm.int.constant-omitted] — The constant is not zero; it is simply the same at both limits, so it subtracts away.
    ✘ Because the answer is a number rather than an expression [fm.int.constant-omitted] — That is true of the answer but it is not the reason. The reason is that the two copies of $c$ cancel.
- d4 (Treat an unknown constant as a constant): In $\int_{0}^{2} (k + 6x)\,dx$ the letter $k$ is a constant. What does it integrate to?
    ✔ $kx$ — A constant behaves as a number does: raise the power of $x^{0}$ to $x^{1}$ and divide by $1$.
    ✘ $\frac{k^{2}}{2}$ [fm.int.unknown-constant-integrated] — That treats $k$ as the variable. It then takes the same value at both limits and cancels, so the $k$ vanishes and the answer collapses to $12$.
    ✘ $k$ [fm.int.unknown-constant-integrated] — Leaving it unchanged is what differentiating would do to $kx$. Integrating goes the other way, so it gains a factor of $x$.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u1.definite-integrals.01 [verified] fm.int.limits-reversed · earned MW1,M1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2018-summer:FM1:Q2
STEM: Aoibhín was asked to find $\int_{-1}^{2} \left(6x - 2\right) dx$. Her working:
    1. 6x - 2
    2. [3x^2 - 2x] from -1 to 2
    3. At x = 2: 8
    4. At x = -1: 5
  ✘ 5. 5 - 8 = -3
  WRONG: Lines 2, 3 and 4 are all correct, including the substitution of a negative lower limit, which is where most slips happen.
Line 5 subtracts the top value from the bottom one. The rule is the other way round: the limit written above the integral sign goes first.
So it is $8 - \left(5\right) = 3$.
  FIX: 8 - (5) = 3
  FEEDBACK: Everything up to line 4 earns its marks, and the arithmetic in line 5 is itself accurate. Only the order is reversed, which turns the sign of the answer over. A habit that prevents it: write the top value first, then a minus, then the bottom value inside its own bracket, before working anything out. The value is $3$.

## RETRIEVAL PROMPTS
- rp.fm.u1.definite-integrals.01 [verified] (formula) How do you evaluate a definite integral between $a$ and $b$?  →  Integrate, write the result in square brackets with the limits, substitute the top limit and subtract the bottom one.
- rp.fm.u1.definite-integrals.02 [verified] (qa) Why is there no constant of integration in a definite integral?  →  It appears in both substitutions and cancels in the subtraction, so the answer is the same whatever the constant was.
- rp.fm.u1.definite-integrals.03 [verified] (trap) You reach the square bracket. Which limit is substituted first?  →  The top one, the number written above the integral sign; subtracting the other way round turns the sign of the answer over.
- rp.fm.u1.definite-integrals.04 [verified] (trap) An integrand contains a letter $k$ that the question calls a constant. What does it integrate to?  →  It integrates to $kx$, exactly as a number would; treating it as the variable makes it cancel between the limits.
- rp.fm.u1.definite-integrals.05 [verified] (procedure) Why simplify each term before substituting the limits?  →  Because substituting into a tidy term such as $x^{3}$ is quicker and safer than into an untidy one left over its divisor.
