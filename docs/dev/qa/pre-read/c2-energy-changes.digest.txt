### we.science.c2.c2-energy-changes.01 [verified] paper C2 calc
STEM: Hydrogen reacts with chlorine: $\ce{H2 + Cl2 -> 2HCl}$. Use the bond energies in the table.
(a) Calculate the energy taken in to break the bonds in the reactants. [1]
(b) Calculate the energy given out when the bonds in the products are made. [1]
(c) Calculate the energy change for the reaction and state whether it is exothermic or endothermic. [2]
  FIG: svg-gen — table
  1. Bonds broken: one H–H and one Cl–Cl. 436 + 242 = 678 kJ.   — Read the formulae, not the table: H₂ has one H–H bond and Cl₂ one Cl–Cl bond, so each energy is counted once here.  (P1)
  2. Bonds made: two molecules of HCl, each with one H–Cl bond. 2 × 431 = 862 kJ.   — The 2 in front of HCl means two molecules, so the H–Cl energy is counted twice. Counting it once gives 431 kJ and the answer that follows is wrong.  (P2)  WHY: ✔Because the balancing number tells you how many molecules there are | ✘Because H–Cl bonds are twice as strong as the others | ✘Because there are two different elements in HCl
  3. Energy change = bonds broken − bonds made = 678 − 862 = -184 kJ.   — Broken first, made second. Doing it the other way round gives 184 kJ — the right size with the wrong sign, which reverses the chemistry.  (P3)
  4. The energy change is negative, so the reaction is exothermic.   — Write the minus sign on the answer line. Summer 2023's report records candidates being reminded that the negative sign is needed for full credit.  (P4)
  FINAL: (a) 678 kJ. (b) 862 kJ. (c) 678 − 862 = -184 kJ, so the reaction is exothermic.
  TWIN: Calculate the energy change for the reverse reaction, $\ce{2HCl -> H2 + Cl2}$, using the same bond energies. Give your answer in kJ.  → numeric 184 kJ tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.5} forms=decimal

### we.science.c2.c2-energy-changes.02 [verified] paper C2 calc
STEM: An endothermic reaction takes in heat from its surroundings.
(a) Complete a reaction profile diagram for the reaction, showing the reactants, the products and the reaction pathway. [2]
(b) Mark the activation energy with an arrow labelled A, and the energy change with an arrow labelled E. [2]
(c) State what is meant by the activation energy. [1]
  FIG: svg — An energy-against-progress graph for an endothermic reaction: the product level sits above the reactant level, and the hump is higher than both.
  1. The products go above the reactants, because the reaction has taken energy in.   — Decide the product level before you draw anything else. Endothermic finishes higher; exothermic finishes lower. Getting this wrong loses the mark that every other mark sits on.  (P1)
  2. One smooth line from the reactant level, up over a hump, down to the product level.   — One line only, and keep the whole of it above the horizontal axis. The 2024 report names two lines and a product line below the axis as errors to avoid.  (P2)
  3. A: a single-headed arrow from the reactant level up to the top of the hump.   — The activation energy is the climb from where the reactants start, not from the product level and not the whole height of the graph.  (P3)
  4. E: a single-headed arrow between the reactant level and the product level.   — The energy change is the gap between the two levels. Use single-headed arrows: the report asks for them by name.  (P4)
  5. The activation energy is the minimum energy needed for a reaction to occur.   — The glossary wording. It is a threshold before the reaction, never the energy the reaction gives out.  (P5)
  FINAL: (a) Products drawn above reactants, joined by one smooth curve over a hump. (b) A from the reactant level to the top of the hump; E between the reactant and product levels. (c) The minimum energy needed for a reaction to occur.
  TWIN: In an exothermic reaction, where is the product level drawn compared with the reactant level, and why?  → text accepted=["below the reactants, because energy has been given out","lower than the reactant level, since heat is given out to the surroundings"] keyWords=[{"any":["below","lower"],"marks":1,"reject":["above","higher"]},{"any":["energy has been given out","heat is given out","given out","released"],"marks":1,"reject":["taken in"]}]

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.c2.c2-energy-changes.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Prerequisite from mathematics — reading a change; you are not expected to have met energy changes yet): A thermometer reads 21.0 °C, then 27.5 °C. What has happened to the temperature?
    ✔ it has risen by 6.5 °C — Finish minus start. Every experiment in this lesson comes down to that subtraction.
    ✘ it has fallen by 6.5 °C [sci.quant.change-subtracted-backwards] — 27.5 is the larger number, so the temperature has gone up. Which way it goes is the whole point of the topic.
    ✘ it has risen by -6.5 °C [sci.quant.change-subtracted-backwards] — That is start minus finish, which gives a negative rise. Take the starting temperature away from the final one.
- p2 (Prerequisite from C1 §1.5 — reading a balanced equation): In the formula 2HCl, what does the 2 in front tell you?
    ✔ there are two molecules of HCl — A number in front multiplies the whole formula. Section 4 uses this to count bonds.
    ✘ there are two hydrogen atoms in the molecule [sci.quant.ar-not-multiplied-by-subscript] — That would be written H₂, with the 2 as a subscript after the H. A number in front counts whole molecules.
    ✘ the bond is twice as strong [sci.quant.ar-not-multiplied-by-subscript] — The number says how many molecules there are; it tells you nothing about bond strength.
- p3 (Prerequisite from C1 §1.4 — bonding): What is a chemical bond?
    ✔ a force holding two atoms together — Holding them together, which is why pulling them apart takes energy. That single fact runs through the whole of section 4.
    ✘ the gap between two atoms [sci.energy.bond-breaking-releases] — A bond is a force of attraction, not a space. Because it holds the atoms together, breaking it needs energy put in.
    ✘ a store of heat inside a molecule [sci.energy.bond-breaking-releases] — Not a store you can tip out: energy has to be supplied to break a bond, and is released when one forms.

### dx.science.c2.c2-energy-changes.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (Tell exothermic from endothermic): A reaction is carried out in a polystyrene cup and the temperature falls. What kind of reaction is it?
    ✔ endothermic, because heat has been taken in — The mixture has taken heat from its surroundings, so the surroundings — and the thermometer — cool down.
    ✘ exothermic, because heat has been taken in [sci.energy.endothermic-exothermic-swapped] — Exothermic means heat is given OUT, and the temperature would rise. The two halves of this answer do not go together.
    ✘ exothermic, because heat has been given out [sci.energy.endothermic-exothermic-swapped] — Heat given out would warm the mixture and the temperature would rise. Here it fell.
    ✘ neither, because the temperature only fell a little [sci.energy.endothermic-exothermic-swapped] — Any measurable fall means heat has been taken in. The size of the change does not decide which word applies.
- 02 (Read a reaction profile): On a reaction profile for an exothermic reaction, where is the product level drawn?
    ✔ below the reactant level — Energy has left the chemicals, so they finish lower than they started.
    ✘ above the reactant level [sci.energy.profile-product-level-wrong] — That is the endothermic profile. Exothermic finishes lower, because energy has been given out.
    ✘ at the same level as the reactants [sci.energy.profile-product-level-wrong] — Level ends would mean no energy change at all, and then the reaction would be neither exothermic nor endothermic.
    ✘ at the top of the hump [sci.energy.profile-product-level-wrong] — The top of the hump is the peak the reaction passes through, not where it finishes.
- 03 (Bond breaking and bond making): Which statement about bonds is correct?
    ✔ breaking bonds takes energy in; making bonds gives energy out — In to break, out to make. Every bond-energy calculation is built on that one line.
    ✘ breaking bonds gives energy out; making bonds takes energy in [sci.energy.bond-breaking-releases] — The exact swap Summer 2023 examiners reported. A bond holds atoms together, so pulling them apart has to be paid for.
    ✘ both breaking and making bonds take energy in [sci.energy.bond-breaking-releases] — If both took energy in, no reaction could ever give out heat. Making a bond releases energy.
    ✘ neither breaking nor making bonds involves energy [sci.energy.bond-breaking-releases] — Both do, and the balance between them is exactly what decides whether a reaction is exothermic or endothermic.
- 04 (Subtract in the right order): For $\ce{H2 + Cl2 -> 2HCl}$, the bonds broken total 678 kJ and the bonds made total 862 kJ. What is the energy change?
    ✔ -184 kJ — Bonds broken − bonds made = 678 − 862 = -184 kJ, and the negative sign says exothermic.
    ✘ 184 kJ [sci.energy.bonds-made-minus-broken] — That is bonds made minus bonds broken. The right size, but the sign now says the reaction takes heat in, which is the opposite of the truth.
    ✘ 1540 kJ [sci.energy.bonds-made-minus-broken] — The two totals are subtracted, not added: one is energy going in and the other energy coming out.
    ✘ 247 kJ [sci.energy.bond-counted-once] — That counts only one H–Cl bond. Two molecules of HCl form, so the H–Cl energy is counted twice.
- 05 (Read the sign): An energy change works out as a negative number. What does that mean?
    ✔ more energy was given out than taken in, so the reaction is exothermic — Negative means exothermic, and the sign is part of the answer: leaving it off loses the mark.
    ✘ more energy was taken in than given out, so the reaction is endothermic [sci.energy.negative-sign-omitted] — That is what a positive answer means. Negative says the products hold less energy than the reactants.
    ✘ a mistake has been made, because energy cannot be negative [sci.energy.negative-sign-omitted] — The minus sign shows the direction the energy travelled, not a negative amount of energy.
    ✘ the reaction does not happen [sci.energy.negative-sign-omitted] — Most reactions you meet have a negative energy change. It tells you the reaction gives out heat, not that it fails.
- 06 (Define activation energy): What is the activation energy?
    ✔ the minimum energy needed for a reaction to occur — A threshold on the way in, drawn as the height of the hump above the reactant level.
    ✘ the energy given out by the reaction [sci.rates.activation-energy-as-output] — That is the energy change, measured between the two levels. The activation energy is the climb before it.
    ✘ the difference between the reactant and product levels [sci.rates.activation-energy-as-output] — That gap is the energy change. The activation energy runs from the reactant level to the top of the hump.
    ✘ the energy stored in the products [sci.rates.activation-energy-as-output] — A profile shows energy differences, not absolute stores, and the activation energy is a difference on the way in.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.science.c2.c2-energy-changes.01 [verified] sci.energy.bonds-made-minus-broken · earned P1,P2 · ccea-cer:science:2024-summer:C2H:Q6
STEM: Fiachra is asked: 'Use the bond energies H–H 436, Cl–Cl 242 and H–Cl 431 kJ to find the energy change for H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl, and say whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic. [3]'
His working:
    1. Bonds broken = 436 + 242 = 678 kJ
    2. Bonds made = 2 × 431 = 862 kJ
  ✘ 3. Energy change = 862 − 678 = 184 kJ, so the reaction is endothermic
  WRONG: Both totals are right. The subtraction is the wrong way round: the rule is bonds broken minus bonds made. Doing it the other way gives the right size with the wrong sign, and the sign is what decides exothermic from endothermic — so the conclusion flips too.
  FIX: Energy change = 678 − 862 = -184 kJ, so the reaction is exothermic
  FEEDBACK: Two of the three marks: both totals stand and they carry forward. Write the rule out before you subtract — energy change = bonds broken − bonds made — and the sign takes care of itself. Summer 2024 examiners record missing and reversed signs as a common error on this question.
