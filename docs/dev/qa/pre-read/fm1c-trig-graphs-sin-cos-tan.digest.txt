### we.fm.u1.trig-graphs-sin-cos-tan.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: (a) Sketch the graph of $y = \sin x$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$.
(b) The line $y = 0.6$ is drawn across it. Use the graph to find both solutions of $\sin x = 0.6$ in that range.
  FIG: svg — The graph of y = sin x from 0 to 360 degrees with the line y = 0.6 drawn across it and dashed lines dropped from the two crossings to the x axis.
  1. Key points: $(0, 0)$, $(90, 1)$, $(180, 0)$, $(270, -1)$, $(360, 0)$.   — Plot before you draw. The five points fix the whole curve, and they are also what the scheme credits when the curve itself is not smooth enough.  (MW1)
  2. Join them with one smooth curve and mark $90$, $180$, $270$, $360$ along the x-axis and $1$ and $-1$ up the y-axis.   — The scale is a mark of its own, and it costs four numbers and two more. Straight sides between the turning points throw away the shape mark.  (W1)  WHY: ✔Because the curve has to be smooth and the axes have to carry a scale | ✘Because the curve is made of straight lines | ✘Because the y-axis runs from 0 to 360
  3. Draw the line $y = 0.6$ across the graph. It cuts the curve $2$ times.   — Counting the crossings first tells you how many answers to look for, so you cannot stop at one by mistake.  (M1)
  4. First crossing: $\sin^{-1} 0.6 = 36.87^\circ$.   — The calculator always gives the angle in the first quadrant here, and that is the one the picture shows on the way up.  (MW1)
  5. Second crossing: $180 - 36.87 = 143.13^\circ$.   — The arch is symmetrical about $x = 90^\circ$, so the second angle is as far past the mirror as the first is short of it. Reading it off the graph gives about $140^\circ$, which agrees.  (W1)
  FINAL: (b) $x = 36.87^\circ$ and $x = 143.13^\circ$
  TWIN: The graph of $y = \cos x$ is drawn for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$ and the line $y = -0.8$ is drawn across it. Write down how many solutions $\cos x = -0.8$ has in that range.  → numeric 2 tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.0005} forms=decimal/fraction

### we.fm.u1.trig-graphs-sin-cos-tan.02 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: Sketch the graph of $y = \tan x$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$, marking the asymptotes and every crossing of the x-axis.
  FIG: svg — The graph of y = tan x from 0 to 360 degrees with dashed vertical asymptotes at 90 and 270 degrees and the crossings at 0, 180 and 360 degrees marked.
  1. Asymptotes first: dashed vertical lines at $x = 90$ and $x = 270$.   — $\tan x$ is the up measurement divided by the across one, and across is zero at those two angles, so there is no value of the function there at all.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because $\cos x$ is zero there, so the division has no answer | ✘Because the curve has a maximum there | ✘Because the angle is too large
  2. Crossings: $\tan x = 0$ at $0^\circ$, $180^\circ$, $360^\circ$.   — These are the anchors between the asymptotes. Mark them before drawing anything curved.  (W1)
  3. In the strip $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$ the curve rises from the origin and climbs steeply towards the dashed line without meeting it.   — Steeply towards, never onto. A sketch whose curve touches an asymptote is marked as a misunderstanding of what an asymptote is.  (W1)
  4. Each of the next two strips holds the same branch again, because the pattern repeats every $180^\circ$.   — Copy, do not invent. One branch drawn well and repeated twice is a complete answer, and the period is the fact that licenses the copying.  (W1)
  FINAL: Three identical branches, with dashed asymptotes at $x = 90$ and $x = 270$ and crossings at $0^\circ$, $180^\circ$, $360^\circ$

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.trig-graphs-sin-cos-tan.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Prerequisite: the three trigonometric ratios): Three quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new work, so answer from what you already know.
In a right-angled triangle, which ratio is $\sin \theta$?
    ✔ opposite divided by hypotenuse — That is the one. It is also why the sine curve never leaves the strip between -1 and 1: the opposite side can never be longer than the hypotenuse.
    ✘ adjacent divided by hypotenuse [fm.trig.sin-cos-swapped] — That is cosine. The two are easy to swap, and swapping them is what makes a sketch start in the wrong place.
    ✘ opposite divided by adjacent [fm.trig.sin-cos-swapped] — That is tangent, which has no hypotenuse in it at all and so is not trapped between -1 and 1.
- p2 (Prerequisite: writing a coordinate pair): A curve passes through the point where $x = 4$ and $y = -3$. How are those coordinates written?
    ✔ $(4, -3)$ — Across first, then up or down. Every coordinate answer in this topic is written the same way.
    ✘ $(-3, 4)$ [fm.csq.coordinates-swapped] — The two numbers are right and they are the wrong way round. The x-value always comes first.
    ✘ $4, -3$ with no brackets needed [fm.csq.coordinates-swapped] — The order here is right. Brackets are how a pair is written, and a mark scheme asking for coordinates expects them.
- p3 (Prerequisite: reading a trigonometric value in degrees): On a calculator set to degrees, what is $\sin 30^\circ$?
    ✔ $0.5$ — Good. If your calculator gives something else, it is in radians, and every answer in Unit 1 would then come out wrong.
    ✘ $30$ [fm.trig.axes-not-marked] — The angle goes in and a ratio comes out. That ratio is always between -1 and 1 for sine.
    ✘ $-0.988$ [fm.trig.axes-not-marked] — That is $\sin 30$ in radians. Set the calculator to degrees before you start.

### dx.fm.u1.trig-graphs-sin-cos-tan.post (post) [verified]
- q1 (Tell the sine and cosine curves apart): Which curve passes through the point $(0, 1)$?
    ✔ $y = \cos x$ — Cosine starts at its maximum, so it is at 1 when the angle is zero.
    ✘ $y = \sin x$ [fm.trig.sin-cos-swapped] — $\sin 0^\circ = 0$, so the sine curve starts at the origin and climbs from there.
    ✘ $y = \tan x$ [fm.trig.sin-cos-swapped] — $\tan 0^\circ = 0$ as well, so the tangent curve also starts on the axis.
- q2 (Recall the period of each curve): What is the period of $y = \tan x$?
    ✔ $180^\circ$ — Half the period of the other two. One branch, then the same branch again.
    ✘ $360^\circ$ [fm.trig.period-confused] — $360^\circ$ is the period of sine and cosine. The tangent curve has already repeated once by then.
    ✘ $90^\circ$ [fm.trig.period-confused] — $90^\circ$ is the gap from a crossing to an asymptote, which is half a branch rather than a whole one.
- q3 (Place the asymptotes of the tangent curve): Where are the asymptotes of $y = \tan x$ in the range $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$?
    ✔ $x = 90$ and $x = 270$ — Those are the angles where the across measurement is zero, so the division that makes tan has no answer.
    ✘ $x = 180$ and $x = 360$ [fm.trig.tan-asymptotes] — Those are the angles where the curve crosses the axis, which is the opposite of a break.
    ✘ $x = 0$ and $x = 180$ [fm.trig.tan-asymptotes] — Both of those are crossings as well. The breaks sit halfway between them.
- q4 (Know what the shape mark is for): A sketch of $y = \sin x$ has all five key points plotted correctly, joined by straight lines. How is it marked?
    ✔ The points earn their mark; the shape mark is lost — Examiners have said this for several series running. Joining the points in one smooth pass is the whole difference.
    ✘ Full marks, because the points are right [fm.trig.curve-too-straight] — The shape is a separate mark, and straight sides between the turning points are what it is checking for.
    ✘ No marks at all [fm.trig.curve-too-straight] — Positive marking credits the right values. Only the shape mark goes.
- q5 (Use the symmetry of the sine curve): Given that $\sin 50^\circ = 0.77$, what is $\sin 130^\circ$?
    ✔ $0.77$ — $180 - 50 = 130$, and the arch is symmetrical about $x = 90^\circ$, so the height is unchanged.
    ✘ $-0.77$ [fm.trig.symmetry-sign-error] — Changing the sign reflects the curve in the x-axis. The symmetry here is sideways, and it leaves the height where it was.
    ✘ $0.64$ [fm.trig.sin-cos-swapped] — That is $\cos 50^\circ$. The question stays on the sine curve throughout.
- q6 (Count the solutions a range holds): The sine curve is drawn for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$ and a horizontal line is drawn at $y = 0.4$. How many solutions does that give?
    ✔ $2$ — The line cuts the arch twice, once going up and once coming down, and the rest of the curve stays below it.
    ✘ $1$ [fm.trig.second-solution-missing] — The calculator gives one angle. The picture shows the curve reaching that height a second time before the range ends.
    ✘ $4$ [fm.trig.second-solution-missing] — There would be four in a range of $720^\circ$, because each period contributes two. This range is one period.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u1.trig-graphs-sin-cos-tan.01 [verified] fm.trig.symmetry-sign-error · earned M1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2019-summer:FM1:Q4
STEM: Órlaith is told that $\sin 50^\circ = 0.77$ and asked for the value of $\sin 130^\circ$. Her working:
    1. 130 is past the maximum, so the curve is on its way down
    2. 180 - 50 = 130, so the two angles are a pair
  ✘ 3. Going down means the value is negative
    4. sin 130 = -0.77
  WRONG: Lines 1 and 2 are exactly right: $130^\circ$ does sit on the falling part of the arch, and it is the mirror image of $50^\circ$ in the line $x = 90^\circ$.
Line 3 confuses falling with negative. Between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ the whole arch sits above the axis, so every value there is positive however steeply the curve is dropping.
The curve only goes below the axis after $180^\circ$, and $130^\circ$ is comfortably short of that.
  FIX: 180 - 50 = 130 / The mirror line x = 90 leaves the height unchanged / sin 130 = sin 50 = 0.77
  FEEDBACK: The method mark is safe: spotting the pair is the part that is worth thinking about, and Órlaith spotted it. Sketch the arch and mark both angles on it before writing the value down, and the sign settles itself.
