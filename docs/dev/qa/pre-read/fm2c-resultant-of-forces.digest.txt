### we.fm.u2.resultant-of-forces.01 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: Throughout, $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ are perpendicular unit vectors, with $\mathbf{i}$ along the positive x-axis.

Three forces act on a body: $(9\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{j})$ N, $(-5\mathbf{i} + 7\mathbf{j})$ N and $(8\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j})$ N.

(i) Find the resultant of the three forces.
(ii) Calculate its magnitude.
(iii) Calculate the angle it makes with the positive x-axis. Round to 2 decimal places.
  FIG: svg — A table of three forces written in i and j with their across and up parts listed: 9 and minus 4, minus 5 and 7, and 8 and 2. The bottom row totals the two columns to give a resultant of 12 across and 5 up.
  1. $\mathbf{i}$ parts: $9 - 5 + 8 = 12$.   — Total one column at a time. Adding $-5$ takes five away, which is where the sign slips happen.  (MW1)
  2. $\mathbf{j}$ parts: $-4 + 7 + 2 = 5$, so the resultant is $12\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j}$ N.   — Nothing crosses between the columns, so the second total is worked out on its own and then written beside the first.  (MW1,W1)  WHY: ✔Because across and up are independent directions | ✘Because the two columns must be added together at the end | ✘Because the resultant is always a single number
  3. Magnitude $= \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13$ N.   — The two totals are perpendicular, so the magnitude is the longest side of their right-angled triangle.  (MW1,W1)
  4. Angle $= \tan^{-1}\dfrac{5}{12} = 22.62°$ above the positive x-axis.   — The up total is opposite the angle, so it goes on top. Measuring from the x-axis is what the question asked for.  (MW1,W1)
  FINAL: (i) $12\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j}$ N  (ii) $13$ N  (iii) $22.62°$
  TWIN: Two forces are $(6\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j})$ N and $(-2\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j})$ N. Find their resultant.

Answer ________ N  → algebraic 4i + 5j (equivalent)

### we.fm.u2.resultant-of-forces.02 [verified] paper FM2 calc

(i) Calculate the total component to the right.
(ii) Calculate the total component upwards.
(iii) Calculate the magnitude of the resultant. Round to 2 decimal places.
  FIG: svg — The same three forces at O without the resultant: 30 N to the right, 24 N at 50 degrees above the horizontal, and 10 N to the left, with dashed horizontal and vertical reference lines.
  1. To the right: $30 + 24\cos 50° - 10 = 35.43$ N.   — Take right as positive, so the 10 N force is subtracted. The 24 N force is at an angle, so only its cosine part lies along this direction.  (MW1,W1)

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u2.resultant-of-forces.pre (pre) [verified]
- 01 (Prerequisite: adding negative numbers): From an earlier topic: work out $-4 + 7 + 2$.
    ✔ $5$ — Adding 7 to $-4$ gives 3, and 2 more gives 5.
    ✘ $13$ — That treats the $-4$ as $+4$.
    ✘ $-9$ — That subtracts both of the other numbers.
    ✘ $1$ — That leaves the 2 out.
- 02 (Prerequisite: Pythagoras): From an earlier topic: a right-angled triangle has shorter sides of $8$ and $6$. How long is the longest side?
    ✔ $10$ — $\sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$.
    ✘ $14$ — That adds the two sides, which would only be right if they pointed the same way.
    ✘ $100$ — That is the sum of the squares, before the square root.
    ✘ $\sqrt{28}$ — That squares the sum instead of summing the squares.
- 03 (Prerequisite: the inverse tangent): From an earlier topic: in a right-angled triangle the side opposite an angle is $5$ and the side beside it is $12$. Which expression gives the angle?
    ✔ $\tan^{-1}\dfrac{5}{12}$ — Tangent is opposite over adjacent, so the 5 goes on top.
    ✘ $\tan^{-1}\dfrac{12}{5}$ — That is the other angle in the same triangle.
    ✘ $\sin^{-1}\dfrac{5}{12}$ — Sine needs the longest side underneath, not the adjacent one.
    ✘ $\tan\dfrac{5}{12}$ — That would take the tangent of a number rather than finding the angle.

### dx.fm.u2.resultant-of-forces.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (What a resultant is): Three forces of $30$ N, $24$ N and $10$ N act at a point in different directions. Which statement about their resultant is correct?
    ✔ its magnitude is less than $64$ N — Forces only add to their total when they point the same way.
    ✘ its magnitude is $64$ N [fm.forces.components-added-as-scalars] — That totals the sizes and ignores the directions altogether.
    ✘ it has a magnitude but no direction [fm.vectors.magnitude-when-vector-asked] — A resultant is a force, so it has both, and an answer needs both.
    ✘ it acts along the largest of the three forces [fm.forces.term-missing-from-equation] — Every force affects the direction of the resultant, not just the largest.
- 02 (Adding in i and j): What is the resultant of $(7\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j})$ N and $(-4\mathbf{i} + 8\mathbf{j})$ N?
    ✔ $(3\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j})$ N — $7 - 4 = 3$ across and $-3 + 8 = 5$ up.
    ✘ $(11\mathbf{i} - 11\mathbf{j})$ N [fm.vectors.subtract-instead-of-add] — That subtracts the second force instead of adding it.
    ✘ $(3\mathbf{i} + 11\mathbf{j})$ N [fm.vectors.component-sign-dropped] — The $-3$ has lost its sign: adding 8 to $-3$ gives 5, not 11.
    ✘ $(11\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j})$ N [fm.vectors.component-sign-dropped] — The $-4$ has lost its sign: adding it takes four away.
- 03 (Totalling components that face each other): One force has a component of $18$ N to the right and another has a component of $25$ N to the left. What is the total component to the right?
    ✔ $-7$ N — $18 - 25 = -7$ N, so the total of 7 N actually points to the left.
    ✘ $43$ N [fm.forces.opposing-component-sign-dropped] — Adding treats the second component as helping the first.
    ✘ $7$ N [fm.forces.opposing-component-sign-dropped] — The size is right but the sign is not: taking right as positive, the total is negative.
    ✘ $450$ N [fm.forces.components-added-as-scalars] — Components are added and subtracted, never multiplied.
- 04 (The magnitude of a resultant): The forces on a body total $9$ N to the right and $12$ N upwards. What is the magnitude of the resultant?
    ✔ $15$ N — $\sqrt{9^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{225} = 15$ N.
    ✘ $21$ N [fm.vectors.magnitude-components-added] — That adds the two totals. They act at right angles, so the resultant is shorter than their sum.
    ✘ $225$ N [fm.vectors.magnitude-root-omitted] — That is the sum of the squares. The square root is the last step.
    ✘ $12$ N [fm.forces.term-missing-from-equation] — That is the larger total on its own, with the other one left out.
- 05 (The direction of a resultant): The forces on a body total $9$ N to the right and $12$ N upwards. Which expression gives the angle the resultant makes with the horizontal?
    ✔ $\tan^{-1}\dfrac{12}{9}$ — The up total is opposite the angle from the horizontal, so it goes on top.
    ✘ $\tan^{-1}\dfrac{9}{12}$ [fm.vectors.direction-ratio-inverted] — That is the angle with the vertical. The reports name this reciprocal as the commonest error in the direction part.
    ✘ $\sin^{-1}\dfrac{12}{9}$ [fm.vectors.direction-ratio-inverted] — Sine needs the resultant underneath, and a sine can never be greater than one.
    ✘ $\cos^{-1}\dfrac{12}{9}$ [fm.vectors.direction-ratio-inverted] — Cosine also needs the resultant underneath, and the fraction is greater than one.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u2.resultant-of-forces.01 [verified] fm.vectors.subtract-instead-of-add · earned MW1 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2022-summer:FM2:Q2
STEM: Rhys is finding the resultant of the forces $(6\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j})$ N and $(-5\mathbf{i} + 9\mathbf{j})$ N. His working:
    1. the resultant is the sum of the forces
    2. $\mathbf{i}$: $6 - 5 = 1$
  ✘ 3. $\mathbf{j}$: $-2 - 9 = -11$
    4. resultant $= \mathbf{i} - 11\mathbf{j}$ N
  WRONG: Line 3 subtracts the second force's $\mathbf{j}$ part where line 1 says the forces are added. The $\mathbf{i}$ line is right: $-5$ is already negative, so adding it takes five away. The $\mathbf{j}$ line should add 9 to $-2$ and give 7.
  FIX: the resultant is the sum of the forces / $\mathbf{i}$: $6 - 5 = 1$ / $\mathbf{j}$: $-2 + 9 = 7$ / resultant $= \mathbf{i} + 7\mathbf{j}$ N
  FEEDBACK: Naming the resultant as the sum and keeping the two columns apart is the method, and it earns the method mark (M). One operation applies to the whole vector: if the i line adds, the j line adds too. Writing the two forces one above the other before you start makes a stray sign hard to miss.
