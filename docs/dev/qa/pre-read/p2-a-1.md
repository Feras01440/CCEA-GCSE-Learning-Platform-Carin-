## B. science.p2.p2-echoes-ultrasound-sonar-radar

### B1 [high] `.0005` part (main): 1500 m/s is given as the speed of sound in air

`q.science.p2.p2-echoes-ultrasound-sonar-radar.0005` (main) [3]:

> "A class stands 45 m from a flat sports hall wall. They clap once and hear the echo 0.06 s later.
> Calculate the speed of sound in the air of the hall."

`answer.value: 1500`, `unit: "m/s"`; scheme P3 "1500 m/s"; worked solution "…90 ÷ 0.06 = 1500 m/s".

The arithmetic is internally consistent — (2 × 45) ÷ 0.06 = 1500 — but 1500 m/s is the speed of sound in
**sea water**, not air; air is ~330–340 m/s. The item therefore teaches a wrong physical constant, and it
contradicts the rest of the pair: this bundle's note gate g5 ("a clap echoes back from a wall 34 m away,
0.2 s later" → 340 m/s in air), `dx…post/05` (85 m, 0.5 s → 340 m/s in air), `we…02`'s twin (85 m, 0.5 s →
340 m/s "in the air"), `we…01` (1500 m/s **in sea water**), and
`we.science.p2.p2-wave-types-and-properties.01` ("sound in air is about 330 m/s, so the answer is
believable"). The whole item was built backwards from 1500, so the stem numbers have to move, not the answer.

Fix (recomputed in node): stem "A class stands **51 m** from a flat sports hall wall… hear the echo
**0.3 s** later", then

- `answer.value`: 1500 → **340**
- scheme P2 "substitution: (2 × 45) ÷ 0.06" → "substitution: (2 × 51) ÷ 0.3"
- scheme P3 "1500 m/s" → "340 m/s"
- worked solution → "…the distance covered is 2 × 51 = 102 m. Speed = distance ÷ time = 102 ÷ 0.3 = 340 m/s."
- common error `{"kind":"numeric","value":750}` → **170** (one leg: 51 ÷ 0.3 = 170), and its feedback text
  "The timed 0.06 s covers 45 m out and 45 m back" → "The timed 0.3 s covers 51 m out and 51 m back"
- hint 2 "The distance the sound covers is not 45 m." → "…is not 51 m."

(43 m and 0.25 s → 344 m/s is the alternative if 51 m reads as too long for a sports hall; 34 m / 0.2 s
gives exactly the note gate's numbers, so avoid it. Worth noting while editing: `we…02`'s twin and
`dx…post/05` already share the identical numbers 85 m / 0.5 s → 340 m/s; varying one of them would stop the
learner meeting the same three numbers twice.)
