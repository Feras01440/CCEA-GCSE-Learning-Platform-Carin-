### we.science.c2.c2-collision-theory-catalysts.01 [verified] paper C2 calc
STEM: A reaction between marble chips and dilute hydrochloric acid is repeated with acid of a higher concentration, at the same temperature.
(a) State the effect on the rate of reaction. [1]
(b) Explain your answer in terms of particles. [2]
  FIG: svg — Four reaction boxes side by side showing the same reaction as it is, more concentrated, hotter and with a catalyst, each with the number of collisions a second and the number that succeed printed underneath.
  1. Effect: the rate of reaction increases.   — Answer the 'state' part first and in one clause. It is a separate mark, and an explanation with no effect stated loses it.  (P1)
  2. There are more acid particles in the same volume of solution.   — Start the explanation with what has changed about the particles themselves. Concentration means how many particles are packed into the same space.  (P2)  WHY: ✔Because concentration is the number of particles in a given volume | ✘Because concentrated acid particles are larger | ✘Because concentrated acid particles move faster
  3. So the particles collide more frequently, and there are more successful collisions per unit time.   — Two words carry this mark: successful, and per unit time. 'More collisions' on its own was not credited in Summer 2025. Note that concentration changes only how often they collide, not how hard, so there is no energy line here.  (P3)
  FINAL: (a) The rate of reaction increases. (b) There are more acid particles in the same volume, so the particles collide more frequently and there are more successful collisions per unit time.

### we.science.c2.c2-collision-theory-catalysts.03 [verified] paper C2 calc
STEM: A cube of calcium carbonate of side 2 cm is cut into 8 equal cubes.
(a) Calculate the total surface area of the 8 small cubes. [2]
(b) Calculate the surface area to volume ratio before and after the cut. [2]
(c) Use your answers to explain why the cut pieces react faster with acid. [2]
  FIG: svg — One cube of side two centimetres beside the eight cubes of side one centimetre it cuts into, with the surface area and the volume of each arrangement printed underneath.
  1. Each small cube has side 1 cm, so its surface area is 6 × 1² = 6 cm².   — Work out one small cube first. Six faces, each a square of the small side.  (P1)
  2. There are 8 of them: 8 × 6 = 48 cm².   — Multiply by the number of pieces. The whole cube had only 24 cm², so cutting has doubled the area without adding any substance.  (P2)
  3. Volume is unchanged: 2³ = 8 cm³ before, and 8 × 1 = 8 cm³ after.   — Check the volume rather than assuming it. Nothing is added or lost by cutting, which is the point of the comparison.  (P3)
  4. Ratios: 24 ÷ 8 = 3 cm⁻¹ before, and 48 ÷ 8 = 6 cm⁻¹ after.   — Surface area on top, volume underneath. Turning the fraction over gives a number that falls when the pieces get smaller, which is the wrong way round.  (P4)
  5. More of the calcium carbonate is exposed, so acid particles collide with its surface more frequently, and there are more successful collisions per unit time.   — Finish in collision language. The ratio is the evidence; the collisions are the explanation.  (P5,P6)
  FINAL: (a) 48 cm². (b) 3 cm⁻¹ before and 6 cm⁻¹ after. (c) A larger surface area to volume ratio exposes more of the solid, so collisions with the acid are more frequent and there are more successful collisions per unit time.
  TWIN: Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a single cube of side 2 cm. Give your answer in cm⁻¹.  → numeric 3 per cm tol={"type":"dp","places":1} forms=decimal

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.c2.c2-collision-theory-catalysts.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Prerequisite from C1 — particle theory; you are not expected to have met collision theory yet): In a solid, a liquid and a gas, what are the particles doing?
    ✔ always moving, even in a solid — Always moving. In a solid they vibrate in place; in a liquid and a gas they travel. This lesson is built on that movement.
    ✘ still in a solid, moving in a liquid and a gas [sci.rates.less-collisions-wording] — Particles in a solid vibrate rather than travel, but they are never still. Section 1 of the lesson needs them moving.
    ✘ only moving when the substance is heated [sci.rates.less-collisions-wording] — Heating makes them move faster, not start moving. They are moving at every temperature.
- p2 (Prerequisite from c2-measuring-rates — what a rate is): Which of these is a rate of reaction, rather than an amount?
    ✔ 0.64 cm³ of gas per second — A rate is an amount in each second, so it always carries a time in its unit.
    ✘ 54 cm³ of gas [sci.rates.rate-inverted] — That is a total volume, with no time in it. A rate needs the time underneath.
    ✘ 50 seconds [sci.rates.rate-inverted] — That is a time on its own. A rate is an amount divided by a time.
- p3 (Prerequisite from mathematics — surface area of a cube): What is the surface area of a cube of side 2 cm?
    ✔ 24 cm² — Six faces, each 2 × 2 = 4 cm², so 24 cm². Section 2 uses this to compare a lump with a powder.
    ✘ 8 cm² [sci.quant.ratio-inverted] — That is the volume, 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 cm³, and it is measured in cm³, not cm². The surface area adds up the six faces.
    ✘ 4 cm² [sci.quant.ratio-inverted] — That is one face. A cube has six of them.

### dx.science.c2.c2-collision-theory-catalysts.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (Define activation energy): What is meant by the activation energy of a reaction?
    ✔ the minimum energy needed for a reaction to occur — A threshold on the way in. A collision with less than this much energy does nothing.
    ✘ the energy given out by the reaction [sci.rates.activation-energy-as-output] — That is the energy change, which is the drop between the reactant and the product levels. Activation energy is the hump on the way there.
    ✘ the energy every collision has [sci.rates.activation-energy-as-output] — Collisions have a range of energies, which is exactly why only some of them succeed.
    ✘ the energy needed to make a catalyst work [sci.rates.catalyst-defined-not-effect] — A catalyst lowers the activation energy, so the idea exists with or without one.
- 02 (Use the examiner's collision wording): The temperature of a reaction mixture is raised. Which answer would the mark scheme credit in full?
    ✔ the particles have more energy, so there are more successful collisions per unit time — Energy first, then successful collisions per unit time. That is the full three-mark shape.
    ✘ there are more collisions [sci.rates.less-collisions-wording] — 'More collisions' on its own is the phrasing Summer 2025 refused to credit. The mark is in the word successful, and now in per unit time.
    ✘ the particles get bigger, so they hit each other more [sci.rates.less-collisions-wording] — Heating does not change the size of a particle. It changes how fast it moves, and that is what the answer must say.
    ✘ the activation energy is lowered [sci.rates.activation-energy-as-output] — Only a catalyst lowers the activation energy. Heating gets more particles over the bar, it does not move the bar.
- 03 (Separate frequency from energy): A solution is made more concentrated. Which single idea explains the faster rate?
    ✔ more particles in the same volume, so collisions are more frequent — Frequency only. Concentration does not change how hard the particles hit, which is why it is worth fewer marks than temperature.
    ✘ the particles move faster, so they collide harder [sci.rates.less-collisions-wording] — That is the temperature explanation. Concentration changes how many particles are in the space, not how fast they move.
    ✘ the activation energy falls [sci.rates.activation-energy-as-output] — Only a catalyst does that. Concentration leaves the activation energy exactly where it was.
    ✘ the reaction makes more product [sci.rates.rate-as-yield] — A faster reaction makes the same product sooner. How much is made depends on the amounts of reactant.
- 04 (Define a catalyst): Which statement about a catalyst is correct?
    ✔ it increases the rate and is not used up — Both halves matter: it speeds the reaction, and it is there at the end with the same mass.
    ✘ it increases the rate and is slowly used up [sci.rates.catalyst-used-up] — A catalyst is recovered chemically unchanged and with the same mass. That is how a data question identifies it.
    ✘ it increases the amount of product formed [sci.rates.rate-as-yield] — The same amounts of reactant make the same amount of product; the catalyst only changes how long it takes.
    ✘ it appears on the left of the balanced equation [sci.rates.catalyst-used-up] — A catalyst is not a reactant, so it is not in the equation. It is written above the arrow when it is shown at all.
- 05 (Explain catalytic action (Higher)): How does a catalyst increase the rate of a reaction?
    ✔ it provides an alternative reaction pathway of lower activation energy — The sentence the mark scheme prints, and it is worth two of the three marks on this part.
    ✘ it gives the particles more energy [sci.rates.catalyst-defined-not-effect] — That is what heating does. A catalyst lowers the bar instead of raising the particles.
    ✘ it makes the particles collide more often [sci.rates.catalyst-defined-not-effect] — The collisions are just as frequent. More of them succeed, because less energy is needed.
    ✘ it increases the energy change of the reaction [sci.rates.activation-energy-as-output] — The energy change is fixed by the reactants and products, and a catalyst leaves both ends of the profile exactly where they were.
- 06 (Surface area to volume ratio): A cube of side 2 cm is cut into 8 equal cubes. What happens to the surface area and the volume?
    ✔ the surface area doubles to 48 cm² and the volume stays at 8 cm³ — Cutting exposes faces that were inside. Nothing is added, so the volume is still 8 cm³ and the ratio rises from 3 to 6 cm⁻¹.
    ✘ the surface area stays at 24 cm² and the volume stays at 8 cm³ [sci.rates.surface-area-not-ratio] — Every cut makes two new faces that were not exposed before, so the total area goes up even though the solid is the same.
    ✘ the surface area is 6 cm² and the volume stays at 8 cm³ [sci.quant.ratio-inverted] — That is the area of one small cube. There are 8 of them.
    ✘ both the surface area and the volume double [sci.rates.surface-area-not-ratio] — Cutting cannot make more substance. The volume is unchanged, and that is what makes the ratio the interesting number.
- 07 (Separate rate from yield): A reaction is repeated with a catalyst. Everything else is the same. What happens to the total volume of gas collected?
    ✔ it is unchanged, but it is collected sooner — The amounts of reactant fix the amount of product. A catalyst changes only the time it takes.
    ✘ it is larger, because the reaction is faster [sci.rates.rate-as-yield] — Faster is not more. On a graph the new curve is steeper but levels off at exactly the same height.
    ✘ it is smaller, because the catalyst uses some up [sci.rates.catalyst-used-up] — A catalyst is not used up and is not a reactant, so it takes nothing away from the product.
    ✘ it depends on how much catalyst is added [sci.rates.rate-as-yield] — More catalyst can make the reaction faster still, but the amount of product is set by the reactants.
- 08 (Recall the kind of substance catalysts are): Which type of substance is most often used as a catalyst?
    ✔ transition metals and their compounds — The specification's own words, and manganese(IV) oxide for hydrogen peroxide is the named example.
    ✘ Group 1 metals [sci.rates.mno2-not-recalled] — Group 1 metals react violently with water rather than catalysing things. The catalysts are the transition metals.
    ✘ noble gases [sci.rates.mno2-not-recalled] — Group 0 gases are unreactive and take no part at all, which is the opposite of what a catalyst does.
    ✘ halogens [sci.rates.mno2-not-recalled] — Halogens are reactive non-metals that take part in reactions and are used up in them.

### q.science.c2.c2-collision-theory-catalysts.0007 [verified] practice · diff 4 · H · C2 calc · 3 marks · (a)state1|(b)explain2 · The effect of a catalyst on the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide

## INSIGHT
RULE: Rate is about collisions. Temperature changes two things (more frequent collisions, and a greater proportion with energy at or above the activation energy); concentration and surface area change one (more frequent collisions). A catalyst increases the rate without being used up by providing an alternative pathway of lower activation energy, and manganese(IV) oxide is the one CCEA names. Every explanation ends in more or fewer successful collisions per unit time, and none of these changes alters how much product is made.
A*: Explaining a change with frequency AND energy where both apply, writing 'successful collisions per unit time' every time, and describing a catalyst's effect on the rate rather than reciting its definition.
