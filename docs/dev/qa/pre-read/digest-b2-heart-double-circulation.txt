## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.science.b2.b2-heart-double-circulation.01 [verified] paper 7 calc
STEM: Cardiac output is the volume of blood the heart pumps out in one minute.
cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate
(a) Calculate the cardiac output of a person at rest whose stroke volume is 70 cm³ and whose heart rate is 68 beats per minute. [2]
(b) After a year of training her stroke volume is 85 cm³ and her resting heart rate is 60. Calculate the percentage increase in her resting cardiac output. [3]
  FIG: svg-gen — table
  1. cardiac output = 70 × 68 = 4760 cm³ per minute   — Multiply, because each beat pushes out one stroke volume and there are that many beats every minute. Adding the two numbers is meaningless: they are not the same kind of quantity.  (P1,P2)
  2. after training: 85 × 60 = 5100 cm³ per minute   — The heart beats more slowly but pushes out more each time. That is what 'strengthened heart muscle' means in practice.  (P3)
  3. increase = 5100 − 4760 = 340 cm³ per minute   — Find the increase before you touch percentages.  (P4)
  4. percentage increase = 340 ÷ 4760 × 100 = 7.1%   — Divide by the untrained output, the one she started from.  (P5)  WHY: ✔Because a percentage increase is always measured against the starting value | ✘Because the untrained output is the smaller number | ✘Because the question mentions it first
  FINAL: (a) 4760 cm³ per minute. (b) 7.1%.
  TWIN: During hard exercise the same person's stroke volume is 110 cm³ and her heart rate is 153 beats per minute. Calculate her cardiac output, in cm³ per minute.  → numeric 16830 cm³ per minute tol={"type":"absolute","value":5} forms=decimal

### we.science.b2.b2-heart-double-circulation.02 [verified] paper B2 calc
STEM: A drop of blood arrives in the heart from the body. Describe its route until it leaves for the body again, naming every chamber and vessel it passes through. [4]
  FIG: svg — The heart in section with the four chambers, the vena cava, pulmonary artery, pulmonary vein and aorta, the valves, the thick left ventricle wall and the coronary vessels labelled.
  1. It arrives in the right atrium through the vena cava.   — Deoxygenated blood comes back from the body in the vena cava, and it always arrives in an atrium. Atria receive; ventricles pump.  (P1)
  2. It passes down into the right ventricle, through the valve between them.   — Down on the same side. The valve stops it coming straight back up when the ventricle squeezes.  (P2)
  3. The right ventricle pumps it out through the pulmonary artery to the lungs, where it picks up oxygen.   — The pulmonary artery is the odd one out: an artery carrying deoxygenated blood. It is an artery because it leaves the heart, not because of what it carries.  (P3)
  4. It comes back in the pulmonary vein to the left atrium, down into the left ventricle, and out through the aorta to the body.   — The second half mirrors the first, on the other side. The aorta is the last vessel, and the left ventricle's thick wall is what gets the blood all the way round.  (P4)
  FINAL: Vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta.
  TWIN: Name the blood vessel that carries blood from the lungs back to the heart.  → text accepted=["the pulmonary vein"] keyWords=[{"any":["pulmonary vein"],"marks":1,"reject":["pulmonary artery"]}]

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.science.b2.b2-heart-double-circulation.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Recall artery and vein (previous topic)): Before we start, three things from the last topic. Which vessel carries blood AWAY from the heart?
    ✔ an artery — An artery. That definition is about direction, not about oxygen — which matters more than you would think in this topic.
    ✘ a vein [sci.vessels.lumen-and-wall-swapped] — Veins carry blood back towards the heart. Arteries carry it away.
    ✘ a capillary [sci.vessels.capillary-role-unknown] — Capillaries are the tiny exchange vessels between the two.
    ✘ whichever one carries oxygenated blood [sci.circulation.artery-defined-by-oxygen] — That is the trap this topic is built on. Artery and vein are defined by direction, not by the blood they carry.
- p2 (Recall valves (previous topic)): What do the valves in a vein do?
    ✔ They stop blood flowing backwards — They prevent backflow. The valves in the heart do exactly the same job.
    ✘ They pump the blood along [sci.heart.valves-pump] — A valve has no muscle: it can only open or shut. The pumping is done by the heart and by the muscles around the vein.
    ✘ They filter the blood [sci.heart.valve-function-unknown] — Filtering blood is the kidney's job.
    ✘ They control how much oxygen the blood carries [sci.heart.valve-function-unknown] — Oxygen is picked up in the lungs and carried by haemoglobin, not controlled by valves.
- p3 (Recall the exchange vessel (previous topic)): Where does blood exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide with the body's tissues?
    ✔ in the capillaries — Capillaries: wall one cell thick and permeable, so the diffusion distance is tiny.
    ✘ in the arteries [sci.vessels.capillary-role-unknown] — An artery's wall is far too thick for anything to diffuse across.
    ✘ in the veins [sci.vessels.capillary-role-unknown] — By the time blood reaches a vein the exchange has already happened.
    ✘ in the heart [sci.vessels.capillary-role-unknown] — The heart is a pump. Exchange happens out in the tissues.

### dx.science.b2.b2-heart-double-circulation (post) [verified]
- 01 (Name the chambers and what leaves them): Which chamber of the heart pumps blood into the aorta?
    ✔ the left ventricle — The left ventricle, with the thickest wall, because the aorta feeds the whole body.
    ✘ the right ventricle [sci.circulation.sides-reversed] — The right ventricle pumps into the pulmonary artery, which goes only as far as the lungs.
    ✘ the left atrium [sci.heart.atrium-pumps-to-body] — An atrium receives blood and passes it down to the ventricle below it. Ventricles do the pumping out.
    ✘ the right atrium [sci.heart.atrium-pumps-to-body] — The right atrium receives deoxygenated blood from the vena cava.
- 02 (Separate direction from oxygen content): Which vessel carries deoxygenated blood away from the heart?
    ✔ the pulmonary artery — The odd one out: an artery carrying deoxygenated blood, because 'artery' means away from the heart.
    ✘ the pulmonary vein [sci.circulation.pulmonary-vessels-swapped] — The pulmonary vein brings oxygenated blood back from the lungs.
    ✘ the vena cava [sci.circulation.artery-defined-by-oxygen] — The vena cava does carry deoxygenated blood, but towards the heart, not away from it.
    ✘ the aorta [sci.circulation.artery-defined-by-oxygen] — The aorta carries oxygenated blood away from the heart to the body.
- 03 (Relate wall thickness to function): Why is the wall of the left ventricle thicker than the wall of the right ventricle?
    ✔ It has to pump blood all the way round the body, not just to the lungs — Further to pump means more pressure needed, which means more muscle.
    ✘ It holds more blood than the right ventricle [sci.heart.left-wall-holds-more-blood] — Both ventricles push out about the same volume each beat — they have to, or blood would pile up on one side.
    ✘ It receives blood from the lungs, which is at higher pressure [sci.heart.left-wall-holds-more-blood] — Blood arriving from the lungs is at low pressure. The thick wall is about what the ventricle has to do next.
    ✘ It contains the valves, which need thicker muscle around them [sci.heart.valves-pump] — Both sides have valves, and valves are flaps of tissue, not muscle.
- 04 (Name the vessels of the organs): Which vessel carries blood containing digested food from the intestine to the liver?
    ✔ the hepatic portal vein — The hepatic portal vein: the one vessel that runs from one organ's capillaries straight to another's.
    ✘ the hepatic artery [sci.circulation.organ-vessels-swapped] — The hepatic artery brings oxygenated blood to the liver from the aorta.
    ✘ the hepatic vein [sci.circulation.organ-vessels-swapped] — The hepatic vein takes blood away from the liver, back towards the vena cava.
    ✘ the renal vein [sci.circulation.organ-vessels-swapped] — Renal means kidney. Hepatic means liver.
- 05 (Calculate cardiac output): A person's stroke volume is 70 cm³ and her heart rate is 68 beats per minute. What is her cardiac output?
    ✔ 4760 cm³ per minute — 70 × 68: one stroke volume for every beat, and that many beats a minute.
    ✘ 138 cm³ per minute [sci.quant.added-instead-of-multiplied] — The two numbers have been added. A volume and a rate cannot be added; multiplying them gives a volume per minute.
    ✘ 1.03 cm³ per minute [sci.quant.divided-instead-of-multiplied] — Dividing gives the volume per beat per beat, which is not a quantity. Multiply.
    ✘ 70 cm³ per minute [sci.quant.divided-instead-of-multiplied] — That is the volume of a single beat. Cardiac output is what the heart pumps in a whole minute.
- 06 (Recall the effects of regular exercise): What happens to the circulatory system after months of regular exercise?
    ✔ The heart muscle is strengthened and cardiac output at rest increases — Those are the specification's own two effects. A stronger heart pushes out more per beat, so it can beat more slowly.
    ✘ The heart muscle is strengthened and cardiac output at rest decreases [sci.exercise.output-falls-with-training] — The resting pulse falls, but the stroke volume rises by more, so the output at rest goes up.
    ✘ The heart grows more valves [sci.heart.valves-pump] — The number of valves never changes: four, for life.
    ✘ The blood carries more carbon dioxide [sci.exercise.output-falls-with-training] — Training changes the pump, not the composition of the blood being pumped.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.science.b2.b2-heart-double-circulation.01 [verified] sci.quant.added-instead-of-multiplied · earned none · ccea-cer:science:2025-summer:B2F:Q3
STEM: Orla is asked: 'A person's stroke volume is 70 cm³ and her heart rate is 68 beats per minute. Calculate her cardiac output. [2]'
Her working:
  ✘ 1. cardiac output = stroke volume + heart rate
    2. = 70 + 68
    3. = 138 cm³ per minute
  WRONG: The equation itself is wrong, and everything after it follows the mistake. Stroke volume is a volume per beat and heart rate is a number of beats per minute; they are multiplied, and the units then give cm³ per minute. Adding them would mean adding cubic centimetres to beats, which is not a quantity at all.
  FIX: cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate / = 70 × 68 / = 4760 cm³ per minute
  FEEDBACK: Nothing yet, because a wrong equation earns nothing and nothing can follow through from it. The quick check is the units: write them beside the numbers, and "cm³ per beat × beats per minute = cm³ per minute" tells you the operation before you have to remember it.

### ftm.science.b2.b2-heart-double-circulation.02 [verified] sci.circulation.pulmonary-vessels-swapped · earned P2 · ccea-cer:science:2025-summer:B2H:Q1
STEM: Daire is asked: 'Describe the route of blood from the right ventricle to the left ventricle. [3]'
His answer:
  ✘ 1. The right ventricle pumps blood into the pulmonary vein.
    2. The pulmonary vein carries it to the lungs, where it picks up oxygen.
    3. It comes back in the pulmonary artery to the left atrium, and then into the left ventricle.
  WRONG: The two pulmonary vessels have been swapped. A vessel is an artery because it carries blood AWAY from the heart, not because of the blood inside it — so the vessel leaving the right ventricle is the pulmonary artery, even though the blood in it is deoxygenated. The one bringing blood back from the lungs is the pulmonary vein.
  FIX: The right ventricle pumps blood into the pulmonary artery. / The pulmonary artery carries it to the lungs, where it picks up oxygen. / It comes back in the pulmonary vein to the left atrium, and then into the left ventricle.
  FEEDBACK: One mark of the three: the lungs and the oxygen are right, and so is the chamber order. The two vessel marks go together, because one swap makes both names wrong. Fix it with the rule rather than the picture: artery means away, vein means towards, and the pulmonary pair is the exception that proves it.
