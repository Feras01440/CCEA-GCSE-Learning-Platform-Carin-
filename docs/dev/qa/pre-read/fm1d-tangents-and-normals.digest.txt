## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: A curve is defined by the equation $y = x^{2} - 6x + 11$
Find the equation of the tangent to this curve at the point where $x = 4$.
  FIG: svg — A parabola with its tangent drawn as a dashed line touching at the point (4, 3), labelled with the tangent's equation.
  1. Differentiate: $\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x - 6$.   — This line is the first mark and it is available before anything can go wrong. Write it even if the rest of the question defeats you.  (MW1)
  2. Substitute $x = 4$: $2(4) - 6 = 2$. The tangent's gradient is $2$.   — The derivative is a rule; this turns it into the one number the straight line needs.  (M1)  WHY: ✔Because the tangent has the curve's gradient at that point | ✘Because 4 is the gradient | ✘Because the tangent passes through the origin
  3. Find the point: substituting $x = 4$ into the **curve** gives $16 - 24 + 11 = 3$, so the point is $(4, 3)$.   — The height comes from the curve. Substituting into the derivative here would hand you the gradient a second time and leave the line unplaced.  (W1)
  4. Put the gradient and the point into $y - y_{1} = m(x - x_{1})$: $y - 3 = 2(x - 4)$.   — Writing the formula out before substituting keeps the signs of the point where they belong, which is where the constant usually goes wrong.  (M2)
  5. Tidy: $y - 3 = 2x - 8$, so $y = 2x - 5$.   — Leave it as y = mx + c unless the question asks for another form. Check the point: putting x = 4 into the tangent gives 3, which is the height you found.  (W2)
  FINAL: $y = 2x - 5$
  TWIN: Find the equation of the tangent to $y = x^{2} + 2x - 5$ at the point where $x = 1$.  → algebraic y = 4x - 6 (equivalent)

### we.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.02 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: A curve is defined by the equation $y = 2x - \frac{3}{x^{1}}$
Find the equation of the normal to this curve at the point where $x = 1$.
  FIG: svg — The curve y = 2x minus 3 over x with its normal at the point (1, minus 1) drawn as a dashed line.
  1. Rewrite the fraction and differentiate: $y = 2x - 3x^{-1}$, so $\frac{dy}{dx} = 2 + \frac{3}{x^{2}}$.   — The negative power has to come up before the rule can reach it, and multiplying -3 by -1 makes that term positive.  (MW1)
  2. At $x = 1$: $2 + 3 = 5$, so the tangent's gradient is $5$.   — One substitution into the derivative. This is the tangent's gradient, not the normal's, and mixing the two up here costs three marks downstream.  (M1)
  3. Normal gradient: $-\frac{1}{5} = -\frac{1}{5}$.   — Turn the number over, then change its sign. CCEA gives a mark for this step on its own, even when the gradient before it was wrong.  (W1)  WHY: ✔Because perpendicular gradients multiply to -1 | ✘Because the normal is always negative | ✘Because the curve is falling there
  4. The point: $2 - 3 = -1$, from the curve, so the normal passes through $(1, -1)$.   — The tangent and the normal share this point, so it is found once and used twice.  (M2)
  5. Substitute: $-1 = -\frac{1}{5}(1) + c$, so $c = -\frac{4}{5}$ and $y = -\frac{1}{5}x - \frac{4}{5}$.   — Fractions in the constant are normal on this paper. Leaving the answer as a decimal is fine, and so is clearing the fraction if the question asks for whole numbers.  (W2)
  FINAL: $y = -\frac{1}{5}x - \frac{4}{5}$
  TWIN: Find the equation of the normal to $y = 3x + \frac{4}{x^{1}}$ at the point where $x = 2$.  → algebraic y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 9 (equivalent)

### we.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.03 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: The tangent to $y = x^{3} - 12x$ at the point where $x = 1$ meets that curve a second time.
Find the coordinates of that second point.
  FIG: svg — A cubic curve with a dashed tangent touching it on the right and crossing it again on the left, both points labelled.
  1. The tangent first: $\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^{2} - 12$, so at $x = 1$ the gradient is $-9$ and the point is $(1, -11)$.   — Nothing can be solved until the tangent has an equation, so the first half of this question is the ordinary four-step method.  (MW1)
  2. So the tangent is $y = -9x - 2$.   — Check it against the point: -9 times 1, take away 2, gives -11, which is the height you found.  (W1)
  3. Set the curve equal to the tangent: $x^{3} - 12x = -9x - 2$.   — Where a line meets a curve, their y values agree. Every crossing is a solution of this one equation, the touching point included.  (M1)
  4. Collect everything on one side: $x^{3} - 3x + 2 = 0$.   — Bring it to zero before factorising. The cubic is about to show you the answer for free.  (W2)
  5. $x = 1$ is a repeated root because the line is a tangent there, so $(x - 1)^{2}$ is a factor: $(x - 1)^{2}(x + 2) = 0$.   — Touching means the root appears twice. Knowing that turns a cubic you would have to solve into one you can simply read.  (M2)  WHY: ✔Because a tangent touches rather than crosses at that point | ✘Because cubics always have repeated roots | ✘Because x = 1 was in the question
  6. The remaining root is $x = -2$, and the curve gives $y = 16$. So the second point is $(-2, 16)$.   — Find the height from the curve or from the tangent: they agree here, because this is a point both pass through. The curve is the safer habit.  (W3)
  FINAL: $(-2, 16)$
  TWIN: The tangent to $y = x^{3} - 3x$ at the point where $x = 2$ meets that curve a second time. Find the coordinates of that second point.  → algebraic (-4, -52) (equivalent)

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Perpendicular gradients): Four quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new method, so answer from what you already know.
What is the gradient of a line perpendicular to $y = 3x + 1$?
    ✔ $-\frac{1}{3}$ — Turn 3 over and change the sign. The two gradients multiply to $-1$, which is what perpendicular means.
    ✘ $-3$ [fm.calc.normal-gradient-not-reciprocated] — The sign changed but the number never turned over. Both moves are needed.
    ✘ $\frac{1}{3}$ [fm.calc.normal-sign-not-flipped] — The number turned over but the sign stayed positive. A perpendicular gradient is the negative reciprocal.
- p2 (Equation of a line from a gradient and a point): A straight line has gradient $4$ and passes through $(2, 5)$. What is its equation?
    ✔ $y = 4x - 3$ — $5 = 4(2) + c$ gives $c = -3$. That substitution is the last step of every tangent and normal question.
    ✘ $y = 4x + 13$ [fm.calc.tangent-constant-slip] — The $8$ was added instead of taken away. From $5 = 8 + c$, $c$ is $-3$.
    ✘ $y = 2x + 5$ [fm.calc.tangent-constant-slip] — The coordinates have been used as the gradient and the intercept. The gradient was given as 4.
- p3 (Differentiate a quadratic (the earlier topic)): Differentiate $y = x^{2} - 6x + 11$.
    ✔ $2x - 6$ — Multiply by the power, step the power down, and the constant goes to zero.
    ✘ $2x + 5$ [fm.calc.constant-term-kept] — The constant differentiates to zero, so it should not appear in the gradient rule.
    ✘ $x - 6$ [fm.calc.coefficient-not-multiplied] — The powers stepped down correctly. Each coefficient is multiplied by its own power as well.
- p4 (Substitute into a quadratic): If $y = x^{2} - 6x + 11$, what is $y$ when $x = 4$?
    ✔ $3$ — $16 - 24 + 11 = 3$. This is the height of the point, and it comes from the curve.
    ✘ $2$ [fm.calc.y-from-line-not-curve] — That is what the derivative gives at $x = 4$, so it is the gradient rather than the height.
    ✘ $51$ [fm.calc.tangent-constant-slip] — The middle term was added instead of subtracted: $16 - 24 + 11$, not $16 + 24 + 11$.

### dx.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.post (post) [verified]
- q1 (Read a tangent gradient from the derivative): A curve has $\frac{dy}{dx} = 6x - 1$. What is the gradient of the **tangent** at the point where $x = 1$?
    ✔ $5$ — $6(1) - 1 = 5$. The tangent shares the curve's gradient at the point of contact.
    ✘ $-\frac{1}{5}$ [fm.calc.normal-gradient-not-reciprocated] — That is the gradient of the normal. The tangent's gradient is the derivative itself, with no reciprocal taken.
    ✘ $-5$ [fm.calc.normal-gradient-not-reciprocated] — The sign has been changed for no reason. Substituting 1 gives a positive 5.
- q2 (Identify which expression gives the height): Where does the $y$-coordinate of the point of contact come from?
    ✔ From the equation of the curve — The point sits on the curve, so substituting its $x$ into the curve gives its height.
    ✘ From $\frac{dy}{dx}$ [fm.calc.y-from-line-not-curve] — The derivative gives the gradient a second time. The height only comes from the curve.
    ✘ From any straight line named in the question [fm.calc.y-from-line-not-curve] — A line mentioned in the question usually passes somewhere else entirely. Take the height from the curve.
- q3 (Form a negative reciprocal): The tangent to a curve at $P$ has gradient $5$. What is the gradient of the normal at $P$?
    ✔ $-\frac{1}{5}$ — Turn it over, change the sign. $5 \times -\frac{1}{5} = -1$, which is what perpendicular means.
    ✘ $-5$ [fm.calc.normal-gradient-not-reciprocated] — The sign changed but the number never turned over. $5 \times -5$ is $-25$, not $-1$.
    ✘ $\frac{1}{5}$ [fm.calc.normal-sign-not-flipped] — The number turned over but kept its sign. $5 \times \frac{1}{5}$ is $1$, not $-1$.
- q4 (Assemble the line from a gradient and a point): A tangent has gradient $2$ and touches at $(4, 3)$. What is its equation?
    ✔ $y = 2x - 5$ — $3 = 2(4) + c$ gives $c = -5$.
    ✘ $y = 2x + 11$ [fm.calc.tangent-constant-slip] — The $8$ was added instead of taken away. From $3 = 8 + c$, $c$ is $-5$.
    ✘ $y = 4x + 3$ [fm.calc.tangent-constant-slip] — The coordinates have been used as the gradient and the intercept. The gradient is the number you found from $\frac{dy}{dx}$.
- q5 (Combine a substitution with a negative reciprocal): A curve has $\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^{2} + 2$. What is the gradient of the **normal** at the point where $x = 1$?
    ✔ $-\frac{1}{5}$ — The tangent gradient is $3 + 2 = 5$, so the normal's is $-\frac{1}{5}$.
    ✘ $5$ [fm.calc.normal-gradient-not-reciprocated] — That is the tangent's gradient. The normal is perpendicular to it, so it still needs the negative reciprocal.
    ✘ $\frac{1}{5}$ [fm.calc.normal-sign-not-flipped] — The number turned over but the sign did not change.
- q6 (Turn a parallel condition into an equation): The tangent to a curve at $P$ is **parallel** to the line $y = 3x - 4$. Which equation do you solve to find $P$?
    ✔ $\frac{dy}{dx} = 3$ — Parallel lines have equal gradients, so the derivative is set equal to the line's gradient.
    ✘ $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$ [fm.calc.default-to-derivative-zero] — Zero belongs there only when the question asks for a flat or stationary point. Here a gradient was given.
    ✘ $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{1}{3}$ [fm.calc.normal-sign-not-flipped] — That is the perpendicular gradient. Parallel means the same gradient, not the negative reciprocal.
- q7 (Work backwards from a normal gradient): The normal to a curve at $A$ has gradient $\frac{1}{2}$. What is $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at $A$?
    ✔ $-2$ — The relationship works both ways: the tangent gradient is the negative reciprocal of the normal's.
    ✘ $\frac{1}{2}$ [fm.calc.normal-gradient-not-reciprocated] — That is the normal's own gradient copied across. The tangent is perpendicular to it.
    ✘ $-\frac{1}{2}$ [fm.calc.normal-sign-not-flipped] — The sign changed but the fraction never turned over. $\frac{1}{2} \times -\frac{1}{2}$ is not $-1$.
- q8 (Choose the method for a second intersection): To find where a tangent meets its curve a second time, what do you do?
    ✔ Set the tangent's equation equal to the curve's and solve — Both graphs share a $y$ wherever they meet, so that single equation finds every crossing, the point of contact included.
    ✘ Set $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$ and solve [fm.calc.default-to-derivative-zero] — That finds where the curve is flat, which is a different question entirely.
    ✘ Set the curve equal to zero and solve [fm.calc.equate-line-and-curve-instead-of-differentiate] — That finds where the curve meets the $x$-axis. The tangent is the other graph in this question.

### q.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.0008 [verified] practice · diff 4 · untiered · FM1 calc · 4 marks · (main)find4 · A tangent to a curve written as a fraction, the shape the 2019 report singled out
  (main) [4] A curve is defined by the equation $y = \frac{8}{x^{1}}$
Find the equation of the tangent to this curve at the point where $x = 2$.
      ANSWER: algebraic y = -2x + 8 (equivalent)
      M1 M1: writing the curve as $y = 8x^{-1}$
      MW1 MW1: $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{8}{x^{2}}$ and $m = -2$

- rp.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.03 [verified] (trap) Where does the $y$-coordinate of the point of contact come from?  →  From the curve. Not from $\frac{dy}{dx}$, and not from any straight line the question mentions.
- rp.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.04 [verified] (trap) A tangent is parallel to $y = 3x - 4$. What equation do you solve?  →  $\frac{dy}{dx} = 3$, because parallel lines have equal gradients.
- rp.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.05 [verified] (trap) The normal at $A$ has gradient $\frac{1}{2}$. What is $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at $A$?  →  $-2$, the negative reciprocal, because the relationship works in both directions.
- rp.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.06 [verified] (procedure) How do you find where a tangent meets its curve again?  →  Set the tangent's equation equal to the curve's, collect to zero and solve; the point of contact is a repeated root.
- rp.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.07 [verified] (trap) Why must a term such as $\frac{3}{x}$ be rewritten before you differentiate it?  →  The power rule is written for powers of $x$, so it becomes $3x^{-1}$ first.
- rp.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.08 [verified] (qa) Which single line earns a mark even when the gradient before it is wrong?  →  The normal's gradient written as the negative reciprocal of your own tangent gradient; CCEA credits it on follow-through.
- rp.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.09 [verified] (trap) A question asks for the coordinates of $Q$ and you have found the perpendicular gradient. What is still to do?  →  Set $\frac{dy}{dx}$ equal to that gradient, solve for $x$, then find $y$ from the curve and write the pair.
- rp.fm.u1.tangents-and-normals.10 [verified] (definition) In one sentence each, what are a tangent and a normal?  →  A tangent is the straight line that touches the curve and travels in its direction; a normal is the straight line through the same point at right angles to the tangent.
