  FIG: svg — A chain of six numbered boxes from an ovum maturing to implantation, with break symbols on the connectors showing where the pill and implant, male sterilisation, the condom and female sterilisation each cut it.
  1. Find the link this method breaks: the pill acts on link 1, before an ovum has developed.   — Every method cuts the chain somewhere. Locating the cut tells you what the answer has to say, and it stops you giving a barrier's answer for a chemical method.  ()  WHY: ✔Because the mechanism mark is about which link is broken | ✘Because the pill is the most reliable method | ✘Because the chain is the same for every method
  2. First half: the pill changes hormone levels.   — That is the specification's own wording, and it is the point that makes the method chemical rather than mechanical.  (P1)
  3. Second half: so no ovum develops or is released.   — A mechanism answer needs the consequence as well as the cause. Writing only 'it changes hormones' leaves the second mark on the table.  (P2)
  4. Check: the answer says nothing about blocking or killing sperm.   — Two reports in a row name this question as one candidates could not do, and a barrier answer is the usual wrong turn.  ()
  FINAL: The pill changes hormone levels, and that stops an ovum developing so none is released.

### dx.science.b2.b2-infertility-contraception.pre (pre) [verified]
- p1 (Recall ovulation (previous topic)): Before we start, three things you have already met. On about which day of a 28-day cycle is an ovum released?
    ✔ day 14 — Day 14. Every method that stops ovulation is aiming at this event.
    ✘ day 1 [sci.cycle.ovulation-day-wrong] — Day 1 is the first day of menstruation.
    ✘ day 5 [sci.cycle.ovulation-day-wrong] — Day 5 is the end of menstruation.
    ✘ day 28 [sci.cycle.ovulation-day-wrong] — Day 28 is the end of the cycle.
- p2 (Recall the site of fertilisation (previous topic)): In which part of the female reproductive system does fertilisation happen?
    ✔ the oviduct — The oviduct. Cutting it is exactly what female sterilisation does.
    ✘ the uterus [sci.fert.site-not-oviduct] — The uterus is where the ball of cells implants afterwards.
    ✘ the ovary [sci.fert.site-not-oviduct] — The ovary releases the ovum; fertilisation happens after it has left.
    ✘ the vagina — Sperm are deposited there, but they have a long way to travel yet.
- p3 (Recall the male reproductive system (earlier topic)): Which tube carries sperm away from the testis?
    ✔ the sperm tube — The sperm tube. Cutting it is male sterilisation.
    ✘ the urethra [sci.repro.male-path-order-wrong] — The urethra carries semen the last part of the way, after the prostate gland.
    ✘ the ureter [sci.repro.urethra-ureter-confused] — The ureter carries urine from the kidney to the bladder.
    ✘ the oviduct [sci.repro.ovary-oviduct-swapped] — The oviduct is part of the female system.

### dx.science.b2.b2-infertility-contraception (post) [verified]
- d1 (Give the pill's mechanism): How does the contraceptive pill prevent pregnancy?
    ✔ it changes hormone levels, so no ovum develops — It changes hormone levels so that no ovum develops or is released.
    ✘ it is a barrier that stops sperm reaching the ovum [sci.contra.pill-mechanism-unknown] — That is a condom. The pill acts inside one body, not between two.
    ✘ it kills the sperm [sci.contra.pill-mechanism-unknown] — Nothing on this specification kills sperm.
    ✘ it blocks the oviducts [sci.contra.sterilisation-wrong-tube] — That is female sterilisation, and it is an operation.
- d2 (Sort a method into CCEA's groups): CCEA sorts methods of contraception into three groups. Which group does the condom belong to?
    ✔ mechanical — Mechanical: it works as a physical barrier.
    ✘ chemical [sci.contra.category-confused] — Chemical methods change hormone levels: the pill and the implant.
    ✘ surgical [sci.contra.category-confused] — Surgical means an operation: sterilisation.
    ✘ hormonal [sci.contra.category-confused] — Hormonal is the grouping other exam boards use. CCEA marks mechanical, chemical and surgical.
- d3 (Tell the two sterilisation operations apart): Which tube is cut in male sterilisation?
    ✔ the sperm tube — The sperm tube, so sperm cannot leave.
    ✘ the oviduct [sci.contra.sterilisation-wrong-tube] — The oviducts are cut in female sterilisation.
    ✘ the urethra [sci.repro.male-path-order-wrong] — Cutting the urethra would stop urine leaving as well, and it is not what is done.
    ✘ the ureter [sci.repro.urethra-ureter-confused] — The ureter is part of the urinary system.
- d4 (Know the one advantage only a barrier has): Which of these is an advantage of a condom that the contraceptive pill does not have?
    ✔ it helps to prevent sexually transmitted infections — Only a barrier stops an infection passing between partners.
    ✘ it is more reliable at preventing pregnancy [sci.contra.pill-prevents-stis] — On survey data the pill is the more reliable of the two.
    ✘ it works for three years at a time [sci.contra.category-confused] — That describes an implant, not a condom.
    ✘ it stops an ovum developing [sci.contra.pill-mechanism-unknown] — That is the pill's mechanism, not the condom's.
- d5 (Give the role of hormones in fertility treatment): Why are hormones given at the start of in vitro fertilisation? (Higher Tier)
    ✔ so that several ova ripen at once — The specification's words are 'to produce multiple ova'.
    ✘ to make the sperm swim faster [sci.ivf.hormones-role-unknown] — The hormones act on the ovaries, not on the sperm.
    ✘ to thicken the uterus lining so an embryo can implant [sci.ivf.hormones-role-unknown] — That is oestrogen's job in the ordinary cycle, and it is not why these hormones are given.
    ✘ to fertilise the ova in the dish [sci.ivf.hormones-role-unknown] — Sperm do the fertilising; hormones only ripen the ova.
- d6 (Turn a count into a percentage): In a survey, 70 of 1000 women using the contraceptive pill for a year became pregnant. What percentage is that?
    ✔ 7.0% — 70 ÷ 1000 × 100 = 7.0%.
    ✘ 93.0% [sci.quant.wrong-percentage-used] — That is the percentage who did not become pregnant.
    ✘ 13.0% [sci.practical.curve-read-from-wrong-line] — That is the figure for the condom, not the pill.
    ✘ 0.5% [sci.practical.curve-read-from-wrong-line] — That is the figure for sterilisation.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.science.b2.b2-infertility-contraception.01 [verified] sci.quant.percentage-wrong-denominator · earned P1 · ccea-cer:science:2024-summer:B2H:Q6
STEM: Ruairí is asked: 'Of the 1250 treatment cycles started by women under 35, 400 ended in a live birth. Calculate the percentage that ended in a live birth. [2]'
His working:
    1. percentage = part ÷ whole × 100
  ✘ 2. = 400 ÷ 850 × 100
    3. = 47.1%
  WRONG: Line 1 is the right rule. Line 2 uses the wrong whole: 850 is the number of cycles that did NOT end in a live birth. The whole is every cycle started, successes included, which is 1250.
  FIX: percentage = part ÷ whole × 100 / = 400 ÷ 1250 × 100 / = 32.0%
  FEEDBACK: One mark of the two: the rule in line 1 stands. The habit that catches this is to ask what the parts add up to — 400 live births and 850 without add to 1250, and that total is always the denominator.

- rp.science.b2.b2-infertility-contraception.04 [verified] (qa) Which tube is cut in male sterilisation, and which in female sterilisation?  →  The sperm tubes are cut in male sterilisation, so sperm cannot leave. The oviducts are cut in female sterilisation, so the ovum and the sperm cannot meet.
- rp.science.b2.b2-infertility-contraception.05 [verified] (procedure) Describe in vitro fertilisation in three steps, as the specification states it. (Higher Tier)  →  Hormones are used to produce multiple ova; the ova are fertilised in a dish in the laboratory; and several embryos are transferred into the uterus.
- rp.science.b2.b2-infertility-contraception.06 [verified] (novel-example) A question asks for an ethical reason why someone might not use contraception. What kind of answer earns the mark?  →  A religious or moral reason: something the person believes to be right or wrong. Cost, side effects and reliability are practical reasons, not ethical ones.
