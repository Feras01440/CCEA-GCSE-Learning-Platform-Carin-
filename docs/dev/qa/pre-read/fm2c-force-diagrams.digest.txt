### we.fm.u2.force-diagrams.01 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: A crate of mass $6$ kg is pulled along a rough horizontal floor by a rope. The rope makes an angle of $26°$ with the floor.

(i) Name the four forces acting on the crate.
(ii) Write down the weight of the crate.
  FIG: svg — A crate of mass 6 kg on a rough horizontal floor. Four arrows leave the crate: R straight up, 60 N straight down, T up to the right along a rope at 26 degrees to the floor, and F to the left along the floor.
  1. The Earth pulls the crate down: the weight, $6 \times 10 = 60$ N, drawn vertically from the centre.   — Start with the Earth, because the weight is on every diagram and never depends on what else is happening.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because every body on the Earth has a weight, whatever it is resting on | ✘Because the crate is moving, so it must have a weight | ✘Because the floor is rough, so the weight is larger
  2. The crate touches the floor, so the floor pushes back: the normal reaction $R$, straight up out of the floor.   — Work round the body in order — Earth, then surfaces, then strings, then sliding — so nothing is forgotten.  (MW1)
  3. The rope pulls along itself, away from the crate: the tension $T$, at $26°$ to the floor.   — A string pulls and never pushes, so the arrow leaves the crate along the rope.  (MW1)
  4. The floor is rough and the crate slides forwards, so friction $F$ acts backwards, along the floor.   — Friction needs a rough surface and a direction of sliding. Both are given, so it is drawn against the motion.  (MW1,W1)
  FINAL: (i) weight, normal reaction, tension and friction  (ii) $60$ N
  TWIN: A crate of mass $11$ kg rests on a rough horizontal floor. Write down its weight, in newtons.  → numeric 110 N tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction

### we.fm.u2.force-diagrams.02 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: A box of mass $9$ kg is held at rest on a smooth slope inclined at $24°$ to the horizontal by a rope lying along the slope.

(i) Name the forces acting on the box.
(ii) State the direction of each one.
  FIG: svg — The same box on the 24 degree slope. A solid arrow labelled R leaves the box at right angles to the slope. A dashed vertical arrow is labelled as not the reaction.
  1. Weight $= 9 \times 10 = 90$ N, vertically downwards from the centre of the box.   — Tilting the surface does not tilt the Earth's pull, so the weight arrow stays vertical.  (MW1)
  2. Normal reaction $R$, at right angles to the slope.   — The surface pushes straight out of itself, so on a slope that is perpendicular to the slope, not vertical.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because the only push a surface makes is the one that stops the box sinking into it | ✘Because the reaction always balances the weight exactly | ✘Because the box is at rest, so every force must be vertical
  3. Tension $T$, up the slope, along the rope.   — The rope pulls away from the box along its own line, and here that line is the slope.  (MW1)
  4. No friction: the slope is smooth.   — Smooth means the surface offers nothing along itself, so drawing friction would be inventing a force.  (W1)
  FINAL: Weight $90$ N vertically down, reaction $R$ at right angles to the slope, tension $T$ up the slope, and no friction
  TWIN: A box of mass $9$ kg rests on a smooth slope. Which force does not act on it?  → mcq ✔friction | ✘the normal reaction [fm.forces.normal-reaction-omitted] | ✘the weight [fm.forces.weight-misplaced]

### we.fm.u2.force-diagrams.03 [verified] paper FM2 calc
STEM: A block of mass $5$ kg rests on a rough horizontal table. A light inextensible string joins it over a smooth pulley at the edge of the table to a block of mass $8$ kg hanging freely.

Name all the forces acting on each block.
  FIG: svg — A block of mass 5 kg on a rough table joined by a string over a pulley at the table's edge to a block of mass 8 kg hanging below. The block on the table carries R up, 50 N down, T towards the pulley and F away from it. The hanging block carries T up and 80 N down.
  1. On the $5$ kg block: weight $50$ N down, normal reaction $R$ up.   — Deal with one body at a time. This one rests on the table, so it has a reaction; write its weight first.  (MW1)
  2. On the $5$ kg block: tension $T$ towards the pulley, friction $F$ away from the pulley.   — The string pulls the block towards the pulley, and the table is rough, so friction opposes that pull.  (MW1)
  3. On the $8$ kg block: weight $80$ N down, tension $T$ up.   — The hanging block touches nothing, so it has no reaction and no friction: two forces only.  (MW1)  WHY: ✔Because a normal reaction needs a surface in contact, and the hanging block has none | ✘Because the hanging block is lighter, so it needs fewer forces | ✘Because the string already includes the reaction
  4. The same letter $T$ is used at both ends of the one string.   — The string is light and the pulley smooth, so the tension does not change along it.  (MW1,W1)
  FINAL: $5$ kg block: $50$ N, $R$, $T$, $F$.  $8$ kg block: $80$ N and $T$.
  TWIN: A different block, of mass $13$ kg, hangs freely from a light string. Write down its weight, in newtons.  → numeric 130 N tol={"type":"absolute","value":0.005} forms=decimal/fraction

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u2.force-diagrams.pre (pre) [verified]
- 01 (Prerequisite: vector or scalar): From an earlier topic: which of these is a vector quantity?
    ✔ force — A force has a size and a direction, which is why it is drawn as an arrow.
    ✘ mass — Mass has a size only. It is measured in kilograms and points nowhere.
    ✘ time — Time has a size only.
    ✘ distance — Distance has a size only; displacement is the vector version.
- 02 (Prerequisite: weight from mass): From an earlier topic: taking $g = 10$ m/s², what is the weight of a body of mass $5$ kg?
    ✔ $50$ N — $5 \times 10 = 50$ N.
    ✘ $5$ N — That is the mass with newtons written after it.
    ✘ $0.5$ N — That divides by $g$ rather than multiplying.
    ✘ $49$ N — That uses $g = 9.8$, which this unit does not.
- 03 (Prerequisite: perpendicular directions): From an earlier topic: a line is drawn at right angles to a surface. What angle does it make with that surface?
    ✔ $90°$ — Right angles and $90°$ are the same thing.
    ✘ $45°$ — That is half a right angle.
    ✘ $180°$ — That is a straight line along the surface and back.
    ✘ $0°$ — That would lie flat along the surface.
- 04 (Prerequisite: reading a vector arrow): From an earlier topic: a quantity is drawn on a diagram as an arrow. What does the arrowhead show?
    ✔ the direction in which the quantity acts — The arrowhead carries the direction; the label carries the size and the name.
    ✘ the size of the quantity — The size is written beside the arrow as a label.
    ✘ the point the quantity acts at — That is where the arrow starts, not where it ends.
    ✘ the unit the quantity is measured in — The unit belongs in the label.

### dx.fm.u2.force-diagrams.post (post) [verified]
- 01 (The normal reaction is never left out): A block rests on a rough horizontal table and is pulled sideways by a string. A student draws the weight, the tension and the friction. Which force has been left out?
    ✔ the normal reaction — The block touches the table, so the table pushes back: one contact, one reaction.
    ✘ a second tension [fm.forces.extra-forces-added] — One string gives one tension. A second one would have to come from a second string.
    ✘ the mass of the block [fm.forces.mass-labelled-not-weight] — Mass is not a force and never appears on a force diagram.
    ✘ nothing: the diagram is complete [fm.forces.normal-reaction-omitted] — The reports name the reaction as the commonest omission of all. Check every contact.
- 02 (The direction of friction): A crate is being pushed to the right across a rough floor. In which direction does the force due to friction on the crate act?
    ✔ to the left, along the floor — Friction opposes the sliding, so it points against the motion, along the surface.
    ✘ to the right, along the floor [fm.forces.friction-direction] — That would help the crate along. Friction always works against the sliding.
    ✘ vertically upwards [fm.forces.reaction-not-perpendicular] — That is the normal reaction. Friction acts along the surface, not out of it.
    ✘ vertically downwards [fm.forces.friction-direction] — Downwards is the weight's direction. Friction lies along the surface.
- 03 (Labelling a weight): A block of mass $12$ kg rests on a table. How should the downward arrow on the force diagram be labelled?
    ✔ $120$ N — $12 \times 10 = 120$ N. A force diagram carries forces, in newtons.
    ✘ $12$ kg [fm.forces.g-omitted-weight] — Kilograms measure mass. Multiply by $g$ to get the weight the arrow shows.
    ✘ $117.6$ N [fm.forces.g-omitted-weight] — That uses $g = 9.8$. The front page of this paper sets $g = 10$ m/s².
    ✘ $1.2$ N [fm.forces.mass-labelled-not-weight] — That divides by $g$. Weight is mass multiplied by $g$.
- 04 (The reaction on a slope): A box rests on a slope inclined at $30°$ to the horizontal. In which direction does the normal reaction act?
    ✔ at right angles to the slope — The surface pushes straight out of itself, so it is perpendicular to the slope.
    ✘ vertically upwards [fm.forces.reaction-not-perpendicular] — That would be right on a horizontal floor. On a slope the push tilts with the surface.
    ✘ up the slope [fm.forces.reaction-not-perpendicular] — Along the slope is where friction and a rope act. The reaction is at right angles to it.
    ✘ at right angles to the ground below the slope [fm.forces.reaction-not-perpendicular] — It is the slope the box rests on that pushes back, not the ground beneath it.
- 05 (The weight on a slope): A box rests on a slope inclined at $30°$ to the horizontal. In which direction does its weight act?
    ✔ vertically downwards — The Earth's pull does not tilt with the surface. The weight stays vertical.
    ✘ down the slope [fm.forces.weight-misplaced] — Part of the weight acts down the slope, but that part is a component, not the weight itself.
    ✘ at right angles into the slope [fm.forces.weight-misplaced] — That is the direction of the other component of the weight, and the opposite of the reaction.
    ✘ at $30°$ to the vertical [fm.forces.weight-misplaced] — The angle of the slope changes the components of the weight, never its direction.
- 06 (Tension in one string): Two blocks are joined by a light inextensible string passing over a smooth pulley. What is true of the tension?
    ✔ it is the same at both ends of the string — A light string over a smooth pulley carries one tension, so one label $T$ serves both ends.
    ✘ it is larger at the end nearer the heavier block [fm.forces.tensions-same-label] — The tension does not change along a light string, whatever hangs from it.
    ✘ the two ends need different letters [fm.forces.tensions-same-label] — Different letters are for different strings. One string takes one letter.
    ✘ it equals the weight of the hanging block [fm.forces.equilibrium-assumed-when-accelerating] — That is only true when the blocks are at rest. If they accelerate, the tension is smaller than that weight.
- 07 (Forces that do not belong): A box slides down a rough slope. Which of these should not be drawn on its force diagram?
    ✔ the component of the weight down the slope — The weight is already drawn. Its components are working, and they belong beside the diagram, not on it.
    ✘ the force due to friction [fm.forces.friction-direction] — The slope is rough and the box slides, so friction is there, acting up the slope.
    ✘ the normal reaction [fm.forces.normal-reaction-omitted] — The box touches the slope, so the slope pushes back.
    ✘ the weight [fm.forces.weight-misplaced] — The weight is on every force diagram.
- 08 (Arrows and labels): A candidate draws every force in the right place but puts no arrowheads on the lines. What happens?
    ✔ marks are lost, because a line without an arrowhead does not say which way the force acts — A force is a vector; the arrowhead is how the direction is written down.
    ✘ nothing is lost, because the positions are right [fm.forces.arrows-or-labels-missing] — The reports return to this every year: no arrowhead, no direction, no mark.
    ✘ only the weight needs an arrowhead [fm.forces.arrows-or-labels-missing] — Every force needs one, and every force needs a label as well.
    ✘ the labels can replace the arrowheads [fm.forces.arrows-or-labels-missing] — A label names the force and gives its size. The arrowhead gives its direction.

- rp.fm.u2.force-diagrams.04 [verified] (definition) In which direction does the normal reaction on a body act?  →  At right angles to the surface the body rests on, pointing away from that surface.
- rp.fm.u2.force-diagrams.05 [verified] (definition) In which direction does friction act on a body that is sliding?  →  Along the surface, opposite to the direction the body is sliding.
- rp.fm.u2.force-diagrams.06 [verified] (qa) A light string passes over a smooth pulley. What can be said about the tension at its two ends?  →  It is the same at both ends, so one label serves for both.
- rp.fm.u2.force-diagrams.07 [verified] (trap) A box rests on a slope. Which two forces are most often left off the diagram?  →  The normal reaction and the force due to friction.
- rp.fm.u2.force-diagrams.08 [verified] (trap) Why is the pull down a slope not drawn as a separate force?  →  It is part of the weight, which is already drawn: its components are working, not forces.
- rp.fm.u2.force-diagrams.09 [verified] (qa) How many normal reactions act on a block resting on one flat surface?  →  One: a single contact gives a single normal reaction.
- rp.fm.u2.force-diagrams.10 [verified] (trap) What is wrong with labelling an arrow on a force diagram $12$ kg?  →  Kilograms measure mass, not force. The label should be the weight, 120 N.

## INSIGHT
RULE: Every force is an arrow with a label: weight mg vertically down from the centre, normal reaction perpendicular to the surface, friction along the surface against motion, tension along each string with its own label - and nothing else.
A*: A clean diagram for a two-body or inclined system that leads straight to correct resolving and F = ma, with 'show that' results derived rather than checked.
