## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.fm.u1.matrix-simultaneous-equations.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: Solve the simultaneous equations
$4x + 3y = 18$
$2x + 5y = 16$
You must use a matrix method.
Give the values of $x$ and $y$.
  FIG: svg — Two simultaneous equations beside the three matrices they become: the coefficients in a box, the unknowns in a column and the constants in a column.
  1. Copy the numbers into three matrices: $\begin{pmatrix}4 & 3 \\ 2 & 5\end{pmatrix}$ for the coefficients, a column for $x$ and $y$, and $\begin{pmatrix}18 \\ 16\end{pmatrix}$ for the constants.   — This line is transcription, not mathematics, and it earns a mark before any arithmetic. Both equations are already in the right form here, so nothing has to be rearranged.  (MW1)
  2. $\det A = (4)(5) - (3)(2) = 14$.   — The determinant on its own line. A zero here would mean the pair has no unique solution, so it is worth knowing before any more work is done.  (M2)
  3. Swap, negate and divide: $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{14}\begin{pmatrix}5 & -3 \\ -2 & 4\end{pmatrix}$.   — The same three actions as any inverse. From this point the question is an ordinary matrix equation.  (M3)  WHY: ✔Because the pair is now AX = B, and an inverse is what undoes a multiplying matrix | ✘Because the determinant has to be cancelled from both sides | ✘Because the two equations can be added together
  4. $X = A^{-1}B = \begin{pmatrix}\frac{5}{14} & -\frac{3}{14} \\ -\frac{1}{7} & \frac{2}{7}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}18 \\ 16\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}3 \\ 2\end{pmatrix}$.   — The inverse goes in front of the constants. The product of a two by two and a column is a column, which is what the two unknowns need.  (M4)
  5. Reading the column off: $x = 3$ and $y = 2$. Checking in both equations: $4(3) + 3(2) = 18$ and $2(3) + 5(2) = 16$.   — The column is not the answer line. Name both values, and substitute them into both original equations: that check costs twenty seconds and catches every slip above it.  (W1)
  FINAL: $x = 3$, $y = 2$
  TWIN: Solve the simultaneous equations $2x + 3y = 14$ and $3x + 5y = 22$, using a matrix method. Give the values of $x$ and $y$.  → algebraic (4, 2) (equivalent)

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.matrix-simultaneous-equations.pre (pre) [draft]
- p1 (Rearranging a linear equation): Three quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new method, so answer from what you already know.
Rearrange $5x = 11 - 2y$ so that both letters are on the left.
    ✔ $5x + 2y = 11$ — The term in $y$ crosses the equals sign and changes sign.
    ✘ $5x - 2y = 11$ — Moving $-2y$ across makes it $+2y$, not $-2y$.
    ✘ $5x + 2y = -11$ — The $11$ was already on the right and does not move, so its sign is unchanged.
- p2 (The inverse of a 2 by 2): What is the inverse of $\begin{pmatrix}2 & 3 \\ 3 & 5\end{pmatrix}$?
    ✔ $\begin{pmatrix}5 & -3 \\ -3 & 2\end{pmatrix}$ — The determinant is $1$, so swapping and negating is the whole of it.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}5 & 3 \\ 3 & 2\end{pmatrix}$ — The leading diagonal swapped and the other one kept its signs.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}2 & -3 \\ -3 & 5\end{pmatrix}$ — The signs changed and the leading diagonal never swapped.
- p3 (The shape of a product): A $2 \times 2$ matrix is multiplied by a $2 \times 1$ column. What shape is the answer?
    ✔ $2 \times 1$ — The inner numbers match and the outer two give the shape, so the answer is a column.
    ✘ $2 \times 2$ — The columns of the answer come from the second matrix, which has one.
    ✘ $1 \times 2$ — Rows come first: the answer has as many rows as the first matrix.

### dx.fm.u1.matrix-simultaneous-equations.post (post) [draft]
- d1 (Transcribing the coefficients): For the pair $4x + 3y = 18$ and $2x + 5y = 16$, which is the coefficient matrix?
    ✔ $\begin{pmatrix}4 & 3 \\ 2 & 5\end{pmatrix}$ — One equation to a row, in the order the terms are written.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}4 & 2 \\ 3 & 5\end{pmatrix}$ [fm.matrix.rows-and-columns-swapped] — The coefficients have been read down the columns instead of across the rows.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}4 & 18 \\ 2 & 16\end{pmatrix}$ [fm.matrix.dimensions-not-conformable] — The constants have gone into the coefficient matrix. They belong in a column of their own.
- d2 (A missing term): An equation in a pair reads $3y = 12$, with no term in $x$. What goes in its $x$ slot?
    ✔ $0$ — A missing term is a zero. The row has to be complete for the product to reproduce the equation.
    ✘ The slot is left empty [fm.matrix.dimensions-not-conformable] — A matrix has no empty slots: leaving one out would change its shape.
    ✘ $1$ [fm.matrix.dimensions-not-conformable] — A coefficient of $1$ would put an $x$ into the equation that is not there.
- d3 (The method once the pair is transcribed): A pair has been written as $AX = B$. What comes next?
    ✔ Find $A^{-1}$ and work out $A^{-1}B$ — From here it is an ordinary matrix equation, with the inverse in front.
    ✘ Work out $BA^{-1}$ [fm.matrix.inverse-order] — The inverse goes in front of both sides. $BA^{-1}$ is a different product, and here it does not even exist.
    ✘ Add the two equations to remove one unknown [fm.matrix.method-instruction-ignored] — That is elimination. When the question names the matrix method, elimination earns nothing.
- d4 (Naming the two values): Solving a pair gives the column $\begin{pmatrix}3 \\ 2\end{pmatrix}$. What should be written on the answer line?
    ✔ $x = 3$, $y = 2$ — The top entry is $x$ and the bottom is $y$.
    ✘ $x = 2$, $y = 3$ [fm.matrix.values-in-wrong-slots] — The two values have swapped. The column is read top to bottom in the order the unknowns were written.
    ✘ The column $\begin{pmatrix}3 \\ 2\end{pmatrix}$ on its own [fm.matrix.values-in-wrong-slots] — The question asked for two values, so both have to be named. A column is not an answer line.
- d5 (The singular case): The coefficient matrix of a pair has determinant $0$. What does that tell you?
    ✔ There is no unique solution, because the matrix has no inverse — The two equations describe parallel lines, so they never meet at a single point.
    ✘ Both unknowns are zero [fm.matrix.singular-not-recognised] — The determinant says nothing about the values; it says the method cannot run.
    ✘ The pair must be solved by elimination instead [fm.matrix.singular-not-recognised] — Elimination would not find a unique solution either: there is not one to find.
