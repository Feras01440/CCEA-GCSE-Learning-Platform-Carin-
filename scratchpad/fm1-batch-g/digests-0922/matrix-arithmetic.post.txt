It also stops you starting a product that cannot be done. No mark is going for it; it protects the ones below.
  2. Row 1 of $A$ against column 1 of $B$: $(3)(5) + (-1)(-1) = 16$.   — Work along the row and down the column at the same time, and write the sum out before evaluating it. ### dx.fm.u1.matrix-arithmetic.pre (pre) [draft]
- p1 (Multiply and add with negatives): Three quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new method, so answer from what you already know.
What is $(2) \times (5) + (4) \times (-1)$?
    ✔ $6$ — $10$ and $-4$ add to $6$.
    ✘ $14$ — The second product is negative, because one of its two numbers is negative.
    ✘ $10$ — Only the first product has been used. Both products are added together.
- p2 (Reading a shape as rows by columns): A rectangle of numbers has 2 rows and 3 columns. Which description is right?
    ✔ It has 6 numbers in it — Two rows of three, so six numbers. That count is worth doing before you start writing an answer.
    ✘ It has 5 numbers in it — The rows and columns are multiplied, not added.
    ✘ It has 2 numbers in it — Each of the two rows holds three numbers.
- p3 (Multiplying a negative by a positive): What is $3 \times (-1)$?
    ✔ $-3$ — A negative multiplied by a positive stays negative.
    ✘ $3$ — The sign has been dropped. Three lots of a negative number is negative.
    ✘ $2$ — That is $3$ added rather than multiplied.

### dx.fm.u1.matrix-arithmetic.post (post) [draft]
- d1 (Adding matrices): Which of these is $A + B$, for $A = \begin{pmatrix}3 & -1 \\ 2 & 4\end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix}5 & 2 \\ -1 & 0\end{pmatrix}$?
    ✔ $\begin{pmatrix}8 & 1 \\ 1 & 4\end{pmatrix}$ — Each entry added to the entry in the same position.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}15 & -2 \\ -2 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ [fm.matrix.elementwise-product] — The entries have been multiplied rather than added.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}2 & 3 \\ -3 & -4\end{pmatrix}$ — That is $B - A$. The question asked for the sum.
- d2 (Multiplying row by column): Which of these is $AB$, for $A = \begin{pmatrix}3 & -1 \\ 2 & 4\end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix}5 & 2 \\ -1 & 0\end{pmatrix}$?
    ✔ $\begin{pmatrix}16 & 6 \\ 6 & 4\end{pmatrix}$ — Each entry is a row of $A$ paired with a column of $B$.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}15 & -2 \\ -2 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ [fm.matrix.elementwise-product] — The entries in matching positions have been multiplied. A product pairs a whole row with a whole column.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}13 & -3 \\ 18 & -2\end{pmatrix}$ [fm.matrix.rows-and-columns-swapped] — Rows have been paired with rows. It is a row of the first against a column of the second.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}19 & 3 \\ -3 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ [fm.matrix.inverse-order] — That is $BA$. The order of a matrix product changes the answer.
- d3 (The shape rule): A matrix $P$ is $2 \times 2$ and a matrix $Q$ is $2 \times 3$. Which products exist?
    ✔ $PQ$ only — For $PQ$ the inner numbers are $2$ and $2$, so it exists and comes out $2 \times 3$. For $QP$ they are $3$ and $2$, which do not match.
    ✘ Both $PQ$ and $QP$ [fm.matrix.dimensions-not-conformable] — $QP$ has inner numbers $3$ and $2$. They do not match, so that product does not exist.
    ✘ Neither, because the shapes are different [fm.matrix.dimensions-not-conformable] — Multiplication does not need identical shapes. It needs the inner two numbers to match, and for $PQ$ they do.
- d4 (Squaring a matrix): For a matrix $M$, what does $M^{2}$ mean?
    ✔ $M \times M$, worked row by column — A power is a repeated product, and matrix products are row by column.
    ✘ Each entry of $M$ squared [fm.matrix.scalar-multiple-squared] — That is the slip the reports name most often here. It gives a different matrix entirely.
    ✘ $2M$, with every entry doubled [fm.matrix.scalar-multiple-squared] — Doubling is $2M$. Squaring is multiplying the matrix by itself.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u1.matrix-arithmetic.01 [draft] fm.matrix.elementwise-product · earned none · ccea-cer:further-maths:2023-summer:FM1:Q8
STEM: Seán was asked to work out $AB$, where $A = \begin{pmatrix}3 & -1 \\ 2 & 4\end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix}5 & 2 \\ -1 & 0\end{pmatrix}$. His working:
    1. A is 2 x 2 and B is 2 x 2, so AB is 2 x 2
  ✘ 2. row 1, column 1: (3)(5) = 15
    3. row 1, column 2: (-1)(2) = -2
    4. row 2, column 1: (2)(-1) = -2
    5. row 2, column 2: (4)(0) = 0
    6. AB = (15 -2 ; -2 0)
  WRONG: Line 1 is right, and the shape he predicted is the shape he produced, so nothing looks wrong on the page.
Line 2 multiplies the two entries sitting in the same position. A matrix product pairs a whole row of the first with a whole column of the second, so that entry is $(3)(5) + (-1)(-1) = 16$.
Every line after it repeats the same pairing, so the whole matrix is wrong even though the arithmetic in each line is right.
  FIX: row 1, column 1: (3)(5) + (-1)(-1) = 16 / row 1, column 2: (3)(2) + (-1)(0) = 6 / AB = (16 6 ; 6 4)
  FEEDBACK: The shape you predicted is right and the arithmetic is accurate, so this is a method problem rather than a careless one. Each entry of a product is a sum of two products, never a single one: if a line of your working has no plus sign in it, you have paired the wrong things. Say "row of the first, column of the second" as you write each line.

## RETRIEVAL PROMPTS
- rp.fm.u1.matrix-arithmetic.01 [draft] (procedure) How do you find the entry in row 2, column 1 of a matrix product?  →  Take row 2 of the first matrix and column 1 of the second, multiply them term by term and add the results.
- rp.fm.u1.matrix-arithmetic.02 [draft] (qa) When can two matrices be added, and when can they be multiplied?  →  Added only when they are the same shape. Multiplied only when the columns of the first match the rows of the second.
- rp.fm.u1.matrix-arithmetic.03 [draft] (formula) A matrix that is $m \times n$ is multiplied by one that is $n \times p$. What shape is the answer?  →  It is $m \times p$: the inner numbers match, and the outer two give the shape of the product.
- rp.fm.u1.matrix-arithmetic.04 [draft] (trap) What does $M^{2}$ mean for a matrix $M$, and what does it not mean?  →  It means $M \times M$, worked row by column. It does not mean squaring each entry.
- rp.fm.u1.matrix-arithmetic.05 [draft] (trap) Is $AB$ the same as $BA$?  →  No. Matrix multiplication depends on the order, and often only one of the two products exists at all.
- rp.fm.u1.matrix-arithmetic.06 [draft] (procedure) What is the first thing to write down before multiplying two matrices?  →  The two shapes, side by side. They say whether the product exists and how many entries the answer needs.
