### we.fm.u1.matrix-equations.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: It is given that $AX = B$, where $A = \begin{pmatrix}3 & 1 \\ 5 & 2\end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix}7 \\ 12\end{pmatrix}$.
Find the matrix $X$.
  FIG: svg — Four lines of working: A X equals B, the inverse pushed in from the left of both sides, the identity times X, and X equals the inverse of A times B.
  1. Check the shapes: $A$ is $2 \times 2$ and $B$ is $2 \times 1$, so $X$ is $2 \times 1$.   — Two seconds, and it rules out writing a square answer. It also confirms the product on the right can be formed at all. No mark is going for it; it protects the ones below.
  2. $\det A = (3)(2) - (1)(5) = 1$, so $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix}2 & -1 \\ -5 & 3\end{pmatrix}$.   — The determinant on its own line, as always. A determinant of zero would stop the question here, so it is worth knowing before any work is done.  (MW1,M2)
  3. Pre-multiply both sides: $A^{-1}AX = A^{-1}B$, so $X = A^{-1}B$.   — Write this line even though it looks obvious. $A^{-1}A$ is the identity, and the identity leaves $X$ alone — that is the justification, and it is a mark.  (M3)  WHY: ✔Because the inverse must sit in front on both sides for the equation to balance | ✘Because a matrix equation can be divided through by $A$ | ✘Because $A^{-1}$ may go on whichever side is easier
  4. Work the product row by column: $X = \begin{pmatrix}2 & -1 \\ -5 & 3\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}7 \\ 12\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}2 \\ 1\end{pmatrix}$.   — Check it by substituting back: $A X$ comes to $(7 ; 12)$, which is $B$. On a calculator paper that check costs nothing.  (W1)
  FINAL: $X = \begin{pmatrix}2 \\ 1\end{pmatrix}$
  TWIN: It is given that $AX = B$, where $A = \begin{pmatrix}5 & 3 \\ 3 & 2\end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix}4 \\ 3\end{pmatrix}$. Find the matrix $X$.  → matrix {"kind":"matrix","rows":2,"cols":1,"entries":[["-1"],["3"]],"tolerance":{"type":"absolute","value":0.0005}}

### we.fm.u1.matrix-equations.02 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: It is given that $AX + C = B$, where $A = \begin{pmatrix}4 & 7 \\ 1 & 2\end{pmatrix}$, $C = \begin{pmatrix}3 \\ 1\end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix}11 \\ 4\end{pmatrix}$.
Find the matrix $X$.
  FIG: svg — Four boxed matrix equations with the method named under each, including the combined form.
  1. Sort it: there is an added matrix and a multiplying one, so the added one goes first.   — The order of operations is the ordinary one. Reaching for the inverse while $C$ is still there solves a different equation. No mark is going for this line; it decides where the marks below come from.
  2. Take $C$ from both sides: $AX = B - C = \begin{pmatrix}11 \\ 4\end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix}3 \\ 1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}8 \\ 3\end{pmatrix}$.   — Now the equation is the familiar kind, with a single column on the right.  (MW1)
  3. $\det A = 1$, so $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix}2 & -7 \\ -1 & 4\end{pmatrix}$, and $X = A^{-1}(B - C)$.   — The inverse multiplies the whole of $B - C$, so the bracket stays until the product is worked out.  (M2,M3)
  4. $X = \begin{pmatrix}2 & -7 \\ -1 & 4\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}8 \\ 3\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}-5 \\ 4\end{pmatrix}$.   — Substituting back, $AX + C$ comes to $(11 ; 4)$, which is $B$.  (W1)
  FINAL: $X = \begin{pmatrix}-5 \\ 4\end{pmatrix}$
  TWIN: It is given that $AX + C = B$, where $A = \begin{pmatrix}5 & 2 \\ 2 & 1\end{pmatrix}$, $C = \begin{pmatrix}2 \\ 1\end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix}15 \\ 6\end{pmatrix}$. ## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.matrix-equations.pre (pre) [draft]
- p1 (The inverse of a 2 by 2): Three quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new method, so answer from what you already know.
What is the inverse of $\begin{pmatrix}3 & 1 \\ 5 & 2\end{pmatrix}$?
    ✔ $\begin{pmatrix}2 & -1 \\ -5 & 3\end{pmatrix}$ — The determinant is $1$, so swapping and negating is the whole of it.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}-2 & 1 \\ 5 & -3\end{pmatrix}$ — Every sign has been changed. Only the other diagonal changes sign.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}3 & -1 \\ -5 & 2\end{pmatrix}$ — The signs changed but the leading diagonal never swapped.
- p2 (Undoing an addition): Solve $x + 7 = 12$.
    ✔ $x = 5$ — Take $7$ from both sides. A matrix equation with an added matrix works the same way.
    ✘ $x = 19$ — The $7$ has been added rather than taken away.
    ✘ $x = \frac{12}{7}$ — The $7$ is added to $x$, not multiplying it, so division does not undo it.
- p3 (Order in a matrix product): For two matrices $P$ and $Q$, is $PQ$ always the same as $QP$?
    ✔ No, the order changes the product — That is why an inverse has to be written on the same side of both sides of an equation.
    ✘ Yes, multiplication can be done in any order — That holds for numbers. Matrix products depend on the order, and often only one of the two exists.
    ✘ Only when both are $2 \times 2$ — Even two square matrices of the same size usually give different products in the two orders.

### dx.fm.u1.matrix-equations.post (post) [draft]
- d1 (Clearing an added matrix): An equation reads $A + X = B$. What is $X$?
    ✔ $B - A$ — Take $A$ from both sides, entry by entry.
    ✘ $A - B$ [fm.matrix.subtract-wrong-way] — The subtraction is the wrong way round, so every entry has the opposite sign.
    ✘ $A^{-1}B$ [fm.matrix.equation-undone-wrongly] — $A$ is added here, not multiplying, so no inverse is needed.
- d2 (Pre-multiplying by the inverse): An equation reads $AX = B$. What is $X$?
    ✔ $A^{-1}B$ — The inverse goes in front of both sides, so $A^{-1}A$ becomes the identity.
    ✘ $BA^{-1}$ [fm.matrix.inverse-order] — That is the inverse behind $B$. Because the order changes a product, it gives a different matrix.
    ✘ $\frac{B}{A}$ [fm.matrix.equation-undone-wrongly] — Division is not defined for matrices. The inverse is what replaces it.
- d3 (Working the product in the right order): Which of these is $X$ for $AX = B$, with $A = \begin{pmatrix}2 & 5 \\ 1 & 3\end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 4 \\ 2 & 3\end{pmatrix}$?
    ✔ $\begin{pmatrix}-7 & -3 \\ 3 & 2\end{pmatrix}$ — $A^{-1}B$, with the inverse in front.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}-1 & 3 \\ 3 & -4\end{pmatrix}$ [fm.matrix.inverse-order] — That is $BA^{-1}$, the inverse behind $B$.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}12 & 23 \\ 7 & 13\end{pmatrix}$ [fm.matrix.equation-undone-wrongly] — That is $AB$: the matrix itself was used where its inverse belonged.
- d4 (The order of operations in a combined equation): An equation reads $AX + C = B$. What is the first step?
    ✔ Take $C$ from both sides — Clear the added matrix first; only then does the inverse come in.
    ✘ Multiply both sides by $A^{-1}$ straight away [fm.matrix.equation-undone-wrongly] — That leaves $C$ inside the equation and solves a different one.
    ✘ Take $A$ from both sides [fm.matrix.equation-undone-wrongly] — $A$ is multiplying $X$, so subtracting it undoes nothing.
- d5 (A singular matrix in an equation): The matrix $S = \begin{pmatrix}2 & 6 \\ 1 & 3\end{pmatrix}$. Can the equation $SX = B$ be solved?
    ✔ No, because $\det S = 0$ and $S$ has no inverse — Without an inverse there is nothing to pre-multiply by, so the equation has no single solution.
    ✘ Yes, by dividing both sides by $S$ [fm.matrix.singular-not-recognised] — Matrices are never divided, and this one has no inverse either.
    ✘ Yes, because $S$ is $2 \times 2$ [fm.matrix.singular-not-recognised] — Being square is not enough. The determinant has to be something other than zero.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u1.matrix-equations.01 [draft] fm.matrix.inverse-order · earned MW1,M2 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2018-summer:FM1:Q5
STEM: Fionn was asked to find $X$ for $AX = B$, where $A = \begin{pmatrix}2 & 5 \\ 1 & 3\end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 4 \\ 2 & 3\end{pmatrix}$. His working:

    2. A inverse = (3 -5 ; -1 2)
  ✘ 3. X = B x A inverse
    4. X = (-1 3 ; 3 -4)
  WRONG: Lines 1 and 2 are both right: the determinant and the inverse are correct, and they carry the first two marks.
Line 3 puts the inverse behind $B$. Pre-multiplying means the inverse arrives in front of each side, so the product is $A^{-1}B$, not $BA^{-1}$.
Because the order of a matrix product changes the answer, line 4 is accurate arithmetic on the wrong product: the answer should be $(-7 -3 ; 3 2)$.
  FIX: X = A inverse x B / X = (-7 -3 ; 3 2)
  FEEDBACK: The determinant and the inverse are right, so the first two marks stand. The order mark and the answer mark both go with line 3, however accurate line 4 is. The habit that fixes it is to write the inverse in front of both sides on one line, before any multiplying: $A^{-1}AX = A^{-1}B$. Then the order is already settled by the time the arithmetic starts.
