## WORKED EXAMPLES
### we.fm.u1.matrix-inverse-2x2.01 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: The matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix}4 & 1 \\ 3 & 2\end{pmatrix}$.
Find $A^{-1}$.
  FIG: svg — Matrix A turned into its inverse in two moves: swapped and negated, then every entry divided by the determinant.
  1. $\det A = (4)(2) - (1)(3) = 5$.   — On its own line, before the matrix is touched. It earns the first mark, and it tells you straight away whether an inverse exists at all.  (MW1)
  2. Swap the leading diagonal and negate the other one: $\begin{pmatrix}2 & -1 \\ -3 & 4\end{pmatrix}$.   — Two separate actions, done in that order. The leading diagonal moves and keeps its signs; the other diagonal stays put and changes sign.  (M2)  WHY: ✔Because swapping and negating is what makes the product come out as the identity | ✘Because every entry of an inverse is the reciprocal of the original entry | ✘Because the two diagonals are always interchangeable
  3. Divide every entry by the determinant: $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{5}\begin{pmatrix}2 & -1 \\ -3 & 4\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\frac{2}{5} & -\frac{1}{5} \\ -\frac{3}{5} & \frac{4}{5}\end{pmatrix}$.   — Keep the $\frac{1}{5}$ outside the brackets or share it through all four entries, but do not do half of each. A determinant with a minus sign keeps it here.  (W1)
  4. Check: $A^{-1}A = \begin{pmatrix}\frac{2}{5} & -\frac{1}{5} \\ -\frac{3}{5} & \frac{4}{5}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}4 & 1 \\ 3 & 2\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$.   — It earns no mark of its own. It takes twenty seconds on a calculator paper, and it catches every sign slip in the three actions above.
  FINAL: $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix}\frac{2}{5} & -\frac{1}{5} \\ -\frac{3}{5} & \frac{4}{5}\end{pmatrix}$
  TWIN: Find $B^{-1}$ for $B = \begin{pmatrix}2 & 6 \\ 1 & 4\end{pmatrix}$.  → matrix {"kind":"matrix","rows":2,"cols":2,"entries":[["2","-3"],["-1/2","1"]],"tolerance":{"type":"absolute","value":0.0005}}

### we.fm.u1.matrix-inverse-2x2.02 [verified] paper FM1 calc
STEM: The matrix $N = \begin{pmatrix}-3 & 2 \\ 4 & -1\end{pmatrix}$.
Find $N^{-1}$.
  FIG: svg — The determinant of matrix N worked out on its own line as ad minus bc, coming out negative.
  1. $\det N = (-3)(-1) - (2)(4) = 3 - 8 = -5$.   — The leading diagonal holds two negatives, so write each product out rather than doing it in your head: $(-3)(-1)$ is $3$, not $-3$. The determinant is negative, and that sign has to survive.  (MW1)
  2. Swap and negate: $\begin{pmatrix}-1 & -2 \\ -4 & -3\end{pmatrix}$.   — Negating an entry that is already negative makes it positive. Write each one down rather than trusting the pattern.  (M2)
  3. Divide by $-5$, with brackets round it: $N^{-1} = \frac{1}{-5}\begin{pmatrix}-1 & -2 \\ -4 & -3\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{5} & \frac{2}{5} \\ \frac{4}{5} & \frac{3}{5}\end{pmatrix}$.   — This is the step the reports name. Writing $\frac{1}{5}$ instead would give $\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{5} & -\frac{2}{5} \\ -\frac{4}{5} & -\frac{3}{5}\end{pmatrix}$, with every sign wrong.  (W1)  WHY: ✔Because the reciprocal of a negative number is negative | ✘Because a determinant is always written as a positive number | ✘Because the minus signs in the matrix cancel it out
  4. Check: $N^{-1}N = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$.   — It earns no mark of its own. If the check gives anything other than the identity, the sign of the determinant is the first place to look.
  FINAL: $N^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{5} & \frac{2}{5} \\ \frac{4}{5} & \frac{3}{5}\end{pmatrix}$
  TWIN: Find $F^{-1}$ for $F = \begin{pmatrix}7 & 3 \\ 4 & 2\end{pmatrix}$.  → matrix {"kind":"matrix","rows":2,"cols":2,"entries":[["1","-3/2"],["-2","7/2"]],"tolerance":{"type":"absolute","value":0.0005}}

## DIAGNOSTICS
### dx.fm.u1.matrix-inverse-2x2.pre (pre) [draft]
- p1 (Multiplying two negatives): Three quick checks on what this lesson is built from. None of them is the new method, so answer from what you already know.
What is $(-3) \times (-1)$?
    ✔ $3$ — Two negatives multiply to a positive.
    ✘ $-3$ — The two minus signs cancel, so the product is positive.
    ✘ $-4$ — That is the sum. The determinant multiplies along each diagonal.
- p2 (Dividing a negative by a whole number): What is $\frac{1}{4}$ of $-3$, written as a decimal?
    ✔ $-0.75$ — Dividing a negative number leaves it negative.
    ✘ $0.75$ — The sign is unchanged by dividing, so the answer stays negative.
    ✘ $-1.33$ — That is $-4$ divided by $3$. Here it is $-3$ divided by $4$.
- p3 (Recognising the identity): Which matrix is the identity for two by two multiplication?
    ✔ $\begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ — Ones on the leading diagonal, zeros elsewhere. Multiplying by it changes nothing.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ — Every entry is one, which is not the identity: multiplying by it changes the matrix.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ — That is the zero matrix. It sends everything to zero.

### dx.fm.u1.matrix-inverse-2x2.post (post) [draft]
- d1 (The determinant): What is $\det A$ for $A = \begin{pmatrix}4 & 1 \\ 3 & 2\end{pmatrix}$?
    ✔ $5$ — $ad - bc$, with the leading diagonal first.
    ✘ $-5$ [fm.matrix.determinant-sign-reversed] — That is $bc - ad$, taken the other way round.
    ✘ $11$ [fm.matrix.determinant-products-added] — That is $ad + bc$, with the two products added. The determinant subtracts them: $8 - 3 = 5$.
- d2 (The singular case): A matrix has determinant $0$. What follows?
    ✔ It has no inverse, and it is called singular — The formula divides by the determinant, so there is nothing to divide by.
    ✘ Its inverse is the zero matrix [fm.matrix.singular-not-recognised] — No matrix at all multiplies it to give the identity, so there is no inverse to name.
    ✘ Its inverse is the identity [fm.matrix.singular-not-recognised] — The identity is its own inverse. A singular matrix has none.
- d3 (The three actions): Which of these is $A^{-1}$ for $A = \begin{pmatrix}4 & 1 \\ 3 & 2\end{pmatrix}$, where $\det A = 5$?
    ✔ $\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2}{5} & -\frac{1}{5} \\ -\frac{3}{5} & \frac{4}{5}\end{pmatrix}$ — Swapped, negated and then divided by $5$.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}2 & -1 \\ -3 & 4\end{pmatrix}$ [fm.matrix.inverse-not-divided-by-det] — Swapped and negated, and then never divided by the determinant.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{5} & -\frac{1}{5} \\ -\frac{3}{5} & \frac{2}{5}\end{pmatrix}$ [fm.matrix.adjugate-not-swapped] — The signs changed but the leading diagonal never swapped.
    ✘ $\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2}{5} & -1 \\ -3 & 4\end{pmatrix}$ [fm.matrix.det-factor-on-one-entry-only] — Only the first entry was divided by the determinant; the other three were left as they were.
- d4 (Keeping the sign of a negative determinant): A matrix has determinant $-5$. What factor goes in front of the swapped and negated matrix?
    ✔ $\frac{1}{-5}$ — The reciprocal of a negative number is negative, and the brackets keep it that way.
    ✘ $\frac{1}{5}$ [fm.matrix.reciprocal-sign-dropped] — The minus sign has been dropped. That single slip changes all four entries, and the reports name it directly.
    ✘ $-5$ [fm.matrix.inverse-not-divided-by-det] — The determinant multiplies rather than divides here. The factor is its reciprocal.
- d5 (The check): How do you check that a matrix you have written down really is the inverse?
    ✔ Multiply it by the original matrix and look for the identity — That is the definition of an inverse, and it catches sign slips in seconds.
    ✘ Add it to the original matrix and look for the zero matrix — An inverse undoes a multiplication, not an addition.
    ✘ Check that every entry is the reciprocal of the original entry [fm.matrix.elementwise-product] — An inverse is not built entry by entry; the whole matrix works together.

## FIND THE MISTAKE
### ftm.fm.u1.matrix-inverse-2x2.01 [draft] fm.matrix.reciprocal-sign-dropped · earned MW1,M2 · ccea-cer:further-maths:2025-summer:FM1:Q5
STEM: Daithí was asked to find $N^{-1}$, where $N = \begin{pmatrix}-3 & 2 \\ 4 & -1\end{pmatrix}$. His working:
    1. det N = (-3)(-1) - (2)(4) = -5
    2. swap and negate: (-1 -2 ; -4 -3)
  ✘ 3. N inverse = 1/5 x (-1 -2 ; -4 -3)
    4. N inverse = (-1/5 -2/5 ; -4/5 -3/5)
  WRONG: Lines 1 and 2 are both right, and they carry most of the marks: the determinant is correct, sign included, and the swapping and negating is exactly right.
Line 3 writes the reciprocal as $\frac{1}{5}$, dropping the minus sign that line 1 had already found. The factor is $\frac{1}{-5}$.
Because the factor multiplies all four entries, one dropped sign makes every entry wrong: the answer should be $(1/5 2/5 ; 4/5 3/5)$.
  FIX: N inverse = 1/(-5) x (-1 -2 ; -4 -3) / N inverse = (1/5 2/5 ; 4/5 3/5)
  FEEDBACK: The determinant and the arrangement are both right, so the method marks stand. The fix is a pair of brackets: write the factor as one over the determinant in brackets, sign attached, straight after you work the determinant out. Then check by multiplying back — the identity will not appear if a sign is wrong.

- rp.fm.u1.matrix-inverse-2x2.02 [draft] (formula) Give the three actions that turn a two by two matrix into its inverse.  →  Swap the leading diagonal, change the signs on the other diagonal, and divide every entry by the determinant.
- rp.fm.u1.matrix-inverse-2x2.03 [draft] (qa) When does a matrix have no inverse, and what is it called?  →  When its determinant is zero, because the formula divides by the determinant. Such a matrix is called singular.
- rp.fm.u1.matrix-inverse-2x2.04 [draft] (trap) A determinant comes out as $-5$. What is the factor in front of the swapped and negated matrix?  →  $\frac{1}{-5}$, with the minus sign kept. Writing $\frac{1}{5}$ changes the sign of all four entries.
- rp.fm.u1.matrix-inverse-2x2.05 [draft] (procedure) How do you check an inverse you have just written down?  →  Multiply it by the original matrix. The answer should be the identity, with ones on the leading diagonal and zeros elsewhere.
- rp.fm.u1.matrix-inverse-2x2.06 [draft] (trap) What does the Unit 1 formula sheet give you about matrices?  →  Nothing. The determinant and the inverse formula are both recall, which is why they are worth holding as a picture of three actions.
- rp.fm.u1.matrix-inverse-2x2.07 [draft] (procedure) A question asks why an equation involving a matrix cannot be solved. What two things does the answer need?  →  That the determinant is zero, and the name of the matrix whose determinant it is: for example, B has no inverse because det B = 0.
